# Generative direction and control budget

## Principle

Lock the commercial idea and the cinematic envelope; delegate the motion choreography that benefits from a video model. More written detail does not always mean more control. Prefer macro-specific direction with micro freedom over frame-by-frame prescription when fluidity, material response, camera movement, or editorial polish is the viewer value.

## Three control modes

### `directed_generation`

Use for a coherent visual sequence whose quality depends on organic camera behavior, spatial depth, material physics, secondary motion, or a unified premium finish. Lock:

- the one visual concept or physical metaphor;
- chronological narrative arc and must-see events;
- subject identity and product facts that cannot drift;
- visual taste, lighting, palette, material behavior, and emotional movement;
- starting situation, required end state, and explicit no-gos;
- exact surfaces that will be replaced or composited later.

Delegate exact trajectories, focus pulls, secondary motion, camera micro-timing, cloth/debris choreography, and transition details unless one is the claim itself. Do not require start, peak, and endpoint keyframes by default.

### `controlled_generation`

Use when the result must connect to a real adjacent frame, preserve a named subject or product, hit a strict endpoint, or reproduce a specific motion path. Add only the control that solves the named risk: first frame, last frame, identity references, motion reference, or a verified intermediate anchor.

Every added control needs a `control_reason`. Remove controls that merely restate the prompt or make the acceptable output unnecessarily narrow.

### `deterministic_composition`

Use for exact Japanese, UI, logo, CTA, price, legal copy, readable reports, final timing, safe zones, and the End Card. Remotion or FFmpeg may also cut, crop, mask, track, layer, mix, and encode accepted generated plates.

Do not use deterministic composition as the default primary motion engine for a scene whose viewer value depends on two or more high-pressure motion qualities, unless a natural-speed human Taste review approves it.

## Generation-unit rule

Use the fewest coherent generation units that preserve the approved idea. A single unit may include several camera moves or meaningful cuts when the model and current endpoint support a contiguous timeline and the subject, world, and visual metaphor remain coherent.

Split only at a named boundary:

- semantic or location change;
- camera reset that the model cannot reliably express;
- identity or product-consistency risk;
- exact-surface or privacy boundary;
- verified provider duration, input, or edit limit;
- an accepted hard cut that does not require visual continuity.

Do not split at every storyboard Beat. Do not combine an entire ad merely because a model supports a long duration; exact copy, UI, proof, and CTA still need deterministic ownership.

## Direction package

For every generative unit, write:

- `viewer_job`;
- `control_mode`;
- `one_visual_concept`;
- `chronological_arc`;
- `must_see_events`;
- `taste_and_finish`;
- `cinematography`;
- `material_and_motion_behavior`;
- `macro_locks`;
- `delegated_to_model`;
- `exact_surface_plan`, including content authority, geometry owner, motion owner, world relation, binding mode, and readable windows;
- `end_state`;
- `rejection_criteria`.

When a selected provider has an official prompt adapter or Skill, compile this direction package into that provider's current format after capability selection. Do not copy a provider-specific prompt format into the universal production contract.

## Review

Judge the Raw first as a coherent piece of moving image:

- does the visual concept read without explanatory overlays;
- does motion progress rather than oscillate or reverse unintentionally;
- does the camera feel authored rather than mechanically interpolated;
- do materials, weight, and secondary motion feel plausible for the chosen style;
- does the sequence reach the required end state;
- can exact text and UI be composited without flattening the image;
- does the result retain enough negative space and timing for copy, narration, and CTA.

Reject a visually polished Raw when it misses the approved commercial event. Do not repair a failed central event with more overlays.
