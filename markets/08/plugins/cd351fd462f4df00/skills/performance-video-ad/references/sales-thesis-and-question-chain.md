# Sales Thesis and Question Chain

## Purpose

Use this contract between Source Coverage and Creative Premise approval. Preserve the audience's lived language, compress the offer into one useful concept, and decide one reason the ad may earn attention before exploring executions.

This is a planning hypothesis, not a performance claim. Do not write `will convert`, `definitely sells`, or an equivalent guarantee.

## Iterative loop

Do not force a linear order inside this stage. Iterate among:

`Raw Moment -> Product Concept <-> Sales Question Chain <-> Single Sell Reason`

Return to source acquisition when the result becomes generic or unsupported. Do not solve weak source understanding by inventing sharper copy.

### Raw Moment

Preserve at least one source-grounded scene in the customer's language. Record:

- the concrete situation;
- a short verbatim fragment or faithful paraphrase;
- what the person did or stopped doing;
- the felt or professional stake;
- derivation type and source refs.

Summaries such as `performance is bad` are insufficient when the source contains a usable moment such as `CPAは下がった。でも、成約しない人ばかりなんですが`.

### Product Concept

Separate what the offer is from how the current ad dramatizes it. Capture:

- `shorthand`: a short useful name, not a slogan contest;
- `what_it_is`;
- `customer_future`;
- `reason_to_believe`.

The product concept remains stable across multiple Creative Territories. The communication concept may change by Territory.

### Communication Concept

Write one sentence that describes the ad's dramatic idea. It should imply a scene, contradiction, or attitude—not restate the service description.

### Single Sell Reason

Write one sentence explaining why this audience may respond to this ad. It must combine audience relevance, one tension or desire, and the offer connection. Treat it as a causal hypothesis to test, never as proof that the ad will win.

Reject a thesis that needs a list of unrelated reasons. Supporting proof may be plural; the response hypothesis stays singular.

### Stop and Continue Reasons

Keep these separate:

- `stop_reason`: why the first frame or event interrupts scrolling;
- `continue_reason`: what unresolved question or desired progress earns the next beat.

Do not require a fixed number of cuts. A quiet work incident may stop attention with one change; a fast organic format may need several beats. Judge comprehension, tension, and continuation—not a universal cut count.

### Sales Question Chain

List three to six viewer questions in the order they are likely to arise. Give each question one answer job; do not write final copy here.

Typical roles include:

1. recognition: `Is this my situation?`
2. category: `What is this?`
3. mechanism or credibility: `Why might this be different?`
4. suitability or boundary: `Can it help a case like mine without replacing the current team?`
5. next action: `What happens if I click?`

The chain is context-dependent. Do not force PASONA or another named formula when the viewer's actual question order differs.

## Organic-reference transfer

Study adjacent ads for proof and category conventions, but allow execution grammar to come from distant organic formats. Record what is borrowed and what is changed. Borrow an attention mechanism, camera grammar, pacing pattern, or reveal—not another creator's protected footage, identity, or distinctive expression.

## Gate

Before Creative Premise approval, require:

- at least one source-grounded Raw Moment;
- a distinct product and communication concept;
- one Single Sell Reason;
- distinct stop and continue reasons;
- a three-to-six-step Sales Question Chain;
- `performance_claim: false`;
- source refs for every Raw Moment and question.

The existing Creative Premise approval covers this thesis. Do not add a separate human approval unless a material offer conflict remains.
