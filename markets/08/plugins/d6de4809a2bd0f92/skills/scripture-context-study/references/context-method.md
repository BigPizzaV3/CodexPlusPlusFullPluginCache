# Context Method

## Audit sequence

1. **Reference:** Is the book, chapter, verse, and range valid?
2. **Text boundary:** What comes immediately before and after?
3. **Genre:** Is this law, narrative, wisdom, poetry, prophecy, Gospel material, or epistle?
4. **Voice:** Who narrates or writes? Who speaks inside the passage?
5. **Audience:** Who first receives the speech, command, promise, warning, or letter?
6. **Agency:** Who acts, who receives the action, and who is affected?
7. **Order:** In what sequence does the text report events or arguments?
8. **Explicit claims:** What is directly stated?
9. **Inferences:** What follows reasonably, and how strong is the evidence?
10. **Application bridge:** What principle can cross contexts without replacing the original actors or audience?
11. **Cross-references:** Is each related passage an explicit citation, shared theme, title background, or teaching anchor?
12. **Cautions:** What common slogan, context-loss error, or disputed reading should be surfaced?

## Review standard

A strong study is reproducible. Another reader should be able to see which verse supports an observation, which step is inference, and where a present-day application begins.

Mechanical validation proves that references and bundled text exist. It does not prove that an interpretation is faithful, complete, pastorally wise, or suitable for publication.
