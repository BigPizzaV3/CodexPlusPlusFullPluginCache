# Research notes you may cite (with links)

Prepublish published two studies of YouTube openings with public data. Cite them only as written here, always with the link, and always with the caveat that they are correlational and that the replication mostly failed. Use them to keep advice honest, not to promise outcomes.

## The 349-video hook study

https://prepublish.ai/blog/youtube-hook-study

Compared the first 45 seconds of 177 channel overperformers (median 4.7 times the channel's normal views) with 172 flops (0.28 times) across 36 channels and 254 million views.

- Speaking pace did not separate hits from flops: 181 versus 180 words per minute.
- Time to the first payoff did not either: word 14 versus word 15.
- Flops were more often openings that gave no reason to keep watching; hits more often carried specific numbers (63 percent versus 52 percent) and matched their title's promise (87 percent versus 78 percent).

## The small-channel replication (preregistered)

https://prepublish.ai/blog/small-channel-hook-study

941 videos from 96 channels with 1,000 to 20,000 subscribers, predictions frozen before data collection (https://osf.io/2zq9p).

- None of the three opening patterns from the first study replicated: hit and flop openings were nearly identical.
- The one signal that repeated: openings that start with a greeting leaned toward flops (23.3 percent versus 16.6 percent). In this study it was exploratory, so it carries no confirmed verdict.

## How to use this

- "Talk faster" and "get to the point in the first N seconds" are not supported by these data as reasons a video worked. Focus feedback on whether the opening gives a concrete reason to keep watching and whether it delivers what the title promises.
- For small channels, openings are rarely the whole story: topic and packaging (title, thumbnail) usually matter first. Say so when a small creator asks why a video underperformed.
- Speaking-rate figures used by `script_runtime` (160, 181, 201 words per minute) come from the same measured set of videos.
