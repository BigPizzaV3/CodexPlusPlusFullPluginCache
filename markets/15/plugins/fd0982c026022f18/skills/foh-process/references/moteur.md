# Industrial HOF — Knowledge base (cognitive blocks)

<a id="b0002"></a>

The GPT's knowledge file. It carries the engine, the reading grid, and the cognitive blocks. The system prompt orchestrates it; the substance is here. Drawn directly from the FOH carnets "Pratiques de Fiabilisation des Interventions / Performance Humaine" (reliability-of-intervention practices) and "Analyse de Risques" (risk analysis).

<a id="b0003"></a>

---

<a id="b0004"></a>

## 0. Frame and posture

<a id="b0005"></a>

### 0.1 Purpose
Help an organization make its **real work explicit, structured, reliable, and usable — including by an AI**. Many companies want to adopt AI while having nothing formalized: the knowledge lives in people's heads. Formalizing a process and briefing an AI are the same gesture. The HOF differentiator: we formalize the **real** work (prescribed/real gap at the core), not the prescribed fiction — an AI briefed on a fiction produces an efficient fiction that breaks on contact with the field.

<a id="b0006"></a>

### 0.2 Anchoring and lineage of the engine
HOF are the **third pillar of safety culture** (with technical reliability and formal organization) — repères méthodologiques en facteurs humains et organisationnels approach. The **SAED-OARPIR** engine is not a distant transposition: **SAED is defined in the FOH carnet as the 7th reliability-of-intervention practice** (the "SAED call protocol"). Sylvain Léauté's concept extends this industrial gesture toward human-AI dialogue (*Briefing pour l'IA*). Display it as a foundation, not as an argument from authority. (SAED ≈ SBAR in English: Situation, Background, Assessment, Request.)

<a id="b0007"></a>

### 0.3 Transversal posture (ALL blocks)
1. **Formalize the real, not the fiction.** The prescribed/real gap is central information, not a fault.
2. **Refutability.** The analysis can be contradicted by the field — that is a quality.
3. **Human error is never a root cause** (the carnet states it explicitly): go back to the conditions. Never a culprit.
4. **Regulated safety + managed safety.** Pre-planned barriers AND room for adaptation.
5. **Anti-drift / anti-over-formalization.** A mechanically applied practice protects nothing. "Observing an unsuitable practice without giving feedback validates it implicitly."

<a id="b0008"></a>

### 0.4 Frameworks referenced
FHOS state of the art (Daniellou, Simard, Boissières — organisme source 2010); FHOS, questions to progress (2012); in-depth event analysis (organisme source 2014-04); drift of PFI (Merle, Largier); FOH carnets PFI & Risk Analysis; *Briefing pour l'IA* (Léauté).

## 1. Internal engine (invariant, back-end of every block)

<a id="b0011"></a>

### 1.1 SAED — entering the dialogue / gathering the real
Structure from the carnet's **SAED call protocol** (communication failure is a major root cause of events).
- **S — Situation**: identity/role, location, activity, reason for the call, current situation on objective facts.
- **A — Antécédents** (Background): history (organisme source it happened before? usual state? since when organisme source it evolved?), lessons learned (REX), document version, how knowledge is transmitted, why formalize now.
- **É — Évaluation** (Assessment): interpretation of the evolution, the essential reason for concern, HOF reading (what is clear, what is missing, where the real knowledge lives).
- **D — Demande** (Request): clear, precise, concise need, with deadlines. We **request**, we do not recommend. Closing loop: the other party **repeats back** what they understood, then **concludes with their decision**.

<a id="b0012"></a>

### 1.2 OARPIR — structure and make reliable (six levers)
- **O — Objectif** (Objective): the real goal, before acting.
- **A — Action**: the concrete sequence of steps, as actually done.
- **R — Risque** (Risk): anything that can make the outcome betray the objective (tacit steps, dependence on one person, special cases).
- **P — Parade** (Safeguard): a concrete measure planned in advance; check it does not create a new risk.
- **I — Indicateur** (Indicator): how you see that it holds, that it is understandable and reproducible.
- **R — Régulation** (Regulation): adjustment along the way and feedback loop.

<a id="b0013"></a>

> The CAUSES block (§3.6) is retrospective: it relies on in-depth event analysis; Regulation returns there as durable corrective actions.

## 2. HOF reading grid (transversal — never an entry point)

<a id="b0016"></a>

| HOF dimension (organisme source) | Relational reading | To question |
|---|---|---|
| **The individual** | relation to the task | competence, workload, fatigue, experience, risk perception |
| **Work situations** | human–machine / human–environment | tools, interfaces, space, access, noise, lighting, conditions |
| **Work collectives** | human–human | communication, cross-check, handover, trades at the interface |
| **Organization and management** | human–document / human–organization | rules, procedures, scheduling, trade-offs, resources, management line |

<a id="b0017"></a>

The Risk carnet mobilizes the same logic as **5M** (Milieu, Matériel, Matières, Main d'œuvre, Méthodes — setting, equipment, materials, workforce, methods): the operational declension of this grid.

## 3. The cognitive blocks

<a id="b0020"></a>

One **spine** (FORMALIZATION) and **six depth blocks** (Document, PFI, Risk, Environment, Causes, Field visit). The depth blocks are usable alone or called from formalization. Constant structure: **trigger · HOF dimensions · SAED sequence · OARPIR application · guardrails · output format · closing**.

<a id="b0021"></a>

---

<a id="b0022"></a>

### 3.1 FORMALIZATION — structure a work process (spine)

<a id="b0023"></a>

**Trigger.** "We have nothing formalized / we want to move to AI but nothing is clear / document a know-how / prepare a brief for an AI."

<a id="b0024"></a>

**HOF dimensions.** All four; mainly work situations, collectives, organization.

<a id="b0025"></a>

**SAED sequence.** S: which process, who does it. A: any written trace even partial? oral/mentoring transmission? why now (AI, departure of an expert, growth, quality)? É: where the real knowledge lives, prescribed/real gap, knacks and special cases known only to veterans. D: create, clarify, or prepare an AI brief.

<a id="b0026"></a>

**OARPIR application.** O: goal and expected result. A: real sequence (inputs → action → outputs, decision points). R: tacit steps never written, dependence on one person, exceptions, breakpoints. P: make the knack explicit, plan for exceptions, secure the knowledge against an expert's departure. I: can a third party who doesn't know it follow it? can an AI execute it without inventing? R: living process, updated when the real changes.

<a id="b0027"></a>

**Guardrails.** Formalize the real, not the fiction. Get the knowledge from those who do the work. Don't over-formalize (capture room for maneuver too). Distinguish the stable (formalizable) from situated judgment (name it, don't freeze it). To capture the real, the **field visit** (§3.7) is the preferred gathering tool.

<a id="b0028"></a>

**Output format.** Structured process: objective, real steps, decision points, exceptions/special cases, roles, indicators of correct execution — **readable by a human AND ready as an AI brief** (SAED header). Offer to chain to Risk / Document / PFI.

<a id="b0029"></a>

**Closing.** "Formalizing is not writing the dreamed work: it is making the real work visible, so that another — human or AI — can take it over without going wrong."

<a id="b0030"></a>

---

<a id="b0031"></a>

### 3.2 DOCUMENT — secure a work document

<a id="b0032"></a>

**Trigger.** Make a procedure, work instruction, guideline, job sheet, or check-list reliable.

<a id="b0033"></a>

**HOF dimensions.** Organization (human–document), work situations. Tension: **prescribed/real**.

<a id="b0034"></a>

**SAED sequence.** S: which document, which activity, who, when. A: version, origin, REX, gaps noted. É: readability, ambiguities, contradictions, gap to the real, what it forces people to do "on the sly." D: clarify / update / simplify / check compliance.

<a id="b0035"></a>

**OARPIR application.** O: what the document guarantees. A: which real action it frames, and when the operator actually reads it. R: ambiguity, illegibility, unrealistic steps, gap pushing to transgression. P: rewording, removing the superfluous, diagram over text, field test. I: **the carnet's decisive test — "could a newly qualified person use it correctly the very first time?"** R: living update circuit.

<a id="b0036"></a>

**Guardrails.** More text ≠ more safety. A document describing work nobody does that way is a risk. Confront with real usage before freezing.

<a id="b0037"></a>

**Output format.** Short diagnosis + points to fix by criticality + rewording of critical passages + one prescribed/real watch point.

<a id="b0038"></a>

**Closing.** "A good document describes the real work; a bad one forces you to betray it to succeed."

### 3.3 PFI — make the activity reliable through the reliability-of-intervention practices

<a id="b0041"></a>

**Trigger.** Secure how an intervention unfolds, prepare a worksite, set up the right reflexes, organize a handover.

<a id="b0042"></a>

**HOF dimensions.** Work situations (human–machine) and collectives (human–human).

<a id="b0043"></a>

**The eight practices of the FOH carnet.**
1. **Check-list** — combines operating mode and recording; anti-omission, created just before by the operator, filled in before/during/after, validated by a mark.
2. **Pré-job briefing** (pre-job briefing) — organized dialogue operators/lead; visualize phases, risks and safeguards, critical steps (no later check, no reverse), surveillance points, worst-case scenario, communication modes, appropriation of REX. **Six key points (output template): Expected result · Risks (incl. the worst) · Error-prone situations · Safeguards (incl. reliability practices) · REX · Go/No-Go.**
3. **Minute d'arrêt** (stop-and-think) — BEFORE any risky intervention, on INTERRUPTION, or facing the UNEXPECTED. 360° look (floor/ceiling), verify right equipment + right procedure + check-list. On the unexpected: stop early, re-analyze the risk, take advice via SAED, resume only once doubts are lifted.
4. **Communication sécurisée** (secured communication) — information **Clear, Complete, Targeted (3 C)**. **Three-way** protocol: send → repeat back identically (read-back) → confirm ("that's correct").
5. **Contrôle croisé** (peer-check) — a second look **BEFORE** the action (independent, by a competent colleague): the operator announces the action by voice and a gesture sketch; the checker verifies and gives agreement; then the action. On doubt: No-Go (right to doubt).
6. **Autocontrôle** (self-check) — **BEFORE** each new action: index finger on the procedure (read the action aloud) + other index on the equipment (read the label). A professional act, not a thought.
7. **SAED call protocol** — structured communication on anomaly or request for support (see §1.1); also valid in writing (emails, technical file).
8. **Débriefing** (debrief) — immediately AFTER, by the operators to the manager. **Guide: was the task done as planned? procedures correct? usable first-time by a newly qualified person? sufficient means? did the pre-job identify all risks? time problems? management support? traps for next time? what to improve?** Trace simply (weak-signal database).

<a id="b0044"></a>

*Other good practices:* "I write what I will do, I do what I wrote, I write what I did"; precise verbs ("inject" rather than "do"); raise weak signals; shared vigilance.

<a id="b0045"></a>

**Collective practice — SHIFT HANDOVER (relève de quart)** (human–human continuity). A full-fledged operating activity, delicate because it marks a discontinuity. Two risks: **missing information** (omissions, overload) and **lacking perspective** (everything is transmitted but the diagnoses were wrong). A good handover ensures continuity, eases the coming shift, gives perspective. It is **prepared** throughout the shift (writing = making reliable; date and quantify everything), **exchanged** in a calm, defined place close to the information sources, without intermediaries ("tell X to warn Y" = risk), and **controlled** as a team (briefing: everyone speaks, in order, starting with the least senior; the manager checks consistency and flags the sensitive activities requiring a pre-job). Interruptions are reserved for emergencies.

<a id="b0046"></a>

**OARPIR application.** OARPIR serves to **write the pre-job briefing** (the 6 key points above) and to choose the practices relevant to *this* intervention.

<a id="b0047"></a>

**Guardrails (anti-drift — essential).** PFI are not a compliance layer. Don't tick all eight on principle: choose the useful practices. The practice serves the questioning attitude, not form-filling.

<a id="b0048"></a>

**Output format.** Ready-to-use pre-job briefing (6 key points) + chosen practices and why + stop-and-think triggers + secured-communication points + debrief reminder.

<a id="b0049"></a>

**Closing.** "A reliability practice is worth the vigilance it sustains, never the box it fills."

### 3.4 RISK — risk analysis of an activity (prospective)

<a id="b0052"></a>

**Trigger.** Analyze the risks of an activity before launching it, or write a risk analysis (ADR).

<a id="b0053"></a>

**Sense (carnet).** A quality analysis **does not aim to remove risk but to master it**; it helps decide, act knowingly, adapt control to the stakes. It addresses the operator first.

<a id="b0054"></a>

**5-step method (core of the block).**
1. **Work situation**: goal of the action? what does the activity concretely consist of?
2. **Requirements**: the "highway code," limits to respect (operation, environment, conventional safety, technical, deadlines, costs). Evolving → seek advice from specialist support.
3. **Means**: human, material, organizational, context/environment, technique.
4. **Risk scenarios**: possible causes of non-compliance with requirements, means failures, **interface risks** in multi-trade settings.
5. **Safeguards**: choose, define "who does what," **check the safeguard does not create a new risk**, plan a **fallback state** depending on the stakes.

<a id="b0055"></a>

**Domain aid grid (summarized — to deploy as the context requires).**
- **Operation safety (5M reading)**: Setting (seismic-event, buried equipment, alarms, scaffolding, equipment confusion) · Equipment (mis-identification, special temporary devices/DMP, new tooling, spare parts, foreign body, dequalification, common-mode) · Materials (non-approved product/equipment, loss of qualification) · Workforce (operator-linked common-mode, competence/certification, regulatory time, fatigue) · Methods (unvalidated/untested procedure, document unsuited to the installation state). + automatic actions (loss of protection/safeguard, tripping of redundant equipment, alarms), material confinement, support functions, incompatibility with other interventions, fallback solution.
- **Fire**: heat/spark source, flammable products, calorific load, fire-sector breach (fire dampers, hatches), at-risk room, impact on fire detection/protection.
- **Environment**: waste (ordinary/special), effluents, gas releases, noise, drips/spills (ground, drains, river).
- **Conventional safety**: electrical shock, falls (height, ladder, hatch, scaffolding, removal of collective protections), hazardous atmospheres/substances (asbestos, gases, toxics), lifting and manual handling, emergency access, lighting, noise (>85 dB), thermal environments. *NB: base PPE (helmet, safety shoes) are not safeguards to list.*

<a id="b0056"></a>

**Transverse analysis** (when several trades or interfering risks). Distinguish **Pilot** (activity owner, decides) and **Facilitator** (guarantor of method). Steps: verify present competences → recall the purpose, break into interventions, have each trade describe its work ("where they put their fingers") and its upstream/downstream requirements → identify **interface risks**. **Cross the preparation/realization views** via a briefing (concise, periodic, preparer present, REX sharing, duty to alert on a planned/real gap).

<a id="b0057"></a>

**Traceability (ADR).** Purposes: communicate to users, build confidence, capitalize, demonstrate compliance. Trace what is relevant **for the operator**. Free support (ADR form, quality plan, work order/operating mode...). ADR **prescribed** notably for availability impact, fire risk, safety/environment risk, foreign body, temporary installation modification (MTI/DMP).

<a id="b0058"></a>

**Guardrails.** Never exhaustive — name what you don't know. Don't convert everything into a regulated barrier (preserve the managed margin). Cross the views rather than analyze in a back room.

<a id="b0059"></a>

**Output format.** Table phases → risks (by 5M / domains) → safeguards (regulated/managed) → alert indicators + interfaces + fallback state + what is traced and where.

<a id="b0060"></a>

**Closing.** "You do not remove risk; you equip it with safeguards and keep a hand free for the unexpected."

### 3.5 ENVIRONMENT — reorganize a work environment

<a id="b0063"></a>

**Trigger.** Rethink the layout, material organization, conditions of a workstation or room.

<a id="b0064"></a>

**HOF dimensions.** Work situations (human–environment), collectives.

<a id="b0065"></a>

**SAED sequence.** S: which environment, which activity, who. A: why now, what was tried, complaints/incidents tied to the place. É: how the environment shapes real activity (what it eases/constrains, informal arrangements). D: re-layout / reorganize flows / improve conditions.

<a id="b0066"></a>

**"Setting" reading (carnet).** Noise (altered communication; historical numerical examples require local verification), lighting (hidden traps, glare), thermal environments, emergency access and exits, circulation and co-activity, proximity of sensitive equipment.

<a id="b0067"></a>

**OARPIR application.** O: what to improve (safety, fatigue, efficiency, communication). A: concrete changes. R: side effects (a gain here, a risk elsewhere). P: layouts, signage, phasing, adaptation period. I: operators' feedback, near-misses after change, usage gaps. R: adjustment after commissioning.

<a id="b0068"></a>

**Guardrails.** The environment interacts with organization and collective. Don't design from above: consult those who work there before freezing. Plan re-appropriation time.

<a id="b0069"></a>

**Output format.** HOF reading of the current environment (what it imposes) + proposals with effects and side effects + transition plan + points to validate with operators.

<a id="b0070"></a>

**Closing.** "The environment is not a backdrop: it partly writes the work gesture."

### 3.6 CAUSES — root causes of an incident or a "human error" (retrospective)

<a id="b0073"></a>

**Trigger.** Something happened / an "error" / a near-miss, understand why.

<a id="b0074"></a>

**Guiding principle (carnet + organisme source).** **Human error is never the sole root cause**: it is a starting point. **Communication failure is a major root cause.** Go beyond apparent causes to reach root causes (technical, human, organizational). No culprit.

<a id="b0075"></a>

**Sequence.** S: exact facts (no interpretation). A: context, conditions of the moment (workload, co-activity, equipment state, rules). É: reconstruct the chain without judgment. D: avoid recurrence / durable corrective actions.

<a id="b0076"></a>

**Method (in place of prospective OARPIR).** 1) Establish the facts. 2) Build the chain (cause-tree logic). 3) For each link, look for the **conditions** across the 4 HOF dimensions (why was this gesture possible, even logical, for the operator?). 4) Distinguish immediate / root causes (installed prescribed/real gap, ignored weak signals). 5) **Regulation**: corrective actions on **conditions**, not on "vigilance"; check there is no new procedural drift.

<a id="b0077"></a>

**Toolbox.** Cause tree, bow-tie, dominoes, point-pivot, HPES (per severity/time: Alarm, Accimap, Tripod Beta, CREAM).

<a id="b0078"></a>

**Guardrails.** "Human error" as a conclusion = a truncated analysis. The person involved is a source, not an accused. "More attention / more rigor" corrects nothing.

<a id="b0079"></a>

**Output format.** Chain of facts + tree of immediate / root causes by HOF dimension + corrective actions acting on conditions + anti-drift check. Chain to Document, Formalization or PFI if the fix runs through there.

<a id="b0080"></a>

**Closing.** "Behind every human error, conditions made that error possible — those are what we correct."

### 3.7 FIELD VISIT — observe the real and drive improvement (managerial)

<a id="b0083"></a>

**Trigger.** "Organize/prepare a field visit," "observe a real activity," "put work at the center of the hierarchy/agents dialogue." The manager's entry door. Also the tool for gathering the real to feed FORMALIZATION.

<a id="b0084"></a>

**HOF dimensions.** All four, seen from management: mainly collectives and organization.

<a id="b0085"></a>

**Five objectives (carnet).** Encourage and valorize good practices; recall and clarify requirements; ease the work (efficiency, conditions); animate teams by confronting viewpoints; animate management collectives on the lines of defense.

<a id="b0086"></a>

**Sequence (improvement loop).**
1. **Objectives defined**: target a domain (safety, error-prone situations, working conditions, lines of defense, reliability practices, competence...) and inform the team.
2. **Preparation**: know the requirements and reference framework of the chosen domain.
3. **Observation**: explain the approach, build trust, manage the interruption caused by one's presence (via PFI), **stay neutral and non-interfering**, seek to understand difficulties, establish **2 to 6 balanced findings** (positive/negative), give signs of recognition, be exemplary. (≈ 15 min prep + 30 min observation + 15 min feedback + 15 min report.)
4. **Feedback — short loop**: factual and precise (no judgment on the person), valorize the positive, signal gaps showing understanding and obtaining agreement on the risks, define solutions **with** the operators, display what one commits to handling, trace it. **Key principle: observing an unsuitable practice without giving feedback validates it implicitly.**
5. **Treatment — deferred loop**: trace and classify findings by lines of defense, prioritize (not all gaps are treated; renunciations are justified), assign owners, inform teams of the follow-up, support implementation, regularly evaluate the loop.

<a id="b0087"></a>

**Success conditions / failure factors.** Success: valorize the positive, seek to understand, frequent and short visits, exemplarity, know and share the reference frameworks, prioritize and communicate progress. Failure: not seeking to understand, seeing only what's wrong, not treating gaps.

<a id="b0088"></a>

**Guardrails.** Not an inspection for the prosecution. No finding without immediate feedback. No promise of treatment without a real deferred loop (management credibility depends on it).

<a id="b0089"></a>

**Output format.** Visit sheet: objective/domain, observation points, balanced findings, feedback (gaps + agreement on risks + co-built solutions), treatment commitments and owners. Feeds FORMALIZATION (capture the real), DOCUMENT (procedure/real gap) and CAUSES (weak signals).

<a id="b0090"></a>

**Closing.** "A visit without feedback validates the reality it uncovers; a visit without follow-up wears out the credibility that makes it useful."

<a id="b0092"></a>

## 4. Common output rules

<a id="b0093"></a>

- **One active block**; FORMALIZATION and FIELD VISIT may call the others; propose the chaining.
- Visibly distinguish **facts** (field) from **hypotheses** (assistant).
- When the real is missing (operators' viewpoint, exact version, conditions), **say so** instead of filling in.
- Any produced process/document must be able to serve as a **brief usable by an AI**: clear structure, explicit context, named exceptions.
- Operational register; at most one closing maxim.
- Never present an analysis as final: it is open to contradiction by the field.
