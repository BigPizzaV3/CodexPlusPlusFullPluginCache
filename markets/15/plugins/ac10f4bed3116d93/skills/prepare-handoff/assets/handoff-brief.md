# Implementation brief

Coverage guide: combine related fields for a small task, omit empty sections, and avoid repeating context already stated. A self-contained brief can serve as its own continuation record.

## Objective and recipient
State the intended outcome, intended users and the person or role who will use this brief.

## Basis and constraints
Record the hard constraints and relevant source references. Separate supplied facts, verified evidence, assumptions and unknowns.

## Decisions
| Decision | Status | Rationale and source | Unresolved objection or dependency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use user-selected, proposed, deferred or rejected status. Preserve decisions already supplied; do not create implied approvals.

## Scope
Describe the first useful deliverable and its principal user journey or operating sequence. List explicit exclusions. Include only necessary technical or operational choices.

## Requirements and acceptance
| Requirement | Observable acceptance check | Evidence needed | Current state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Checks describe an input or situation, action and observable expected result. Use planned, performed or verified state only when supported. Connect checks to decision or requirement identifiers if useful.

## Delivery sequence
| Step and deliverable | Dependencies or decision needed | Proposed or assigned owner | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Leave dates and owners unassigned when absent. Clearly label any proposed schedule or owner role.

## Open decisions and changes
Identify remaining conflicts, missing evidence and rejected alternatives that must stay outside scope. On revision, explain the user correction, displaced work and downstream changes.

## Continuation record
Summarize objective; hard constraints; selected and proposed decisions; existing artifact references with revision and current or stale status; active settings if the session will continue; first unfinished deliverable; unresolved blockers; next owner action. Use a version only when its basis is present in the conversation.
