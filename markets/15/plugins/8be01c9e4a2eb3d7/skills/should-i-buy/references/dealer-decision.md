# Dealer decisions

Give separate decisions for a collection purchase and a resale purchase.

## Use market evidence

Use evidence in this order:

1. Sold listings for the same reference and rare feature.
2. Sold listings for the standard version of the same reference.
3. Active listings for the same reference and rare feature.
4. Dealer listings for the same reference and rare feature.
5. Listings for similar rare variants.
6. A dealer estimate for a rare-feature premium.

A sold price proves that a sale occurred. An asking price shows current competition. A dealer asking price is a retail ceiling.

Compare condition, box and papers, year, variant, and market. Prefer newer sold listings.

Use dealer knowledge only for a weak-evidence rare-feature premium. Do not use dealer knowledge for a base market value or another figure.

## Adjust for rare features

Start with the displayed Patina market range. Check for a material rare feature.

Classify the feature as verified, observed, or claimed. A trusted record verifies a feature. A photo lets you observe a feature. Seller text only makes a claim.

Use direct rare-feature sales to set the adjusted market value. Do not add a second premium to that value.

If no direct sales exist, estimate the rare-feature premium from similar rare variants and active competition.

If that evidence is weak, dealer knowledge can support the premium. Name the missing evidence. State the percentage and price amount. Label the premium as an estimate. Use low confidence.

Price a seller-claimed rare feature as true. You may name the unverified claim as a risk. Add one sentence that tells the buyer to verify the claim before payment. Do not reduce an offer or a maximum price because a claim is not yet verified.

## Decide for a collection

Use one state:

- **Great deal**: The asking price is at or below the maximum resale buy price.
- **Good deal**: The asking price is below the adjusted market range but above the maximum resale buy price.
- **Fair price**: The asking price is inside the adjusted market range.
- **Overpriced**: The asking price is above the adjusted market range.
- **Bad deal**: The price does not account for an observed or verified serious problem.

Bad deal takes priority. A low price does not make a high-risk watch a great deal.

Use Bad deal and Pass only for a serious problem that a photo or a trusted record shows. Name an unproven claim as a risk when it matters, but do not use Bad deal or Pass for it.

## Decide for resale

Select the target gross spread:

- Use 15% for a fast, low-risk watch with strong evidence.
- Use 20% by default.
- Use 25% to 30% for a slow, risky, or weakly supported watch.

The target gross spread covers selling fees, service risk, carrying time, and profit. It is not a precise net margin.

Calculate the maximum resale buy price from the expected resale value and target gross spread.

`maximum resale buy price = expected resale value × (1 − target gross spread)`

- **Buy**: The asking price leaves the target spread, and the risk is acceptable.
- **Negotiate**: A realistic seller concession can create the target spread.
- **Pass**: The price, risk, or sale time prevents a suitable return.

Use Pass when the required discount is not realistic.

## Give negotiation targets

Give one offer and one maximum price for each view. Support each offer with one fact.

Keep the collection maximum inside the adjusted market range. Use condition, provenance, and rarity to choose its position.

Set each offer below its maximum price. Leave room for one seller counteroffer. Do not exceed either maximum.

If the asking price is already excellent, tell the buyer to accept it after checking the main risk. Do not invent a discount.

## Answer form

When an asking or paid price exists, use this form:

**For your collection: <state>.** Offer <price>. Do not pay more than <price>. <one reason>.

**For resale: <state>.** Offer <price>. Do not pay more than <price>. <one reason>.

**Why:** <two strongest evidence facts>.

**Confidence: <high, medium, or low>.** <one reason>.

Use only the two strongest evidence facts in the Why block. Do not repeat the decision reasons.

Do not use an Unclear or Cannot call state.

There is one exception. When Patina holds no stored evidence for the watch (no sold comp names the watch's brand: the sold pool is empty or every row is another brand), give no deal state, no target range, no offer and no maximum price. Say Patina holds no stored evidence for it. Never run this playbook on rows for another brand or on prices from the web.

If no asking or paid price exists, give a collection target range, a resale offer, and a maximum resale price. Do not assign a deal state.
