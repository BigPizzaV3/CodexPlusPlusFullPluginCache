# Expanded theological framework

This file expands the core skill and preserves the distinctive reasoning logic of 《小楊活出屬神的榮耀》.

## 1. Scripture, Christ, and priority

- The whole Bible is the highest canonical basis for Christian theology.
- Jesus Christ is the absolute center and highest interpretive key of the biblical storyline.
- Scripture testifies to Christ; do not turn “high view of Scripture” into a functional replacement of Christ by the text.
- Study should move the reader toward knowing Christ, understanding Christ, becoming like Christ, and living Christ.
- Biblical knowledge, memorization, original-language study, and theology are means, not the final center.

## 2. Absolute Christology

“Absolute Christology” here goes beyond a narrow discussion of two natures, person, or incarnation. It treats Jesus Christ as the highest key for understanding:

- creation
- humanity
- revelation
- covenant
- redemption
- Kingdom
- resurrection
- judgment
- the Millennium
- new creation
- the defeat of Satan
- spiritual maturity

Jesus is not merely the solution to individual guilt. He is also:

- Son of Man
- the human archetype
- the visible YHWH in this framework
- King of God's Kingdom
- center of creation and new creation
- center of resurrection
- center of judgment
- center of millennial government
- center of the New Heavens and New Earth
- supreme model of Christian character

## 3. Trinity without three interchangeable centers

Affirm:

- the Father is God
- the Son is God
- the Holy Spirit is God
- Father, Son, and Spirit are personally distinct

Do not turn the Trinity into three interchangeable saving entrances. The Son's mediation, name, redemption, incarnation, resurrection, and kingship have an irreplaceable place in the economy of salvation and revelation.

The framework may use these descriptive emphases where useful:

- Father: invisible YHWH
- Son: visible YHWH / Jesus Christ
- Holy Spirit: the Spirit of God

These descriptions must not erase the full deity or personal distinction of Father, Son, and Spirit.

## 4. Three foundational doctrines

Give special defensive weight to:

1. Trinity
2. Christology
3. Soteriology

When evaluating a disputed interpretation, ask whether it damages any of these.

## 5. Justification, salvation, maturity, and overcoming

Strictly distinguish:

### Justification by faith / salvation

- Salvation rests on genuine faith in Jesus Christ, not on sinless performance.
- Do not make later victory, sanctification, reward, or maturity the basis of justification.
- In this framework, genuine salvation is not cancelled simply because a believer later sins.

### Spiritual maturity / overcoming / reward / Kingdom consequence

A believer's post-conversion life still matters profoundly. Serious consequences may concern:

- immaturity
- failure to overcome
- loss of reward
- Kingdom qualification or position
- ruling with Christ
- city-inside / city-outside imagery in the framework
- discipline
- millennial outcomes

Do not use such warnings to overturn justification by faith. Do not use justification by faith to make sin morally trivial.

### Assurance and warning

Humans cannot infallibly determine whether another person is truly justified, and people should not turn one decision, one baptism, or one feeling into careless self-certainty.

If someone says, “I am saved, therefore I can sin however I want,” treat that attitude itself as a serious warning sign rather than a healthy expression of assurance.

## 6. Read for the main point

Preferred order:

**main point → context → detail → application**

Do not measure maturity primarily by how many verses one can quote. Memorization is useful, but understanding context, book-level purpose, whole-Bible structure, and transferable principles matters more for mature interpretation.

## 7. Unequal structural weight does not mean unequal authority

All Scripture is authoritative and valuable, but passages and books do not always carry identical structural weight when constructing a theological system.

### Torah / Pentateuch

Treat Torah as a major conceptual foundation for:

- creation
- sin
- covenant
- law
- priesthood
- sacrifice
- sanctuary
- Passover
- lamb imagery
- cleansing
- Israel
- Kingdom
- redemption

### Gospels

Treat the Gospels as indispensable for understanding:

- who Jesus is
- His life
- His character
- His teaching
- His Kingdom
- the proper Christological frame for Revelation

### Revelation

Treat Revelation as a highly significant book of consummation. Never interpret it in isolation. Read it through Torah, the wider Old Testament, the Gospels, and Jesus Christ.

Macro-pattern:

**Torah → Gospels → Revelation**

**foundation → Christ revealed → consummation**

## 8. The central miracle: resurrection

When users overfocus on spectacular manifestations, restore the hierarchy: Jesus Christ's resurrection is a decisive center of the Christian faith. Healing, exorcism, signs, and wonders do not outrank it.

## 9. Original languages, translations, manuscripts, and history

Use Chinese and English translations, Hebrew, Greek, manuscripts, and historical context as important corrective tools. Do not absolutize one translation or one lexical item into the entire theology.

Priority remains:

**book-level main point + whole-Bible main point + Christ-centered reading**

Original-language precision can still be wrong if the large-scale interpretive direction is wrong.

## 10. Old Testament theophany

When YHWH/God appears in clearly human form, this framework strongly prefers the identification:

**the Son / Christ / visible YHWH**

Reasons include:

- the Father's invisibility
- the Son's revelatory role
- the Holy Spirit's non-fixed human-form manifestations
- the identity and speech of the appearing figure
- later New Testament Christology

The Holy Spirit may manifest in ways described as like a dove or like tongues of fire. “Wind” is usually better treated as metaphor/phenomenon than a fixed bodily form.

If the Old Testament manifestation is not clearly human, keep the conclusion open and reason from context.

## 11. Son of Man, human form, and human archetype

Treat “Son of Man” as theologically important. Explore:

- Christ as the human archetype
- Adam made in God's image and likeness
- the relationship between human form and the visible Christ
- possible implications for Genesis 6 and corruption of the human / Son-of-Man image order

Genesis 6 extensions are exploratory and should be expressed as possible framework reasoning, not as universal Christian consensus.

## 12. Multiple typological layers

Do not assume one figure/event/institution/vision has only one valid typological layer.

Method:

1. establish the most direct meaning first;
2. test additional layers against the whole biblical storyline;
3. preserve Christological priority without excluding secondary Kingdom or maturity layers;
4. avoid unlimited allegory.

Examples:

### Joseph

May point to:

- Jesus Christ
- overcomers
- people carrying governing responsibility under God's rule

### Isaac

May point to:

- Christ offered up
- trust in God
- full surrender of self

## 13. Ezekiel's dry bones as a layered model

Possible layers:

1. direct level: Israel's restoration / revival-from-death imagery;
2. spiritual extension: regeneration / new life after faith in Christ;
3. eschatological extension: resurrection of saints and Israel-related restoration at Christ's return;
4. final extension: resurrection of all the dead before final judgment.

These layers need not mutually exclude one another.

Do not casually equate the passage with a modern “church revival” slogan unless whole-Bible support is actually present.

## 14. Large movements and “revival”

Do not assume that numerical growth, mass unity, excitement, or a large movement equals divine approval.

Useful counterquestions include:

- Was Babel a large unified movement?
- Did rapid growth in Jerusalem prevent later scattering?

Evaluate movements by fidelity to Christ, biblical center, God's larger plan, and life maturity, not scale alone.

## 15. Millennium

Do not reduce the Millennium to pure spirituality or to an ordinary political state. Preferred formulation:

**spiritual authority actually enters and governs the material world.**

The Millennium fulfills both Old and New Testaments:

- Old Testament: Kingdom blueprint and promises
- New Testament: the fulfiller and the mode of fulfillment
- Revelation: consummation and governing execution

Questions to keep central:

- Why does Jesus reign?
- What is He completing?
- Why do overcomers reign with Him?
- Why is Satan released afterward?
- How does the Millennium lead into the New Creation?

## 16. Israel and Church

Reject a simplistic complete replacement model. Also reject making ethnic/national Israel the final theological center.

Both Israel and the Church must be interpreted in Christ.

Preserve:

- Israel's history, covenant, promises, and role
- the Church's mission, life, and Kingdom role

Do not mechanically collapse one into the other. The final center is the Lamb.

## 17. Miracles, dreams, visions, impressions

Avoid two extremes:

- total denial that God may still guide, act, speak, give dreams, visions, or miracles;
- total credulity toward every supernatural claim.

Human feelings and “revelations” can be wrong. “God told me,” angelic encounters, heaven/hell journeys, and visions all require discernment.

Remember biblical warnings about deceptive spiritual appearances and the historical fact that some heterodox movements began with private revelation claims.

## 18. Heaven / hell testimony

Remain open but highly cautious.

Distinguish:

- Sheol / Hades
- lake of fire
- abyss

Do not flatten them into one generic “hell.”

The New Heavens and New Earth are not yet fully realized. Claims of fully entering the completed future New Creation should therefore be tested carefully. At the same time, do not deny that God could grant a visionary preview, as biblical prophets received future visions.

## 19. Discernment principle

Preferred pattern:

**contact → discern → filter → retain what is good → reject what is harmful**

Do not isolate from everything. Do not absorb everything.

Primary filter: Jesus Christ—His life, character, teaching, humility, holiness, love, cross, and Kingdom values.

## 20. Humility, simplicity, self-control, lowliness

Treat these as serious components of Christlike maturity and Kingdom fitness, related in this framework to:

- Christ's life
- overcoming
- maturity
- millennial government
- New Creation
- holy-city themes

Christianity is not merely “How little can I do and still be saved?”

## 21. 至聖至愛 / holiness and love

Do not split holiness and love.

Mature life is not:

- holiness without love, nor
- love without holiness.

Connect the integrated holiness-love axis with:

- Christlike maturity
- overcomers
- fruit of the Spirit
- Kingdom responsibility
- the life suitable for God's holy city / New Creation

## 22. Jewish New Testament Guide

For users grounded in the Hebrew Bible, start with categories they know:

- Abraham
- Torah
- covenant
- Passover
- sacrifices
- Temple
- priesthood
- David
- messianic prophecy
- Israel's history
- Hebrew/Jewish thought

Then bridge toward:

- Jesus
- cross
- resurrection
- apostles
- New Covenant
- Revelation

Present continuity and fulfillment, not a detached new religion.

## 23. Whole-Bible consistency test

A theological proposal should survive questions such as:

- Can Torah explain it?
- Can the Old Testament explain it?
- Can the Gospels explain it?
- Can apostolic writings explain it?
- Can Revelation explain it?
- Does it preserve Trinity?
- Does it preserve Christology?
- Does it preserve salvation by faith?
- Can it connect creation, redemption, Kingdom, and consummation?

Apply this test to Xiaoyang's own proposals as rigorously as to outside traditions.

## 24. Final theological aim

The goal is not to create disciples of a human teacher. The goal is to help users:

- identify the main point
- see the whole biblical storyline
- know Jesus Christ
- understand underlying logic
- reason independently
- discern and filter
- test claims
- live out Christ
