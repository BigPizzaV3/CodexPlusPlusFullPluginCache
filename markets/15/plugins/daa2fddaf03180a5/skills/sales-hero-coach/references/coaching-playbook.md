# Sales Hero | Original Field Coaching Playbook

## Conversational sales arc

Use the rep's supplied process when one exists. Otherwise adapt these flexible customer-centered phases:

1. Open warmly and confirm time expectations.
2. Understand the customer's goal in their language.
3. Learn the cost of the current problem and what a good outcome would change.
4. Confirm decision process, priorities, and investment constraints respectfully.
5. Explain what is in scope, out of scope, and not yet verified.
6. Demonstrate the relevant fit or difference using accurate sources.
7. Present a written, understandable choice with total cost and timing.
8. Invite the customer's reaction and clarify misunderstandings.
9. Address concerns with evidence, not rebuttals or pressure.
10. Ask for an appropriate decision or next agreed step.
11. Confirm documented scope, consent, expectations, and handoff.
12. Follow up only as agreed.

This is an original teaching framework, not a claim about any company's mandatory process.

## Customer-friendly questions

- “What got you thinking about changing this now?”
- “Which part of that is the biggest headache?”
- “When you picture this fixed, what would be different?”
- “How are you comparing the choices?”
- “What would you need to feel comfortable moving forward?”

Don't fire these off as a questionnaire; listen, paraphrase, ask the next relevant one.

## Objection isolation

When a buyer says “The price is much higher than I expected,” respond first with curiosity, not defense: “I hear you. What range were you expecting?” Follow by exploring what they are comparing and whether scope, quality, timeline, and financing information are comparable. If cost doesn't fit, acknowledge it without manufacturing concessions.

When a buyer says “I need to talk to my partner,” respond: “Absolutely. What information would be most helpful for that conversation?” Ask permission for a joint follow-up, not an unauthorized close.

When a buyer says “We need to think,” respond: “Of course. What part would be most useful to clarify before I leave?” Respect the answer and propose an optional next step.

## Sincere close

“Based on what matters to you, does this option feel like the right fit, or is there something important we're missing?” Ask once, listen, and document the actual outcome. A sale made through confusion or scope misrepresentation is not a successful outcome.

## Safe factual appointment note

**Problem and scope:** [customer's own words; what is confirmed].
**What mattered:** [priority and concerns].
**Decision:** [what happened and why; unknowns stated].
**Next step:** [specific owner/action/date if confirmed; otherwise none agreed].

## Roleplay drill protocol

One buyer line per turn; await answer; evaluate clarity, empathy, evidence, accuracy, and next question. On a retry, change an important detail to prevent rote memorization. Never fabricate a scorecard score.
