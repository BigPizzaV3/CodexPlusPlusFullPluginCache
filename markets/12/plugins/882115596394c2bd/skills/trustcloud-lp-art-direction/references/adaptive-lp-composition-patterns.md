# Adaptive LP Composition Patterns

These are pattern libraries, not fixed templates. Select, omit, repeat, or
reorder sections only when event evidence and visitor decision-making justify
it. The reservation chain remains hero, proof, logistics, calendar, and CTA.

## Pattern A: Tour / Open House / Site Visit

Use when visitors must understand several physical experiences before booking.

1. Finished hero key visual with event identity, strongest visual promise, and
   date integrated into the image. Put the first real CTA in the following
   narrative or reservation transition.
2. Audience-fit section listing the concrete concerns the event resolves.
3. Event overview explaining what is covered in one visit.
4. Develop the distinct highlights through their photographic relationships and
   visitor questions: structural site, completed owner home, or money mini-lecture.
   A large scene, paired photographs, observations or a photo-and-diagram spread
   can carry different parts of that narrative. Author the intended composition
   with HTML/CSS or a matching native block; a highlight is not a prescribed
   `section` + `media` pair.
5. Source-backed customer voices in a dedicated `testimonials` block, only
   when exact voices were provided. Do not render voices as generic cards.
6. Detailed day schedule using up to eight `process` steps when useful.
7. FAQ addressing fee, reservation, duration, children, transport, and attire
   only when supported or safely answerable.
8. Access and venue information bound to the event location.
9. Source-backed offer or participation benefit, if one exists.
10. Event specs such as date, assembly time, fee, capacity, deadline, and
    included items.
11. Reservation notice, calendar, organizer contact, and final/sticky CTA.

The older bus-tour example demonstrates grouping distinct experiences, not a
target length or image budget. For a full LP, develop the individual places,
activities and visitor decisions as meaningful image-bearing chapters, rather
than compressing different homes or learning topics into one generic photograph.
Follow the shared body-chapter goal without inventing unsupported facts.

## Pattern B: Consultation / Seminar

1. Hero stating the problem and concrete outcome.
2. Audience anxieties and what attendees can organize or decide.
3. Curriculum or consultation topics, grouped by decision area.
4. Speaker/advisor proof only from verified profile facts.
5. Format, duration, preparation, and what visitors take away.
6. Process, FAQ, access or online method, calendar, and CTA.

Use imagery for the consultation atmosphere, concrete subject matter, or an
educational diagram when it adds information. Do not fill the page with generic
people-at-a-table stock scenes.

## Pattern C: Workshop / Family Experience

1. Hero showing the finished experience or outcome.
2. Who can participate and age/skill fit.
3. What participants make, learn, or take home.
4. Step-by-step experience and required belongings.
5. Instructor or safety information when verified.
6. Variations gallery only when several examples genuinely help selection.
7. Schedule, capacity, fee, FAQ, venue, calendar, and CTA.

## Pattern D: Fair / Campaign / Multi-offer Event

1. Hero with one umbrella promise.
2. Audience-fit and reasons to attend now.
3. Separate offer or program sections, ordered by visitor value.
4. Comparison/spec blocks when visitors need to choose among options.
5. Benefits and conditions with precise source-backed wording.
6. Schedule, venue map, FAQ, calendar, and CTA.

## Density and Rhythm Rules

- Do not target a fixed number of blocks. Use enough blocks to preserve useful
  source facts and remove any block that only repeats another.
- Vary section rhythm: full-bleed hero, concise transition, split media,
  open-list proof, process band, FAQ, map, and reservation area.
- Avoid repeating the same centered heading plus identical card grid.
- In a newly generated hero, integrate only exact event identity, date, and the
  shortest promise. Keep all supporting images text-free. Never render a fake
  CTA button inside an image.
- Treat mobile continuation as part of composition: image focal points, text
  order, CTA visibility, and section length must remain understandable.
