# Profit Margin Calculator

Answers: What are my gross, operating and net profit margins?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/profit-margin

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $ | none, ask for it | Total sales for the period being looked at. |
| Cost of goods sold | $ | none, ask for it | The direct costs of producing what was sold. It may be 0. |
| Operating expenses | $ | none, ask for it | Overhead for the same period: rent, office salaries, marketing, insurance, software. |
| Other income or expenses | $ | $0 | Anything outside normal operations, such as interest. Enter income as a positive figure and an expense as a negative one. Use 0 if there is none. |

## Before you calculate

- Total revenue must be above 0.
- Cost of goods sold cannot be negative.

## Formula

1. Gross profit = total revenue - cost of goods sold
2. Gross margin = gross profit / total revenue x 100
3. Operating profit = gross profit - operating expenses
4. Operating margin = operating profit / total revenue x 100
5. Net profit = operating profit + other income or expenses (an expense is negative, so it reduces the profit)
6. Net margin = net profit / total revenue x 100

## Rounding

Give dollars to the nearest whole dollar and percentages to one decimal place.

## Worked example

Inputs: Total revenue $500,000; Cost of goods sold $200,000; Operating expenses $150,000; Other income or expenses -$5,000.

1. Gross profit = $500,000 - $200,000 = $300,000, a gross margin of 60.0%
2. Operating profit = $300,000 - $150,000 = $150,000, an operating margin of 30.0%
3. Net profit = $150,000 + (-$5,000) = $145,000, a net margin of 29.0%

Result for these inputs:

- Net margin: 29.0%
- Net profit: $145,000
- Gross margin: 60.0%
- Gross profit: $300,000
- Operating margin: 30.0%
- Operating profit: $150,000
- Profit kept from each dollar of revenue: $0.29

Rule of thumb for this result: Strong net margin.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The net margin is over 10%: **Strong net margin.** Healthy for most industries. There is room to reinvest, build reserves and absorb surprises. Keep watching the gross margin as the business grows.
- The net margin is 5% to 10%: **Moderate net margin.** Sustainable, with little room for error. Check whether operating expenses are high for the gross profit earned. A small cut in overhead moves the result noticeably.
- The net margin is 0% or more and under 5%: **Thin net margin.** Almost no buffer. One bad month, one large unexpected expense or one slow-paying customer could turn it into a loss. Raising prices or reducing the largest cost categories comes first.
- The net margin is below 0%: **Operating at a loss.** Look at both the gross margin and the operating expenses. A healthy gross margin eaten by operating costs is an overhead problem. A thin gross margin means prices or direct costs come first.

## Notes

- All four figures must cover the same period.
