# Gross Profit Calculator

Answers: What is my gross profit and gross margin?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/gross-margin

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $ | none, ask for it | Total sales for the period being looked at. |
| Cost of goods sold | $ | none, ask for it | The direct costs of producing what was sold: materials, direct labor, subcontractors. Not overhead. |

## Before you calculate

- Revenue must be above 0.
- Cost of goods sold must be above 0.
- Cost of goods sold must be less than revenue.

## Formula

1. Gross profit = revenue - cost of goods sold
2. Gross margin = gross profit / revenue x 100
3. Cost of goods sold as a share of revenue = cost of goods sold / revenue x 100

## Rounding

Give dollars to the nearest whole dollar and percentages to one decimal place.

## Worked example

Inputs: Revenue $500,000; Cost of goods sold $300,000.

1. Gross profit = $500,000 - $300,000 = $200,000
2. Gross margin = $200,000 / $500,000 x 100 = 40.0%
3. Cost of goods sold as a share of revenue = $300,000 / $500,000 x 100 = 60.0%

Result for these inputs:

- Gross margin: 40.0%
- Gross profit: $200,000
- Cost of goods sold as a share of revenue: 60.0%

Rule of thumb for this result: Moderate gross margin.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The gross margin is over 40%: **Strong gross margin.** A large share of every dollar is left to cover overhead, pay the owner and leave a profit. There is room to absorb cost increases and to invest.
- The gross margin is 20% to 40%: **Moderate gross margin.** Workable, with less cushion. After overhead and the owner's pay the net profit may be thin. Look for ways to lower direct costs or raise prices.
- The gross margin is under 20%: **Low gross margin.** Most of the revenue goes straight to direct costs, and after overhead and the owner's pay there may be nothing left. Prices need to rise, direct costs need to fall, or the model needs a rethink.

## Notes

- Gross profit is before overhead and the owner's pay. For what is left after those, use the profit margin calculation.
