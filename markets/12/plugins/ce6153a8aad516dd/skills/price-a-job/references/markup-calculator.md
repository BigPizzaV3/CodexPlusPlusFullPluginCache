# Markup vs Margin Calculator

Answers: What is the difference between my markup and my margin, and what price does it give?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/markup-vs-margin

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per unit | $ | none, ask for it | What one unit, job or hour costs to deliver. |
| Which percentage the owner has | choose one | Markup % | Markup is profit as a share of cost. Margin is profit as a share of the selling price. Ask which one the owner means if it is not clear. One of: Markup %; Margin %. |
| The markup or the margin | % | none, ask for it | The percentage the owner wants to apply. |

## Before you calculate

- Cost per unit must be above 0.
- The percentage must be above 0.
- A margin must be less than 100. At 100% the price would be infinite.

## Formula

1. Given a markup: selling price = cost per unit x (1 + markup / 100), and margin = markup / (100 + markup) x 100
2. Given a margin: selling price = cost per unit / (1 - margin / 100), and markup = margin / (100 - margin) x 100
3. Profit per unit = selling price - cost per unit

## Rounding

Give the markup and the margin to one decimal place. Give the selling price and the profit per unit to the cent. The Playbook's own calculator shows those two rounded to the whole dollar, so its figures can differ from yours by up to 50 cents.

## Worked example

Inputs: Cost per unit $75; Which percentage the owner has Markup %; The markup or the margin 50%.

1. Selling price = $75.00 x (1 + 50 / 100) = $112.50
2. Margin = 50 / (100 + 50) x 100 = 33.3%
3. Profit per unit = $112.50 - $75.00 = $37.50

Result for these inputs:

- Markup: 50.0%
- Margin: 33.3%
- Selling price: $112.50
- Profit per unit: $37.50

Rule of thumb for this result: Moderate margin.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The margin is over 35%: **Healthy margin.** There is room to cover overhead, absorb mistakes and invest in growth.
- The margin is 20% to 35%: **Moderate margin.** It can work, depending on the overhead the business carries. Watch costs closely and consider whether prices can rise.
- The margin is under 20%: **Low margin.** Very little is left for overhead, errors or growth. Most businesses need more than this to last. The pricing needs a review.

## Notes

- The same number means different things as a markup and as a margin. A 50% markup is a 33.3% margin. A 50% margin is a 100% markup.
- Owners often price with a markup while thinking of it as their margin, and earn less than they expect. Say which one the result is.
