# Hourly Rate Calculator

Answers: What do I need to charge an hour?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/job-pricing

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target annual income | $ a year | none, ask for it | What the owner wants to take home in a year, before personal taxes. This is their own pay. |
| Annual business expenses | $ a year | none, ask for it | Everything the business pays for in a year that is not the owner's pay: software, insurance, accounting, space, marketing, equipment. |
| Desired profit margin | % | 20% | The share of revenue the owner wants left after their pay and the expenses. |
| Billable hours a week | hours | 30 | Hours actually billed to clients in a typical week, not hours worked. |
| Weeks worked a year | weeks | 48 | 52 less holidays, time off and sick time. |

## Before you calculate

- Target annual income must be above 0.
- Billable hours a week must be above 0.
- Weeks worked a year must be above 0.
- Profit margin must be 0 or more and less than 100. At 100% no amount of revenue covers the costs.

## Formula

1. Costs before profit = target annual income + annual business expenses
2. Revenue needed a year = costs before profit / (1 - profit margin / 100)
3. Billable hours a year = billable hours a week x weeks worked a year
4. Hourly rate = revenue needed a year / billable hours a year
5. Daily rate = hourly rate x 8
6. Revenue needed a month = revenue needed a year / 12
7. Expenses carried by each billable hour = annual business expenses / billable hours a year

## Rounding

Round the hourly rate, the daily rate, the monthly revenue and the yearly revenue UP to the next whole dollar. Work out the daily rate from the unrounded hourly rate. Round the expenses carried by each billable hour to the nearest dollar.

## Worked example

Inputs: Target annual income $100,000; Annual business expenses $30,000; Desired profit margin 20%; Billable hours a week 30; Weeks worked a year 48.

1. Costs before profit = $100,000 + $30,000 = $130,000
2. Revenue needed a year = $130,000 / (1 - 20 / 100) = $162,500.00
3. Billable hours a year = 30 x 48 = 1,440
4. Hourly rate = $162,500.00 / 1,440 = $112.85, rounded up to $113
5. Daily rate = $112.85 x 8 = $902.78, rounded up to $903
6. Revenue needed a month = $162,500.00 / 12 = $13,541.67, rounded up to $13,542

Result for these inputs:

- Hourly rate: $113 an hour
- Daily rate (8-hour day): $903
- Revenue needed a month: $13,542
- Revenue needed a year: $162,500
- Billable hours a year: 1,440
- Expenses carried by each billable hour: $21
- Share of revenue that is the owner's pay: 61.5%
- Share of revenue that is expenses: 18.5%
- Profit a year: $32,500

Rule of thumb for this result: Premium rate territory.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The hourly rate is over $100: **Premium rate territory.** This is the range of specialized professionals and consultants. It holds when the owner has deep expertise, a strong reputation or a high-value niche, and sells the outcome more than the hours.
- The hourly rate is $50 to $100: **Competitive mid-range rate.** Typical for experienced freelancers and small operators. It covers costs and leaves room for profit, but only with steady work. An owner who keeps discounting to win jobs should look at a narrower niche or pricing by the project.
- The hourly rate is under $50: **Rate may be too low.** This leaves little room for expenses and profit once unbilled time is counted. The owner can cut expenses, raise the income target in steps, or find ways to bill more hours.

## Notes

- The target income is the owner's own pay. It is not the same as profit, which is what is left for the business after that pay and the expenses.
