# DSCR Calculator

Answers: Does my business earn enough to cover its loan payments?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/debt-service-coverage

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual net operating income | $ a year | none, ask for it | Profit before loan payments and income taxes, for a year. |
| Annual debt payments | $ a year | none, ask for it | Every loan payment for a year, principal and interest, including any new loan being considered. |

## Before you calculate

- Annual net operating income must be above 0.
- Annual debt payments must be above 0.

## Formula

1. Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) = annual net operating income / annual debt payments
2. Excess or shortfall = annual net operating income - annual debt payments
3. Cushion = (DSCR - 1) x 100, the percentage by which income exceeds the payments
4. Room for more yearly loan payments = the excess, when the DSCR is above 1

## Rounding

Give the DSCR to two decimal places, dollars to the nearest whole dollar and the cushion to one decimal place.

## Worked example

Inputs: Annual net operating income $250,000; Annual debt payments $180,000.

1. DSCR = $250,000 / $180,000 = 1.39
2. Income left after loan payments = $250,000 - $180,000 = $70,000
3. Cushion = (1.39 - 1) x 100 = 38.9%

Result for these inputs:

- DSCR: 1.39
- Income left after loan payments: $70,000
- Cushion: 38.9%
- Room for more yearly loan payments: $70,000

Rule of thumb for this result: Strong coverage.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The DSCR is over 1.25: **Strong coverage.** Income covers the loan payments with room to spare, which puts the business in a good position with lenders.
- The DSCR is 1.0 to 1.25: **Tight coverage.** The payments are covered, but thinly. A small dip in revenue or a surprise cost could push it below 1. Raising income or lowering payments comes before taking on more debt.
- The DSCR is under 1.0: **Cannot cover the debt payments.** Operating income does not cover the loan payments, so the business is drawing on reserves or borrowing to pay them. That cannot go on.

## Notes

- Lenders set their own minimum DSCR and work out income their own way. Treat this as the owner's own check, not a lender's answer.
