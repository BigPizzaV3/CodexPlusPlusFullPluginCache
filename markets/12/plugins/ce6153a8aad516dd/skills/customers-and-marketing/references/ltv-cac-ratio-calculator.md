# LTV:CAC Ratio Calculator

Answers: Is a customer worth more than it costs me to win them?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/customer-acquisition-cost

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer lifetime value | $ | none, ask for it | What a customer is worth over the time they stay, as lifetime profit. |
| Customer acquisition cost | $ | none, ask for it | What it costs to win one new customer. |
| Revenue a month from a customer | $ a month | optional, leave out if not known | What a typical customer pays each month. With it, the months to earn back the cost of winning them can be worked out. |

## Before you calculate

- Customer lifetime value must be above 0.
- Customer acquisition cost must be above 0.

## Formula

1. LTV to CAC ratio = customer lifetime value / customer acquisition cost
2. Value left after winning the customer = lifetime value - acquisition cost
3. If revenue a month is given: months to earn back the acquisition cost = acquisition cost / revenue a month

## Rounding

Give the ratio to one decimal place, months to one decimal place and dollars to the nearest whole dollar.

## Worked example

Inputs: Customer lifetime value $5,000; Customer acquisition cost $1,200; Revenue a month from a customer $250.

1. LTV to CAC ratio = $5,000 / $1,200 = 4.2 to 1
2. Value left after winning the customer = $5,000 - $1,200 = $3,800
3. Months to earn back = $1,200 / $250 = 4.8

Result for these inputs:

- LTV to CAC ratio: 4.2 to 1
- Value left after winning the customer: $3,800
- Months to earn back the acquisition cost: 4.8

Rule of thumb for this result: Healthy.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The ratio is under 1 to 1: **Losing money on every customer.** Winning a customer costs more than they are worth, so growth makes the losses bigger. Fix the lifetime value or the cost of winning customers before spending more on growth.
- The ratio is 1 to 1 up to 3 to 1: **Viable but tight.** Each customer makes money, with little room for error. A costly month of marketing or a rise in customers leaving could push it below break-even.
- The ratio is over 3 to 1, up to 5 to 1: **Healthy.** Each customer brings in well more than they cost to win. The model can grow.
- The ratio is over 5 to 1: **Room to invest more in growth.** A strong signal, and possibly a sign the business could spend more on winning customers and still do well. Test spending more and watch the ratio.

## Notes

- Use the same basis for both figures: if the lifetime value is profit, the cost of winning a customer should include everything spent on marketing and sales.
