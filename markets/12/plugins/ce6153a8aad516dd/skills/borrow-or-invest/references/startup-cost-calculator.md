# Startup Cost Estimator

Answers: How much do I need to start the business?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/business-plan

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal and formation | $ | $0 | Registering the business, filings and the like. Use 0 if none. |
| Equipment and furniture | $ | $0 | Tools, machines, vehicles, desks. Use 0 if none. |
| Initial inventory | $ | $0 | Stock and materials to open with. Use 0 if none. |
| Marketing and branding | $ | $0 | Name, logo, website, launch advertising. Use 0 if none. |
| Office or lease deposits | $ | $0 | Deposits and first payments on premises. Use 0 if none. |
| Licenses and permits | $ | $0 | What the trade and the town require. Use 0 if none. |
| Professional services | $ | $0 | Accountant and attorney fees to get set up. Use 0 if none. |
| Technology and software | $ | $0 | Computers, phones, software subscriptions. Use 0 if none. |
| Insurance for the first year | $ | $0 | Liability and any other cover the business needs. Use 0 if none. |
| Working capital reserve | $ | $0 | Cash set aside to pay running costs until revenue covers them, as months of expenses. Use 0 if none. |

## Before you calculate

- At least one cost must be above 0.

## Formula

1. Total startup cost = every category added up, the working capital reserve included
2. Contingency buffer = total startup cost x 0.2
3. Total with the buffer = total startup cost + contingency buffer
4. Other costs = total startup cost - working capital reserve. The bands compare the reserve to these.

## Rounding

Give dollars to the nearest whole dollar and percentages to one decimal place.

## Worked example

Inputs: Legal and formation $2,000; Equipment and furniture $10,000; Initial inventory $5,000; Marketing and branding $5,000; Office or lease deposits $3,000; Licenses and permits $500; Professional services $3,000; Technology and software $2,000; Insurance for the first year $3,000; Working capital reserve $15,000.

1. Total startup cost = $2,000 + $10,000 + $5,000 + $5,000 + $3,000 + $500 + $3,000 + $2,000 + $3,000 + $15,000 = $48,500
2. Contingency buffer = $48,500 x 0.2 = $9,700
3. Total with the buffer = $48,500 + $9,700 = $58,200
4. Reserve against other costs = $15,000 / $33,500 x 100 = 44.8%

Result for these inputs:

- Total startup cost: $48,500
- Contingency buffer (20%): $9,700
- Total with the buffer: $58,200
- Largest category: Working capital reserve, $15,000 (30.9%)

Rule of thumb for this result: Solid working capital reserve.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- There is no working capital reserve: **No working capital reserve.** The most dangerous gap in a startup budget. Even a launch on time and on budget has rent, payroll and supplies to pay before customers pay.
- The reserve is under 25% of the other costs: **Working capital may be tight.** There is some reserve, but the runway will be short if revenue is slow to arrive. The Playbook suggests a reserve of three to six months of expenses.
- The reserve is 25% or more of the other costs: **Solid working capital reserve.** There is real runway to cover running costs while revenue builds.
- The only cost entered is the reserve: **Reserve only.** Only the reserve has been entered. Add the other startup costs for a full estimate.

## Notes

- The 20% buffer is the Playbook's allowance for costs that run over. It is added on top of the working capital reserve, not in place of it.
