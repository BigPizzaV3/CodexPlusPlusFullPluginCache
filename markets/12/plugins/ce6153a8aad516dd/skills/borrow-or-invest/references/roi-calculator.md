# ROI Calculator

Answers: What return did, or will, an investment make?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/return-on-investment

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total investment cost | $ | none, ask for it | Everything spent on it. |
| Net profit or return | $ | none, ask for it | What it made after its own costs. Enter a loss as a negative figure. |
| Time period | choose one | 12 months (1 year) | How long the return took, or will take. One of: 1 month; 3 months; 6 months; 12 months (1 year); 24 months (2 years); 36 months (3 years); 60 months (5 years). |

## Before you calculate

- Total investment cost must be above 0.

## Formula

1. ROI = net profit or return / total investment cost x 100
2. Annualized ROI = ROI / (time period in months / 12). This spreads the return evenly over the years; it does not compound.
3. Total value = total investment cost + net profit or return

## Rounding

Give percentages to one decimal place and dollars to the nearest whole dollar.

## Worked example

Inputs: Total investment cost $10,000; Net profit or return $5,000; Time period 12 months (1 year).

1. ROI = $5,000 / $10,000 x 100 = 50.0%
2. Annualized ROI = 50.0% / (12 / 12) = 50.0%
3. Total value = $10,000 + $5,000 = $15,000

Result for these inputs:

- ROI: 50.0%
- Annualized ROI: 50.0%
- Gain: $5,000
- Total value: $15,000

Rule of thumb for this result: Strong return.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The ROI is below 0: **Losing money.** It costs more than it returns. Decide whether it needs more time, whether something outside it changed, or whether to cut the loss. Spotting it early is what matters.
- The ROI is 0% to 20%: **Positive but modest.** It makes money, modestly. Consider whether the same money could earn more elsewhere in the business.
- The ROI is over 20%: **Strong return.** The question now is whether it can be scaled or repeated elsewhere in the business at the same return.

## Notes

- The bands judge the total ROI, as the Playbook's own calculator does, whatever the period. For a period longer than a year, look at the annualized figure as well before calling a return strong.
