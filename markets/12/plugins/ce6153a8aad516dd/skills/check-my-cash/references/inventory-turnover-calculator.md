# Inventory Turnover Calculator

Answers: How fast does my inventory sell, and how much cash is sitting in it?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/inventory-turns

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual cost of goods sold | $ a year | none, ask for it | The cost of what was sold in a year, not the sales figure. |
| Average inventory | $ | none, ask for it | The usual value of stock on hand, at cost. The start and end of the year averaged is a fair figure. |

## Before you calculate

- Annual cost of goods sold must be above 0.
- Average inventory must be above 0.

## Formula

1. Inventory turnover = annual cost of goods sold / average inventory
2. Days of supply on hand = 365 / inventory turnover
3. Months of supply on hand = days of supply / 30
4. Estimated cost of holding the inventory for a year = average inventory x 0.25. This assumes holding stock costs a quarter of its value a year in capital, storage, insurance and stock that goes out of date.

## Rounding

Give the turnover and the months of supply to one decimal place, the days of supply to the nearest whole day and dollars to the nearest whole dollar.

## Worked example

Inputs: Annual cost of goods sold $500,000; Average inventory $75,000.

1. Inventory turnover = $500,000 / $75,000 = 6.7
2. Days of supply = 365 / 6.7 = 55 days
3. Estimated yearly holding cost = $75,000 x 0.25 = $18,750

Result for these inputs:

- Inventory turnover: 6.7 times a year
- Days of supply on hand: 55 days
- Months of supply on hand: 1.8
- Estimated yearly cost of holding it (assumed at 25% of its value): $18,750

Rule of thumb for this result: High turnover.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The turnover is over 6 times a year: **High turnover.** Stock is moving efficiently. Check that it is not so lean that sales are lost to items being out of stock.
- The turnover is 2 to 6 times a year: **Moderate turnover.** An average range for many industries, with room to improve: shorter supplier lead times, better forecasting, and finding the slow-moving items.
- The turnover is under 2 times a year: **Low turnover.** A lot of cash is tied up in stock that is not selling, and it costs money to keep. Clear the dead stock and buy less of what moves slowly.

## Notes

- What counts as good turnover differs a great deal between kinds of business. The bands here are the Playbook's general rule of thumb, not a standard for any one industry.
