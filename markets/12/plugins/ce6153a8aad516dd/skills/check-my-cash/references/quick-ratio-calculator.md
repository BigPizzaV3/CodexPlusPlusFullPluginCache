# Quick Ratio Calculator

Answers: Could I pay what I owe soon without selling inventory?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/liquidity-ratios

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash and cash equivalents | $ | $0 | Bank balances and anything as good as cash. Use 0 if there is none. |
| Marketable securities | $ | $0 | Investments that can be sold within days. Use 0 if there are none. |
| Accounts receivable | $ | $0 | What customers owe on invoices already sent. Use 0 if there is none. |
| Total current liabilities | $ | none, ask for it | Everything due within a year: payables, short-term debt, accrued expenses. |

## Before you calculate

- Total current liabilities must be above 0.
- Cash, securities and receivables must add up to more than 0.

## Formula

1. Quick assets = cash and cash equivalents + marketable securities + accounts receivable. Inventory is left out on purpose.
2. Quick ratio = quick assets / total current liabilities
3. Excess or shortfall = quick assets - total current liabilities
4. Share of quick assets that is cash, securities or receivables = that item / quick assets x 100

## Rounding

Give the ratio to two decimal places, dollars to the nearest whole dollar and percentages to one decimal place.

## Worked example

Inputs: Cash and cash equivalents $50,000; Marketable securities $10,000; Accounts receivable $35,000; Total current liabilities $60,000.

1. Quick assets = $50,000 + $10,000 + $35,000 = $95,000
2. Quick ratio = $95,000 / $60,000 = 1.58
3. Excess or shortfall = $95,000 - $60,000 = $35,000

Result for these inputs:

- Quick ratio: 1.58
- Quick assets: $95,000
- Excess over what is owed: $35,000
- Share that is cash: 52.6%
- Share that is securities: 10.5%
- Share that is receivables: 36.8%

Rule of thumb for this result: Strong liquidity.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The ratio is over 1.0: **Strong liquidity.** Everything due soon could be paid from liquid assets alone, without touching inventory. Lenders and suppliers look on this well. Check that the surplus is not sitting idle.
- The ratio is 0.5 to 1.0: **Adequate but tight.** Liquid assets cover some of what is due, not all. The business may have to lean on inventory sales or credit. One slow-paying customer could cause a squeeze. Collect faster and build cash.
- The ratio is under 0.5: **Liquidity warning.** The business is well short of liquid assets for what it owes, and a surprise cost or a delayed payment could cause real trouble. Collecting receivables comes first.

## Notes

- The quick ratio is stricter than the working capital ratio because it leaves inventory out. Receivables that are unlikely to be collected should be left out too.
