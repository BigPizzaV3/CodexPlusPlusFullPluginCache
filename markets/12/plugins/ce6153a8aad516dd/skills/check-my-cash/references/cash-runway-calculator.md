# Cash Runway Calculator

Answers: How many months of cash do I have?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/cash-runway

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash on hand | $ | none, ask for it | What is in the bank now: checking, savings and any cash reserves. |
| Average monthly revenue | $ a month | none, ask for it | What comes in each month, averaged over the last three to six months. |
| Average monthly expenses | $ a month | none, ask for it | Everything that goes out in a month: payroll, rent, materials, insurance, utilities, debt payments. |

## Before you calculate

- Cash on hand must be above 0.
- Average monthly expenses must be above 0.

## Formula

1. Monthly burn = average monthly expenses - average monthly revenue
2. If the monthly burn is 0 or less, the business is cash flow positive and has no runway to count: it adds that amount each month, and cash in 12 months = cash on hand + (amount added each month x 12)
3. Otherwise, runway in months = cash on hand / monthly burn
4. Months until a quarter of the cash is gone = (cash on hand x 0.25) / monthly burn
5. Months until half of the cash is gone = (cash on hand x 0.5) / monthly burn
6. Months until three quarters of the cash is gone = (cash on hand x 0.75) / monthly burn

## Rounding

Give months to one decimal place. From 24 months up, give years to one decimal place (months / 12). From 120 months up, say "10+ years". Give dollars to the nearest whole dollar.

## Worked example

Inputs: Cash on hand $50,000; Average monthly revenue $25,000; Average monthly expenses $30,000.

1. Monthly burn = $30,000 - $25,000 = $5,000
2. Runway = $50,000 / $5,000 = 10.0 months
3. Half of the cash gone in ($50,000 x 0.5) / $5,000 = 5.0 months

Result for these inputs:

- Cash runway: 10.0 months
- Monthly burn: $5,000
- A quarter of the cash gone in: 2.5 months
- Half of the cash gone in: 5.0 months
- Three quarters of the cash gone in: 7.5 months
- Cash at zero in: 10.0 months

Rule of thumb for this result: Healthy runway.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The monthly burn is 0 or less: **Cash flow positive.** Revenue covers expenses and the cash balance is growing.
- The runway is under 3 months: **Critical.** The owner needs to cut expenses now, collect what is owed faster, or bring in more capital. Most businesses need three to six months of runway at the least.
- The runway is 3 months or more and under 6: **Warning.** There is some room but little margin for error. Improving cash flow now avoids forced decisions later.
- The runway is 6 months or more: **Healthy runway.** There is time to make considered decisions. It is best used to improve margins rather than only hold them.

## Notes

- Use averages over several months. One unusual month, good or bad, makes the answer misleading.
- The runway assumes revenue and expenses stay as they are. Say so when giving the answer.
