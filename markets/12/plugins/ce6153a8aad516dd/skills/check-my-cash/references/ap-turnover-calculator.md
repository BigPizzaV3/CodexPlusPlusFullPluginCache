# Accounts Payable Turnover Calculator

Answers: How long do I take to pay my suppliers?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/days-to-pay-bills

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purchases or cost of goods sold | $ | none, ask for it | What the business bought from suppliers in the period. Cost of goods sold is a fair stand-in. |
| Average accounts payable | $ | none, ask for it | The usual balance owed to suppliers over the period. |
| Period the purchases cover | choose one | Annually | Whether the purchases figure is for a month, a quarter or a year. One of: Monthly; Quarterly; Annually. |

## Before you calculate

- Purchases must be above 0.
- Average accounts payable must be above 0.

## Formula

1. Purchases for a year = the purchases figure x 12 if it is monthly, x 4 if it is quarterly, or as given if it is yearly
2. Payables turnover = purchases for a year / average accounts payable
3. Days to pay (DPO) = 365 / payables turnover
4. Purchases a day = purchases for a year / 365
5. Cash kept in the business by paying on this schedule and not at once = purchases a day x days to pay
6. Cash kept by paying 15 days later = purchases a day x 15
7. Cash given up by paying 15 days sooner = purchases a day x 15, or x the days to pay if that is fewer than 15

## Rounding

Give days to the nearest whole day, the turnover to one decimal place and dollars to the nearest whole dollar.

## Worked example

Inputs: Purchases or cost of goods sold $600,000; Average accounts payable $75,000; Period the purchases cover Annually.

1. Purchases for a year = $600,000
2. Payables turnover = $600,000 / $75,000 = 8.0
3. Days to pay = 365 / 8.0 = 46 days
4. Purchases a day = $600,000 / 365 = $1,644
5. Cash kept by paying 15 days later = $1,644 x 15 = $24,658

Result for these inputs:

- Days to pay (DPO): 46 days
- Payables turnover: 8.0 times a year
- Purchases a day: $1,644
- Cash kept by paying on this schedule and not at once: $75,000
- Cash kept by paying 15 days later: $24,658
- Cash given up by paying 15 days sooner: $24,658

Rule of thumb for this result: Normal range.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- Days to pay is under 30: **Fast payer.** Suppliers like it, but unless it earns early-payment discounts the business is giving up free use of its own cash. Paying faster than customers pay the business means financing the gap from its own pocket.
- Days to pay is 30 to 60: **Normal range.** Within typical terms: cash is preserved without straining supplier relationships. Check whether any supplier offers a discount for paying early that is worth taking.
- Days to pay is over 60: **Late payer.** This keeps cash for now at real risk: suppliers may stop extending credit, charge late fees or serve other customers first. If payments are stretched because cash is short, the cash shortage is the problem to fix.

## Notes

- Compare this with the days customers take to pay. A business that pays in 20 days and is paid in 50 is funding 30 days of its own sales.
