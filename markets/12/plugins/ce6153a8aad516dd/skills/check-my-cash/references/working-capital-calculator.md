# Working Capital Calculator

Answers: Do I have enough working capital to cover what I owe in the short term?
Learn more: https://omnyra.ai/wiki/business/working-capital

## Inputs

| Input | Unit | Default | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash and cash equivalents | $ | $0 | Bank balances and anything as good as cash. Use 0 if there is none. |
| Accounts receivable | $ | $0 | What customers owe on invoices already sent. Use 0 if there is none. |
| Inventory | $ | $0 | Stock and materials on hand, at cost. Use 0 if there is none. |
| Other current assets | $ | $0 | Prepaid expenses, deposits and anything else that turns to cash within a year. Use 0 if there is none. |
| Accounts payable | $ | $0 | What the business owes suppliers. Use 0 if there is none. |
| Short-term debt | $ | $0 | Loan payments, credit lines and credit cards due within a year. Use 0 if there is none. |
| Accrued expenses | $ | $0 | Wages, taxes and other costs incurred but not yet paid. Use 0 if there is none. |
| Other current liabilities | $ | $0 | Customer deposits and anything else due within a year. Use 0 if there is none. |

## Before you calculate

- The current assets must add up to more than 0.
- The current liabilities must add up to more than 0.

## Formula

1. Current assets = cash and cash equivalents + accounts receivable + inventory + other current assets
2. Current liabilities = accounts payable + short-term debt + accrued expenses + other current liabilities
3. Working capital = current assets - current liabilities
4. Working capital ratio = current assets / current liabilities

## Rounding

Give dollars to the nearest whole dollar and the ratio to two decimal places.

## Worked example

Inputs: Cash and cash equivalents $50,000; Accounts receivable $35,000; Inventory $20,000; Other current assets $5,000; Accounts payable $25,000; Short-term debt $15,000; Accrued expenses $10,000; Other current liabilities $5,000.

1. Current assets = $50,000 + $35,000 + $20,000 + $5,000 = $110,000
2. Current liabilities = $25,000 + $15,000 + $10,000 + $5,000 = $55,000
3. Working capital = $110,000 - $55,000 = $55,000
4. Working capital ratio = $110,000 / $55,000 = 2.00

Result for these inputs:

- Working capital ratio: 2.00 to 1
- Working capital: $55,000
- Current assets: $110,000
- Current liabilities: $55,000

Rule of thumb for this result: Strong liquidity.

## The Playbook's rule of thumb

- The ratio is over 1.5: **Strong liquidity.** The business can meet its obligations comfortably, absorb surprises and take up opportunities. Check that the surplus is not simply idle cash.
- The ratio is 1.0 to 1.5: **Adequate but tight.** Obligations are covered with little cushion. A slow month, a late-paying customer or a surprise cost could create pressure. Collect receivables faster, hold less inventory and build a larger cash buffer.
- The ratio is under 1.0: **Negative working capital.** The business owes more in the short term than it has to pay with, and may struggle with bills, payroll or suppliers. Collecting what is owed and easing what is due come first.

## Notes

- Count only what is due or turns to cash within a year. Long-term loans and equipment do not belong here.
