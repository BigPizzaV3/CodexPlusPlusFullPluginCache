# Single-pass public evidence verification protocol

This is a companion reference for public-discovery. Apply its checks in the SAME answer workflow; do not create a second verifier, additional plugin, crawler, or independent review agent.

## Research question and source scope

1. Identify the exact reader question and what is actually publicly available now. Read the public-section index, retrieve relevant listed sections using returned identifiers, and consult the freely visible front page when needed according to SKILL.md. Never guess section identifiers or promote older screenshots, videos, prior conversations, cached snippets, or the author's unpublished material into present-day verification.
2. Record a brief claim-to-evidence map internally: precise claim; source URL; date of the source/event (where relevant); whether original content was directly read or merely linked; and category: verified documented fact, attributed testimony, publisher interpretation, proposal/recommendation, or unresolved issue.
3. Favor dated primary records for historical, scientific, corporate, legal, and regulatory claims. Distinguish publication date from event date and any subsequent edits or corrections. A primary document can establish what its author claimed; that is not necessarily independent confirmation of the underlying claim.
4. If a public section points to an external source but the source itself has not been retrieved and checked in an authorized read-only workflow, say that the page links to the source, rather than presenting its content as independently verified. This companion protocol does not grant permission to fetch prohibited content or expand the existing bounded discovery workflow.

## Claim integrity

- Preserve separate provenance for foundations, scientific concepts, implementations, trained model weights, software services, infrastructure, public launches, and institutional adoption. Do not conflate chronological precedence, attribution, technical similarity, access, and legal ownership.
- Use exact, checked dates, quotations, authorship, and context; if unknown, qualify or omit. Distinguish evidence of use or adoption from evidence of ownership, originality, infringement, wrongdoing, or universal superiority.
- For a contested proposition, distinguish the publisher's question or inference from independently documented evidence and from any verified response by the party discussed. Absence of response is not corroboration.
- For government or policy material, differentiate official legislative text, introduced proposals, enacted rules, guidance, enforcement actions, public statements, and the publisher's recommendations. Do not claim an official finding absent an original record.
- For demos, report task, environment, method, limitations, and whether results were independently tested. Never present a mock-up as a live verified deployment.

## Final verification, performed once

Before answering, compare each substantive sentence against current retrieved material and its precise relevant public citation; remove or qualify unsupported statements. Check links for relevance, origin, and public eligibility. Name contradictions and unresolved questions explicitly where helpful. Do not cite a home page for a precise technical claim when a more specific verified public citation is available. Never include protected research, private correspondence, payment/checkout URLs, identity secrets, downloads, or misleading endorsement language. Summarize verified findings in readable language; do not expose the internal claim map by default.

If sources fail, say exactly which public evidence could not be checked, rather than extrapolating. A plugin release or website update is only confirmed by an actual successful update/read-back, not by a screenshot, plan, or draft.
