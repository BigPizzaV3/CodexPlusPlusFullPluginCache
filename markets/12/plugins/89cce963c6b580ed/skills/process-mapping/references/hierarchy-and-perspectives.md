# Hierarchy, handovers and perspectives

Departments describe organisational homes; macro-processes deliver coherent outcomes; procedures are repeatable bounded parts; instructions explain detailed task methods. An org chart informs roles/reporting, not proof of the actual process. Do not derive processes mechanically from departments.

Separate contains (hierarchy), happens-next/depends-on (flow), accountable/contributes (responsibility) and generic traceability. A macro-process crossing departments gets one agreed canonical home and explicit participants/handovers, not copies under every department. If no real home fits, propose an organisation-wide grouping for approval; do not silently invent departments.

Hierarchy levels are department, macro_process, procedure, work_instruction in the observed API. A department text label is not a department parent record. Do not normalise an existing structure or add levels silently. Prevent cycles in hierarchy; process loops/exception routes belong in flow.

Process cards capture purpose, trigger/end, inputs and suppliers, outputs and recipients, accountable role/contributors, activities, handover acceptance, decisions/exceptions, controls, records, systems, measures, sources and gaps. Instructions add known prerequisites/tools/checks; do not invent tolerances, settings or safety-critical methods. Preserve competent-review gaps.

Invite frontline, upstream/downstream, manager, site/shift and relevant external perspectives. Draft questions for the user to take to others; do not message anyone. Record role/person, date/source, stated view, supporting example, affected process and open issue. Model-generated views remain hypotheses.

Different terminology needs definitions; conflicting facts need evidence; valid variations need conditions; ownership gaps need role/hand-off agreement; competing needs may warrant a cloud. Hierarchy neatness or seniority is not proof. Explain who actually reviewed and what remains unagreed.
