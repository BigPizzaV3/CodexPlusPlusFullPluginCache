# Criteria, evidence and fair assessment

Audit method and audit criteria are different. ISO 19011 provides auditing guidance; selected standards, procedures and agreed customer requirements provide applicable criteria. Confirm edition/amendments. Do not silently switch an existing organisation to a new edition.

A standards profile needs identifier, edition/amendments, scope, authoritative source and classification rules. Do not bundle protected standard text or assume ownership permits AI processing. Use authorised content; if unavailable, provide a clearly limited general quality review with proposed clause references marked unverified. Do not issue definitive clause failures from memory.

Combined audits use shared evidence but assess each validated requirement separately. Do not assume clauses from different standards are equivalent.

Plan around work and interfaces; use checklists as flexible aids. Sampling must state the population/context, actual selection, rationale and limits. Avoid universal sample sizes/frequencies presented as ISO requirements. One completed checklist is not proof of effective implementation.

Evidence must be relevant and verifiable. Missing chat detail/access is not automatically failure. A record reference is not proof the model examined the file. A signed/closed action is not proof the change worked. Follow contradictory examples fairly. Audit coaching recommendations cannot be presented as current practice simply because the user repeats the ideal answer.

Report limitations of self-review and remote methods. Human auditor judgement remains identifiable. The plugin produces draft findings and reviewed records, not certification. Useful public method references: https://committee.iso.org/home/tc176/iso-9001-auditing-practices-group.html and https://www.iso.org/standard/19011 . APG papers are guidance, not additional requirements.
