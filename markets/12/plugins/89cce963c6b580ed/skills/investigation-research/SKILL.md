# Investigation research

Read [modes](../instructions/references/modes.md). Before saving also read [connection](../instructions/references/connection.md), [change control](../instructions/references/change-control.md) and [human decisions](../instructions/references/human-decisions.md).

Help with the investigation before requesting a complete questionnaire. Establish observed failure versus expected behaviour, scope, timing, affected/unaffected examples and urgent containment considerations. Ask the few questions that change useful next steps.

Select relevant perspectives: design, materials, manufacturing, measurement, maintenance, supplier control, logistics, human factors or data analysis. Explain why they matter. These are analytical perspectives, not independent experts, simulated participants or evidence that anyone reviewed the case. No fixed expert count or multi-agent execution is required.

Generate a few distinct causal mechanisms, including occurrence, escape/detection and wider system conditions where relevant. Avoid generic lists and unsupported human-error/training conclusions. Check credible alternatives, including measurement or interpretation error. Expand only as needed.

Research using available host tools; read [research rules](references/research-rules.md). Connect each mechanism to case observations or explicit gaps. External publications establish plausibility, not what happened here.

For material hypotheses produce: ID, mechanism, perspective, case evidence for/against, research sources/applicability, discriminating check, expected findings, evidence state and proposed next action. States are suggested, investigating, supported, contradicted or verified; they are not API enums. Give a concise useful summary first.

Prioritise the next information-gathering step by its discriminatory value, feasibility, cost and risk. If causes cannot be ranked, say so. Use qualitative evidence strength with reasons; invent no probabilities. Revise or retire hypotheses when results contradict them. Do not force one cause or five levels of why.

Verification requires supporting case evidence and reviewed conclusions, not agreement to investigate. Do not turn brainstorming into rootCauseSummary or overwrite an established conclusion. Save only the approved user-facing synthesis and actions into supported visible fields; never save hidden reasoning or fabricate native hypothesis fields. Preserve unresolved alternatives and readable source references.

Example: a recurring dimensional discrepancy may warrant process variation, material condition and measurement checks. Request useful comparisons; do not declare the instrument faulty. If a checked record contradicts the leading cause, revise the investigation instead of inventing supporting evidence.

If research is unavailable, label the output unverified hypotheses and give a research/check plan. Standalone mode still receives useful reasoning and a portable brief. Connected mode may add relevant authorised history, without treating similarity as proof or a sparse pattern as statistically established.
