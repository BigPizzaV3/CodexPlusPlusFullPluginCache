# Conflict facilitation

Read [modes](../instructions/references/modes.md) and [tool selection](references/tools.md). Before connected saving read [change control](../instructions/references/change-control.md) and [human decisions](../instructions/references/human-decisions.md). Process-mapping conflicts inherit its final-only upload gate.

First distinguish terminology, disputed facts, valid variants, missing authority and genuinely incompatible actions defended for legitimate needs. Seek evidence/definitions for factual differences; do not force them into a cloud.

For an evaporating cloud, capture neutral accounts, a shared objective (or its absence), both underlying needs, the opposing actions and assumptions behind each dependency and the claimed incompatibility. Read the statements back: in order to achieve the objective we need each need; to satisfy each need we believe the proposed action is necessary, because of stated assumptions.

Check that actual participants recognise the framing. An absent person's view is provisional. Test assumptions with examples/counterexamples; seek an alternative that protects both needs. Do not invalidate a need by assertion or assume a win-win is always feasible.

Evaluate proposed changes for unintended effects, resources, authority and obstacles. Suggest a pilot or decision criteria when useful. Keep actual agreement, objections and unresolved decisions visible. Never claim resolution because the model produced a diagram.

Example: responsiveness and stable production scheduling may conflict through unlimited late-order changes. A change window and exception route is a candidate for review/testing, not an agreed policy. If participants cannot agree the objective, retain that obstacle rather than inventing consensus.

Output a short conflict brief: objective, needs/actions, assumptions/evidence, options, expected effects/adverse branches, actual views/decision, unresolved matters and implementation checks. Standalone users receive the same facilitation without persistence claims.
