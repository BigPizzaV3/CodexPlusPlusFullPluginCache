## Purpose

Create a distinctive contemporary website that references a coherent part of the recent design past through typography, composition, imagery, color relationships, surface character, and interaction cues while preserving current usability and canonical Alpy implementation.

Retro is not one movement or a synonym for old. It reinterprets recognizable relationships from an earlier period, medium, or visual culture without collapsing decades and cultures into a generic vintage look or claiming historical authenticity.

After the shared chat confirmation, use this profile only when Retro was selected under confirmed delegation or preserved from the confirmed restatement. Apply it with relevant Design intent skills and the canonical Alpy package.

This profile describes semantic relationships and selection priorities. It does not define CSS, raw values, named typefaces, fixed colors, assets, components, tokens, modes, breakpoints, effects, motion, product behavior, or canonical interaction patterns.

## Identity

### Principle

Reference the past coherently; design the experience for the present.

Build recognition through a small, consistent set of historically informed relationships. Preserve modern information architecture, responsive behavior, accessibility requirements, component semantics, and interaction conventions.

### Definition and scope

Use **Retro** for new work that consciously references the relatively recent past. Use **Vintage** for genuine objects or assets from an earlier period. A contemporary reinterpretation may use vintage material, but the terms are not interchangeable.

Retro can be affectionate, critical, playful, ironic, archival, or commercially expressive. Nostalgia may occur, but it is audience- and context-dependent; do not make nostalgia, familiarity, authenticity, comfort, or trust a guaranteed outcome.

Historical source material demonstrates many distinct movements, technologies, cultures, functions, and political settings. Choose one coherent reference family. Do not treat the past as a neutral motif library.

### Character

Retro should feel:

- Familiar without becoming derivative.
- Characterful without becoming theatrical.
- Historically suggestive without claiming authenticity.
- Crafted without appearing damaged or unfinished.
- Expressive without overwhelming content.
- Playful, critical, calm, or energetic according to context rather than formula.
- Contemporary in use even when historical in appearance.
- Coherent across pages rather than assembled from souvenirs.

### Anti-goals

Do not turn Retro into:

- A random mixture of decades, movements, media, and cultural references.
- Beige paper, faded ink, warm browns, neon, or another fixed palette.
- A novelty font, grain overlay, halftone, sunburst, checkerboard, or sticker collection.
- Artificial aging, damage, dust, scratches, fold marks, tape, or print errors.
- An imitation of obsolete software, hardware, browser behavior, or technical limits.
- A nostalgic or authenticity claim unsupported by the audience, brand, or evidence.
- A generic American or Western past presented as universal history.
- Propaganda, stereotypes, exclusion, or outdated language used as atmosphere.
- Direct imitation of a recognizable designer, artwork, brand, publication, interface, or campaign.

### Signature traits

Build the direction through:

- One coherent reference family selected from confirmed context or the safe default foundation.
- A deliberate relationship among type, imagery, color, framing, surface, and composition.
- Decisive display-versus-reading hierarchy using canonical typography roles.
- Repetition resembling an intentional publishing, packaging, broadcast, or early-digital system.
- Strong section identities connected by one visual grammar.
- Period-suggestive relationships translated through current Alpy components and tokens.
- Supported assets with known rights, provenance, relevance, and context.
- Modern navigation, controls, applicable states, focus, and responsive behavior.

The result must remain recognizable without relying on a decade label, cultural stereotype, fixed palette, named typeface, texture, motif, obsolete control, or animation.

## Expression Model

Infer one dominant reference family internally from confirmed purpose, audience, brand history, content, supplied assets, and any requested era. Do not expose the families as another configuration step or combine them indiscriminately.

Do not infer a period, culture, country, or subculture from brand mood, geography, audience stereotypes, or visual resemblance. Use the default foundation when authoritative context is absent.

Use a secondary influence only when authoritative project context establishes a real relationship. Apply the selected reference across the website; page-family variation comes from confirmed layout and content, not a new era per page. Describe the result as a contemporary interpretation unless evidence supports a more specific claim.

### Print and commercial

Use decisive display hierarchy, purposeful approved illustration or product imagery, concise copy, framed information, repeated promotional formats, and controlled color relationships. Favor this for hospitality, food, retail, events, entertainment, campaigns, and consumer brands.

Do not invent packaging history, period advertisements, endorsements, prices, seals, mascots, or claims.

### Editorial and cultural

Use strong type–image relationships, column rhythm, captions, credits, recurring departments, issue-like sections, and controlled expressive variation. Favor this for music, film, publishing, portfolios, festivals, galleries, and cultural programs.

Do not confuse editorial energy with Newsprint, collage every section, or fabricate archival material.

### Broadcast and consumer technology

Use bold information bands, explicit labels, repeated modules, product framing, and supported panel relationships. Favor this for media, audio, games, and products with genuine analog or earlier-digital lineage.

Do not create fake hardware controls, technical readouts, signal noise, scan lines, or specifications.

### Early digital and web

Use direct navigation, visible links, modular content, explicit labels, compact metadata, simple surface relationships, and approved low-resolution or pixel-based assets when supplied. Preserve earlier websites' candid, constructed character without reproducing technical constraints.

Do not use obsolete layout, tiny bitmap text, image-map navigation, auto-playing media, splash gates, cursor trails, marquees, blinking content, browser chrome, fake system dialogs, or broken responsive behavior.

### Archival and heritage

Use calm hierarchy, provenance, dates, captions, records, collections, and supplied historical material to connect past and present. Favor this for institutions, anniversaries, archives, long-lived organizations, and documented brand heritage.

Do not invent provenance, age, continuity, founders, milestones, cultural authority, or institutional endorsement.

### Default foundation

When no project-specific period or lineage is supported, use the declared late-1960s–1970s print-led synthesis. Translate its broad commercial and editorial logic through direct display hierarchy, framed content, repeated graphic relationships, controlled accent contrast, clear type–image structure, and contemporary components. Do not present it as an authentic reconstruction or add details that imply a particular country, community, movement, technology, or event.

### Functional restraint

Reduce historical expression around navigation, forms, authentication, transactions, dense data, legal content, consent, errors, and recovery. Preserve the direction through supported type relationships, framing, surface contrast, spacing, and repetition without weakening conventional interaction or consequence.

### Minimum signature

Establish all of the following when canonical support and content permit:

- One supported reference family—or the declared print-led synthesis—applied consistently across page families.
- One recognizable display-to-reading hierarchy.
- One repeated framing, divider, surface, or section-label relationship.
- One coherent image, illustration, or asset treatment based on supplied material.
- One contemporary interaction system compatible with the reference.

If canonical support or supplied assets cannot preserve a recognizable minimum signature, report a blocking style gap instead of fabricating historical detail. Use the nearest supported type, color, surface, spacing, repetition, and framing relationships only when the minimum signature survives.

### Website and Product Design boundary

Use this profile for public, evaluative, editorial, campaign, cultural, archival, service, and bounded transactional surfaces.

For authenticated workflows, editable data, persistent operational context, administration, professional tools, or application shells, use Product Design when available. Otherwise keep the work bounded to confirmed responsibilities and report unresolved product scope. Historical interface language does not create product architecture, data, hardware behavior, or controls.

## Visual Expression

### Hierarchy

Lead with the confirmed subject, proposition, artifact, or action. Establish a decisive display layer, readable supporting layer, and consistent labels or metadata through canonical roles.

Use scale, placement, spacing, surface, framing, and image relationships to evoke the selected reference family. Keep one dominant focal moment and quieter reading or task regions.

Do not make every section a poster, every heading oversized, or every label a decorative badge.

### Typography character

Use only typefaces, roles, complete tuples, and mappings available in `alpy/type-scale.md`. Resolve component-owned typography slots and bindings through `alpy/component-tokens.md`.

Select the closest supported character to the confirmed reference only when canonical metadata or a confirmed project source establishes that quality; otherwise choose by semantic role and complete tuple. Editorial, geometric, humanist, grotesque, serif, slab, monospaced, condensed, rounded, or display qualities may be relevant when available. Preserve readable body, label, helper, control, and metadata roles.

Do not add novelty or distressed fonts, imitate hand lettering, reconstruct period typography, manufacture weights, or force case, outline, shadow, tracking, compression, or line breaks outside canonical definitions.

### Color behavior

Resolve component-owned color through `alpy/component-tokens.md` and remaining intent through `alpy/semantic-tokens.md` and explicit mode mappings.

Use a restrained relational palette with a stable field, strong foreground contrast, purposeful emphasis, and clear semantic roles. Warm, cool, muted, saturated, earthy, synthetic, or high-contrast relationships may support a reference only when the confirmed direction and canonical mappings support them.

Do not invent period colors, fade the interface for age, reuse functional roles decoratively, or rely on color alone. Historical association is evidence, not permission to bypass semantic mappings.

### Shape, surface, and production character

Use canonical surfaces, borders, radii, dividers, and component anatomy. Let the reference guide whether the system feels crisp, soft, framed, banded, panel-like, or print-like within supported values.

Suggest production through composition, repetition, type–image relationships, and approved assets before texture. Use texture only when supplied, licensed, canonically supported, and meaningful.

Do not add custom badges, bursts, scallops, tickets, tabs, labels, chrome, knobs, stickers, torn edges, ornamental frames, grain, ink bleed, noise, photocopying, halftone, misregistration, film burn, tape, scratches, scan lines, compression, or screen glow.

### Imagery and illustration

Use only supplied and approved imagery with supported media behavior. Direct product studies, archival records, editorial crops, period photography, graphic illustration, ephemera, or early-digital assets may support the reference when present.

Preserve subject clarity, captions, dates, creators, sources, rights, and alternatives. Do not recolor, distress, crop, relabel, or decontextualize historical material in ways that change meaning. Do not invent archival images or present contemporary media as evidence.

### Iconography

Use only canonical icons and semantic mappings. Keep icons recognizable, consistently placed, and paired with labels when meaning is not self-evident.

Do not substitute clip art, dingbats, emoji, pixel symbols, arrows, stars, seals, or historical marks for canonical icons.

## Spatial Expression

### Layout bias

Preserve confirmed layout and hierarchy. Retro changes their expression; it does not silently select or replace structure.

Match the treatment to the selected reference without recreating obsolete media constraints. Reduce historical expression when the confirmed layout needs stronger scanning, task clarity, or information density.

### Grid and alignment

Favor a stable canonical grid with visible alignment, repeated modules, and purposeful scale changes. Print-led references may use columns and framing; early-digital references may use explicit modules; broadcast references may use bands or panels.

Use exact containers, columns, spans, gutters, and responsive behavior only through Alpy. Keep headings, images, captions, metadata, and actions connected through shared edges. Do not reproduce print dimensions, fixed canvases, table layouts, imperfect registration, hand placement, or early-web misalignment.

### Density and spacing rhythm

Choose density from content and task first. Comfortable density may support narrative or image-led work; default or compact density may support catalogs, schedules, listings, archives, or early-digital references when legibility and targets remain intact.

Use spacing roles and component slots from `alpy/foundations.md` and `alpy/component-tokens.md`. Build rhythm through repeated modules, labels, image-caption relationships, bands, dividers, and pauses without inventing another spacing system.

### Content measure

Keep sustained reading within a comfortable canonical measure. Display text, short promotions, labels, captions, and metadata may take more expressive positions, but long content must remain stable and readable.

## Component Expression

Use canonical components for their documented purpose. Retro may guide selection and supported expression but cannot create anatomy, variants, states, values, content, or behavior.

### Navigation

Keep destinations explicit, stable, and scannable. Use direct labels, canonical selection, and clear orientation. Supported framing, typography, or surface contrast may shape the shell.

Do not recreate magazine contents as navigation, hide destinations in imagery, or imitate browser, television, radio, or hardware controls.

### Actions and links

Use canonical action hierarchy and specific contemporary language. Let supported type, border, surface, and placement relationships make actions compatible with the reference.

Keep links visibly identifiable. Do not turn every label into a badge or button, decorate static text like a link, or make an action look like an obsolete control.

### Controls and forms

Preserve documented anatomy, persistent labels, instructions, targets, focus, validation, consent, and recovery. Use current conventions even when surface expression references an earlier period.

Do not build typewriter fields, rotary controls, physical switches, terminal prompts, paper forms, or fake system widgets unless canonical components and confirmed purpose establish them.

### Containers and cards

Use containers for meaningful grouping, repeated items, summaries, and actions. Apply one coherent framing or surface logic derived from the reference.

Do not turn every section into a ticket, clipping, advertisement, record sleeve, window, or trading card.

### Temporary surfaces

Preserve canonical modality, dismissal, focus management, placement, and layering for dialogs, menus, popovers, tooltips, and alerts. Make them contemporary and unmistakable even when framed by a historical expression.

Do not invent temporary components, fake operating-system dialogs, or rely on nostalgic window chrome to communicate consequence.

### Data and metadata

Use consistent canonical roles for dates, values, units, identifiers, prices, credits, citations, and status. Historical dates and claims require authoritative project sources.

Do not invent serial numbers, issue numbers, timestamps, copyright lines, archive stamps, ratings, or technical readouts as decoration.

## Page And Section Expression

### Header and navigation

Establish identity, navigation, utilities, and current location through one repeatable shell. Reference the selected family with supported framing, typography, labels, or surface contrast while retaining contemporary wayfinding.

### Hero and opening region

Lead with one confirmed proposition, subject, event, product, or artifact. Use one decisive type–image, framed-media, promotional, or archival relationship and keep the primary action clear.

Do not default to a nostalgic slogan, fake advertisement, record player, television, computer window, or collage of period objects.

### Section rhythm

Repeat a small set of section labels, dividers, bands, image formats, and type relationships. Alternate expressive focal regions with calmer content and task regions.

### Content and collection regions

Give each region one purpose. Use repeated image, title, summary, metadata, and action relationships for collections. Let actual artifacts, products, stories, or events provide diversity.

Do not vary every card to imitate found ephemera or assign false historical meaning to contemporary content.

### Evidence and provenance

Place dates, sources, creators, rights, credits, and context near historical material. Distinguish original archival content, later reproduction, contemporary reinterpretation, and illustrative reference when it matters.

### Calls to action

Place actions where their purpose, outcome, conditions, and consequences are clear. Use direct contemporary wording and canonical hierarchy; do not imitate period sales pressure, sensational claims, or urgency devices.

### Footer

Use the footer for durable navigation, contact, resources, credits, and confirmed policy destinations. A restrained colophon or publication relationship may support the direction when content is real and canonical roles permit it.

## Historical And Cultural Responsibility

Retro references carry social, political, commercial, and cultural context. Treat historical design as evidence of a time and place, not a neutral motif library.

Do not combine eras or cultures merely because their forms appear compatible. Do not remove political, activist, colonial, labor, racial, gender, disability, or community context from material used for atmosphere. Do not revive exclusion, stereotypes, propaganda, or outdated terminology as brand character.

Do not imply that an organization, product, or brand is older, established, handmade, local, authentic, culturally rooted, or historically endorsed without confirmed evidence. Visual treatment is not proof of heritage or authenticity.

Use historical works, logos, advertisements, publications, photographs, illustrations, interfaces, characters, and recordings only when applicable rights, source, purpose, and attribution are confirmed. Age, online access, or collection inclusion does not independently establish reuse permission. Authoritative project sources and qualified specialists own policy and legal requirements; report missing authority rather than making a legal conclusion.

Respect cultural sensitivity, privacy, publicity, trademark, donor, and community restrictions when authoritative sources identify them, even if copyright permits reuse. Prefer project-supplied or clearly licensed material. Record creator, title, date, source, license, and required credit when available. Do not closely imitate an identifiable creator or reconstruct referenced work without confirmed purpose, rights, and attribution.

## State Expression

Apply every applicable, confirmed, canonically supported state. Historical appearance must never make state behavior obsolete, ambiguous, or decorative. Do not invent state truth to make Retro visible.

- **Default:** Make role and interactivity recognizable before hover or focus.
- **Hover:** Use supported behavior to clarify interactivity; do not reveal essential content only on hover.
- **Focus:** Preserve canonical visible focus above patterns, imagery, borders, and historical surfaces.
- **Pressed:** Use documented behavior; do not imitate mechanical failure or print damage.
- **Selected:** Use persistent canonical selection distinct from hover, focus, and decoration.
- **Disabled:** Preserve documented behavior and readable identification; do not imitate faded ink or broken hardware.
- **Loading:** Preserve context and supported progress communication; decorative retro loaders cannot be the only status.
- **Empty:** Explain the region and offer a relevant supported action when available.
- **Success:** Use confirmed meaning, canonical feedback, and direct language.
- **Warning:** State the condition, consequence, and available response through canonical roles.
- **Error:** Identify the affected content or action, explain the problem, and support recovery.

Do not represent state through color, distress, flicker, static, stamps, badges, sound, or animation alone.

## Interaction Expression

### Affordance

Use current, familiar interaction conventions. Links should look like links, buttons like buttons, inputs like inputs, and static historical material like content.

Only interactive elements should respond as interactive. Preserve back behavior, scrolling, keyboard access, logical focus order, target usability, and input-method parity.

### [Motion character](../ui-design/motion-transition.md)

Motion is optional. Retro must remain complete and recognizable statically.

When supported, use concise motion that reinforces sequence, reveal, paging, broadcast transition, direct manipulation, or state change appropriate to the reference. Respect confirmed reduced-motion requirements.

Do not invent film jitter, projector flicker, scan lines, channel noise, blinking text, marquees, cursor trails, bouncing icons, auto-playing audio, parallax, or continuous loops.

### Feedback character

Keep feedback immediate, plain, and connected to the affected component. Use canonical labels, icons, placement, and state roles; historical tone must not obscure consequences or recovery.

## Adaptation

### Responsive priorities

Preserve purpose, navigation, reading order, logical focus order, primary actions, components, captions, provenance, and state meaning as space changes. Retain the reference grammar before decorative arrangement.

Reflow poster-like, editorial, banded, or panel-based regions into a clear sequence. Reduce nonessential motifs, framing, texture, and decorative metadata before compressing text, clipping imagery, or detaching captions and actions.

Use only supported responsive behavior. Report missing layout coverage instead of creating custom breakpoints or fixed historical canvases.

### Mode behavior

Resolve an explicit `light` or `dark` mode through the package and use its mode-specific semantic mappings and component bindings. Preserve hierarchy, contrast, reference character, focus, and state clarity independently in every supported mode.

Do not treat cream paper, black screens, faded surfaces, or any light or dark appearance as universally Retro. Do not infer one mode from another or invent local mode styling.

### Content resilience

Support long and translated text, right-to-left reading, real names, variable metadata, missing imagery, forms, errors, user text spacing, zoom, reflow, and content extremes.

Let frames, labels, display roles, and image relationships adapt. Do not truncate essential language, split words for effect, preserve decorative composition over reading order, or embed translatable text in imagery.

### Distinction from adjacent directions

- **Vintage:** Describes genuine objects or assets from an earlier period. Retro describes newly made work that references the past.
- **Art Deco and Bauhaus:** Historically specific directions with their own contexts and principles. Use their registered profiles when clearly requested.
- **Newsprint:** Centers editorial medium, information density, and newspaper-like structure. Retro may be editorial but requires broader historical reference.
- **Terminal:** Centers command-line and system-interface language. Early-digital Retro may reference older web culture without requiring terminal behavior.
- **Vaporwave and Cyberpunk:** Have distinct cultural and speculative languages. Neon, grids, chrome, or digital artifacts are not interchangeable Retro cues.
- **Retrofuturism:** Reinterprets a past culture's vision of the future. Retro more broadly reinterprets past design and does not imply speculative technology.

Do not infer compatibility or combine profiles until the registered catalog or selected profiles establishes that relationship.

## Success Criteria

The result should:

- Feel Retro through one coherent supported reference or the declared print-led synthesis expressed across type, imagery, color, framing, composition, and component treatment rather than clichés.
- Present decisive contemporary hierarchy and an understandable action path.
- Use historical cues to support meaning, identity, or atmosphere without claiming authenticity.
- Make pages, sections, components, applicable states, and transitions part of one reference system.
- Preserve current navigation, affordance, access requirements, responsive behavior, and recovery.
- Remain recognizable across supported modes and viewports without relying on a decade label, cultural stereotype, fixed palette, named typeface, texture, motif, or obsolete interaction.
- Use only canonical Alpy components, tokens, roles, mappings, values, approved assets, applicable states, and behavior.
- Remain accurate, rights-aware, culturally responsible, and resilient across content extremes and localization.

If the result appears Retro only because it uses faded colors, novelty type, paper texture, halftone, neon, pixel graphics, or old objects, the direction has collapsed into a costume and failed.

If historical reference weakens truth, rights, cultural responsibility, navigation, readability, or task completion, the direction has failed.
