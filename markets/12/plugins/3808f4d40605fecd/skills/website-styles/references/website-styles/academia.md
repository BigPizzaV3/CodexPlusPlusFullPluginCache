### UI Skill Coordination

Academia coordinates the linked UI skills into one scholarly aesthetic. Each Art Direction section names the disciplines responsible for its visual expression; Academia does not replace them or invent their values.

[Hero](../hero.md), [User Experience](../user-experience.md), [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md), and [Accessibility Audit](../accessibility-audit.md) own opening structure, semantics, reflow, and verification. Academia directs how their visible result belongs to the same aesthetic world.

## Art Direction

Create a calm knowledge environment in which subject, evidence, attribution, and context form one editorial world. Knowledge is the visual protagonist. Academia is neither an institutional website category nor a heritage theme.

The rendered direction must share:

- One primary knowledge subject: a document, argument, person, place, object, artwork, figure, dataset, research work, teaching material, or collection.
- One dominant editorial relationship between the primary field and its supporting evidence, annotation, or metadata.
- One research, teaching, people, place, object, document, diagram, or product-media family.
- One typographic relationship among display, reading, quotation, and scholarly-apparatus roles.
- One canvas–surface–rule–accent–depth relationship.
- One dominant visual mass and one quieter counterweight.

Carry these decisions through the complete page. Create richness through hierarchy, real material, measured spacing, captions, annotation, evidence, and precise repetition—not academic decoration. The atmosphere may be contemporary or historical, light or dark, austere or warm, but it must remain rigorous, curious, calm, and visibly grounded in its subject.

Academia does not require serif typography, muted or dark palettes, institutional architecture, archival textures, crests, seals, columns, books, ceremonial language, or a light canvas. None of those references creates the style by itself.

### Composition and Editorial [Grid](../ui-design/grid-layout.md)

[Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) establishes the editorial field, [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md) its reading edges, [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) its spans, [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) its density, and [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) its focal order.

- Use one aligned editorial grid with a primary reading or content field. Add a supporting rail only when navigation, metadata, notes, references, or related material needs it.
- Organize three visual levels: primary material; supporting explanation or evidence; quiet metadata, navigation, and utilities.
- Use strong shared edges, stable section starts, measured reading widths, and open space around important material.
- Give figures, tables, timelines, maps, documents, artwork, collections, and product views the width their content requires. Let the true subject dominate.
- Keep supporting information subordinate. Avoid equal-weight sections, repetitive card walls, and institutional promotion competing with knowledge or evidence.
- Let [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md) recompose the editorial system for narrow viewports; never shrink the desktop arrangement.

### Opening Composition

[Hero](../hero.md) establishes the opening structure; [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md), [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md), [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md), and [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) turn it into an Academia composition.

- Make one title, person, program, research subject, collection object, publication, figure, or primary task the focal point.
- Stage approved subject matter at meaningful scale. Keep title, identity, summary, attribution, action, and media in one direct visual sequence.
- Add one compact evidence or context group when it strengthens orientation or authority; keep it subordinate to the subject.
- Use captions or credits when the opening media requires them, and keep them visually attached.
- Do not substitute a library photograph, classical building, manuscript texture, crest, or oversized quotation for a specific subject and purpose.

### Scholarly [Typography](../ui-design/typography-readability.md)

[Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) establishes the complete type system; [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md), [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md), and [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) shape its editorial expression.

- Distinguish title, argument, body, quotation, annotation, citation, metadata, navigation, and action through a restrained role system.
- Use one canonical display or editorial role, a highly readable body role, and compact but legible scholarly-apparatus roles.
- Keep long-form text calm, consistently aligned, comfortably measured, and easy to resume. Use emphasis sparingly so quotations, notes, and references remain distinct.
- Display type may become a compositional mass when a smaller caption, index, or metadata block counterbalances it.
- Serif and monospaced faces are optional. Do not imitate scholarship through justified body text, tiny metadata, decorative italics, forced uppercase, invented metrics, or hardcoded line breaks.

### Measured Asymmetrical [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md)

[Balance](../ui-design/balance.md) distributes visual weight, [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) establishes emphasis, [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) creates contrast, [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) controls open–dense cadence, and [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) preserves grouping.

- Give each major region one dominant reading or media mass and one quieter counterweight: a rail, caption, quotation, annotation, metadata block, rule, or open margin.
- Create tension through unequal columns, scale, density, imagery, and negative space—not arbitrary offsets.
- Anchor asymmetry to shared edges and recurring intervals so the composition feels deliberate rather than scattered.
- Let negative space carry visual weight. A small, precise scholarly apparatus may balance a large field of text, media, or data.

### Documentary Media

[Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) sets emphasis, [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) placement, [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md) shared edges, [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) media proportions, [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) media-caption relationships, and [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) supported treatment.

- Use approved photography, portraits, places, objects, documents, research imagery, diagrams, maps, figures, collections, artwork, data, or genuine product views that explain the subject or establish real context.
- Coordinate one crop logic, palette relationship, edge treatment, caption system, and credit treatment. Keep focal subjects visible and figures legible.
- Distinguish primary material from interpretation. Attach captions, dates, credits, labels, and sources to the correct media on a shared edge.
- Use decisive scale and crop; do not reduce meaningful media to repetitive thumbnail decoration.
- Never fabricate people, institutions, publications, archives, research, citations, credentials, data, or provenance. Avoid generic books, graduation caps, columns, microscopes, campuses, classical buildings, and manuscripts used as academic filler.

### Scholarly Apparatus: References, Metadata, and Annotation

[Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md) creates scanning edges, [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) attaches related information, [Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) establishes apparatus roles, and [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) places columns, rails, and marginal structures. [User Experience](../user-experience.md) preserves factual meaning.

- Treat author, institution, date, source, version, status, rights, identifier, edition, citation, and provenance as visible graphic material when relevant and confirmed.
- Attach captions to figures and notes to references. Align metadata in columns, rails, labels, folios, indices, or marginal structures that remain visibly tied to the primary material.
- Use compact rules, numbering, annotation marks, and typographic contrast to clarify relationships—not as faux-scholarly decoration.
- Visually distinguish scholarly apparatus from navigation and controls, institutional identity from individual authorship, and primary sources from interpretation through hierarchy, alignment, labels, and attachment.
- Use scholarly apparatus only for confirmed material. Decorative authority cues never substitute for real sources; [User Experience](../user-experience.md) owns truth and trust decisions.

### Quiet Surfaces, Rules, and Restrained [Color](../ui-design/color-discipline.md)

[Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) creates the palette and contrast, [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md) the rules and boundaries, and [Elevation](../ui-design/elevation.md) the depth relationship.

- Use a quiet canvas, clear content surfaces, precise dividers, and restrained depth. Let whitespace and rules separate chapters, records, figures, notes, results, and utilities before adding containment.
- Maintain one coordinated canvas, surface, text, accent, and functional color system. Use accent to clarify hierarchy, categories, links, actions, current location, and semantic states—not to manufacture heritage.
- Let borders and dividers continue the editorial grid through shared edges, captions, metadata columns, figures, and section thresholds.
- Keep depth subordinate to text, media, and rules. Academia may be flat or lightly layered, but it should not become a wall of floating cards.
- Do not invent parchment, ink, sepia, burgundy, forest green, brass, or archival colors. Do not add paper texture, aging, embossing, ornamental frames, seals, crests, or ceremonial motifs unless they are approved brand or content assets.

### Page Rhythm and [Motion](../ui-design/motion-transition.md)

[Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) creates visual pacing, [Motion and Transition](../ui-design/motion-transition.md) temporal pacing, and [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) continuity across regions.

- Alternate sustained reading with structured reference, discovery, figures, or documentary media. Follow dense evidence with open space or a quiet transition.
- Repeat a reading edge, rule, caption position, media proportion, annotation rhythm, or surface relationship to connect sections without making them identical.
- Let one opening subject establish the visual argument; let later regions deepen it; let the closing echo one earlier alignment, rule, media treatment, or typographic relationship.
- Reveal figures, references, annotations, and related material in their reading relationships. Avoid theatrical motion that turns scholarship into spectacle or separates evidence from its source.
- Do not create rhythm through ceremonial banners, repetitive promotional cards, constant calls to action, or a new visual grammar in every section.

## Practical Website Applications

| Application | Academia art direction |
|---|---|
| Article or publication | Use a measured reading field, decisive title hierarchy, and an optional annotation or reference rail with visibly attached authorship, dates, notes, and citations. |
| Research or data | Give the primary figure, table, map, timeline, or data view the dominant span; keep source, caption, legend, and interpretation subordinate but attached. |
| Collection or archive | Pair the object, artwork, image, or document with aligned title, date, creator, rights, identifier, and provenance. |
| Program, person, or institution | Lead with real work, portraiture, place, teaching, or research material; use factual attribution instead of ceremonial imagery or repetitive promotional cards. |
| Product or digital scholarship | Treat genuine interfaces, tools, or interactive figures as documentary media with clear scale, captions, context, and source relationships. |
| Section transition | Continue a reading edge, rule, annotation rhythm, crop, media proportion, or open field instead of introducing ornamental dividers. |

## Canonical Resolution

Resolve mode, components, and values through the linked UI skills and canonical Alpy system. Use only confirmed, permissioned media, scholarship, metadata, and institutional identity; never invent unsupported values, content, components, evidence, credentials, behavior, or capability.

If the Art Direction cannot remain recognizably Academia after canonical resolution, report the exact gap.

## Mandatory Visual Compliance Check

Approve the rendered style only when every condition is visible:

1. Art Direction unifies one knowledge subject, editorial relationship, media family, typographic relationship, material atmosphere, and visual counterweight across the page.
2. [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md), [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md), [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md), [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md), and [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) preserve one editorial field with measured widths, decisive spans, strong reading edges, and open space.
3. [Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md), [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md), and [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) create a scholarly hierarchy with a deliberate dominant mass, quieter counterweight, and legible apparatus.
4. Documentary media remains meaningful, decisively scaled, consistently treated, and visibly attached to captions, credits, sources, references, and provenance.
5. [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md), [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md), [Elevation](../ui-design/elevation.md), and [Motion and Transition](../ui-design/motion-transition.md) preserve a quiet atmosphere, precise rules, restrained depth, and calm pacing.
6. The result remains recognizably Academia without relying on serif type, parchment, books, columns, crests, classical buildings, ceremonial effects, or other academic clichés.
7. [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md) and [Accessibility Audit](../accessibility-audit.md) preserve the Art Direction, reading comfort, visual hierarchy, attached apparatus, and media legibility across supported viewports.

If any condition fails, revise the Art Direction or do not use Academia for that region.
