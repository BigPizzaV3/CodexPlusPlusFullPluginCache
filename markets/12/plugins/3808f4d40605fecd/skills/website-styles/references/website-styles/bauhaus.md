### UI Skill Coordination

Bauhaus coordinates the linked UI skills into one functional-modern aesthetic. Each Art Direction section names the disciplines responsible for its visual expression; Bauhaus does not replace them or invent their values.

[Hero](../hero.md), [User Experience](../user-experience.md), [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md), and [Accessibility Audit](../accessibility-audit.md) own opening structure, semantics, reflow, and verification. Bauhaus directs how their visible result belongs to the same constructed system.

## Art Direction

Create a functional-modern website whose construction is visible through modular order, elementary geometry, bold type–image relationships, purposeful color fields, and disciplined experiment. Content, typography, media, geometry, components, and technology must read as one useful system.

The rendered direction must share:

- One primary function and one dominant subject, statement, product, or task.
- One modular grid and one dominant span relationship.
- One primary geometric family and one quieter supporting family.
- One functional color-field rule.
- One coordinated media treatment.
- One dominant visual mass and one deliberate counterweight.

Carry these decisions through the complete page. Create energy through scale, asymmetry, cropping, contrast, repetition, and visible construction—not ornament.

Bauhaus does not require red, blue, yellow, black, white, circles, squares, triangles, industrial texture, lowercase type, or a geometric font. Those references become costume when they are not integrated into function and structure.

### Required Bauhaus System

The following constraints are mandatory across every Art Direction section:

1. Give every visible element a communicative, structural, orienting, or interactive purpose. Remove decoration that does not clarify content, hierarchy, navigation, sequence, grouping, direction, or action.
2. Use one explicit modular [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) system across the page. Repeat its columns, shared [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md), gaps, dividers, and span relationships instead of inventing a layout for every section.
3. Establish one dominant subject, statement, product, or task in each major region. Use [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) and [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) to keep supporting modules subordinate.
4. Choose one primary geometric family and one supporting family. Use circles, rectangles, triangles, lines, arcs, or planes as containers, crops, connectors, dividers, or counterweights—not stickers.
5. Create asymmetric [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md) through one dominant mass and one deliberate counterweight. Every offset must align to the grid or balance a specific element.
6. Use [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) to make color fields group, separate, direct, identify, or emphasize. Keep the palette constrained; primary colors are optional, not a required formula.
7. Integrate [Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) and media into the grid. Their edges, scale, crop, and alignment must participate in the same construction as components and surfaces.
8. Repeat modules and components consistently. Variation must communicate real priority, content type, or behavior.
9. Keep navigation, forms, pricing, data, and routine tasks direct and visually calm. Bauhaus experimentation must not reduce clarity.
10. Let [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md) preserve function, hierarchy, source order, and the dominant grid relationship; remove decorative offsets and secondary geometry first.

### Composition and Modular [Grid](../ui-design/grid-layout.md)

[Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) establishes the modular construction, [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md) its shared edges, [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) its spans, [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md) its dominant mass and counterweight, [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) its intervals, and [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) its focal order.

- Use one explicit modular grid across navigation, typography, media, surfaces, components, sections, and footer.
- Begin each major region with one dominant span, then balance it with a smaller contrasting span, color field, image, type block, or empty field.
- Reveal the construction through repeated columns, gaps, dividers, alignments, and span relationships—not decorative grid lines.
- Repeat modules consistently. Vary span, scale, color, or density only when it communicates real priority, content type, or behavior.
- Keep navigation, forms, pricing, comparison, data, and routine tasks structurally direct and visually calm.
- Let [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md) recompose the modular system for narrow viewports; remove secondary offsets and geometry before weakening the dominant relationship.

### Opening Composition

[Hero](../hero.md) establishes the opening structure; [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md), [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md), [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md), [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md), and [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md) turn it into a Bauhaus composition.

- Make one headline, primary task, product, image, or statement the dominant element.
- Use one decisive grid span and one deliberate counterweight. Integrate type, media, geometry, and color through shared edges.
- Keep the proposition and actions in one direct visual path; geometry and color should direct attention or expose structure.
- Avoid scattered shapes, badges, claims, and actions. The opening may feel poster-like, but it must not become a poster that delays the primary subject or task.

### Elementary Geometry and Asymmetrical [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md)

[Balance](../ui-design/balance.md) distributes weight, [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) establishes geometric relationships, [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) and [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md) anchor placement, [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) controls contrast, and [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md) resolves edges.

- Choose one primary family and one supporting family from circles, rectangles, triangles, lines, planes, arcs, or cropped forms.
- Use geometry as containers, crops, connectors, dividers, directional devices, or counterweights—not stickers or background confetti.
- Give each major region one dominant mass and one smaller counterweight. Every offset, overlap, crop, or empty field must align to the grid or balance a specific element.
- Expose relationships through shared edges, repeated spans, numbering, connecting rules, common surfaces, and negative space.
- Keep the geometry visibly purposeful. Remove arbitrary coordinates, historical imitation, and shape combinations chosen only because they look Bauhaus.

### [Typography](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) as Structure

[Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) establishes the complete type system; [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md), [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md), [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md), and [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) make it part of the construction.

- Use one canonical display role for short focal statements and one calm canonical system for body, navigation, controls, captions, and metadata.
- Align type blocks to the modular grid. Use decisive scale, weight, and placement to reveal hierarchy before adding geometry or effects.
- Treat display type as a visual mass that can span modules or counterbalance media while remaining real, readable text.
- Keep body copy on a stable reading edge. Use numbers, labels, indices, and short metadata as functional orientation devices.
- Do not force lowercase or uppercase, rotate essential text, fragment words, imitate historical lettering, invent metrics, or use display typography for sustained reading and controls.

### Functional [Color Fields](../ui-design/color-discipline.md)

[Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) establishes the palette and contrast, [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) grouping, [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) emphasis, and [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md) field boundaries.

- Assign every color field one visible role: grouping, separation, direction, orientation, identity, emphasis, or state.
- Keep the palette constrained and repeat color roles consistently across geometry, modules, media, and actions.
- Use broad planes or decisive blocks when color carries structure; use smaller accents when it marks sequence, category, or focus.
- Primary colors are optional. Never apply red, blue, and yellow as a default Bauhaus formula or let decorative color compete with functional states.

### Integrated Media and Montage

[Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) sets emphasis, [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) placement, [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md) shared edges, [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) media proportions, [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) media-caption relationships, [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) treatment, and [Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) captions. [User Experience](../user-experience.md) preserves truth and relevance.

- Use approved photography, product views, diagrams, illustrations, data, or motion to explain the subject, mechanism, material, process, or outcome.
- Coordinate one crop family, grid alignment, contrast treatment, edge relationship, and caption system.
- Integrate media with typography, geometry, color fields, and components through shared edges and spans rather than placing it in a detached card.
- Use montage, overlap, cutout, or split framing only when it clarifies comparison, sequence, scale, material, or connection.
- Never fabricate product interfaces, diagrams, data, results, historical material, or industrial imagery. Do not use icons or geometric placeholders as substitutes for meaningful media.

### Flat Surfaces, Crisp Edges, and Depth

[Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) establishes surface relationships, [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md) crisp boundaries and rules, and [Elevation](../ui-design/elevation.md) supported depth.

- Build primarily with flat fields, crisp boundaries, shared edges, dividers, and visibly constructed modules.
- Let whitespace, alignment, surface contrast, and rules separate content before adding cards or elevation.
- Use depth only for a real layer relationship. Keep routine components calmer than the expressive composition around them.
- Gradients, shadows, glow, texture, masks, and patterns are optional only when they clarify structure or belong to approved media; none is inherently Bauhaus.

### Page Rhythm and [Motion](../ui-design/motion-transition.md)

[Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) creates spatial pacing, [Motion and Transition](../ui-design/motion-transition.md) temporal pacing, and [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) continuity across regions.

- Alternate expressive constructed regions with quieter reading, explanation, or task regions.
- Repeat a grid span, color-field role, divider, crop, number, or alignment to connect sections without making them identical.
- Let the opening establish the primary construction; let later regions vary it through content; let the closing echo one earlier grid, color, or divider relationship.
- Use motion to reveal grouping, sequence, connection, or transformation. Avoid constant floating shapes, decorative rotation, and movement that obscures the grid.
- Keep navigation, forms, pricing, comparison, dense data, support, and footer low in visual experimentation.

## Practical Website Applications

| Application | Bauhaus art direction |
|---|---|
| Feature or content system | Use one dominant module, smaller supporting spans, and one repeated geometric or color-field relationship. |
| Process or timeline | Use aligned numbering, directional rules, repeated modules, and controlled color fields to expose sequence. |
| Gallery or case study | Let decisive media crops span the grid; attach captions and metadata on shared edges rather than floating cards. |
| Product or diagram | Make the genuine interface, mechanism, or figure the dominant mass; use geometry only to frame, connect, compare, or explain it. |
| Forms, pricing, or data | Keep the functional region calm and flat while preserving the same grid, type, boundary, and color-role system. |
| Transition or footer | Echo an earlier span, rule, number, crop, or color field instead of introducing a new decorative composition. |

## Mandatory Visual Compliance Check

Approve the rendered style only when every condition is visible:

1. Art Direction and the Required Bauhaus System unify one primary function, dominant subject, modular grid, geometric family, color-field rule, media treatment, and counterweight across the page.
2. [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md), [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md), [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md), [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md), and [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) preserve one visible modular construction with clear dominant and supporting spans.
3. [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md), [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md), and [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md) keep every geometric form, offset, overlap, rule, and empty field purposeful and structurally anchored.
4. [Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) and [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) make typography and color active structural elements without historical costume or primary-color shorthand.
5. Integrated media remains meaningful, decisively scaled, consistently treated, and visibly connected to its captions, geometry, and surrounding modules.
6. [Elevation](../ui-design/elevation.md) and [Motion and Transition](../ui-design/motion-transition.md) preserve flat construction, restrained real depth, and movement that explains sequence or relationship.
7. The result remains recognizably Bauhaus without relying on red–blue–yellow, geometric type, industrial texture, shape confetti, decorative grids, or arbitrary asymmetry.
8. [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md) and [Accessibility Audit](../accessibility-audit.md) preserve the Art Direction, Required Bauhaus System, hierarchy, functional clarity, media legibility, and dominant grid relationship across supported viewports.

If any condition fails, revise the Art Direction or do not use Bauhaus for that region.
