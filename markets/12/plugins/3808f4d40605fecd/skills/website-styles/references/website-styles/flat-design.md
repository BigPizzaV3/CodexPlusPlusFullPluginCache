## Creative Purpose

Use the confirmed direction to resolve purpose, audience, and character; use [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) for structure and [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) for perceptual priority. This section only translates those decisions into Flat Design expression.

Create a direct digital website through two-dimensional planes, clear typography, simple shapes, purposeful color, clean alignment, and minimal visual effects.

Distinguish Flat Design from generic minimalism through its coordinated planar system. Flatness must simplify presentation without removing the boundaries, labels, states, and signifiers needed to understand and operate the interface.

## Required Flat Design System

Apply [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md), [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md), [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md), [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md), and [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md) for supported layout, scale, spacing, source order, and reflow. The rules below define the Flat Design-specific system.

1. Build with two-dimensional planes, solid or semantically defined surfaces, crisp boundaries, simple shapes, and clean edges.
2. Use one visible grid and spacing system across the page. Repeat alignments, gaps, insets, and section relationships.
3. Establish one dominant subject, statement, product, or action in each major region. Use type, position, scale, color, and space—not decorative depth—to create hierarchy.
4. Give every color field one purpose: grouping, emphasis, orientation, state, or separation. Bold color is optional; uncontrolled color variety is not.
5. Use simple icons, illustrations, and controls whose meaning remains immediately recognizable. Simplification must not remove identity or function.
6. Group content first through proximity, alignment, shared surfaces, and boundaries. Do not turn every group into a floating card.
7. Keep interactive and static elements visibly distinct in default, focus, hover, active, selected, disabled, loading, success, warning, and error states when applicable.
8. Use canonical borders, surface changes, or restrained depth when literal flatness makes an action, layer, or state ambiguous. Apply one correction consistently.
9. Use one typography hierarchy, one color system, one icon or illustration language, one boundary family, and one depth model across the page.
10. Preserve hierarchy, action recognition, labels, state meaning, source order, and usable targets during responsive reflow.

- One canvas-to-surface relationship.
- One functional accent rule.
- One icon or illustration treatment.

Carry those decisions through the complete page. Create personality through typography, composition, color relationships, approved media, and brand voice—not gloss, realism, or effect stacks.

Flat Design does not require bright colors, geometric sans typography, square corners, universal tiles, flat illustrations, or complete removal of depth.

## Composition

Apply [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md), [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md), and [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) for structural hierarchy, relationships, focal priority, and reading order. This section only defines Flat Design-specific planar composition.

Start with a stable grid and one dominant plane. Arrange supporting content through shared edges, open space, contrasting fields, or repeated modules.

Use scale, alignment, surface, and color to make priority obvious. Avoid equal-weight sections, endless tiles, and ambiguous borderless groups.

Keep reading paths direct. Let media or illustration break the grid only when it becomes the focal subject and preserves the content order.

## Surface, Color, and Depth

This profile defines its Flat Design shape language. Apply [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md), [Elevation](../ui-design/elevation.md), and [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) for supported boundary, surface, depth, and color values.

Build primarily with flat surfaces, solid or semantic color fields, crisp boundaries, and open space. Use gradients, gloss, bevels, realistic textures, and dramatic shadows only when required by confirmed media or canonical behavior—not as the page language.

Use very subtle canonical background tints in selected sections or blocks to preserve contrast and separation between adjacent regions without adding more cards or heavy dividers. Keep most regions on the primary canvas, tint only the regions that need distinction, reserve stronger color for focal content and actions, and preserve canonical text and control contrast on every tint.

When depth is required for interaction, layering, focus, or state clarity, use one subtle canonical elevation or boundary treatment consistently. This is Flat Design 2.0, not a failure of flatness.

Never remove canonical borders, labels, focus indicators, state cues, or layer distinctions merely to make the interface flatter.

Do not rely on hue, hover, opacity, disappearance, or motion alone. If supported canonical anatomy cannot make an interactive element or state recognizable, report the gap instead of flattening it further.

## Page Rhythm

Apply [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) for pacing and continuity. Use this sequence for Flat Design-specific rhythm.

1. A clear opening or primary task.
2. A quiet explanation or orientation region.
3. A structured feature, collection, process, or proof region.
4. A calm decision, reading, or functional region.
5. One closing action that repeats an earlier plane, alignment, or accent relationship.

Alternate open and structured regions. Do not create rhythm through arbitrary color stripes, endless card walls, or repeated high-emphasis calls to action.

## Creative Workflow

Use the confirmed direction with [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) and [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) to guide structure and priority. This sequence orders only Flat Design-specific creative work.

1. Identify the primary content, task, and action hierarchy.
2. Inventory approved media and remove visual effects or filler that do not communicate.
3. Establish one grid, typography hierarchy, canvas-to-surface relationship, and accent rule.
4. Compose the page as planes and content masses before styling individual components.
5. Apply one icon or illustration language and one boundary or depth model.
6. Verify action recognition and states without hover, color-only meaning, or decorative depth.
7. Review at thumbnail and narrow-screen scale; remove every effect or module that weakens hierarchy or clarity.

## Success Criteria

Validate with the applicable UI skills and [Accessibility Audit](../accessibility-audit.md). Approve Flat Design only when all rules pass:

1. Use one planar grid system, one clear focal hierarchy, and one consistent relationship among typography, color fields, surfaces, boundaries, and media.
2. Give every plane, color, icon, boundary, and depth cue a clear structural, communicative, semantic, or interactive purpose.
3. Keep interactive and static elements, applicable states, focus, current location, validation, status, and recovery visibly distinct without relying on color or hover alone.
4. Resolve complete canonical typography through [Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) and color through [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md). Never invent typography or color values.
5. Use coordinated, permissioned media at meaningful scale. Preserve hierarchy, action recognition, source order, labels, states, and media during responsive reflow.
6. Remove unnecessary realism, effect stacks, filler modules, placeholder assets, invented content, and simplification that erases function or meaning.

If any condition fails, revise the composition or do not use Flat Design for that region.
