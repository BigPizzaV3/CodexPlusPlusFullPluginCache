## Swiss Style Characteristics

Simple and orderly, Swiss design is classic yet flexible across different contexts. It becomes Swiss only when all five characteristics operate as one visible system—not through minimalism or historical clichés alone.

### Geometric Minimalism

Swiss defines the geometric character. [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) owns perceived relationships; [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md), [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md), and [Elevation](../ui-design/elevation.md) resolve supported surfaces, edges, and depth.

- Build with pure lines, simple forms, planar surfaces, crisp boundaries, and deliberate negative space.
- Use geometry only to communicate category, sequence, scale, direction, data, or identity. Reduction must remove ornament without removing meaning.
- Use a restrained canvas and one consistent expressive accent role; Swiss does not require red or a black-and-white palette.
- Prefer alignment, surface contrast, and functional rules over decoration or heavy depth.
- Avoid exposed full grids, arbitrary coordinates or numbers, registration and crop marks, filler geometry, repetitive card walls, and print effects used as Swiss shorthand.

### [Modular Grids](../ui-design/grid-layout.md)

Swiss requires modular order. [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) owns structure, [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md) shared edges, [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) spans, [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) intervals and density, and [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md) reflow.

- Establish one governing modular grid across navigation, headings, body copy, media, captions, metadata, actions, sections, and footer.
- Reveal the system through shared edges, recurring intervals, deliberate spans, and consistent section starts—not visible grid lines.
- Give each major region one clear entry and assign every supporting element a purposeful span or alignment.
- Keep repeated content in aligned rows, lists, or modular fields. Use cards only when items genuinely require independent containment.
- A focal image, word, or rule may break the grid when the underlying order remains legible and supporting content returns to it.
- Recompose the grid for narrow viewports; never shrink the desktop arrangement.

### Sans-Serif [Typography](../ui-design/typography-readability.md)

Swiss requires the sans-serif expression. [Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) owns the complete type system, while [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) owns its perceptual priority.

- Use the clearest canonical sans-serif or grotesk compatible with the brand and language. Helvetica and Univers are possible references, not requirements or local imports.
- Build a restrained role set with decisive differences in scale and weight; typography should carry the hierarchy before decoration or depth.
- Favor flush-start alignment, shared reading edges, controlled measure, and natural wrapping. Keep body, labels, captions, navigation, and data easy to scan.
- Treat display type as a compositional mass while keeping it real and readable. Avoid justified body copy, thin type as refinement, long all-caps passages, and hardcoded headline breaks.

### Asymmetrical [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md)

Swiss defines the asymmetrical character. [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md) owns visual-weight distribution, [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) emphasis, [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) non-type proportion, [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) open–dense cadence, and [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) grouping.

- Give each major region one dominant visual mass and one smaller counterweight.
- Create emphasis through unequal spans, scale, density, photography, color, and open space—not arbitrary offsets.
- Anchor the tension to shared edges or recurring intervals so the composition feels constructed rather than scattered.
- Use negative space as an active counterweight and vary open and dense fields without losing the governing grid.
- Useful relationships include headline to index, photograph to caption, label to value, and large media to compact text. Preserve reading and focus order while creating visual asymmetry.

### Photography

Swiss defines the direct media character. [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) owns emphasis, [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md) placement, [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md) shared edges, [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) media proportions, and [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) media-caption relationships. [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) resolves supported image treatments.

- Use approved, subject-relevant photography as content, evidence, identity, or explanation—not module filler.
- Prefer decisive rectangular crops aligned to the grid. Focal photography may span modules or run full bleed; supporting images should share a crop and edge system.
- Attach captions, dates, credits, titles, and provenance to the correct image on a shared edge.
- Monochrome, duotone, photomontage, and high contrast are optional treatments only when approved and supported.
- When photography is not the right medium, apply the same direct, grid-bound treatment to approved product views, diagrams, artwork, data, or video.
- Never substitute generic stock, fabricated media, invented interfaces, fake data, or decorative placeholders.

Swiss does not require Helvetica, black-white-red, visible grids, lowercase, all caps, oversized headings, square corners, or a light canvas. Its identity comes from the coordinated five-part system above.

## Practical Website Applications

| Application | Swiss treatment |
|---|---|
| Opening | Let [Hero](../hero.md) choose the structure; stage one decisive headline or photograph with a smaller aligned counterweight. |
| Feature or evidence | Pair one large grid-bound image, product view, or diagram with compact copy and attached provenance. |
| Index or collection | Use aligned rows, recurring spans, and stable metadata columns instead of equal card tiles. |
| Product or data region | Give the primary interface, table, or figure the dominant span; keep labels, values, and explanation on shared edges. |
| Section transition | Continue a baseline, rule, image proportion, or open field from the governing system instead of introducing decorative dividers. |

## Mandatory Visual Compliance Check

Approve the rendered style only when every condition is visible:

1. [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md), [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md), [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md), and [Elevation](../ui-design/elevation.md) preserve pure geometry, planar surfaces, functional rules, and restrained color without decorative filler.
2. [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md), [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md), and [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) make one modular system legible across typography, imagery, metadata, actions, and page regions.
3. [Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) and [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) preserve a restrained canonical sans-serif hierarchy through role, weight, alignment, and spacing.
4. [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md), [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md), and [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) keep unequal visual masses balanced through shared edges, proportion, grouping, and deliberate negative space.
5. [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md), [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md), [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md), and [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md) keep approved photography or subject media decisive, consistently scaled, grid-aligned, meaning-bearing, and correctly captioned.
6. The direction remains recognizably Swiss without relying on Helvetica, red, visible grids, square corners, or other clichés.
7. [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md) preserves the five-part system without changing content, source order, or meaning.

If any condition fails, revise the art direction or do not use Swiss for that region.
