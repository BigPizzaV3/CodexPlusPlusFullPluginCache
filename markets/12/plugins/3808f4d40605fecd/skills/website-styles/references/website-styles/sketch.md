## Purpose

Create a resolved website treatment with the immediacy, observation, visible thought, and human character of sketching.

Sketch is an illustrative treatment with a complete default foundation, not an unfinished wireframe, handwritten-font preset, or permission to make controls look improvised. Its identity comes from purposeful line, gesture, annotation, layering, construction, and evidence of process. Canonical interface structure remains clear, complete, and reliable.

After the shared chat confirmation, use this profile only when Sketch was selected under confirmed delegation or preserved from the confirmed restatement. Apply it with the relevant Design intent skills and canonical Alpy package.

This profile defines semantic relationships and selection priorities. It does not define CSS, raw values, named typefaces, fixed colors, components, tokens, modes, breakpoints, assets, effects, or canonical interaction behavior.

## Classification

- **Kind:** Treatment.
- **Default foundation:** A complete, resolved website foundation with stable canonical structure, quiet functional regions, one coherent approved sketch vocabulary, visible thought where it adds meaning, and selective expressive emphasis. It requires no second style selection.
- **Best suited for:** Creative studios, architecture, design, engineering, craft, education, portfolios, workshops, cultural projects, editorial experiences, product stories, innovation programs, maker brands, and subjects that benefit from observation or process.
- **Use selectively for:** Consequential services, transactional flows, dense operational tools, formal records, high-trust tasks, and subjects where marks could imply uncertainty, provisional status, or lack of care.
- **Compatible with:** None declared until adjacent profiles have been researched and cross-style validation confirms a coherent combination.
- **Conflicts with:** None declared until adjacent profiles have been researched and cross-style validation confirms a material conflict. Do not infer either relationship.

Treat Sketch as a complete user-facing preset. Do not load or combine another profile unless the style-selection contract explicitly supports it.

Sketch does not confirm that an image is an authentic concept drawing, field note, design artifact, signature, handwriting sample, or record of a real process. Treat provenance as product truth.

Do not ask the user to choose a paper color, drawing medium, line weight, handwriting style, roughness level, annotation system, or second aesthetic after Sketch is selected. Infer the dominant emphasis from the confirmed direction, content, supplied and approved assets, composition, accessibility needs, and canonical coverage.

Sketch does not select the canonical `light` or `dark` mode. Resolve mode explicitly through the package workflow.

## Identity

### Principle

Let people see the thinking without making the experience feel unfinished.

### Character

Sketch should feel:

- Observational rather than generic.
- Exploratory without becoming uncertain.
- Human without becoming careless.
- Immediate without becoming rushed.
- Imperfect in mark, precise in meaning.
- Expressive without obscuring function.
- Warm without becoming juvenile-coded by default.
- Process-visible without exposing irrelevant internal work.
- Informal only when the subject and brand support it.
- Complete even when the drawing language suggests an open idea.

### Signature traits

Build the treatment through:

- A stable canonical grid and fully resolved interface beneath the expressive layer.
- One coherent sketch vocabulary from canonical support or supplied and approved assets rather than unrelated doodles.
- Purposeful line, gesture, construction, annotation, sequence, or layering.
- Sketch material that observes, explains, explores, or reveals something specific.
- A deliberate contrast between expressive sketch regions and quiet functional surfaces.
- Live canonical text for headings, labels, actions, instructions, and annotations.
- Clear provenance when process artifacts or real drawings are presented as evidence.
- Selective use so sketch marks retain meaning and visual energy.

The result must remain recognizably Sketch without paper texture, pencil gray, black ink, handwriting, arrows, circles, cross-outs, tape, sticky notes, rough borders, drawing animation, or visible construction lines.

### Anti-goals

Do not reduce Sketch to:

- Wobbly borders around every card, button, field, or container.
- A fake wireframe presented as a finished website.
- Generic arrows, stars, underlines, circles, squiggles, or decorative doodles.
- A handwriting, marker, chalk, or scribble typeface.
- Paper grain, notebook lines, torn edges, coffee stains, smudges, tape, or photocopy noise.
- Rotating or misaligning items to simulate spontaneity.
- Missing content, incomplete states, placeholder copy, or ambiguous controls.
- Juvenile-coded drawing unless that character is explicitly appropriate to the confirmed audience and context.
- A polished visual layer used to conceal truthful `Draft`, `Beta`, `Preview`, `Demo`, or `Sandbox` status, incomplete behavior, placeholders, dead actions, fake loading, or invented evidence.
- Decorative marks that resemble focus, selection, validation, status, or annotation.
- Fabricated sketches, signatures, notes, or process artifacts presented as authentic evidence.

## Expression Model

Infer one dominant emphasis from the confirmed purpose, content, assets, and page role. Combine emphases only when each has a distinct job. Do not ask the user to configure a Sketch subtype.

Use **line, thought, and rest** as one system: line gives approved marks a coherent character; thought gives every observation, annotation, sequence, or layer a content responsibility; rest keeps reading and task regions calm enough for both to remain meaningful.

### Observational illustration emphasis

Use canonically supported or supplied and approved line drawings to reveal the form, character, context, or detail of a subject. Let the drawing carry a clear content responsibility rather than functioning as generic decoration.

Favor this for architecture, objects, places, people, nature, craft, products, exhibitions, education, and stories grounded in close observation.

### Process and iteration emphasis

Use confirmed sketches, alternatives, studies, sequences, and annotations to explain how work developed. Show only stages that are accurate, useful, and appropriate to disclose.

Favor this for case studies, portfolios, making, design, engineering, research, workshops, product development, and behind-the-scenes narratives.

Do not manufacture discarded alternatives, corrections, dates, authorship, or progression to make the process seem richer.

### Diagrammatic emphasis

Use approved diagrams, maps, exploded views, construction studies, systems, and annotated relationships to make complex information easier to understand.

Favor this for explanation, education, architecture, engineering, services, methods, spatial relationships, and system narratives.

Preserve labels, reading order, legends, relationships, and equivalent explanations. A sketch-like diagram remains information, not decoration.

### Annotated editorial emphasis

Use live canonical captions, marginal notes, labels, callouts, highlights, or commentary to create the feeling of a considered working page. Annotations should add context, interpretation, or navigation.

Favor this for editorial stories, essays, portfolios, cultural work, educational material, and selected landing pages.

Do not place critical instructions, actions, or product truth in handwriting images or decorative marginalia.

### Material and collage emphasis

Use supplied and approved scans, cutouts, paper artifacts, photographs, or mixed-media compositions when their material qualities are relevant to the brand or content.

Favor this for art, culture, craft, archives, publications, exhibitions, and projects where source material is itself meaningful.

Do not simulate found material, aging, tape, folds, provenance, or physical process through local effects.

### Functional restraint

Use unchanged canonical task presentation for consent, pricing, checkout, authentication, legal, medical, financial, security, deletion, and other consequential actions. Keep Sketch material outside the control, label, help, error, action, and confirmation grouping.

State the affected object or data, amount or scope, recipient, consequence, and permanence when applicable. Preserve canonical review, correction, confirmation, cancellation, recovery, and completion feedback. Missing required protection blocks the affected flow; Sketch never supplies a substitute.

### Minimum signature

A recognizable Sketch direction requires:

- A complete canonical website structure and interaction model.
- One coherent sketch vocabulary from canonical support or supplied and approved assets.
- A meaningful line, gesture, observational, or construction relationship.
- At least one purposeful use of process, annotation, layering, sequence, or visible thought.
- Deliberate contrast between expressive material and quiet functional UI.
- Readable live text and unambiguous controls, states, and focus.

Use the nearest supported Sketch expression only when these relationships, confirmed meaning, familiar interaction, and accessibility survive. Otherwise report the missing coverage instead of fabricating assets or approximating the style locally.

### Website and Product Design boundary

Use Sketch for public marketing, editorial, education, culture, portfolios, case studies, process stories, collections, events, and bounded canonical conversion surfaces.

Product Design, when available, owns authenticated workspaces, persistent editing, version histories, diagramming, whiteboards, drawing tools, live collaboration, permissions, complex records, and operational workflows. Otherwise keep the work within confirmed public or bounded scope and report unresolved product behavior.

Sketch may shape the expression of confirmed content and structure. It cannot create sketches, process stages, annotations, authorship, evidence, tools, editable canvases, collaboration, versioning, or product behavior.

### Distinction from adjacent directions

- **From a wireframe or low-fidelity prototype:** A Sketch website has a resolved interface treatment appropriate to its truthful product stage. Its marks are an intentional visual language, not evidence that structure, content, behavior, or states are unresolved.
- **From handwritten design:** Sketch does not require handwritten typography. Interface text and annotations remain live, canonical, and readable.
- **From Neo Brutalism:** Neo Brutalism uses assertive structure, direct contrast, and deliberately raw visual force. Sketch uses observational or process-led marks and does not roughen the interface shell.
- **From Academia:** Academia draws from scholarly, archival, institutional, and intellectual traditions. Sketch can show notes or studies without implying scholarship or historical authority.
- **From Bauhaus:** Bauhaus coordinates geometry, type, image, color, craft, and production into functional construction. Sketch is identified by human observation, thought, and mark-making.
- **From Playful Geometric:** Playful Geometric builds character through clear geometric systems and controlled variation. Sketch favors gestural, observational, or constructed marks.
- **From Retro:** Retro deliberately references a historical visual language. Sketch may feel analog without referencing a particular era.
- **From Flat Design:** Flat Design is a primary direction built through planar structure, direct hierarchy, symbolic economy, and restrained depth. Sketch is a treatment identified by visible human observation, thought, and mark-making.

These distinctions do not make adjacent profiles available, compatible, or conflicting. Do not load or combine another profile without validated support through the style-selection contract.

## Visual Expression

### Hierarchy

Begin with the confirmed content and visitor outcome. Use sketch material to focus attention, establish context, reveal a relationship, or explain an idea.

Let one expressive region lead at a time. Secondary marks should support reading order rather than compete with headings, evidence, navigation, actions, or status.

Do not make every sketch, note, line, and annotation a focal point. Visible human variation does not remove the need for clear hierarchy.

### Typography character

Use only typefaces, typography roles, complete tuples, and mappings available in `alpy/type-scale.md`. Resolve component-owned typography slots and bindings through `alpy/component-tokens.md`.

Choose canonical roles that keep the page readable and let the illustrative mark provide most of the handmade character. A compatible type system may be editorial, neutral, technical, humanist, geometric, or expressive when the confirmed direction and language coverage support it.

Keep annotations, captions, labels, and callouts as live canonical text. Use hierarchy, placement, and association to make them feel integrated with the sketch material.

Do not infer a handwriting, marker, chalk, monospaced, serif, condensed, or all-caps type treatment. Do not convert text to images to imitate notes or lettering.

### Color behavior

Resolve component-owned color through `alpy/component-tokens.md` and remaining intent through `alpy/semantic-tokens.md` and explicit mode mappings.

Use color to establish hierarchy, brand recognition, subject relationships, or controlled contrast between interface and illustration. Reserve functional roles for focus, validation, status, selection, and consequence.

Sketch may be monochrome, limited, colorful, soft, vivid, light, or dark. The drawing medium does not dictate the website palette.

Do not default to paper white, cream, graphite, charcoal, black ink, blue pencil, sepia, or one accent. Do not recolor supplied and approved drawings unless the asset guidance and canonical mappings support it.

### Line, mark, and construction

Use canonically supported or supplied and approved illustration assets with canonical borders, dividers, icons, and component anatomy.

Favor a consistent relationship among line character, subject, scale, detail, and purpose. Construction lines, hatching, corrections, overlaps, and varied pressure may appear only when present in approved assets or supported by the system.

Do not draw custom control outlines, roughen canonical geometry, add arbitrary strokes, trace components, or create a local sketch effect with filters, masks, scripts, SVG paths, or generated noise.

### Shape, surface, and depth

Use canonical components, radii, borders, surfaces, depth, and anatomy to communicate grouping, hierarchy, state, and interaction.

Keep functional surfaces stable. Sketch assets may overlap, sit beside, or appear within supported regions when reading order, hit areas, contrast, and responsive behavior remain clear.

Do not make every surface resemble a sheet of paper, notebook card, pinned note, torn fragment, or taped artifact. Use material containment only when it represents approved content and canonical capability.

### Texture and material

Use texture only when it belongs to a supplied and approved drawing, scan, photograph, or material artifact. Preserve the source's character without degrading legibility or making the entire interface appear dirty or damaged.

Do not generate fake paper, grain, erasure, bleed, photocopy, fold, shadow, stain, or wear. Material character is content or an approved asset treatment, not a local decoration recipe.

### Imagery and media

Classify every sketch image as informative, functional, complex, decorative, or evidence-bearing. Provide the appropriate text alternative, caption, description, or exclusion from the accessibility tree.

Use authentic process material only when its provenance, rights, privacy, and meaning are confirmed. Label illustration, reconstruction, concept art, or generated imagery honestly when that distinction matters.

Do not use stock doodles or synthetic process imagery as proof of how a person, team, building, product, or project was developed.

### Iconography and graphic elements

Use only canonical Alpy icons, sizes, roles, and component bindings for navigation, controls, status, and system meaning.

Keep illustrative arrows, circles, underlines, and marks visually and semantically distinct from interactive icons, focus indicators, selections, errors, and validation cues.

Do not redraw canonical icons in a sketch style or substitute unfamiliar hand-drawn symbols for established controls.

## Spatial Expression

### Layout bias

Preserve the primary layout, hierarchy, density, and any deliberate supporting treatment in the confirmed direction. Any suitable primary layout may support Sketch when its documented purpose fits.

Apply `Editorial`, `Asymmetric`, or `Immersive` only when the confirmed direction calls for it. Annotation, apparent looseness, overlap, layering, or uneven visual weight does not independently select another composition or treatment.

Use one dominant composition per page or route family. Sketch does not independently select the layout.

Apparent looseness should sit on top of a stable reading and interaction structure. Do not confuse the irregularity of a drawing with arbitrary page composition.

### Grid and alignment

Use exact containers, columns, spans, gutters, and responsive behavior only through Grid and Layout and explicit canonical package support.

Anchor headings, text, controls, media, captions, and major sections to coherent relationships. Sketch material may cross or soften visible alignments when its containment, order, and responsive behavior are supported.

Do not offset, rotate, overlap, or misalign interface elements merely to look handmade.

### Rhythm and density

Alternate resolved content regions with selective sketch moments. Use pauses so marks can be understood and functional information can recover visual quiet.

Vary scale and detail according to meaning: a leading observation may carry more presence, while notes, construction studies, and secondary diagrams remain subordinate.

Preserve the confirmed density. Do not use visual clutter or excessive empty paper-like space as a style shortcut.

### Content measure and flow

Keep body copy, annotations, captions, labels, evidence, and instructions readable. Establish an explicit association between each annotation and the subject it explains.

Use supported wider regions for diagrams, process sequences, maps, portfolios, and comparative studies only when the content requires them.

Do not create ambiguous reading order through scattered notes, overlapping text, diagonal text, or disconnected callouts.

## Component Expression

Select only components whose documented purpose, anatomy, content options, variants, states, and behavior in `alpy/components.md` fit the confirmed task, and resolve component-owned styling through `alpy/component-tokens.md`. Sketch guides selection, composition, and expressive restraint; it never alters anatomy, creates variants, or restyles components locally.

### Navigation and orientation

Keep global and local navigation canonical, predictable, and visually distinct from illustrative marks. Preserve current location, labels, focus, and responsive behavior.

Sketch elements may frame a destination, introduce a section, or support wayfinding only when they do not resemble navigation controls.

### Actions and links

Use canonical buttons, links, action hierarchies, states, and bindings. Let labels and semantic emphasis communicate priority.

Do not circle a label, draw an arrow, add a rough underline, or use a handwritten note as the only indication that something is actionable.

### Cards and containers

Use canonical containers when grouping, comparison, interaction, or repeated anatomy requires them. Place approved sketch assets within supported media, illustration, or content regions.

Do not roughen every card edge, make panels look like loose paper, or use arbitrary rotation and overlap to simulate a desk or sketchbook.

### Forms and structured tasks

Keep inputs, controls, labels, descriptions, validation, progress, consent, and recovery canonical. Exclude Sketch marks from the functional grouping around data entry and consequential decisions.

Do not imitate form controls with drawn boxes, lines, circles, checkmarks, or handwritten prompts.

### Diagrams, data, and process evidence

Use a sketch diagram only when it accurately explains a confirmed relationship. Preserve titles, labels, legends, units, sequence, source, and equivalent text where needed.

Do not sacrifice comprehension to gestural character or use a sketch as decoration when the same marks could be mistaken for data or process evidence.

## Page And Section Expression

### Header and navigation

Keep the shell calm and dependable. Use sketch material sparingly near identity or orientation, never at the expense of navigation clarity.

### Hero and opening region

Use one leading sketch, construction, observational study, annotated subject, or process relationship to establish the direction. Pair it with a clear proposition or page purpose and an unambiguous primary action when needed.

Do not fill the hero with unrelated doodles or use unfinished-looking text and controls to create character.

### Story and explanation

Use sketches to reveal subject, sequence, relationship, alternatives, or reasoning. Pair marks with concise live text so people can understand why the material matters.

### Work, cases, and process

Show real drawings and stages only when confirmed. Identify creator, project, stage, date, or context when those details affect interpretation.

Separate approved public process from internal, confidential, speculative, or misleading material.

### People and authorship

Use portraits, signatures, notes, and personal drawings only with confirmed rights and meaning. Do not manufacture handwriting or personal marks to make an organization feel more human.

### Evidence and trust

Place evidence near the claim it supports. A sketch can document process or observation, but it does not by itself prove quality, authorship, performance, approval, or outcome.

### Calls to action and completion

Return to a quiet, canonical action region. Keep choice, consequence, expectation, and next step clear.

### Footer and utility regions

Preserve durable access to navigation, contact, policies, attribution, accessibility information, and required utility content. Decorative sketch marks remain subordinate.

## State Expression

Use only canonical Alpy state, focus, feedback, validation, loading, disabled, selected, pressed, expanded, and recovery behavior.

Keep hover, focus, active, selected, expanded, disabled, loading, empty, success, warning, and error states visually distinct from illustrative marks. State meaning must remain available without color, position, gesture, or sketch convention alone.

Decorative corrections, circles, arrows, underlines, cross-outs, highlights, and construction lines must never resemble or replace focus, validation, selection, progress, or status.

## Interaction Expression

### [Motion](../ui-design/motion-transition.md)

Static is the default. Selecting Sketch never authorizes new motion, sound, or haptics. Canonically supported motion may explain change, sequence, relationship, or progression only when the confirmed experience requires it; the treatment must remain recognizable and complete when static.

Do not add perpetual drawing loops, scribble hovers, animated underlines, handwriting simulations, pencil cursors, erasure effects, parallax, flashing, strobing, cursor following, scratch audio, vibration, or scroll-jacking.

Use only controls and reduced-motion behavior documented by canonical support and applicable confirmed requirements. Preserve equivalent static understanding, and remove or replace nonessential spatial motion when required. Motion never carries order or meaning alone.

### Interaction clarity

Keep controls distinct from illustration at rest, hover, focus, active, selected, disabled, loading, success, and error states.

Do not let sketch marks intercept input, reduce target size, hide focus, change reading order, or create false affordances.

### Corrections and reversibility

Visual cross-outs, erasures, alternates, and revisions may describe approved process material. They do not replace actual edit, undo, validation, version, or recovery behavior.

## Adaptation

### Responsive priorities

Preserve content hierarchy, drawing meaning, annotation association, and task completion as space changes. Recompose supported assets, simplify overlaps, move annotations into readable sequences, and remove decorative detail before shrinking essential content.

Do not assume a desktop canvas or position notes by fixed coordinates outside canonical responsive behavior.

### Mode behavior

Sketch does not select mode. Resolve an explicit canonical `light` or `dark` mode before rendered work or canonical component selection. Use that mode's separate semantic mappings and component bindings; do not default, fuzzy-match, or derive one mode from the other.

Verify the legibility and meaning of lines, scans, transparent assets, captions, and annotations in the resolved mode. Do not automatically invert, tint, recolor, or blend source drawings; use an approved asset variant or report the gap.

### Content and localization resilience

Keep live annotations, labels, captions, and calls to action adaptable to longer text, different scripts, and reading directions. Preserve logical association instead of relying on a drawn arrow or left-to-right placement alone.

Meaningful handwritten artifacts need a verified live transcript, translation when required, correct language identification, and honest marking of uncertain or illegible passages. Do not invent missing words, embed required translatable text in images, blindly mirror evidence-bearing drawings, or assume canonical glyph coverage, shaping, expansion, bidirectional, or vertical-script behavior.

### Inclusive and sensory resilience

Preserve semantic structure, keyboard access, focus visibility, contrast, reflow, zoom, target clarity, and non-color communication.

Do not rely solely on position, shape, line direction, handwriting, color, or visual resemblance. Decorative sketch material should not add screen-reader noise; informative and complex drawings need meaningful alternatives.

Control irregularity and visual density so people can distinguish content, navigation, actions, status, and decoration.

### Performance and asset restraint

Treat scans, textured images, transparent illustrations, and animation as performance-sensitive assets. Use approved formats, variants, crops, and loading behavior without losing important meaning.

Do not add large raster textures or repeated decorative assets merely to make the treatment visible.

## Boundaries

### Must preserve

- A resolved treatment with content, structure, behavior, states, and recovery complete for the confirmed scope and truthful product stage.
- One coherent sketch vocabulary with a specific observational, explanatory, process, or editorial responsibility.
- Stable hierarchy, source order, alignment, interaction, and functional regions beneath expressive marks.
- Live canonical text for headings, labels, actions, instructions, annotations, captions, and status.
- Clear distinction among illustration, evidence, decoration, interaction, focus, validation, selection, and status.

- Logical semantic, reading, and focus order with conventional task paths. Decorative or supporting annotations remain secondary and skippable; informative annotations preserve required relationships and equivalents without interrupting a control, label, help, or error.
- Predictable navigation, controls, forms, feedback, status, and recovery.
- Canonical Alpy components, roles, mappings, values, modes, states, assets, and behavior.

### Avoid

- Hardcoded CSS or raw design values.
- New fonts, colors, icons, imagery, tokens, components, variants, states, modes, breakpoints, effects, assets, or motion.
- Fabricated sketches, annotations, handwriting, signatures, corrections, studies, diagrams, drafts, dates, stages, or authorship.
- Generated or reconstructed material presented as an authentic record.
- Simulated age, disability, literacy, neurodivergence, cultural tradition, Indigenous visual language, or a named creator's hand used to manufacture human character; do not alter confirmed identity, cultural, religious, or assistive details.
- Redrawn controls, states, icons, charts, or diagrams made to look hand-drawn.
- Incomplete content or behavior treated as part of the aesthetic.
- Sketch marks as the only carrier of action, status, validation, sequence, relationship, or instruction.
- Paper, pencil, ink, handwriting, roughness, texture, doodles, or animation treated as mandatory.
- Combining Sketch with another profile without validated support through the style-selection contract.

### Accessibility and verification risks

- Keep meaningful text live and adaptable; do not embed required, translatable, or interactive text in drawings.
- Preserve contrast and visible focus for canonical controls and meaningful graphic boundaries against lines, scans, textures, and transparent assets.
- Never rely on position, shape, direction, color, handwriting, resemblance, gesture, or motion as the only carrier of meaning.
- Classify sketches as informative, functional, complex, decorative, or evidence-bearing and provide the corresponding alternative, caption, description, or exclusion from the accessibility tree. Complex diagrams, maps, sequences, and evidence also need a linked live-text equivalent that preserves labels, relationships, order, units, sources, and conclusions.
- Keep decorative marks inert, nonfocusable, and outside accessible names; do not let them intercept input or imply affordance.
- Linearize annotations, overlaps, sequences, and diagrams when constrained space, zoom, reflow, or reading direction makes their relationship ambiguous.
- Respect reduced motion, forced colors, user spacing, zoom, reflow, input preferences, missing-media conditions, and supported modes.
- Verify the resolved mode, every applicable state, and each supported responsive layout for semantic and focus order, keyboard and touch use, visible focus, actual text and meaningful-line contrast, zoom and reflow, text-spacing changes, target clarity, disclosures, live regions, and screen-reader alternatives. Informative annotations may recompose but cannot disappear.
- Do not claim accessibility, authenticity, authorship, provenance, or process evidence from appearance, source research, component names, or visual inspection alone.
- Distinguish confirmed requirements, canonical support, implemented behavior, verified evidence, assumptions, and untested gaps.
