## Art Direction and Atmospheric Composition

Build a deliberate dark environment, not a light composition with its colors inverted.

- Let deep negative space dominate so a few illuminated subjects carry the scene.
- Choose one primary atmosphere carrier per focal region and at most one supporting light or texture treatment.
- Keep light direction, color family, edge character, and media grade coherent across the page; vary crop, scale, and intensity.
- Allow atmospheric fields to be bounded or edge-to-edge when the brief supports them.
- Avoid a wall of equal charcoal cards, unrelated neon glows, muddy gray overlays, and decorative effects with no visual source.

## Dark Canvas and Tonal Architecture

Modern Dark may use neutral black, blue-black, violet-black, brown-black, or another canonical near-black.

- Use a small tonal ladder: the deepest field for rest, restrained lighter planes for grouping, and bright values for focal content.
- Make section changes feel like shifts in light, depth, image, or texture—not accidental stripes of slightly different gray.
- Preserve true dark release areas around luminous or information-dense regions.
- Use [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) to resolve the exact canvas, surface, foreground, accent, semantic, and contrast values.

## Atmosphere Carriers

| Primary carrier | Outcome | Practical treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Quiet dark field | Severe, minimal focus | Use tonal depth and negative space with little decoration. |
| Gradient light | Abstract or product energy | Anchor the field to a subject, horizon, beam, or plausible light source; keep one coherent spectrum. |
| Background image | Narrative or cinematic identity | Grade and mask the image into the dark field while preserving a meaningful crop and a clean reading zone. |
| Background video | Ambient or cinematic atmosphere | Keep luminance and framing stable; the poster or static state must preserve the intended composition. |
| Pattern or texture | Technical, spatial, or tactile atmosphere | Use one low-contrast motif and fade or reduce it around reading and control zones. |

Use one carrier as the scene; do not stack every technique in the same region.

## Gradient, Glow, and Luminous Color

- Use radial gradients for localized halos, linear gradients for horizons and media fades, and conic or angled fields for directional beams or edge energy.
- Place the brightest point where attention should land and fade it fully into the receiving dark field.
- Use one coherent cool, warm, spectral, or brand-led color family. Modern Dark does not require violet, blue, coral, or any other preset accent.
- Treat glow as emitted or environmental light from a subject, product state, edge, or source. Never use it as a substitute for hierarchy, focus, state, or elevation.
- Keep body copy and dense controls outside unstable luminous fields. [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) owns the color and contrast; [Elevation](../ui-design/elevation.md) owns supported layer and shadow relationships.

## Background Image and Video

- Use a background image when its subject, place, texture, or light gives the page an identity that a gradient cannot.
- Crop around the subject, dark-grade the periphery, and mask edges into the canvas; do not leave an untreated stock rectangle behind the content.
- Use background video only when movement materially strengthens the atmosphere. Favor stable framing, restrained luminance changes, and low visual churn over rapid cuts or high-frequency detail.
- Keep essential text away from volatile detail and preserve the composition without motion. [Motion and Transition](../ui-design/motion-transition.md) owns playback, choreography, interruption, and reduced-motion behavior.
- Use only approved imagery, footage, and product media. Never invent a product view or disguise a placeholder as real evidence.

## Pattern and Texture

- Choose one family such as structural grids, sparse dots, star or dust fields, contour lines, orbital geometry, scan detail, or fine grain.
- Align the motif with the composition or light perspective; crop, dissolve, or occlude it rather than repeating it as page-wide wallpaper.
- Keep the pattern subordinate to text, controls, and product detail. Reduce its contrast and frequency near reading zones.
- Repeat the same motif selectively so it becomes a recognizable environmental cue, not a new decoration in every section.

## Surface, Edge, Light, and Depth

Modern Dark defines the visible relationship; [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md) and [Elevation](../ui-design/elevation.md) resolve supported values.

- Define a dark surface with the lightest sufficient cue: a tonal step, thin boundary, upper rim, partial gradient edge, reflection, or restrained shadow.
- Use bounded surfaces only where grouping or staging needs them. Prefer one shared atmospheric field over a grid of identical floating cards.
- Use translucent panes only when visible overlap or background light creates a real spatial relationship; transparency is not the default.
- Build depth through overlap, occlusion, tonal separation, light direction, and selective edge light before adding shadow.
- Keep one edge and illumination language across product frames, media shells, controls, diagrams, and decorative objects.

## Typography, Iconography, and Media Finish

- Keep body text and interface copy crisp and planar. A restrained display-text gradient may echo the scene's light direction; never glow or texture dense reading text.
- Let icons remain legible silhouettes or line forms. Treat an icon as luminous only when it is the intentional focal object.
- Grade photography, illustration, 3D objects, and product UI into the same atmosphere while preserving their useful color, identity, and detail.
- For product UI, darken or fade the frame and periphery—not the information the visitor must inspect.
- [Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md), [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md), and [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md) retain ownership of supported type, contrast, and relative size.

## Practical Website Applications

- **Opening focal scene:** Use one dominant carrier behind or around the subject, with the brightest light concentrated on the intended focal content. [Hero](../hero.md) selects the opening structure.
- **Product or media stage:** Blend real media into a dark frame or field with one coherent edge light, crop, and localized bloom; keep the important detail readable.
- **Feature group:** Place related items in one atmospheric field and distinguish only the surfaces that need containment instead of carding every item.
- **Section transition:** Let a gradient, image fade, pattern crop, or return to quiet darkness create the handoff without inventing a new palette.
- **Closing focal region:** Reuse the established light color, motif, or media finish at a new scale; do not introduce a second atmosphere for emphasis.

## Mandatory Visual Compliance Check

Approve the rendered style only when every condition is visible:

1. The page reads as an intentional dark environment, not a mechanically inverted light page.
2. Dark negative space and a few concentrated luminous zones create clear atmospheric hierarchy.
3. Each focal region has one dominant carrier and one coherent light and color logic.
4. Gradients, images, video, patterns, and texture preserve clean reading and control zones.
5. Media is purposefully cropped, graded, masked, framed, or blended into the atmosphere.
6. Surfaces use selective tonal and edge cues rather than becoming a uniform charcoal-card wall.
7. Glow has a visible source, remains localized, and does not carry semantic meaning.
8. Text, icons, controls, and product detail remain crisp and readable inside the dark treatment.
9. The visual direction remains recognizably Modern Dark even when the `Dark` mode label is hidden.

If any condition fails, revise the art direction or do not use Modern Dark for that region.
