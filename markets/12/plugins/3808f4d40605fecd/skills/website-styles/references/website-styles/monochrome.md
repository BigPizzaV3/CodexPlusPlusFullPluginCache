## Purpose

Create a focused website through one dominant color family, deliberate tonal relationships, decisive hierarchy, expressive form, and disciplined contrast without reducing the experience to a flat black-and-white treatment.

Monochrome is a color-led treatment with a complete default foundation. It may be achromatic or organized around one confirmed chromatic family. Its identity comes from limiting hue so typography, scale, spacing, line, shape, surface, imagery, and content relationships carry more visual meaning.

After the shared chat confirmation, use this profile only when Monochrome was selected under confirmed delegation or preserved from the confirmed restatement. Apply it with relevant Design intent skills and the canonical Alpy package.

This profile defines semantic relationships and selection priorities. It does not define CSS, raw values, named typefaces, fixed colors, components, tokens, modes, breakpoints, assets, effects, motion, content, or canonical behavior.

## Classification

- **Default foundation:** A complete achromatic-first editorial–graphic foundation with clear hierarchy, deliberate tonal intervals, one content-led anchor, repeated figure–ground relationships, approved imagery, conventional states, and quiet task regions. It requires no second style selection.
- **Alternate expression:** Use one confirmed chromatic family only when the confirmed direction and canonical mappings provide a coherent, accessible range across the required content, surfaces, components, states, and modes.
- **Best suited for:** Portfolios, architecture, fashion, photography, art, culture, editorial features, studios, agencies, products, campaigns, exhibitions, publishing, and identity-led websites.
- **Use selectively for:** Dense data, status-heavy products, marketplaces, educational diagrams, health or safety information, complex transactions, and experiences where many categories or values require distinct visual encoding.
- **Compatible with:** None declared until cross-style validation confirms a coherent combination.
- **Conflicts with:** None declared until cross-style validation confirms a material conflict. Do not infer either relationship.

Treat Monochrome as a complete user-facing preset. Once selected, do not ask the user to choose achromatic versus single-hue expression, a grayscale recipe, an accent, a tonal ladder, a contrast level, an image filter, a typography style, a foundation, or a second style.

## Identity

### Principle

Use deliberate color limitation to make structure, content, form, and meaning more visible.

### Character

Monochrome should feel:

- Focused without becoming empty.
- Restrained without becoming generic.
- Graphic and tonal without becoming harsh or muddy.
- Precise and expressive without requiring Swiss Minimalism or multiple hues.
- Cohesive without flattening every distinction.
- Confident without making every region high contrast.
- Contemporary, historical, quiet, or dramatic only when confirmed context supports it.
- Understandable when hue, subtle tone, or authored color is unavailable.

### Anti-goals

Do not turn Monochrome into:

- Every color mechanically converted to gray.
- Only the lightest and darkest available values.
- A grayscale, tint, duotone, inversion, or contrast filter applied to supplied media.
- Surfaces, borders, labels, controls, and states separated by imperceptible tonal shifts.
- Canonical functional colors removed or remapped for palette purity.
- One unrelated bright accent added because the hierarchy cannot work without it.
- Low saturation, low contrast, reduced opacity, or faint type presented as sophistication.
- Black and white treated as automatic minimalism, elegance, luxury, seriousness, modernity, or accessibility.
- Swiss grids, newsprint columns, oversized type, or dark mode inferred from limited color.
- Palette consistency placed above product truth, state clarity, data comprehension, identity, dignity, or safety.

### Signature traits

Build the treatment through:

- One dominant canonical neutral or confirmed chromatic family across expressive color roles.
- Deliberate tonal relationships that separate field, content, surfaces, boundaries, actions, and states.
- Context-appropriate canonical typography, scale, alignment, contour, silhouette, imagery, or negative space as supporting carriers.
- One unmistakable focal relationship created without an unrelated accent hue.
- Repetition of tonal, border, fill, image, typographic, or spatial relationships across the website.
- Meaningful alternation between graphic focal moments and quieter reading or task regions.
- Approved monochrome imagery or truthful full-color content treated as a deliberate exception.
- Canonical functional color contained within its semantic responsibility when meaning requires it.
- Conventional navigation, controls, focus, validation, status, and recovery.

The result must remain recognizably Monochrome without pure black, pure white, grayscale photography, minimal layout, large typography, dark mode, grain, texture, or a bright accent.

### Minimum signature

Every Monochrome result should preserve:

- One dominant neutral or confirmed chromatic family across expressive roles.
- Sufficient supported tonal separation for content, surfaces, boundaries, actions, and states.
- One clear focal relationship created through tone, typography, scale, image, placement, contour, or supported inversion.
- One repeated graphic or tonal relationship that establishes family resemblance.
- Persistent non-color cues for interaction, current location, selection, status, validation, and data meaning.
- Functional color restricted to documented semantic roles and truthful full-color content preserved when material.

If canonical mappings cannot establish the minimum signature, use the nearest supported expression only when it preserves the confirmed direction, meaning, and access, and report partial style support. Otherwise report the gap. Do not derive shades, invent colors, or alter assets locally.

## Expression Model

Infer one dominant emphasis from confirmed purpose, content, layout, assets, mode, and canonical coverage. Use other emphases only when they strengthen the same direction; do not ask the user to configure a subtype.

- **Achromatic:** Use documented mappings that already produce coherent neutral expressive roles. Build distinction through tone, surface, typography, image, contour, and shape. This is the default when no confirmed brand hue should dominate or complete single-hue mappings are unavailable.
- **Single-hue:** Use one confirmed chromatic relationship only when documented mappings already support it across expressive roles. Preserve neutral and functional roles where canonical meaning requires them; never select primitives or rewrite bindings.
- **Typographic and graphic:** Use canonical type roles, rules, framing, shape, scale, alignment, supported inversion, and negative space to carry the primary visual energy.
- **Image-led:** Let approved monochrome photography, illustration, artwork, product imagery, or archival media become the primary expressive field while the interface maintains stable tonal relationships.
- **Tonal-surface:** Use only the supported surface and border relationships needed to create grouping and measured depth within the dominant family.
- **Functional restraint:** Prioritize comprehension around navigation, reading, forms, checkout, permissions, consent, account access, tables, charts, legal content, errors, recovery, and consequential actions. Permit canonical functional color and redundant non-color cues wherever strict monochrome would weaken meaning.

### Website and Product Design boundary

Use Monochrome for public editorial, portfolios, collections, campaigns, exhibitions, product marketing, documentation, service information, comparison, browsing, and bounded canonical conversion surfaces.

Product Design, when available, owns authenticated workflows, persistent state, dashboards, complex data, accounts, permissions, editing, and frequent interaction. Otherwise keep the work within confirmed public or bounded scope and report unresolved product behavior.

Monochrome may shape confirmed expression. It cannot introduce charts, filters, categories, workflows, status systems, commerce, products, evidence, content, or component behavior.

Front-end Build, when available, owns implementation of confirmed canonical appearance, asset delivery, user adaptations, and performance. Otherwise use the current implementation workflow in `AGENTS.md`.

### Distinction from adjacent directions

- **Swiss:** Governs a specific typographic, grid, alignment, and information-order tradition and may use multiple colors; Monochrome limits expressive hue and does not select that structure.
- **Modern Dark:** Requires an explicit dark appearance and ordered dark-surface hierarchy; Monochrome requires neither.
- **Bold Typography:** Makes language the dominant visual material; Monochrome may rely on image, contour, surface, object, or line instead.
- **Flat Design:** Defines a depth, illustration, and component-treatment model; Monochrome limits expressive hue and may use supported depth.

These distinctions do not make adjacent profiles available, compatible, or conflicting. Do not load or combine another profile without validated support through the style-selection contract.

## Visual Expression

### Hierarchy

Lead with the confirmed subject, message, evidence, or action. Establish order through canonical typography, scale, placement, spacing, grouping, surface, border, image, contour, and measured tonal contrast.

Reserve the strongest supported contrast for the most important information and actions within each scope. Step supporting content down visibly without making it faint or ambiguous.

Because hue is limited, reinforce relationships with a persistent non-tonal cue such as text, icon, shape, position, underline, pattern, or structure. Fill, border, and tone may supplement it; a subtle shift alone cannot distinguish navigation, links, controls, selection, or status.

### Typography character

Use only typefaces, typography roles, complete tuples, and mappings available in `alpy/type-scale.md`. Resolve component-owned typography slots and bindings through `alpy/component-tokens.md`.

Let display, heading, body, label, and metadata roles carry clear differences in purpose. Create expression through supported scale, weight, case, measure, alignment, rhythm, and repetition.

Monochrome does not require sans serif, serif, condensed, grotesque, editorial, or display typography. Follow the confirmed direction and canonical roles.

Do not invent typefaces, weights, tracking, opacity, outlined type, text masking, image-based text, or extreme contrast. Keep essential copy, labels, metadata, legal text, and actions live, selectable, translatable, and readable.

### Color behavior

Resolve component-owned color through `alpy/component-tokens.md` and remaining intent through `alpy/semantic-tokens.md` and explicit mode mappings.

Use documented semantic and component mappings that already produce a coherent neutral expression. Use one chromatic relationship only when the confirmed direction calls for it and existing mappings cover required roles, surfaces, states, and modes. Never select primitives or rewrite bindings.

Preserve canonical roles for success, warning, error, information, focus, selection, and status. These may introduce additional hues when the package requires them. Treat them as meaningful exceptions, not decoration.

Do not invent tints, shades, tones, alpha values, gradients, or an accent. Do not remap functional meaning into the dominant family merely to preserve visual purity.

### Tone, contour, surface, and depth

Use the supported tonal range deliberately. Separate foreground from background, primary from supporting, grouped from ungrouped, enabled from disabled, interactive from static, and current from available.

Use canonical components, anatomy, radii, borders, surfaces, and overlays. Use depth only through explicit canonical component or effect support; otherwise let outline, fill, silhouette, edge, surface, and negative space carry more visual responsibility because hue is limited.

Surface and border changes must communicate real grouping, object, state, layer, or action. Do not enclose every item or stack slightly different tones merely to display a monochrome ladder.

Do not invent rules, frames, cutouts, textures, shadows, patterns, or shape systems. Validate adjacent relationships rather than assuming that tones within one family remain distinguishable.

### Imagery and iconography

Use only supplied and approved photography, illustration, video, artwork, product media, interface captures, diagrams, patterns, marks, and brand assets.

Prefer approved monochrome or single-hue variants only when they preserve subject, evidence, product, identity, and meaning. Do not apply grayscale, tint, duotone, blend, inversion, desaturation, contrast, halftone, grain, or other effects locally.

Keep full-color content truthful when color is material to identity, people, art, products, materials, data, status, culture, health, safety, or comprehension. Treat it as a deliberate content exception.

Preserve focal points, skin tones, material qualities, captions, credits, rights, provenance, controls, and applicable alternatives. Report the need for a suitable asset variant instead of fabricating one.

Use canonical Alpy icons, sizes, roles, and bindings. Keep interactive and semantic icons distinct through shape, placement, label, and state—not color alone. Do not create filled or outlined icon variants locally.

## Tone, Form, And Exception Grammar

Use this internal grammar to coordinate the profile:

- **Field:** The dominant neutral or confirmed chromatic family that establishes cohesion.
- **Interval:** A supported tonal relationship with a clear hierarchy or structural purpose.
- **Anchor:** The confirmed content, image, object, type, evidence, or action receiving the strongest appropriate contrast.
- **Contour:** A canonical edge, line, silhouette, border, or shape that clarifies identity or relationship.
- **Rhythm:** A repeated tonal, typographic, image, contour, or spatial relationship that creates continuity.
- **Exception:** Canonical functional color or truthful full-color content whose meaning must remain intact.
- **Rest:** A stable region for reading, forms, decisions, consent, or recovery.

These are reasoning aids, not new tokens, components, values, or content types.

Apply these rules:

- Give every interval a structural or hierarchical purpose.
- Let one anchor lead within each scope instead of maximizing contrast everywhere.
- Use contour and content to reinforce differences that tone alone cannot carry.
- Repeat a small set of relationships rather than inventing a new shade for every region.
- Keep exceptions meaningful, contained, and visually distinct from decoration.
- Preserve complete structure and meaning when authored color or subtle tone is overridden.

Apply `Editorial`, `Asymmetric`, or `Immersive` only when the confirmed direction calls for it. Graphic contrast, limited color, large type, unequal tone, or image-led drama does not independently select another composition.

Do not infer Focused, Linear, Split, Editorial, sparse density, or a modernist grid from the style name.

### Grid and alignment

Use exact containers, columns, spans, gutters, and responsive behavior through Grid and Layout and canonical Alpy guidance and mappings. Report missing coverage instead of creating a local layout substitute.

Maintain clear shared edges among navigation, type, media, controls, dividers, and section boundaries. Use supported breaks only when confirmed layout preserves reading order, focus order, reflow, and target access.

Do not prescribe a monochrome grid, centered minimal layout, editorial columns, full-bleed imagery, off-canvas staging, unsupported overlap, or arbitrary offsets.

### Rhythm, density, and measure

Preserve density defined by Rhythm and Space. Create rhythm through supported alternation among scale, spacing, tone, contour, surface, image, content density, and open regions.

Monochrome may be sparse or dense. Open space should clarify attention; compact regions should preserve scanning and boundaries. Do not create uniform gray blocks, repetitive card grids, or excessive emptiness that makes the site feel unfinished.

Keep sustained reading on stable canonical surfaces with clear headings, grouping, links, and transitions. Preserve logical order and direct action paths; do not require visitors to infer sequence from tone or visual position alone.

## Content And Media Integrity

### Claims and meaning

Monochrome presentation is not evidence of elegance, luxury, quality, seriousness, authenticity, historical importance, artistic value, professionalism, accessibility, sustainability, or product capability.

Use current authoritative evidence for objective claims, testimonials, metrics, prices, availability, outcomes, certifications, health, safety, and technical statements. Preserve date, scope, method, source, and adjacent readable qualifiers. Distinguish facts, approved narrative, interpretation, and unknowns.

Do not add generic black-and-white photography, abstract geometry, historic imagery, quotes, badges, charts, or high-contrast statistics to make missing content feel designed.

### Color as evidence

Color can be part of the fact being communicated. Preserve approved full-color content when visitors need it to identify a person or organization, evaluate art, fashion, food, material, condition, product variant, map, chart, scientific or medical image, warning, status, or cultural artifact.

Do not imply that a transformed asset is original evidence. Do not invent monochrome variants, restore color, recolor, crop away qualifying details, or convert supplied content without authorization.

Keep captions, legends, labels, units, sources, credits, rights, alternative text, transcripts, descriptions, and media controls where applicable. Report missing coverage instead of using the style to conceal it.

## Component Expression

### Navigation

Use canonical navigation and disclosure patterns. Keep destinations, current location, hierarchy, focus, and responsive behavior explicit.

Differentiate current, available, expanded, and disabled destinations through canonical state plus at least one persistent non-tonal cue such as label, icon, position, underline, shape, or structure. Type, fill, and border may reinforce the distinction but cannot rely on a faint tonal change alone.

### Actions and links

Use canonical action hierarchy and direct language. A primary action may use the strongest supported fill, inverse, border, or foreground relationship within its scope.

Keep supporting actions subordinate. Distinguish inline links from surrounding text through a supported persistent non-color cue; tone alone is insufficient.

Do not introduce a bright accent, invert every action, or make every control equally high contrast.

### Containers and cards

Use canonical containers and cards for real groups, independent objects, media, states, or actions. Establish boundaries through supported surface, border, spacing, heading, and content relationships.

Avoid making every item a slightly different tonal card. Prefer open sections, lists, dividers, or shared regions when they communicate structure more clearly.

### Forms

Use canonical fields and controls with persistent labels, requirements, guidance, visible boundaries, validation, correction, and recovery.

Keep fields distinguishable from surrounding surfaces. Make selected, checked, invalid, focused, disabled, unavailable, and read-only states clear through more than color or opacity.

Do not hide input boundaries, rely on placeholders, or weaken error and requirement communication for visual restraint.

### Data and metadata

Use canonical structures with consistent attributes, labels, units, precision, provenance, timestamps, sorting, filtering, comparison, selection, and status.

Use direct labels, position, markers, line styles, shapes, patterns, annotation, or supported functional color when tone cannot carry enough categories. Every chart or diagram needs a canonical structured-text or table equivalent and verification after user color transformation; otherwise report unsupported coverage.

Do not force complex charts into indistinguishable gray series, remove meaningful data color, or invent data to create rhythm.

## Page And Section Expression

### Header and navigation

Establish identity, primary paths, current location, and principal action through a stable structure. Introduce Monochrome through one tonal, border, typographic, image, contour, or supported inversion relationship.

Do not make the header disappear into the opening region or use a different high-contrast treatment for every destination.

### Hero

Create one unmistakable anchor using a clear proposition and approved media, product, typography, contour, surface, or tonal composition. Keep the primary action and essential context immediately findable.

Do not add generic black-and-white imagery, abstract shapes, oversized type, or contrasting color merely to make the opening feel designed.

### Section rhythm and [transitions](../ui-design/motion-transition.md)

Create progression through purpose, content, canonical spacing, alignment, type, imagery, contour, and meaningful tonal or surface relationships.

Do not alternate light and dark sections mechanically, place every feature in a bordered tile, or replace substantive differentiation with shade changes.

### Content and collection regions

Use clear section purpose, hierarchy, evidence, media, metadata, and action paths. Collections need consistent anatomy, meaningful crops, labels, filtering, comparison, and item distinction.

Approved monochrome media may create unity. Preserve truthful full-color works or products when color is part of what visitors evaluate.

### Evidence and calls to action

Present real testimonials, customers, specifications, outcomes, pricing, security information, press, or proof through canonical structures and supplied content. Keep authoritative qualifiers adjacent and comparably prominent; use hierarchy and contrast rather than decorative accent.

Match calls to action to visitor readiness. Keep conditions, commitment, price, privacy, and consequence close enough to understand before activation.

Do not invent logos, ratings, awards, metrics, quotes, scarcity, endorsements, or coercive contrast.

### Footer

Keep durable navigation, legal, privacy, accessibility, locale, contact, support, provenance, and credits complete and readable. The footer may be quieter than primary content but cannot become faint or detached.

## State Expression

### Hover, focus, and selected

Use canonical state mappings. Keep keyboard focus unmistakable around every interactive element and distinct from borders, selection, current location, and decorative framing. Expose supported name, role, value, current, expanded, selected, invalid, busy, and status meaning through canonical semantics.

Hover may reinforce affordance but cannot reveal essential meaning or be the only cue. Selection and current state need persistent supported differences beyond hue or subtle tone.

### Disabled and unavailable

Use canonical semantics and preserve readable labels. Distinguish disabled, unavailable, read-only, pending, and permission-restricted states when the product does.

Do not reduce opacity locally or make inactive controls disappear into the field.

### Loading and empty

Keep loading and progress visible, stable, and meaningfully announced. Empty states should explain the condition and supported next step without inventing data, permissions, or actions.

Approved monochrome imagery may support a state but cannot replace explanation, status, or recovery.

### Success, warning, and error

Use canonical semantic roles, icons, labels, placement, persistence, and status behavior. Functional color may break the expressive family when canonical meaning requires it.

Keep status color contained to meaning and reinforce it with text, icon shape, heading, or other supported cues. Do not mute status to preserve palette purity.

## Interaction Expression

### Accessibility and verification risks

Verify actual rendered text, icons, controls, links, boundaries, focus, selection, statuses, charts, diagrams, and adjacent tones. Thin lines, faint type, low-contrast surfaces, and subtle state shifts can disappear even when the palette appears orderly.

Keep information available without hue, tone, position, shape, motion, sound, or imagery alone. One non-color cue must itself remain perceivable and understandable; combining two ambiguous cues is insufficient.

Test color-vision differences, limited-color presentation, grayscale perception, supported increased, reduced, and custom contrast preferences, forced colors, zoom, reflow, text spacing, keyboard, touch, alternative input, screen readers, missing media, and loading.

A grayscale preview does not simulate all color-vision differences. Generated design, token mappings, simulations, automated contrast results, or visual inspection do not establish accessibility conformance. Report untested behavior and missing support.

### Avoid

- Hardcoded CSS or raw design values.
- New fonts, colors, icons, assets, tokens, components, variants, states, modes, breakpoints, effects, or motion.
- A profile-prescribed, locally constructed, or hardcoded black-and-white, grayscale, or single-hue palette, including derived tones, gradients, alpha values, filters, or local accents.
- Global grayscale, tint, duotone, inversion, desaturation, or contrast effects on media and components.
- Faint text, invisible boundaries, ambiguous controls, opacity-only states, tone carrying meaning alone, or semantic status and focus remapped for aesthetic purity.
- Minimalism, elegance, luxury, seriousness, modernism, or dark mode inferred from color limitation.
- Generic imagery, abstract geometry, borders, cards, rules, or oversized type used as filler.
- A result that feels unfinished because color limitation was mistaken for a complete direction.
