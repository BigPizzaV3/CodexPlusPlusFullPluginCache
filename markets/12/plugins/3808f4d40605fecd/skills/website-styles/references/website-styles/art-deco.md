## Creative Purpose

## Required Art Deco System

Apply [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md), [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md), [Scale and Proportion](../ui-design/scale-proportion.md), [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md), and [Responsive Adaptation](../responsive-adaptation.md) for supported layout, scale, spacing, source order, and reflow. The rules below define the Art Deco-specific system.

1. Establish one dominant axis for each major region: centered symmetry or a precise asymmetric counterweight. Do not mix both inside one region.
2. Create one monumental focal relationship among the headline, subject, image, frame, or surface. Keep all supporting elements visibly subordinate.
3. Choose one primary geometric family and one supporting family. Use stepped or chevron forms, arches or fans, radiating forms, or linear banding—not all of them together.
4. Repeat the chosen geometry through framing, dividers, crops, section thresholds, or alignment. Every motif must frame, divide, direct, sequence, or balance content.
5. Concentrate ornament in the opening, major transitions, and one closing moment. Keep navigation, forms, pricing, data, and routine content restrained.
6. Use three intensity levels: ceremonial for one major focal region, composed for storytelling and proof, and functional for tasks and controls.
7. Follow every ceremonial region with a quieter reading, proof, or task region.
8. Use one typography hierarchy, one color system, one surface family, one border language, and one light-and-shadow model across the page.
9. Use real, permissioned media as the visual subject. Do not use geometry, icons, or empty frames as substitutes for meaningful content.
10. On narrow screens, preserve the focal subject, axis, and reading order; simplify secondary ornament, overlap, and framing before reducing legibility.

## Art Direction

Apply [Balance](../ui-design/balance.md), [Gestalt](../ui-design/gestalt.md), [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md), and [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) for weight, relationships, emphasis, and supported color use. This section only defines one coherent Art Deco world.

Before composing, define exactly four items from confirmed content and assets:

- One primary subject.
- One dominant media family.
- One primary geometric family.
- One canvas-to-surface contrast relationship.

Carry those four decisions through the complete page. Use a second media or geometric family only as quiet support.

Create richness through proportion, framing, crop, linework, surface contrast, and precise repetition. Do not default to black, gold, cream, marble, chrome, glitter, or generic luxury styling.

## Composition

Apply [Grid and Layout](../ui-design/grid-layout.md), [Alignment](../ui-design/alignment.md), and [Visual Hierarchy](../ui-design/visual-hierarchy.md) for structural hierarchy, relationships, focal priority, and reading order. This section only defines Art Deco-specific staging.

Build the page around one dominant axis and one focal subject. Use frames, bands, shared edges, or stepped relationships to pull supporting content toward that subject.

Use symmetry only for ceremonial moments. Use aligned editorial compositions for explanations, evidence, and functional content. Never center long reading, forms, tables, or repeated utility content for visual effect.

Repeat a small number of proportions and alignments. Introduce one counterweight, crop, or break in symmetry only when it strengthens the focal hierarchy.

## Surface, Color, Ornament, and Detail

This profile defines its Art Deco shape language. Apply [Border and Divider](../ui-design/border-divider.md), [Elevation](../ui-design/elevation.md), and [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md) for supported boundary, surface, depth, and color values.

Build the style primarily with flat surfaces, crisp boundaries, shared edges, linework, and controlled contrast. Use depth only for a real layer relationship.

Use one primary ornament family and one quiet supporting family. Repeat them at major frames, dividers, image crops, or section thresholds; never apply ornament independently to every card, button, and heading.

Use gradients, shine, metallic effects, shadow, glow, texture, and masks only when canonical or approved project support exists. None is required for Art Deco.

## Page Rhythm

Apply [Rhythm and Space](../ui-design/rhythm-space.md) for pacing and continuity. Use this sequence for Art Deco-specific intensity.

1. A decisive arrival or page opening.
2. A quiet explanation or orientation region.
3. A composed story, feature, collection, or proof region.
4. A restrained functional or reading region.
5. One closing statement that echoes the opening axis, frame, or geometry.

Do not place two ceremonial regions together unless they form one continuous composition. Keep navigation, forms, booking, pricing, comparison, dense data, support, and the footer calm.

## Success Criteria

Validate with the applicable UI skills and [Accessibility Audit](../accessibility-audit.md). Approve Art Deco only when all rules pass:

1. Use one dominant axis, one monumental focal relationship, one primary geometric family, and one supporting family.
2. Repeat geometry only where it frames, divides, directs, sequences, or balances real content. Concentrate ornament in focal moments and thresholds.
3. Alternate ceremonial, composed, and functional intensity. Keep navigation, reading, forms, pricing, data, and controls calm and clear.
4. Resolve complete canonical typography through [Typography and Readability](../ui-design/typography-readability.md) and color through [Color Discipline](../ui-design/color-discipline.md). Never invent typography or color values.
5. Use coordinated, permissioned media at meaningful scale. Preserve the confirmed hero structure, focal subject, reading order, actions, and media during responsive reflow.
6. Remove historical costume, unsupported cultural references, placeholder assets, filler ornament, invented claims, and effects used as substitutes for composition.

If any condition fails, revise the composition or do not use Art Deco for that region.
