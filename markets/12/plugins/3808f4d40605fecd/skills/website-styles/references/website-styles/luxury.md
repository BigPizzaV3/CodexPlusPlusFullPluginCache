## Purpose

Create a considered website expression that makes confirmed quality, craft, provenance, distinction, service, or experience perceptible through deliberate presentation and precise interaction.

Luxury is a value-and-experience archetype, not a black-and-gold palette, serif treatment, sparse layout, high price, or claim of exclusivity. Its identity comes from the relationship among verified substance, selective emphasis, approved media, assured hierarchy, purposeful space, clear service, and dignified behavior.

After the shared chat confirmation, use this profile only when Luxury was selected under confirmed delegation or preserved from the confirmed restatement. Apply it with relevant Design intent skills and the canonical Alpy package.

This profile defines semantic relationships and selection priorities. It does not define CSS, raw values, named typefaces, fixed colors, components, tokens, modes, breakpoints, media, claims, pricing, availability, provenance, policies, or canonical behavior.

## Identity

### Principle

Make verified value perceptible through deliberate presentation, then make every interaction worthy of the attention the website asks for.

### Meaning and scope

Luxury changes across social, cultural, economic, and ecological contexts. It may come from confirmed craft, precision, design, material, place, time, service, knowledge, experience, meaning, innovation, continuity, repair, or longevity.

No single visual convention establishes luxury. A restrained contemporary brand, richly editorial collection, craft workshop, destination, cultural object, and high-touch service may all express it differently. The website must reveal the specific basis of value rather than apply a generic premium surface.

Aesthetic appeal may influence perception but cannot establish authenticity, quality, provenance, ethics, scarcity, or luxury status.

### Character

Luxury should feel:

- Assured without becoming aloof.
- Refined without becoming faint or fragile.
- Sensory without depending on one sense.
- Distinctive without becoming ornamental by default.
- Emotional while remaining specific and truthful.
- Attentive rather than slow for its own sake.
- Service-led without inventing personalization.
- Desirable without equating ownership with personal worth.
- Culturally situated without treating one tradition as universal.
- Precise around price, availability, conditions, and consequence.

### Anti-goals

Do not turn Luxury into:

- Black, gold, cream, jewel tones, serif typography, thin text, or extreme whitespace treated as sufficient.
- Generic premium minimalism with little information or evidence.
- Ornamental excess confused with value.
- Cinematic media, parallax, or slow transitions used as proof of distinction.
- Hidden navigation, cryptic labels, faint controls, or artificial friction presented as exclusivity.
- A European aristocratic, Parisian fashion, palace, heraldic, or “old money” stereotype.
- A collection of material or cultural references detached from their people, place, rights, and meaning.
- Invented heritage, artisanship, rarity, origin, awards, sustainability, reviews, pricing, stock, demand, access, or prestige.
- Status language that devalues people as ordinary, basic, cheap, unsophisticated, or unworthy.
- Product, booking, membership, clienteling, or commerce behavior that has not been confirmed.

### Signature traits

Build the archetype through:

- One clear basis of value grounded in confirmed product, service, place, craft, knowledge, experience, or evidence.
- Deliberate selection rather than visual emptiness.
- Assured hierarchy and controlled emphasis around a primary object, proposition, place, experience, or action.
- Purposeful quiet around approved focal media and evidence-rich detail.
- Precise relationships among image, language, object, space, and action.
- Approved media that reveals material, scale, process, use, place, expertise, or atmosphere.
- Inspectable facts close to the claims and decisions they qualify.
- Visible service, care, availability, and next steps when supported.
- Restrained canonical surfaces and depth rather than simulated precious materials.
- Conventional, discoverable interaction and calm transactional regions.

The result must remain recognizably Luxury without a fixed palette, type category, mode, historical reference, metallic effect, expensive material, fullscreen film, sparse density, or exclusive-access mechanism.

### Minimum signature

Every Luxury result should preserve:

- One confirmed and visible basis for exceptional or considered value.
- One assured focal hierarchy supported by deliberate secondary detail.
- One approved media or content relationship that communicates quality, craft, service, place, or experience.
- One evidence pattern that lets people inspect relevant claims rather than merely absorb atmosphere.
- One purposeful rhythm of emphasis and rest.
- Clear service, action, status, and recovery in functional regions.
- Complete meaning when expressive media, motion, or high-resolution assets are unavailable.

If confirmed evidence or canonical support cannot establish the minimum signature, report partial style support. Do not compensate with invented prestige language, claims, assets, or local styling.

## Value, Desire, And Evidence Grammar

### Value before spectacle

Identify the confirmed basis of value before selecting expressive treatment. Quality may be functional, material, experiential, cultural, emotional, technical, service-based, or symbolic; do not reduce it to price or scarcity.

Presentation must make that basis understandable. Polish and price cannot prove authenticity, quality, ethics, durability, or cultural significance.

### Desire without pressure

Create desire through relevance, imagination, sensory detail, distinction, service, and a clear relationship between the offering and the visitor's goal. Preserve time to inspect facts and consequences.

Do not manufacture urgency through countdowns, low-stock messages, artificial queues, social proof, invitation theater, preselected add-ons, visually weakened decline paths, or shame. Confirmed scarcity must remain factual, contextual, and non-coercive.

### Evidence pattern

Attach authoritative evidence to the claim or decision it qualifies. Depending on the confirmed offering, this may include materials, technique, dimensions, origin, condition, maker, date, edition, care, repair, warranty, service, availability, price, delivery, certification, or source.

Distinguish documented facts, approved narrative, interpretation, and unknown information. Report missing truth rather than filling the gap with elevated language, symbolic imagery, flags, maps, workshop scenes, awards, or implied origin.

## Content, Commerce, And Cultural Integrity

### Claims and provenance

Use authoritative project sources for heritage, dates, archives, makers, materials, techniques, place, origin, rarity, editions, awards, certifications, partnerships, endorsements, reviews, ethical sourcing, sustainability, durability, labor, environmental impact, and community benefit.

Do not invent “handmade,” “bespoke,” “rare,” “heirloom,” “atelier,” “responsibly sourced,” “limited edition,” “private,” “exclusive,” or geographic-origin language. Imagery and layout can imply claims as strongly as copy; decorative atmosphere is not substantiation.

When an applicable legal, policy, environmental, origin, endorsement, or disclosure question is unresolved, preserve confirmed content and report the required owner or specialist decision. Research sources do not establish jurisdiction-specific obligations.

### Commerce and service truth

Use confirmed sources for price, currency, taxes, mandatory fees, inventory, availability, variants, delivery, returns, warranty, cancellation, repair, care, appointments, response expectations, and support.

Keep material terms, price, availability, conditions, support, cancellation, and primary actions easy to find and operate. Do not hide them to preserve mystery, imply white-glove service where none exists, or make recovery more difficult than commitment.

### Cultural integrity

Do not use people, communities, ceremonies, crafts, textiles, architecture, places, languages, sacred symbols, or traditional cultural expressions as signs of refinement without confirmed context and any required rights, approvals, attribution, or benefit-sharing arrangement. Report unresolved authority instead of deciding what is required.

Distinguish inspiration from documented collaboration. Represent craftspeople as people and knowledge holders rather than anonymous evidence of brand prestige. Do not romanticize hazardous labor, animal materials, extractive materials, colonial provenance, or environmental harm.

Do not treat one geography, aristocratic tradition, fashion system, hospitality convention, accent, body, age, gender, race, ethnicity, nationality, disability, or class position as the universal image of luxury.

### Dignity and choice

Preserve equal access to information, support, consent, decline, recovery, and primary capability. Do not equate wealth, taste, ownership, lineage, invitation, or access with personal worth.

Use clear, respectful language for navigation, eligibility, conditions, actions, errors, and service. “Concierge,” “private appointment,” “request access,” “join waitlist,” and similar language may appear only when the supported destination and governing rules are confirmed.

Clearly identify sponsored editorial, paid endorsement, gifted product, affiliate relationship, or other material connection when authoritative requirements establish it. Do not style promotion as independent evidence.
