# Commercial feasibility framework

Use a qualitative matrix. For each relevant dimension record evidence, source,
implication and an unresolved question. Rate readiness as ready, needs adaptation,
blocked or unknown, with a reason. These are planning judgments, not measured
probabilities or a prediction of market demand.

| Dimension | Decision it informs |
| --- | --- |
| Buyer and problem | Who can buy, which problem warrants paying, and what evidence supports that connection? |
| Offer and alternatives | What workflow or outcome is distinctive relative to named, verified alternatives, including doing nothing? |
| Language and trust | Are product, onboarding, support and proof actually suitable for the selected buyer? |
| Channel access | Can this buyer be reached through a specific, feasible channel within stated resources? |
| Price and collection | What is known about price, currency, payment acceptance, collection and refund friction? |
| Delivery and support | Can the business fulfil the offer in the country with its current team and partners? |
| Local requirements | Which applicable regulatory, tax, contracting or import questions require verification before launch? |
| Economics and evidence | What margins, acquisition costs and observed buying signals are known, and what remains hypothetical? |

Country choice must reflect this product and this user's constraints. Large
population, translated pages or a generic growth narrative alone are insufficient.
Compare like-for-like segments rather than national averages. Do not imply
competitor presence proves an accessible opportunity for this business.

When inputs allow a calculation, show the formula, units, currency and period.
For example, contribution per sale = collected revenue minus variable delivery,
payment and support costs. A test acquisition cost is test spend divided by new
paying customers, not clicks or email opens. With zero customers it is undefined,
not zero. Do not substitute assumptions for missing margin or retention data.
Illustrative scenarios must be explicitly labelled and never become forecasts.

Use stronger evidence for stronger recommendations. Product fit suggests a
hypothesis; qualified conversations suggest interest; willingness to pay and
completed sales add commercial evidence. Even a small successful test does not
prove scalable acquisition or long-term retention.
