# Ball handling

Ball handling is controlling the ball with the hands, above all the dribble: the skill that lets a child move with the ball, get out of trouble and attack the basket.

## By age

- **6–8:** Use a smaller, lighter ball if you can. Teach mostly through dribble games — games where everyone has a ball and dribbling is how you play. One cue at a time. At six, just bouncing and catching the ball is an achievement. Eyes will drop to the ball and balls will roll away: that is normal.
- **9–11:** Prime years for clean habits with both hands. Add the crossover — switching hands in front of the body — and changes of speed. Play 1-on-1 and 2-on-2 so every child dribbles against a defender many times. Give a home routine of five to ten minutes.
- **12–14:** The question moves from how to dribble to when. Teach moves that have a reason, like the hesitation, the in-and-out and between the legs, and end every rep with a choice: pass, drive or shoot. Handling can turn clumsy during a growth spurt.
- **15–18:** Work at game speed against live defenders. Touch comes from two-ball drills and short, hard handling blocks. Decisions come from live 1-on-1 and advantage games. Players set their own goals and own their work at home.

## Progression

1. **Alone:** A ball each, in place first. Pound dribble — a hard, waist-high bounce — with the strong hand, then the weak hand. Then low dribbles, switching hands, around the legs. Then on the move: walk, jog, stop on a call, change hands at cones, change speed.
2. **With a partner:** A partner holds up fingers and the dribbler calls the number while dribbling. Swap every round. Short of balls? Share in pairs, never in lines.
3. **Against a defender:** 1-on-1 to a cone or a line. With a defender close, drop the dribble lower and raise the other arm to shield the ball, never to push.
4. **In a game:** Red Light Green Light Dribble, Dribble Tag, Sharks and Minnows and Knockout Dribble are all in the library. Nobody sits out: a tagged or knocked-out player comes straight back in.

Visit every step in one practice. A full minute with no lost balls means the task is too easy: speed it up, get lower or add pressure.

## Coaching points

- "Spider hand, push it down." Finger pads, not the palm: a quiet dribble instead of a slap.
- "Eyes up." Lets the player see teammates, the basket and the defender. Hard at first, so praise every heads-up moment.
- "Ball by your hip, body in the way." Low and to the side, so the body shields the ball.
- "Push it ahead, then catch up." Keeps the ball in front of the feet on the move.
- "Good try, go get it." A lost ball means the player is trying something hard.

## Common mistakes

| What you see | What to say |
|---|---|
| Slaps the ball with the palm | "Spider hand." Spread the fingers and keep the palm off the ball. |
| Eyes on the ball | Give the eyes a job: call out the fingers you hold up, or play Red Light Green Light Dribble. |
| Swings the whole arm from the shoulder | A minute of dribbling on the knees or sitting down, so only fingers and wrist can do it. |
| Ball bounces up to the chest | "Ball by your hip." Knees bent, and a harder push down. |
| Ball straight in front; kicks it or trips on it | "Ball to the side, body in the way." |
| Uses the strong hand only | Weak-hand rounds in games. Sometimes start with the weak hand. Cheer every attempt. |
| Picks up the dribble with nowhere to go | "Keep it alive until you see a pass or a shot." |

## In a practice

- For beginners, 10–15 focused minutes in most practices, plus dribbling inside the games. For 6–8, 30 to 60 seconds per hand, then a new challenge.
- Open with a dribble game as the warm-up and close the block with another.
- Pairs well with finishing and passing: a dribble should lead somewhere, to a pass, a layup or a shot.
- Keep lines short: one ball per child, each in their own space. For cone work, three or four short lanes so nobody waits more than one turn.
- Both hands in every drill, from the first practice. Slow and controlled with the weak hand beats fast and wild.
- Never answer a lost ball with running or push-ups. That teaches kids to play it safe.
- Add a move once a player can jog and glance up while dribbling with either hand. Start with the crossover, and give every move a reason.
