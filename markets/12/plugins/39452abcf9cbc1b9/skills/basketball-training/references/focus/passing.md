# Passing

Passing and catching are one skill shared by two players, so teach both halves on every rep. A team that passes keeps every child in the game, and the ball finds the open player.

## By age

- **6–8:** Catching first. Soft passes, short distances and a smaller, lighter ball. Teach the chest pass and the bounce pass with "show a target" and "step to pass". Cheer every clean catch.
- **9–11:** The best window to make passing automatic. Add the overhead pass, passing on the move, and simple choices: a bounce pass to a cutter, a chest pass to an open teammate.
- **12–14:** Passing under pressure: pass fakes and pivoting away from a defender. Add the skip pass — a pass across the court, over the defense — and the outlet pass, the first pass after a rebound.
- **15–18:** Speed and accuracy in game situations: entry passes, passes out of a ball screen, the extra pass for a better shot. Ask players what the right pass was instead of telling them.

## Progression

1. **Alone:** Passes against a wall: step, push, thumbs down. Catch the return with two hands and soft fingers.
2. **With a partner:** Chest and bounce passes about 2.5 meters apart. Then on the move: side by side down the court, a star where each player passes and follows the pass, and the three-man weave.
3. **Against a defender:** Monkey in the middle (two passers, one defender between them), then 2-on-1. Players pivot, fake and pick the pass that beats the defender's hands.
4. **In a game:** 3-on-3 where a basket counts only after three passes, or a short no-dribble game. Passing becomes the way to win.

## Coaching points

- "Show a target." Hands up and open before the ball comes. Fixes most dropped passes.
- "Step to pass." A step toward the teammate adds power. Fixes slow, floating passes.
- "Thumbs down." Finish the chest pass with a full follow-through. The ball flies straight and is easier to catch.
- "Catch with your hands." Reach out, then pull the ball in. Fixes catching against the chest.
- "Pass and move." Cut, fill or relocate after every pass. Fixes standing and watching.

## Common mistakes

| What you see | What to say |
|---|---|
| Passes float and get stolen | "Step and snap." |
| Easy passes are fumbled | "No target, no pass." The passer waits for open hands. |
| A young player flinches or turns away | "Step closer and pass it softer." Cheer the catch: flinching fades with confidence. |
| Bounce passes arrive at the ankles | "Bounce it three quarters of the way." Mark the spot with a cone. |
| Overhead passes are slow | "Ball above your forehead, not behind your head." |
| Hands high on the defender | "Hands up? Bounce it under." |
| The pass lands behind a cutter | "Pass to where your teammate is going." Cutters keep running through the catch. |
| Players stand still after passing | "Pass and move: cut, fill or find a new spot." |
| Two or three players do all the passing | "Everybody touches the ball before we shoot." |
| A smart pass is dropped | "Great look. That was the right pass." |

## In a practice

- **Minutes:** One block of 5–8 minutes for ages 6–8, 8–12 for 9–11, and 10–15 for 12–14 and 15–18. Once players have the basics, spend most of it against a defender or in a game.
- **Where:** Early, right after the warm-up. Bring it back in the closing game with a passing rule.
- **Pairs well with:** Footwork (jump stop and pivot on the catch), cutting and the give-and-go — pass, cut to the basket, get the ball back — and rebounding and transition, through the outlet pass.
- **Short lines:** Pairs and groups of three, one ball per group. Never more than three players in a line.
- **Defender in the middle:** Swap the defender often, so everyone passes and everyone defends.
- **Keep it kind:** Soft passes at short range for beginners. Teach the catch before you add speed.
