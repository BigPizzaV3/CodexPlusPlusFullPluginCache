# Transition

Transition is the few seconds after the ball changes hands: the fast break one way, the sprint back the other. It needs no plays, it ends in layups, and young players love it.

## By age

- **6–8:** One rule each way: "pass ahead and run" when we get the ball, run back when we lose it. Short court, small numbers, lots of layup tries.
- **9–11:** Teach the outlet pass — the first pass after a defensive rebound, to a teammate near the sideline. Start with rebound, pivot, pass to a teammate standing still on the wing. Then three wide lanes with no defense, and 2-on-1.
- **12–14:** The receiver sprints to the outlet spot, and a defender tries to deny the pass. Add 3-on-2 and the trailer — the player who runs behind the ball. Around 13–14, a team that runs a basic pick-and-roll can add pistol action — a pass or handoff to the wing on the run, then a ball screen right away.
- **15–18:** Continuous 4-on-4, full court, and early offense that flows straight from the break. Teach when to push and when to pull the ball out.

## Progression

1. **No defense:** Rebound, outlet pass, three lanes, layup. Walk the lanes once, then run them both ways.
2. **With an advantage:** 2-on-1, then 3-on-2. Attack the basket and read the defender: steps up, pass. Drops back, finish.
3. **Continuous:** 3-on-2 one way, then the two defenders attack 2-on-1 the other way. Every possession ends with someone sprinting back.
4. **In a game:** 3-on-3 or 4-on-4, full court. The team that rebounds runs. A basket within 5 seconds of a rebound or steal counts double.

Easier: start the defender at the free throw line, so the read happens close to the basket. Harder: add a second defender who chases from behind.

## Coaching points

- "Chin, pivot, look, pass." Protects the rebound. A half-second look beats a rushed outlet.
- "Run wide." Wings stay near the sideline until the catch. Fixes crowding the ball.
- "Pass ahead." The ball moves faster than any dribbler. Three dribbles at most.
- "Decide early." By the free throw line the ball handler has passed, driven or shot.
- "I got the ball!" "I got the rim!" Defenders sprint back and say who takes what. The basket comes first.

## Common mistakes

| What you see | What to say |
|---|---|
| The rebounder throws without looking | "Chin and pivot first." Two hands on the ball, find the outlet, then pass |
| The outlet pass flies too far ahead | "Hit the runner in stride." Practice the outlet on its own first |
| Wings drift to the middle | Mark the wide lanes with cones or tape. Run outside the mark |
| One player dribbles the whole floor | "Three dribbles, then pass ahead." |
| Two runners in the same lane | Name the lanes — left, middle, right — and pick one before half court |
| The ball handler stops or picks up the ball early | "Attack the elbow." Make the defender choose, then pass or finish |
| The second attacker crowds the ball | "Stay wide." Give the defender two players to choose between |
| Layups missed at full speed | Take a little speed off. Five clean finishes beat ten rushed ones |
| Nobody gets back on defense | Praise the first player back, by name, every time |

## In a practice

- **Minutes:** One or two blocks of 10–12 from age 9. Younger teams: a short-court game of 5–8 minutes is enough.
- **Where:** The middle of practice, after ball handling and passing, and again in the closing game.
- **Pairs well with:** Rebounding (the outlet starts there), passing and finishing at speed. For younger teams it is also the conditioning.
- **Short lines:** Run in waves — one group attacks, the next gets ready — and rotate lanes so everyone rebounds, passes and finishes. With six or seven players, play 2-on-1 back and forth.
- **Half a gym:** Start the break at half court with a toss from the coach.
- **Never a consequence:** No sprints for a missed layup or a slow trip back. Keep live rounds short, with water breaks.
