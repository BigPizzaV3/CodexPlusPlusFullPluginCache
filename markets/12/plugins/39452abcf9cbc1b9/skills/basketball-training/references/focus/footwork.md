# Footwork

Footwork is how a player stops, turns and stays balanced. Shooting, passing, finishing and defense all start from the feet, and good feet mean fewer travel calls.

## By age

- **6–8:** Short and full of play. Teach the athletic stance — feet about shoulder-width apart, knees bent, hips back, chest up — and the jump stop — landing on both feet at once — through games, like red light, green light with a jump stop on red. A pivot is "a door that swings on its hinge." Skip the traveling rule's fine print.
- **9–11:** Prime years for clean habits. Add the stride stop — a one-two stop — and front and reverse pivots on both feet. Teach the triple threat position — holding the ball ready to shoot, pass or dribble — with simple decisions. Explain the traveling rule in plain words.
- **12–14:** After a growth spurt, quick feet can turn clumsy for a while. Be patient. Tie footwork to the game: feet set for catch-and-shoot, the jab step — a short, quick step at the defender — and the drop step for post players. Reward a fast decision on the catch over a long hold.
- **15–18:** Game speed, real situations: shooting on the move, post moves, the Euro step — a side-step gather around a defender — and closeouts on defense. Players pick their own weak-foot work and track it. Film a few reps so they see their own feet.

## Progression

1. **Alone:** On a line, no defender, no rush. Teach in order: stance, stop, pivot, triple threat. Jump stop from a small hop, then a jog, then a dribble. Pivot on the whistle, a quarter turn and then a half turn, on both feet.
2. **With a partner:** Catch a pass, jump stop, pivot to face the basket, pass back. Then catch, face up and look: the triple threat in three beats.
3. **Against a defender:** One player holds the ball and pivots to keep it away from a defender who may only tap at it. Count the taps, then swap.
4. **In a game:** Shout "Freeze!" in any drill or game and check stances. Ask for a decision on the catch: "Open? Shoot. Teammate open? Pass. Space in front? Drive."

Move on when most players get it right most of the time. If it falls apart, go back one step.

## Coaching points

- "Sit in a chair that isn't there." Athletic stance: fixes stiff legs and standing tall.
- "Land like a cat: quiet feet, both at once." Jump stop: two thumps means it wasn't one.
- "Pivot foot is glued, heel up." The pivot foot — the one that stays on the floor — spins on the ball of the foot without sliding.
- "Ball first, then feet." Starting a dribble, the ball leaves the hand before the pivot foot lifts. Fixes most travels.
- "Catch, face up, look." Triple threat is a quick decision, not a place to stand.

## Common mistakes

| What you see | What to say |
|---|---|
| A jump stop with two thumps | "Quiet feet, both at once." Start from a small hop and listen for one sound. |
| Stands up tall on the stop | "Land in your chair." Call a freeze and look at the knees. |
| Pivot foot slides | "Heel up, spin on the ball of your foot." Put tape on the floor as a target. |
| Travels on the first dribble | "Ball first, then feet." Rehearse in slow motion before full speed. |
| Wrong pivot foot after a stride stop | "First foot down is your pivot." After a jump stop, either foot works. |
| Turns their back to the basket every time | "Turn, then look." After a reverse pivot to protect the ball, front pivot to face up. |
| Holds the ball too long in triple threat | Count "one, two" aloud. The decision comes before "two." |
| Feet cross on defensive slides | "Don't let your feet kiss." Short side steps, wide base. |

## In a practice

- A focused ten minutes does more than a long footwork block: two minutes of demo and "Freeze!" checks, five of moving reps, three of a game. Shorter and more playful for 6–8.
- It works as the warm-up: a stance freeze game, then stops, then pivots.
- New team? One skill a week: the jump stop first, then pivots, then triple threat.
- Pairs well with everything: a shot needs balanced feet, and a pivot protects a pass.
- Keep lines short: spread the group out and let everyone move on your whistle. Pairs for catching and pivoting.
- Travels are part of learning. Give one cue, run it again, and praise good stops by name. Say what to do, not what to avoid: "Land on both feet."
- Traveling details differ by league: teach what your referees will call.
