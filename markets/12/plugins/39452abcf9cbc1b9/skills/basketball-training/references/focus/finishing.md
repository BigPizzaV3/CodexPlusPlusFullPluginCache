# Finishing

Finishing is scoring at the basket, and it starts with the layup: the shot young players take most in games, and the one that turns a drive or a fast break into points.

## By age

- **6–8:** Footwork with no ball and one-step layups from the block — the spot beside the lane, next to the basket — plus a little one-dribble work. Use a smaller ball if your league allows. Same-foot layups are fine. Keep it playful, with short turns.
- **9–11:** The years to make the layup automatic on both sides. Add one dribble, then two from the wing, then a chaser. Start the power layup: gather on two feet and go up strong off both. Count team makes, and make noise for every weak-hand attempt.
- **12–14:** New here: the reverse layup — under the rim to finish on the other side — and finishing through light contact. Coordination can dip while players grow: drop back a step without fuss. Make reps look like games: finish off a pass, off a cut, and on 2-on-1 and 3-on-2 breaks.
- **15–18:** The work is now choosing under pressure: hand, foot and finish. Add the Euro step — a side-step gather around a defender — and reads against help defense. Let players run their own reps.

## Progression

1. **Alone:** No ball first: walk the steps until the rhythm comes easily. For a right-hand layup, the last step is the left foot, the right knee lifts, and the hand reaches for the square on the backboard. Then one-step layups from the block with the ball. Then one dribble, then two from the wing. Speed comes last.
2. **With a partner:** Pairs on the Mikan drill — nonstop close layups, alternating hands. One finishes, one counts makes. Then layups off a pass, and two-line layups with a rebounder so everyone keeps moving.
3. **Against a defender:** A chaser: the finisher gets a head start and a defender runs after from behind. Keep the ball high and on the outside. Shorten the head start as players improve.
4. **In a game:** Simple 1-on-1 and 2-on-1 games. In scrimmages, make a layup worth an extra point so players look for the drive.

A player is ready for the next step when this one looks relaxed on both sides. Players at one basket can work on different steps. Add other types of layups one at a time, once the basic one is automatic on both sides.

## Coaching points

- "Opposite foot jumps." Right hand means left foot. The left side is the mirror: "same dance, other side."
- "Knee to the sky." The knee on the shooting-hand side lifts the player up to the rim.
- "Kiss the corner." Eyes find the top corner of the square before the jump. Soft off the glass.
- "High, not far." Stops the long jump past the rim. Land on two feet, under control.
- "Outside hip, then up." Keeps the ball away from the defender.

## Common mistakes

| What you see | What to say |
|---|---|
| Jumps off the same-side foot | Celebrate the make: it's a normal start. Then walk no-ball reps: "left foot, right knee." |
| Long-jumps under or past the rim | "High, not far." Slow down, and put a cone or tape where the jump starts. |
| Throws the ball hard off the glass | "Lay it, don't throw it." Move closer. |
| Looks at the ball or the floor | "Find the corner first." Go back to one-step layups. |
| Avoids the weak hand | Start a step closer and slower on that side. Mikan drill every practice. Praise every attempt. |
| Swings the ball low or toward the middle | "Outside hip, then up." A gentle chaser gives them a reason. |
| Takes extra steps after the dribble | Chant the rhythm together: "bounce, one, two, up." Walk first, then jog. |

## In a practice

- About 10 minutes of layups in most practices does more than one long layup day a month. Age changes the step you work on, not the habit.
- No-ball footwork and the Mikan drill make a good warm-up. End with a team count: how many makes in 60 seconds?
- Pairs well with footwork and ball handling: layups grow out of clean steps and a confident dribble.
- Keep lines short: groups of three on each side of every basket, and a left side at every station.
- Safe before fast: keep the speed down when a wall is close behind the basket, and have the rebounder wait until the ball is released.
- A miss is part of learning. Never answer one with running or push-ups. Count weak-hand makes double instead.
- A lower basket brings softer layups and more makes: 2.4 meters for 6–8 and 2.7 meters for 9–11 where you can.
