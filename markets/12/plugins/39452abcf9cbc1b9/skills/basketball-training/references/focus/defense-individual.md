# Individual defense

Individual defense is guarding one player, built in layers: stance, slides, guarding the ball, and the closeout — a sprint out to the player catching the ball, then short, choppy steps and a high hand. Each layer takes one clear cue and a few minutes, so a young team, and a less skilled player, can feel success fast.

## By age

- **6–8:** One job: "stay between your player and the basket." The defensive stance — lower and wider than the athletic stance: feet wider than the shoulders, knees bent, hips back, chest up — and slides, through mirror and tag games. A closeout is just "hands up, don't foul." Play 2-on-2 and 3-on-3 so everyone guards someone.
- **9–11:** The best age to build habits. On-ball defense with real footwork, and the full closeout technique: sprint, chop, high hand.
- **12–14:** When your player doesn't have the ball, stand a step off them toward the ball. Add defending simple screens. Footwork can turn clumsy for a while as players grow. Be patient and keep praising effort.
- **15–18:** Closeouts that read shooter or driver, and on-ball defense at game speed. Players lead the talk and help set defensive goals for each game.

## Progression

1. **Alone:** The stance in short holds on the whistle. Then the defensive slide — the lead foot steps, the back foot pushes, and the feet never cross.
2. **With a partner:** Mirror games: one player moves, the other copies. Then zig-zag slides against a dribbler at half speed.
3. **Against a ball handler:** 1-on-1 in a lane as wide as the paint. A stop is three dribbles without a score, or a dribbler who picks up the ball.
4. **In a small-sided game:** Close out to a player who may shoot or drive. Then 3-on-3 where a stop counts double.

## Coaching points

- "Sit in the chair." Knees bent, hips back, balance on the balls of the feet. Fixes a tall or leaning stance.
- "Push, don't cross." The back foot pushes the slide. Fixes crossed feet.
- "Watch the belly button." Heads and shoulders fake; the hips don't. Stay an arm's length away. Fixes falling for fakes.
- "Feet, not hands." One hand traces the ball while the feet do the work. Fixes reaching.
- "Sprint, chop, high hand." Fast out, short steps to stop, one hand up. Fixes jogging out and flying past.

## Common mistakes

| What you see | What to say |
|---|---|
| A defender stands up tall | "Stay under the ceiling." Shorten the reps: young legs tire fast. |
| Bending at the waist | "Hands over your head for a second." The chest comes up. |
| Feet cross on the slide | "Push, don't cross." |
| The dribbler gets a step ahead | "Turn and run, cut them off, then back in your stance." |
| Reaching for the ball | "Feet do the work." Praise the stop louder than the steal. |
| Jogging out to the shooter | "Sprint while the ball is in the air." |
| Flying past on the closeout | "Chop your feet." Put a cone two steps short of the shooter. |
| Beaten by one dribble after the closeout | "Stop a step shorter." For young players, a contested shot is a win. |
| Running under a shooter | "Stop in front, hand straight up." |
| The dribbler stops and the defender backs off | "Step up, both hands high. Make the pass hard." |

## In a practice

- **Minutes:** One layer, one cue, about 10 minutes, then straight into a game. Stance work comes in 30-second bursts. For ages 6–8, keep it all inside mirror and tag games. For 12–14 and 15–18, 1-on-1 and closeouts can run 10–15 minutes.
- **Where:** Early, while legs are fresh. A stance or mirror game works as the warm-up.
- **Pairs well with:** Footwork and agility, ball handling (the dribbler in 1-on-1 practices too), and team defense as the next layer.
- **Short lines:** Everyone works at once in pairs. Run 1-on-1 in several narrow lanes side by side.
- **Make it count:** Count stops out loud and name a stop of the day, a different player each time.
- **Never a consequence:** Slides and sprints are skills to be proud of. Keep them out of any penalty.
- **Charges:** Don't teach young players to take charges. They beat the driver to the spot with their feet and stay straight up.
