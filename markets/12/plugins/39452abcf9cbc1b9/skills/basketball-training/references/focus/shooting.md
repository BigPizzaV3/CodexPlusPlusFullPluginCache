# Shooting

Shooting is the skill kids most want to practice. Build it from the basket outward: first make good form possible with the right ball, a lower basket and a short distance, then turn it into a habit.

## By age

- **6–8:** Makes and fun come first. Use a size 5 ball or smaller and a 2.4-meter basket where you can. Shoot right under the rim, one hand when possible. A two-hand shot is fine while kids build strength: adjust the ball and the basket before the form.
- **9–11:** The prime years for building form. Teach BEEF (Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through) at short range, with a size 6 ball and a 2.7-meter basket where you can. Keep most shots inside 2.5 meters. Add catch-and-shoot — shooting straight off a pass — and simple shooting games.
- **12–14:** A growing body can throw a shot off for a while. When it does, return to close-range form. Add footwork into the shot, catch-and-shoot from both wings and mid-range shots off one dribble.
- **15–18:** Shoot at game speed: off the pass, off the dribble, when tired. Use BEEF as a quick warm-up check, then coach rhythm, footwork and shot selection. The shot belongs to the player: help them build their own routine.

## Progression

1. **Alone:** One hand, under the rim: ball on the finger pads, elbow under the ball, shoot up and in. Hold the follow-through — arm up, wrist snapped — until the ball lands. Next, a step or two back, add the legs. Then add the guide hand on the side of the ball. Then put BEEF together from 1–2.5 meters.
2. **With a partner:** Catch-and-shoot: catch with feet set and the ball in the shot pocket — between waist and chest on the shooting-hand side — then shoot. Partners also watch each other's elbow and follow-through.
3. **Against a defender:** The passer closes out — runs at the shooter with a hand up — at walking pace, without trying to block. Form slips at first. Step closer and build it back.
4. **In a game:** Shooting games, team challenges and small-sided games, with a bonus point for a held follow-through.

Add distance only while the shot still looks clean. Expect beginners to spend several weeks on step 1.

## Coaching points

- "Sit in the chair, then jump out of it." Balance: the legs power the shot, so the arms don't have to throw.
- "Eyes on the rim, not the ball." Eyes: pick the target early and keep the head still.
- "Elbow points at the rim." Elbow: the shot goes straight, so misses stop going left and right.
- "Reach into the cookie jar and hold it." Follow-through: a full finish on every shot.
- "Shoot up, not out." Arc: a high, soft shot in place of a flat one.

## Common mistakes

| What you see | What to say |
|---|---|
| A push shot — the ball shoved from the chest with both hands | "Come closer." One-hand shots at the rim, starting from the shot pocket. A smaller ball or a lower basket helps. |
| Elbow sticks out; misses go left or right | "Elbow points at the rim." Shoot one-handed, standing sideways next to a wall. |
| Guide hand pushes; the ball spins sideways | "Guide hand waves goodbye." One hand first, then bring the guide hand back. |
| A flat shot off the back of the rim | "Shoot up, not out." Shoot over a partner's raised arm from close in. |
| Falls forward or jumps sideways | "Land where you took off." Mark the spot with tape. |
| Eyes follow the ball | "Eyes on the rim until it lands." Call "in" or "out" before looking down. |
| Ball rests on the palm | "Finger pads." Leave a gap you can see between palm and ball. |

## In a practice

- Short and often wins: about 10 minutes of form shooting close to the basket in most practices, not one long session a week. Shorter and closer for 6–8; add game-speed shots for 12–14 and 15–18.
- Put form work early, and end with a shooting game.
- Pairs well with footwork (a balanced stop before the shot) and passing (catch-and-shoot starts with the pass).
- Keep lines short: one ball per player if you can, pairs at every basket, ten makes and switch.
- Few baskets? Use a wall, or aim at the square on the backboard, which also teaches the bank shot — a shot off the backboard.
- Give one cue per rep. When you see it done, say so right away. Once the form is there, cue the target more than the body: "over the front of the rim."
- Reward form as well as makes. Never laugh at an unusual shot or rebuild it in front of the team. Correct quietly, never right before a game.
