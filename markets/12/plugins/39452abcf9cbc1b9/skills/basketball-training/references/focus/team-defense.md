# Team defense

Team defense is what the players away from the ball do: see the ball and their own player, move on every pass, and play help defense — leaving their own player for a moment to stop a drive. Young teams learn it in man-to-man defense, where every player guards one opponent. A zone, where each defender guards an area, comes later.

## By age

- **6–8:** Man-to-man only, kept simple: find your player, stay between them and the basket, hands up. Help can wait: a young player told to help mostly just leaves their player. Play 3-on-3 or smaller.
- **9–11:** A first taste of help: "see the ball and your player". Add jumping to the ball — every defender moves while a pass is in the air. Use the shell drill — offense around the 3-point line, defense shifting on every pass — at 2-on-2 and 3-on-3. No zone.
- **12–14:** Clear help triggers, recovery, and the first defensive rotation — the next defender covers the player the helper left. The 4-on-4 shell. Once man-to-man is solid and the league allows it, one simple zone can come in as a second defense.
- **15–18:** Full team rules: rotations, game situations and pick-and-roll coverages — the team's ways to guard a ball screen. Man-to-man stays the base. Players learn a zone and its weak spots, and lead the talk.

## Progression

1. **Off the ball:** A coach passes around the perimeter. Each defender points one hand at the ball and one at their player. One pass away: close, a hand in the passing lane. Two passes away, on the weak side — the side away from the ball — sag toward the middle.
2. **On every pass:** The shell at 2-on-2 and 3-on-3, passing only. Every defender moves while the ball is in the air, not after the catch.
3. **Against a drive:** The offense may drive once. The nearest defender steps in when the dribbler gets a foot in the paint, then recovers to their own player with a closeout — a sprint, short choppy steps and a high hand.
4. **In a small-sided game:** The shell drill live at 4-on-4, with a point for every stop. Everyone plays every spot.

## Coaching points

- "Point at the ball and your player." Can't see both? Step toward the ball. Fixes ball watching.
- "Move on the pass." Jump to the ball while it is in the air. Fixes late help.
- "Foot in the paint." The trigger to help, and not before. Fixes helping too early.
- "Be a wall." Wide stance, hands straight up. Fixes swiping at the driver.
- "Ball. Help. I've got you." Three calls. Praise every player who uses them.

## Common mistakes

| What you see | What to say |
|---|---|
| Eyes only on the ball | Freeze the drill: "Can you point at both?" |
| Standing still when the ball is passed | "Move on the pass." |
| Hugging their own player on the weak side | "Sag to the middle. You are the help." |
| Helping too early | "Wait for the foot in the paint." |
| Helping, then staying in the paint | "Help, then sprint back to yours." |
| Nobody covers the helper's player (12 and up) | "Next one over: who has the helper's player?" |
| Nobody talks | "Three words: ball, help, I've got you." |
| Standing in a spot in a zone | "Guard the ball in your area. Move on the pass." |
| Nobody boxes out in a zone | "Find a player first, then the ball." |

## In a practice

- **Minutes:** No separate block for ages 6–8. 8–10 minutes for 9–11, 12–15 for 12–14, and up to 20 for 15–18.
- **Where:** After individual defense, leading into the closing game. Count stops there.
- **Pairs well with:** Closeouts, rebounding (everyone finds a player on the shot) and team offense: one group attacks while the other defends.
- **Short lines:** Run the shell in groups of four to eight. Swap offense and defense often. Freeze and ask instead of long walk-throughs.
- **Zones:** Man-to-man first. Many youth leagues don't allow a zone up to age 11: check the league's rule. A team that plays a zone in games still spends most of its defensive practice time on man-to-man.
- **Facing a zone:** Give the offense a few minutes against one: find a gap, pass and cut, keep moving. Older teams add a player in the middle and the skip pass — a pass across the court, over the defense.
