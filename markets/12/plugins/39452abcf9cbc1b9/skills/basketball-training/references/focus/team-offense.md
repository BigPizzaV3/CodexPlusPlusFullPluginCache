# Team offense

Team offense is how five players share the floor: spread out, move without the ball, find the open teammate. For young players, spacing and cutting come before plays, and the habits carry into every offense they run later.

## By age

- **6–8:** No plays. "Spread out" and "pass, then go", with cones or tape for spots and no defender at first. The give-and-go — pass, cut to the basket, get the ball back — is the only team play they need.
- **9–11:** Spots about 3.5–4.5 meters apart. Add the V-cut, the backdoor cut and the idea of a legal screen. One simple set and one inbounds play are plenty: a 5-out shape — all five spread around the 3-point line — or 1-4 high — a guard on top, four teammates in a line at the free throw line. Everybody plays every spot.
- **12–14:** Read the defender on every catch. Add screening away, the down screen — set toward the baseline for a teammate who cuts up to the ball — and moving when a teammate drives. Two or three sets, all built on the same habits.
- **15–18:** Motion rules plus a few named actions that fit the players, such as the pick-and-roll — a screen for the ball handler. Players cut, fill and relocate without being told, and explain their own reads.

## Progression

1. **No defense:** Five players on five spots: top, two wings, two corners. Pass, cut to the basket, fill the empty spot. A few minutes is enough.
2. **With a guide defender:** One cut at a time. The defender plays at half speed and overplays on purpose, so the cutter learns when to go backdoor.
3. **Live, with a rule:** 3-on-3 where a dribble is a turnover, or "cut after every pass". Freeze once and ask, "Where's the open space?"
4. **In a game:** 4-on-4 or 5-on-5 where a basket off a cut or a give-and-go counts double.

Move on when most of the group is ready, not when everyone is perfect.

## Coaching points

- "Too close for a high-five? Move." Fixes crowding around the ball.
- "Pass, then go." The passer cuts hard. Fixes standing and watching.
- "Fill the empty spot." The passer always has a target after a cut.
- "Walk in, plant, pop out." The V-cut — toward the basket, then back out to the wing — works on the change of speed.
- "Too close? Go behind!" The backdoor cut — behind a defender who overplays the pass.

## Common mistakes

| What you see | What to say |
|---|---|
| Everyone runs to the ball | "Spots!" Mark five spots and check the spacing on a freeze |
| Nobody moves after a pass | "Pass, then go." Someone cuts, fills or screens within two seconds |
| The cutter stops under the basket | "No ball? Keep going." Cut through to the open corner or wing |
| The cut starts before the passer is ready | "Cut when the passer looks at you." |
| A jogged cut | "Slow, then explode." Praise the burst, not only the catch |
| Two players cut to the same spot | "One cutter at a time. Everyone else fills." |
| A push to get open | "Get open with your feet, not your hands." |
| Teammates stand in the driver's way | "Driven at you? Move away. Driven away from you? Follow." |
| The same two players have the ball | Rotate who brings it up. A pass to a cutter earns a point |

## In a practice

- **Minutes:** Blocks of 8–12 for ages 9–11, with the small-sided game the longest. Shorter and more playful for 6–8, a little longer from 12.
- **Where:** After the warm-up and ball skills. Build from no defense to 3-on-3, and let the closing game reward the same habit.
- **Pairs well with:** Passing, footwork (the plant on a cut, the catch ready to shoot, pass or dribble) and finishing off a cut.
- **Short lines:** Three or four players per basket, from both sides of the floor. Keep the space under the hoop clear.
- **One thing at a time:** One principle per practice, and pass and cut comes first. If a group can't get open, take the defender out for a few reps.
- **Celebrate the cut:** Even when the pass never comes. It is how the player who rarely gets the ball joins the offense.
