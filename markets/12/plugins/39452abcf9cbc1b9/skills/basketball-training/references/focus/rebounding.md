# Rebounding

Rebounding is a habit any player can learn, whatever their size: expect every shot to miss, find a player, go get the ball. Each rebound gives the team one more turn with the ball.

## By age

- **6–8:** Make it a game: everyone calls "shot!", then races to the ball. Teach "find a body" and a gentle turn, with light contact only. Cheer two-hand catches.
- **9–11:** Teach the box out — getting between your player and the basket and holding that spot — in four steps: see, find, hit, get. One pivot, taught well. Then chin the ball (two hands, under the chin, elbows out) and make the outlet pass, the first pass after a rebound.
- **12–14:** Both pivots, rebounding on the weak side — the side away from the ball, opposite the shot — and offensive rebounding around a box out. Everyone finds someone on the shot.
- **15–18:** A team standard: all five box out, then the rebound and the outlet pass start the fast break. Players track team rebounds in practice games and set their own goals.

## Progression

1. **Alone:** Toss the ball off the backboard or a wall. Catch it with two hands at its highest point, land on two feet and chin it. Count clean catches.
2. **With a partner:** On "shot!", find your partner, turn and make light contact. Walk it, then jog it. Hold for a count of two, then switch.
3. **Against a defender:** Live 1-on-1: the coach shoots, the defender boxes out, and both players go for the ball. Then several pairs at once around the lane. Short rounds, many turns.
4. **In a game:** Add the outlet pass after the rebound. Then play 3-on-3 with a bonus point for a box-out rebound.

## Coaching points

- "Shot!" Everyone calls it on the release. Fixes standing and watching the ball.
- "Find a body." Eyes go to your player first, then to the ball.
- "Butt on gut." Low hips, wide base, back and hips on your player. Fixes pushing with the hands.
- "Two hands, chin it." Catch high, land on two feet. Fixes rebounds that get knocked loose.
- "Pivot and outlet." Turn toward the sideline and find a guard. Turns a rebound into a fast break.

## Common mistakes

| What you see | What to say |
|---|---|
| Players watch the shot | "Call it: shot!" |
| A player gets pushed under the basket | "Meet your player early, a step or two out." |
| Pushing with the hands | "Arms up, hands showing. Back and hips do the work." |
| Turning for the ball too early | "Hold until the ball hits the rim, then go." |
| One-hand grabs | "Two hands, then chin it." |
| Swinging elbows after the catch | "Elbows wide and still." |
| Standing under the rim on a long shot | "Long shot, long rebound." |
| The shooting team stands still | "Expect a miss. First step to the basket." |
| A dribble after an offensive rebound at the rim | "Straight back up." That is a putback — scoring right off the rebound, no dribble. |
| A good box out, and a teammate gets the ball | "You held your player off. That rebound is yours too." |

## In a practice

- **Minutes:** About 5 minutes for ages 6–8, as a game. 8–10 for 9–11, and 10–12 for 12–14 and 15–18. Keep contact rounds short.
- **Where:** After the warm-up and before the closing game, so the bonus-point rule has something to build on.
- **Pairs well with:** Footwork (the same pivots), passing (the outlet pass), transition, and team defense: everyone finds someone on the shot.
- **Short lines:** Pairs spread around the lane, all moving on the same "shot!" call. Never more than three players in a line.
- **Team jobs:** Players near the basket box out, then go get the ball. Players on the perimeter box out, then step toward the free throw line for the long rebound.
- **Contact:** "Firm, not rough." Match partners by size and strength, most of all at 12–14, when players grow at different speeds.
- **Praise:** Cheer effort on the boards, with or without the ball. A rebounding drill is never a consequence.
