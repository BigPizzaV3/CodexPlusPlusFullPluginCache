# FIDIC 2017 Contract Reference

Detailed clause reference for the FIDIC Conditions of Contract (Second Edition, 2017). This document covers the core clauses most relevant to commercial management. References are to the Red Book unless otherwise noted.

> **Note:** This reference covers the standard FIDIC 2017 form. Particular Conditions, special provisions, and employer-specific amendments may modify these provisions. Always check the specific contract.

> **Provenance:** clause citations verified 2026-07 against FIDIC 2017 secondary commentary from named firms and practitioner sources (e.g. Howard Kennedy's clause-by-clause commentary on Clause 14, CMS's guide to the FIDIC 2017 suite, Fenwick Elliott). The exact lettered sub-paragraph of sub-clause 14.6 governing withholding for a Contractor's failure to submit the programme could not be pinned down from available secondary sources, so that citation remains hedged in the text. ## Clause 8: Commencement, Delays, and Suspension

### Sub-Clause 8.1 — Commencement of Works

The Contractor must commence the Works within 42 days after receiving the Letter of Acceptance (or as stated).

### Sub-Clause 8.3 — Programme

The Contractor must submit a programme within **28 days** of the Commencement Date. The programme must show:
- The order of work, including contractor's equipment, temporary works, and specified methods
- Each stage of design, Contractor's Documents, procurement, manufacture, inspection, delivery, erection, testing, and commissioning
- The supporting report describing the methods, staging, and resources

The Engineer may give notice that the programme does not comply. If the Contractor does not submit a compliant programme, the Engineer may withhold interim payments.

### Sub-Clause 8.4 — Advance Warning (2017)

Each Party must advise the other Party and the Engineer in advance of any known or probable future events or circumstances which may adversely affect the work, increase the Contract Price, or delay the execution of the Works — the FIDIC analogue of NEC's early warning.

**Renumbering trap:** in the 1999 edition, sub-clause 8.4 is the EOT clause; in 2017 EOT moved to 8.5. There is no 1999 equivalent of advance warning.

### Sub-Clause 8.5 — Extension of Time for Completion (2017) / 8.4 (1999)

The Contractor is entitled to an extension if completion is delayed by:
- A Variation (unless an adjustment to the Time for Completion has already been agreed)
- A cause of delay giving entitlement under any sub-clause
- Exceptionally adverse climatic conditions
- Unforeseeable shortages caused by epidemic or governmental actions
- Any delay, impediment, or prevention caused by the Employer

The Contractor must give notice under clause 20.2.1 (28 days) and submit supporting analysis (the detailed claim with supporting programme analysis). The Engineer assesses the entitlement, determines a fair extension, and notifies the determination under clause 3.7.

Key points:
- The Contractor is **not entitled** to EOT for delays caused by the Contractor's own default, subcontractor delays (unless caused by an Employer risk event), or delays covered by the Contractor's float.
- Float in the programme is generally considered to belong to the project, not either party. This is an area of ongoing debate in FIDIC jurisprudence.
- Concurrent delay: where employer delay and contractor delay overlap, the prevailing approach (following the Malmaison principle) is that the contractor is still entitled to EOT for the employer's delay.

### Sub-Clause 8.8 — Delay Damages (2017) / 8.7 (1999)

If the Contractor fails to complete by the Time for Completion, the Contractor pays delay damages at the rate stated in the Contract Data.

The Employer may deduct delay damages from amounts otherwise due. The Employer must give notice before deducting.

Maximum delay damages are typically capped (stated in the Contract Data).

### Sub-Clause 8.10 — Consequences of Employer's Suspension (2017) / 8.9 (1999)

If the Engineer instructs suspension under sub-clause 8.9 (Employer's Suspension; 8.8 in the 1999 edition), and the suspension is not caused by the Contractor's fault, the Contractor is entitled to:
- An extension of time
- Payment of **Cost only — no profit** (in both the 1999 and 2017 editions)

If the suspension continues for more than 84 days (Prolonged Suspension — sub-clause 8.12 in 2017; 8.11 in 1999), the Contractor may request permission to proceed. If permission is not granted within 28 days, the Contractor may treat the suspension as an omission (if it affects only part of the works) or give notice of termination.

## Clause 15: Termination by the Employer

The Employer may terminate if:
- The Contractor fails to comply with a notice under sub-clause 15.1
- The Contractor abandons the works
- The Contractor subcontracts the whole of the works without consent
- The Contractor becomes bankrupt or insolvent

The Employer gives 14 days' notice before termination takes effect.

---

## Clause 16: Suspension and Termination by the Contractor

The Contractor may suspend work if:
- The Engineer fails to issue an IPC
- The Employer fails to provide evidence of financial arrangements
- The Employer fails to pay amounts due

The Contractor must give **21 days'** notice before suspending.

The Contractor may terminate if:
- The Employer fails to pay within 42 days of the payment due date
- The Employer substantially fails to perform their obligations
- Prolonged suspension (more than 84 days)
- The Employer becomes bankrupt or insolvent

## Clause 17: Care of the Works and Indemnities (2017)

The 2017 edition restructured this clause. The 1999 headings ("17.2 Contractor's Care of the Works", "17.3 Employer's Risks") no longer exist — the 1999 "Employer's Risks" concept is now dealt with through the exceptions in sub-clause 17.2 and the Exceptional Events regime in Clause 18.

### Sub-Clause 17.1 — Responsibility for Care of the Works

The Contractor is responsible for the care of the Works and Goods from the Commencement Date until the Taking-Over Certificate is issued, when responsibility passes to the Employer.

### Sub-Clause 17.2 — Liability for Care of the Works

If loss or damage occurs during the Contractor's period of responsibility, the Contractor must rectify it at its own risk and cost — except to the extent the loss or damage is caused by listed events for which the Contractor is not liable (e.g. use or occupation of the works by the Employer, design of the works by the Employer, and events of the kind now covered by Clause 18).

**1999 comparison:** under 1999 clause 17.4, rectifying loss or damage from an Employer's Risk entitled the Contractor to Cost, with profit added **only for certain of the Employer's Risks** (use or occupation of the works by the Employer, and design by the Employer's Personnel) — not all of them.

## Clause 18: Exceptional Events (2017)

Replaces 1999 Clause 19 (Force Majeure). An **Exceptional Event** is an event or circumstance which:
- is beyond a Party's control;
- the Party could not reasonably have provided against before entering into the Contract;
- the Party could not reasonably have avoided or overcome once it arose; and
- is not substantially attributable to the other Party.

**Notice:** the affected Party must give Notice within **14 days** of becoming aware (or when it should have become aware) of the Exceptional Event.

**Relief:** the affected Party is excused performance of the prevented obligations, and the Contractor is entitled to EOT. Cost is recoverable only for the listed sub-set of events (war, hostilities, rebellion, terrorism, etc.) occurring in the Country.

**Termination:** either party may terminate after prolonged prevention — where execution of substantially all the Works is prevented for **84 continuous days** or **140 days in aggregate**.
