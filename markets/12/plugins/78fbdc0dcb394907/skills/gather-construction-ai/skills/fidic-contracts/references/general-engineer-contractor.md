# FIDIC 2017 Contract Reference

Detailed clause reference for the FIDIC Conditions of Contract (Second Edition, 2017). This document covers the core clauses most relevant to commercial management. References are to the Red Book unless otherwise noted.

> **Note:** This reference covers the standard FIDIC 2017 form. Particular Conditions, special provisions, and employer-specific amendments may modify these provisions. Always check the specific contract.

> **Provenance:** clause citations verified 2026-07 against FIDIC 2017 secondary commentary from named firms and practitioner sources (e.g. Howard Kennedy's clause-by-clause commentary on Clause 14, CMS's guide to the FIDIC 2017 suite, Fenwick Elliott). The exact lettered sub-paragraph of sub-clause 14.6 governing withholding for a Contractor's failure to submit the programme could not be pinned down from available secondary sources, so that citation remains hedged in the text. Report suspected errors via the repository issue tracker.

## Clause 1: General Provisions

### Sub-Clause 1.1 — Definitions

Key defined terms:
- **Contract** — the Contract Agreement, the Letter of Acceptance, the Letter of Tender, the Particular Conditions, the General Conditions, the Employer's Requirements (Yellow/Silver) or Specification (Red), Drawings, Schedules, and further documents listed in the Contract Agreement.
- **Cost** — all expenditure reasonably incurred (or to be incurred) by the Contractor, whether on or off the Site, including overhead and similar charges, but does not include profit.
- **Time for Completion** — the time for completing the Works stated in the Contract Data, calculated from the Commencement Date.
- **Unforeseeable** — not reasonably foreseeable by an experienced contractor by the date for submission of the Tender.
- **Variation** — any change to the Works instructed or approved as a Variation under clause 13.

### Sub-Clause 1.3 — Communications

All communications under the contract must be in writing. Oral instructions are not binding unless confirmed in writing.

### Sub-Clause 1.5 — Priority of Documents

In the event of ambiguity or discrepancy, the priority is:
1. The Contract Agreement
2. The Letter of Acceptance
3. The Letter of Tender
4. The Particular Conditions (Part A and Part B)
5. The General Conditions
6. The Employer's Requirements / Specification
7. The Drawings
8. The Schedules and any other documents forming part of the Contract

## Clause 3: The Engineer

### Sub-Clause 3.1 — The Engineer's Duties and Authority

The Engineer carries out the duties assigned in the Contract. The Engineer has no authority to amend the Contract.

### Sub-Clause 3.5 — Engineer's Instructions

The Engineer may issue instructions necessary for the execution of the Works. The Contractor must comply, provided the instruction is within the Engineer's authority.

If the Contractor considers an instruction constitutes a Variation, the Contractor must give notice under clause 13.

### Sub-Clause 3.7 — Agreement or Determination (2017)

This is a significant change from the 1999 edition. When the Engineer must make a determination:

1. The Engineer first consults with both parties to try to reach agreement.
2. If agreement is reached within the time limit, the Engineer records the agreement.
3. If no agreement, the Engineer makes a **fair determination** taking due regard of all relevant circumstances.
4. Time limits (sub-clause 3.7.3): the Engineer has **42 days** to reach agreement between the parties, failing which a further **42 days** to issue the determination.
5. If no notice of determination is issued within the time limit, the Engineer is **deemed to have given a determination rejecting the Claim** — opening the route to the DAAB.

**Commercial significance:** Under the 2017 edition, the Engineer must first attempt agreement before making a determination. This gives both parties a structured negotiation period. Under the 1999 edition, the Engineer simply made the determination.

## Clause 4: The Contractor

### Sub-Clause 4.1 — Contractor's General Obligations

The Contractor shall design (to the extent stated), execute, and complete the Works in accordance with the Contract, and shall remedy any defects in the Works.

### Sub-Clause 4.12 — Unforeseeable Physical Conditions

**Red Book and Yellow Book:**

If the Contractor encounters physical conditions which are Unforeseeable, the Contractor shall give notice **as soon as practicable**. The notice must describe the conditions. The **28-day** clock for the Notice of Claim runs separately under sub-clause 20.2.1.

The Contractor is entitled to:
- An extension of the Time for Completion (sub-clause 8.5)
- Payment of **Cost only — no profit** (in both the 1999 and 2017 editions)

The Engineer must inspect and make a determination. The Engineer may take into account whether the Contractor has taken appropriate measures to overcome the conditions, and whether the conditions were foreseeable. If the Engineer determines the conditions were foreseeable, no additional payment is due.

**Silver Book:** the Red/Yellow relief is **not available**. The Silver Book replaces sub-clause 4.12 with "Unforeseeable Difficulties": the Contractor is deemed to have foreseen all difficulties and costs, and the Contract Price is not adjusted for them. The Contractor accepts the risk of all physical conditions.

**Commercial significance:** Clause 4.12 is one of the most litigated FIDIC provisions. The test is whether an "experienced contractor" would have foreseen the conditions at tender stage, based on data reasonably available. Site Information provided by the Employer is relevant but does not limit the assessment.

### Sub-Clause 4.20 — Progress Reports

The Contractor must submit monthly progress reports including:
- Charts and detailed descriptions of progress
- Photographs
- Details of Contractor's personnel and equipment
- Quality assurance records
- Safety statistics
- Comparisons of actual and planned progress
- Details of any events affecting or likely to affect progress

## Clause 10: Taking-Over

### Sub-Clause 10.1 — Taking-Over of the Works

When the Works are completed in accordance with the Contract, the Contractor may apply for a Taking-Over Certificate. The Engineer must either:
- Issue the Taking-Over Certificate within **28 days**, or
- Reject the application with reasons, specifying what work remains to be done

**Commercial significance:** Taking-Over is equivalent to NEC's Completion and JCT's Practical Completion. It triggers:
1. Release of first half of retention
2. Start of the Defects Notification Period
3. Transfer of risk for care of the works
4. End of delay damages liability

## Key Terminology Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Employer** | The party who commissions the works. Named in the Contract Agreement. Equivalent of NEC's Client and JCT's Employer. |
| **Contractor** | The party who carries out the works. |
| **Engineer** | The person named in the Contract Data who administers the contract on behalf of the Employer. Has authority to issue instructions, grant EOTs, and make determinations. Equivalent of NEC's Project Manager and JCT's Architect/CA. Present in Red Book and Yellow Book — in Silver Book, the Employer administers directly. |
| **Employer's Representative** | In the Silver Book, the person who acts for the Employer (since there is no independent Engineer). |
| **DAB / DAAB** | Dispute Adjudication Board (1999) / Dispute Avoidance/Adjudication Board (2017). A standing board that resolves disputes. Equivalent of NEC's adjudicator under W1/W2. |
| **Variation** | A change to the Works instructed by the Engineer under clause 13. Includes changes in quantities, quality, form, character, kind, position, dimension, level, sequence, timing, or method. |
| **Claim** | Under FIDIC, a claim is a formal request by either party for additional payment or extension of time under clause 20. Unlike NEC (where "claim" is incorrect terminology), FIDIC uses "claim" as the standard contractual term. |
| **Time for Completion** | The period stated in the Contract Data within which the works must be completed. Equivalent of NEC's Completion Date and JCT's Date for Completion. |
| **Taking-Over** | When the Employer takes over the works (or a section). Equivalent of NEC's Completion and JCT's Practical Completion. |
| **Defects Notification Period (DNP)** | The period after Taking-Over during which the Contractor must remedy defects. Called Defects Liability Period in 1999 edition. Equivalent of NEC's defect correction period and JCT's Defects Liability Period. |
| **Contract Price** | The agreed price stated in the Contract Agreement, subject to adjustment. Equivalent of NEC's Prices and JCT's Contract Sum. |
| **Interim Payment Certificate (IPC)** | The Engineer's periodic certification of amounts due to the Contractor. |
| **Statement at Completion** | The Contractor's statement submitted within 84 days of Taking-Over, showing the final value. |
| **Final Statement** | The Contractor's final account submission — the draft is submitted within 56 days after issue of the Performance Certificate (which itself follows expiry of the last DNP). |
| **Cost** | Under FIDIC, "Cost" means all expenditure reasonably incurred (or to be incurred) by the Contractor, including overhead and similar charges but not profit. |
| **Cost Plus Profit** | Cost plus profit. The basis of recovery only where the specific clause expressly says so — typically Employer-caused events (e.g. 1.9 delayed drawings, 2.1 right of access). Many claim clauses (e.g. 4.12, suspension) entitle Cost only. In the 2017 edition the profit element is 5% unless a different rate is stated in the Contract Data. |
| **Provisional Sum** | An amount included in the Contract Price for work or services not fully defined at tender. The Engineer instructs the expenditure. |
| **Delay Damages** | Pre-agreed damages payable by the Contractor for late completion. Stated in the Contract Data. Equivalent of NEC's delay damages and JCT's LADs. |
| **Performance Security** | A bond or guarantee provided by the Contractor to secure performance. Typically 10% of the Contract Price. |
| **Retention Money** | A percentage (typically 5%) retained from interim payments as security. Half released at Taking-Over, remainder at DNP expiry. |

## FIDIC Books Comparison

| Feature | Red Book (Construction) | Yellow Book (Plant & Design-Build) | Silver Book (EPC/Turnkey) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Design responsibility** | Employer provides design | Contractor designs and builds to Employer's Requirements | Contractor takes full design, construction, and completion responsibility |
| **Contract administrator** | Engineer (independent) | Engineer (independent) | Employer's Representative (not independent) |
| **Pricing basis** | Bill of Quantities (re-measurement) | Lump sum | Lump sum |
| **Risk allocation** | Balanced | Contractor takes more design risk | Maximum risk on Contractor |
| **Unforeseeable conditions** | Clause 4.12 applies | Clause 4.12 applies | **No relief** — 4.12 becomes "Unforeseeable Difficulties"; Contractor accepts all sub-surface risk |
| **Engineer's determination** | Yes (clause 3.7) | Yes (clause 3.7) | No independent Engineer |
| **Typical use** | Traditional construction, civil engineering | Process plant, industrial, some building | Power stations, BOT, major infrastructure PPPs |
