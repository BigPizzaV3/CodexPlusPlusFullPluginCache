# FIDIC 2017 Contract Reference

Detailed clause reference for the FIDIC Conditions of Contract (Second Edition, 2017). This document covers the core clauses most relevant to commercial management. References are to the Red Book unless otherwise noted.

> **Note:** This reference covers the standard FIDIC 2017 form. Particular Conditions, special provisions, and employer-specific amendments may modify these provisions. Always check the specific contract.

> **Provenance:** clause citations verified 2026-07 against FIDIC 2017 secondary commentary from named firms and practitioner sources (e.g. Howard Kennedy's clause-by-clause commentary on Clause 14, CMS's guide to the FIDIC 2017 suite, Fenwick Elliott). The exact lettered sub-paragraph of sub-clause 14.6 governing withholding for a Contractor's failure to submit the programme could not be pinned down from available secondary sources, so that citation remains hedged in the text. ## Common Pitfalls

1. **Missing the 28-day notice deadline** — this is a condition precedent. No notice = no claim. The most common and most costly mistake in FIDIC contracts.
2. **Not submitting the fully detailed claim within 84 days** — the 2017 edition extended this period from 42 days (1999), but missing it means the Notice of Claim is deemed to have lapsed (subject to 20.2.5).
3. **Confusing Cost with Cost Plus Profit** — some clauses entitle Cost only, others Cost Plus Profit. Read the specific clause carefully.
4. **Assuming Silver Book has the same protections as Red Book** — clause 4.12 gives no relief in the Silver Book (it is replaced by "Unforeseeable Difficulties", allocating the risk to the Contractor). The Contractor accepts vastly more risk.
5. **Not maintaining a standing DAAB** — under the 2017 edition, failure to constitute the DAAB can create procedural difficulties for dispute resolution.
6. **Not submitting monthly interim claims** — both editions require interim claims at monthly intervals for continuing events (20.2.6 in 2017). Failure to update can weaken the claim.
7. **Relying on oral instructions** — all instructions must be confirmed in writing. Oral instructions are not binding.
