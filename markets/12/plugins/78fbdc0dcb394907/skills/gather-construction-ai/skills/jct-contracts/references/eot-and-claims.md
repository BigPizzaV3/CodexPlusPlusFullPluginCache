# JCT Standard Building Contract 2016 — Clause Reference

Detailed clause reference for the JCT Standard Building Contract (SBC) 2016. This document covers the core clauses most relevant to commercial management and quantity surveying.

> **Note:** This reference covers the standard form SBC 2016. JCT contracts are almost always amended by a schedule of amendments, which frequently inserts condition-precedent notice regimes — always check the contract as executed. JCT 2024 editions are now published — confirm which edition applies before citing clauses; see workflow.md for the Edition Differences (2016 → 2024) table. This document is contract-administration guidance, not legal advice.

> **Provenance:** clause citations verified 2026-07 against the official JCT SBC/DB/ICD/MW 2016 contents pages (jctltd.co.uk) and published commentary/case law, including named-firm and practitioner commentary (e.g. Ramskill Martin's "Back to Basics" series on Schedule 2 quotations and provisional sums, Fenwick Elliott, and contract-consultants.com) cross-checking Schedule 2 timescales, clause 4.22.1's exclusion of defined-work provisional sums, and clauses 2.28.5/2.28.6.1. Items that could only be verified against secondary sources are hedged in the text. Report suspected errors via the repository issue tracker.

## Section 2: Carrying Out the Works

### Clause 2.1 — Contractor's Obligations

The Contractor shall carry out and complete the Works in a proper and workmanlike manner and in compliance with the Contract Documents, the CDM Regulations, and Statutory Requirements.

### Clause 2.3 — Materials and Workmanship

Materials, goods, and workmanship shall be of the kinds and standards described in the Contract Bills or the Specification. Where described as being to the Architect/CA's approval, they shall be to their reasonable satisfaction.

### Clause 2.4 — Date of Possession

On the Date of Possession, the Employer shall give the Contractor possession of the site.

**2.5** (Where deferment of possession applies): The Employer may defer giving possession for a period not exceeding 6 weeks (or such lesser period as stated) from the Date of Possession.

**Commercial significance:** Deferment of possession is a Relevant Event (clause 2.29.3), and its cost consequences are recoverable as an impediment/prevention/default Relevant Matter (clause 4.22.5). If the contract does not include the deferment provision and the Employer is late, it may constitute a breach of contract.

### Clauses 2.27-2.29 — Extensions of Time

**2.27.1** If and whenever it becomes reasonably apparent that the progress of the Works or any Section is being or is likely to be delayed, the Contractor shall forthwith give written notice to the Architect/CA of the material circumstances, including the cause or causes of the delay, and shall identify in the notice any event which in his opinion is a Relevant Event.

**2.27.2** In respect of each Relevant Event identified in the notice, the Contractor shall, if practicable in such notice or otherwise in writing as soon as possible after the notice, give particulars of the expected effects, including an estimate of the expected delay in the completion of the Works beyond the Completion Date.

**2.27.3** The Contractor shall give such further written notices as may be reasonably necessary for keeping up to date the information in the notices, particulars, and estimates.

**2.28.1** If, in the opinion of the Architect/CA, on receipt of any notice, particulars, and estimate, the completion of the Works or any Section is likely to be delayed beyond the relevant Completion Date by any of the Relevant Events, the Architect/CA shall give an extension of time by fixing such later date as the Completion Date as they consider fair and reasonable.

**2.28.2** The Architect/CA shall notify their decision **as soon as is reasonably practicable and in any event within 12 weeks** of receipt of the required particulars. Where less than 12 weeks remain to the Completion Date, the Architect/CA shall endeavour to notify the decision before it. (JCT 2024 reduces this period to 8 weeks.)

**2.28.3** The Architect/CA shall, in fixing a later Completion Date, state which Relevant Events have been taken into account and the extent (if any) to which they have had regard to any instruction requiring a Variation which is an omission. The Architect/CA may fix a later Completion Date, fix the same date (no extension), or, in limited circumstances, fix an earlier Completion Date (only where an omission variation shortens the work).

**2.28.5** After Practical Completion, the Architect/CA shall, within 12 weeks, fix a Completion Date (whether earlier than, the same as, or later than the previously fixed date) having regard to any Relevant Events.

**2.28.6.1** Proviso: the Contractor shall constantly use their **best endeavours** to prevent delay in the progress of the Works, however caused. This is a standard Employer defence to EOT claims — mitigation evidence matters.

**2.29** The Relevant Events — the events that entitle the Contractor to an extension of time. Each Relevant Event corresponds to a specific clause reference.

| # | Clause | Relevant Event | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.29.1 | Variations | Variations, and any other matters or instructions which under the Conditions are to be treated as, or as requiring, a Variation |
| 2 | 2.29.2 | Architect/CA instructions | Compliance with Architect/CA instructions — including postponement of work under clause 3.15, expenditure of provisional sums, and opening up for inspection or testing (unless the test shows non-compliance) |
| 3 | 2.29.3 | Deferment of possession | The Employer defers giving possession of the site under clause 2.5 (only if the contract provides for deferment) |
| 4 | 2.29.4 | Approximate Quantity | Execution of work for which an Approximate Quantity in the Contract Bills was not a reasonably accurate forecast of the quantity of work required |
| 5 | 2.29.5 | Contractor suspension | Suspension by the Contractor of its obligations under clause 4.13 for non-payment (HGCRA 1996) |
| 6 | 2.29.6 | Employer impediment, prevention or default | Any impediment, prevention or default, by act or omission, by the Employer, the Architect/CA, the QS or any Employer's Persons — this consolidated event covers late information and failure to give access |
| 7 | 2.29.7 | Statutory undertakers | Work carried out (or not carried out) by statutory undertakers (utility companies) in pursuance of their statutory obligations |
| 8 | 2.29.8 | Exceptionally adverse weather | Weather conditions that are exceptionally adverse — more extreme than could reasonably be expected at the relevant time of year |
| 9 | 2.29.9 | Loss or damage to the works | Loss or damage caused by Specified Perils (fire, flood, etc.) |
| 10 | 2.29.10 | Civil commotion or terrorism | Civil commotion or the use or threat of terrorism, and the activities of the relevant authorities in dealing with it |
| 11 | 2.29.11 | Strike or lock-out | Strike, lock-out or local combination of workmen affecting the Works or the preparation or transport of goods and materials for them |
| 12 | 2.29.12 | Statutory power | Exercise after the Base Date by the UK Government or a local authority of a statutory power directly affecting the execution of the Works |
| 13 | 2.29.13 | Force majeure | Force majeure — an event beyond the control of either party |

**Commercial significance:** The 12-week retrospective review is unique to JCT. The Architect/CA can revise previous EOT decisions — both upward and downward. This creates uncertainty for both parties until the final review is complete. Note also that the contract is silent on **concurrent delay**: under English law the *Malmaison* approach generally gives the Contractor an EOT where a Relevant Event is concurrent with the Contractor's own delay, but bespoke amendments often reverse this — check the schedule of amendments.

### Clause 2.30 — Practical Completion

The Architect/CA shall, when in their opinion Practical Completion of the Works is achieved, issue a certificate to that effect (the Practical Completion Certificate).

**Commercial significance:** Practical Completion triggers:
1. Release of half the retention
2. Start of the Rectification Period
3. End of liquidated damages liability
4. Transfer of responsibility for insuring the works to the Employer (in most cases)
5. The 12-week period for the Architect/CA's retrospective EOT review

### Clause 2.32 — Liquidated Damages

If the Contractor fails to complete the Works by the Completion Date, the Employer may give notice under clause 2.32.1. The Employer may then:
- Require the Contractor to pay liquidated damages at the rate stated in the Contract Particulars, or
- Deduct liquidated damages from amounts otherwise due

**2.32.3** If the Architect/CA fixes a later Completion Date after LADs have been deducted, the Employer must repay the LADs for the extended period.
