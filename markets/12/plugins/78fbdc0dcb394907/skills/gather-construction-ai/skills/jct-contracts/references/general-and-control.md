# JCT Standard Building Contract 2016 — Clause Reference

Detailed clause reference for the JCT Standard Building Contract (SBC) 2016. This document covers the core clauses most relevant to commercial management and quantity surveying.

> **Note:** This reference covers the standard form SBC 2016. JCT contracts are almost always amended by a schedule of amendments, which frequently inserts condition-precedent notice regimes — always check the contract as executed. JCT 2024 editions are now published — confirm which edition applies before citing clauses; see workflow.md for the Edition Differences (2016 → 2024) table. This document is contract-administration guidance, not legal advice.

> **Provenance:** clause citations verified 2026-07 against the official JCT SBC/DB/ICD/MW 2016 contents pages (jctltd.co.uk) and published commentary/case law, including named-firm and practitioner commentary (e.g. Ramskill Martin's "Back to Basics" series on Schedule 2 quotations and provisional sums, Fenwick Elliott, and contract-consultants.com) cross-checking Schedule 2 timescales, clause 4.22.1's exclusion of defined-work provisional sums, and clauses 2.28.5/2.28.6.1. Items that could only be verified against secondary sources are hedged in the text. Report suspected errors via the repository issue tracker.

## Section 1: Definitions and Interpretation

### Clause 1.1 — Definitions

Key defined terms QSs must understand:
- **Base Date** — the date stated in the Contract Particulars. Statutory and other changes after this date may be Relevant Events/Matters.
- **CDM Regulations** — Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015.
- **Completion Date** — the Date for Completion as adjusted by any extensions of time.
- **Contract Documents** — the Agreement, Conditions, Contract Drawings, Contract Bills, and any other documents listed.
- **Contract Sum** — the total price stated in the Contract Particulars, subject to adjustment under the contract.
- **Date for Completion** — the date stated in the Contract Particulars by which the Contractor must achieve Practical Completion.
- **Date of Possession** — the date stated in the Contract Particulars on which the Employer gives possession of the site.
- **Rectification Period** — the period stated in the Contract Particulars after Practical Completion during which the Contractor must make good defects. This is the current JCT term (2005 editions onward); "Defects Liability Period" is the legacy term.
- **Practical Completion** — not defined in the contract. The Architect/CA determines when the works are practically complete. Generally means complete for all practical purposes, with only minor snagging items remaining.
- **Retention Percentage** — the percentage stated in the Contract Particulars to be deducted from interim payments as security.
- **Specified Perils** — fire, lightning, explosion, storm, flood, bursting or overflowing of water tanks/pipes/appliances, earthquake, aircraft and aerial devices and articles dropped from them, riot and civil commotion.
- **Variation** — defined in clause 5.1 as including alteration of design, quality, quantity; addition, omission, substitution of work; alteration of materials/goods standards; removal from site; imposition of obligations/restrictions.

### Clause 1.9 — Effect of Final Certificate

The Final Certificate (issued under clause 4.26) is conclusive evidence that:
- Where the quality of materials or standards of workmanship are to be to the Architect/CA's reasonable satisfaction, they are to such satisfaction
- Any necessary effect has been given to all the terms of the contract requiring adjustment of the Contract Sum
- All extensions of time due have been given
- The reimbursement of direct loss and/or expense is in final settlement of all claims

**Unless** adjudication, arbitration or legal proceedings are commenced within **28 days** of issue (clause 1.9.3). Where an adjudicator gives their decision after the Final Certificate has been issued, clause 1.9.4 allows a further 28 days from the decision to commence arbitration or legal proceedings on that dispute.

**Commercial significance:** This is one of the most powerful provisions in JCT. Once 28 days have passed without challenge, the Final Certificate closes down many avenues for dispute. QSs must review the Final Certificate immediately on receipt.

## Section 3: Control of the Works

### Clause 3.10 — Architect's Instructions

The Architect/CA may issue instructions which the Contractor shall forthwith comply with.

**3.12** All instructions shall be in writing. If the Architect/CA gives an oral instruction, it has no effect unless confirmed in writing within 7 days.

**3.14** The Architect/CA may issue instructions requiring a Variation — the general power under which changes to the Works are instructed.

**3.15** The Architect/CA may issue an instruction requiring the postponement of any work to be executed under the contract.

**3.17** The Architect/CA may issue instructions requiring the Contractor to open up for inspection any work covered up, or to arrange for or carry out any test. If the test shows the work is in accordance with the contract, the cost is added to the Contract Sum and the delay is a Relevant Event.

**Commercial significance:** Only written instructions from the Architect/CA are valid. If a site instruction is given verbally, the Contractor should confirm it in writing within 7 days to protect their position. An instruction that changes the works is a variation.

## Section 6: Injury, Damage, and Insurance

### Clause 6.1 — Injury to Persons and Property

The Contractor is liable for and shall indemnify the Employer against personal injury or death arising from the carrying out of the works (unless due to the Employer's act or neglect).

### Clause 6.7 and Schedule 3 — Insurance of the Works

Works insurance is undertaken in accordance with whichever Insurance Option is stated in the Contract Particulars (clause 6.7); the Options themselves are set out in Schedule 3:
- **Insurance Option A** (new buildings — Contractor takes out all risks insurance of the works in joint names)
- **Insurance Option B** (new buildings — Employer takes out all risks insurance of the works in joint names)
- **Insurance Option C** (works in or extensions to existing structures — Employer insures the existing structures and the works in joint names)

**Commercial significance:** The insurance options affect who bears the risk of loss or damage to the works from Specified Perils. Under Option A, the Contractor insures but damage from Specified Perils is still a Relevant Event entitling EOT.

## Comparison: JCT vs NEC Key Differences

| Feature | JCT SBC | NEC4 ECC |
|---|---|---|
| **Philosophy** | Traditional, adversarial roots (evolving) | Collaborative, proactive management |
| **Change mechanism** | Variation + EOT + Loss & Expense (3 separate processes) | Compensation Event (single mechanism for time + cost) |
| **Time bar** | No absolute time bar; "reasonably apparent" | 8-week bar — condition precedent |
| **Notification timescale** | "Forthwith" / "as soon as reasonably apparent" | Within 8 weeks of awareness |
| **Cost assessment** | Contract rates first, then fair rates | Defined Cost + Fee |
| **Programme** | Not contractual unless expressly stated | Contractual document — Accepted Programme |
| **CA/PM response timescale** | Within 12 weeks of particulars (2.28.2); 8 weeks in JCT 2024 | 1 week (both NEC3 and NEC4); a further 2 weeks applies only after the Contractor notifies the PM's failure to reply (leading to deemed acceptance in NEC4) |
| **Deemed acceptance** | No | Yes — failure to respond = deemed acceptance |
| **Retrospective review** | Yes — 12 weeks post-PC | No — CEs are prospective |
| **Final account** | Final Certificate (conclusive after 28 days) | Final assessment of PWDD |
| **Dispute resolution** | Adjudication → Arbitration/Litigation | Adjudication (W1/W2) → Tribunal |

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Not giving notice of delay promptly** — unlike NEC there is no time bar, but "forthwith" means without delay. Late notice weakens the claim.
2. **Confusing Relevant Events with Relevant Matters** — not all events that give time also give money. Weather gives time (2.29.8) but is not a Relevant Matter.
3. **Not confirming oral instructions in writing** — an oral instruction has no contractual effect unless confirmed within 7 days.
4. **Missing the Final Certificate challenge window** — 28 days. Once passed, the certificate is conclusive.
5. **Not separating prolongation from disruption** — these are different heads of claim with different proof requirements. Don't lump them together.
6. **Failing to substantiate loss & expense** — an application alone is not enough. The Contractor must provide supporting information when requested.

## Key Terminology Glossary

| Abbreviation | Full Term | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **SBC** | Standard Building Contract | The most comprehensive JCT contract form. Used for larger building projects where the Employer appoints an Architect/CA to administer the contract. |
| **DB** | Design and Build Contract | The Contractor takes responsibility for design and construction. The Employer issues Employer's Requirements; the Contractor responds with Contractor's Proposals. |
| **ICD** | Intermediate Building Contract | For medium-value projects of straightforward scope. Less detailed provisions than SBC. |
| **MW** | Minor Works Building Contract | For small, straightforward projects. Simplified provisions with fewer administrative requirements. |
| **Architect/CA** | Architect / Contract Administrator | The person named in the contract to administer it on behalf of the Employer. Issues instructions, grants EOTs, certifies practical completion and payments. Equivalent of NEC's Project Manager. |
| **QS** | Quantity Surveyor | The person (if appointed) who values variations, ascertains loss and expense, and prepares interim valuations. |
| **EOT** | Extension of Time | An extension to the Date for Completion granted by the Architect/CA under clauses 2.27-2.28. Equivalent of NEC's change to the Completion Date via CE. |
| **Relevant Event** | Relevant Event | An event listed in clause 2.29 that entitles the Contractor to an extension of time. Equivalent of NEC's compensation event grounds (for time). |
| **Relevant Matter** | Relevant Matter | A matter listed in clause 4.22 that entitles the Contractor to loss and/or expense. Equivalent of NEC's compensation event grounds (for cost). |
| **Practical Completion** | Practical Completion | The point at which the works are complete for all practical purposes. Not defined in the contract — determined by the Architect/CA. Equivalent of NEC's Completion. |
| **Rectification Period** | Rectification Period | The period (stated in the contract) after Practical Completion during which the Contractor must rectify defects. This is the current JCT term (2005 editions onward); "Defects Liability Period" (DLP) is the legacy term still heard on site. |
| **LADs** | Liquidated and Ascertained Damages | Pre-agreed damages payable by the Contractor for late completion. Equivalent of NEC's delay damages under Option X7. |
| **Variation** | Variation | A change to the design, quality, or quantity of the works, as defined in clause 5.1 and instructed by the Architect/CA under clause 3.14. Equivalent of NEC's change to the Scope/Works Information via a PM instruction. |
| **Loss and Expense** | Loss and/or Expense | Additional cost recoverable by the Contractor due to matters that are not the Contractor's fault, under clauses 4.20-4.24. Not the same as a variation valuation — it covers consequential costs (prolongation, disruption). |
| **Interim Certificate** | Interim Certificate | The Architect/CA's periodic valuation and certification of payment. Equivalent of NEC's payment assessment. |
| **Final Certificate** | Final Certificate | Issued under clause 4.26 after the end of the Rectification Period and final account agreement. Has contractual significance — it is conclusive evidence of certain matters under clause 1.9 unless challenged within 28 days. |
| **CDP** | Contractor's Designed Portion | In SBC, a portion of the works designed by the Contractor. The design obligation for CDP follows DB principles within an SBC contract. |
| **AI** | Architect's Instruction | A formal instruction from the Architect/CA. Must be in writing (clause 3.12). Only valid instructions can be treated as variations. |
| **CIS** | Construction Industry Scheme | Tax deduction scheme applicable to construction payments in the UK. |
| **JCT Fluctuations** | Fluctuations Options | Options A (contribution/levy), B (labour/materials cost), or C (formula adjustment) for price inflation. Similar to NEC's X1 (price adjustment for inflation). |
