# Attack surface detail (Review Mode, Steps 1, 3 and 4)

## Step 1 — attack surface by document type
State which document type is under attack, because the attack surface differs: a CE notification is attacked on notification timeliness, facts, and grounds; a quotation on quantum method (dividing date, actual/forecast split, TRA); an application on valuation basis and certainty of included CEs; an EOT on critical-path entitlement and concurrency; a final account on time-barred heads never previously notified and global-claim exposure; a tender settlement pack on its assumptions register (every user-stated figure is a question), LOW BASIS exposure (how much of the price rests on thin norms), UNPRICED items surviving to submission, and SILENT quote-scope items priced as if included.

## Step 3 — the INDICATIVE-as-Accepted-Programme attack line
Where the draft cites a `scripts/cpm.py` recalculation, a `$programme-impact` run, or any other engine-derived date without its INDICATIVE banner and self-check %, or presents it as "the programme says" / "the Accepted Programme shows" rather than as an indicative recalculation — flag it by name: **"the draft presents an indicative recalculation as the programme"** (a Pattern 15 rule 2 violation). This is NOT SUPPORTED BY RECORDS regardless of whether the underlying arithmetic is correct, because the draft has misstated which class of figure it is; the fix is to re-label the figure INDICATIVE with its self-check %, or replace it with the STORED date it was substituting for.

## Step 3 — procedural claims and the correspondence register (Pattern 17)
Where `correspondence/correspondence-register.md` exists, verify "we notified on [date]" / "the PM replied on [date]" claims against it — a confirmed register item is cited "[COR-NNN] ([date], [from] → [to])"; an UNCLASSIFIED item is "correspondence dated [date]" and does not, by itself, prove the notice (say so). Absent register → these claims stay NO RECORDS with the standing flag, and the fix list points to `$correspondence-import`.

## Step 4 — contractual attack surface, in detail
Build a ranked, clause-cited attack register — call `contract-advisor` for the resolved family's clause content rather than loading it yourself; attack **only** what the contract text actually supports:
- **Time-bar exposure** — NEC clause 61.3's 8-week bar and its exempt grounds (events arising from a PM/Supervisor instruction, certificate, changed decision, or corrected assumption are not barred); the JCT "forthwith" notice test (and any amendment inserting a condition precedent); the FIDIC 20.2.1 28-day condition-precedent bar and the 2017-only 20.2.5 relief valve. Compute the window from the stated awareness date and give the actual deadline date, per Pattern 6 — use `$nec-deadlines` for a precise NEC countdown.
- **Wrong or weak clause ground** — does the draft cite the ground the facts actually support, per Pattern 7's event-to-clause mapping?
- **Quantum method attacks** — NEC dividing-date errors (63.1/63.5: is the split between actual and forecast Defined Cost drawn at the correct date, not the assessment date?), the actual-vs-forecast split itself, and TRA (time risk allowance) treatment.
- **Programme status attacks** — no Accepted Programme, a stale data date, or a Completion Date shown as moved by an unimplemented CE — checked against the imported baseline (Pattern 15) where one exists. Where an imported programme exists, cite the baseline's own **self-check reproduction %** (from `programme-baseline.md`'s provenance block) as part of this attack line — e.g. "the file's own network model reproduces only 71% of its stored dates — expect the other side to attack any float-based argument." A low self-check % is a genuine weakness in any float-based argument the draft relies on, whether or not the draft itself ran the engine.
- **Procedural opportunities the draft MISSED** — deemed-acceptance routes it should have reserved (61.4, 62.6, 64.4, and for NEC4 also 31.3 and 50.9) but didn't mention.
