# Sparring Scenarios — Red Team Training Pack

Five seeded-flaw drafts for Sparring Mode. **Every scenario is fictional** — the project
(Marsh Lane Underpass), the parties, the dates, and the records summaries exist only on this
page. Present a scenario's **Draft only** (with its banner) to the trainee; the **Answer Key**
is revealed only after their attempt. Keys list the seeded flaws — a floor, not a ceiling:
credit valid trainee findings beyond the key as BONUS.

All scenarios assume **NEC4 ECC, unamended** unless the scenario says otherwise. Clause
content is per `skills/nec-contracts/references/ce-and-early-warning.md` and
`skills/nec-contracts/references/programme-and-time.md` — cite it in lessons. APC tags name
RICS APC competencies.

The "records summary" in each scenario stands in for Gather data — in sparring, verification
runs against that summary, not the live workspace.

## Scenario 1 — "The Late Discovery" (theme: time bar)

**Records summary (fictional):** Diary 3 Feb 2026: "excavation at chainage 120 hit running
sand, gang stood down pm, temporary works redesign needed." Redesign instruction from
Contractor's own engineer 10 Feb. Work resumed 24 Feb. No early warning notified. Today is
15 July 2026.

### Draft (present this, only this)

> **TRAINING SCENARIO — FICTIONAL PROJECT — Marsh Lane Underpass**
>
> To the Project Manager: we notify a compensation event under clause 60.1(12). On 3 February
> 2026 we encountered running sand at chainage 120 which an experienced contractor would not
> have allowed for. The event caused 3 weeks of delay and additional cost of £74,000, details
> to follow. We trust this notification is in good time as the effects are only now fully
> understood.

### Answer Key
1. **Time bar passed — fatal.** Aware 3 Feb 2026; notified ~15 July — far beyond 8 weeks
   (deadline 31 Mar 2026). 60.1(12) is Contractor-notifiable and **not** on the 61.3 exempt
   list (60.1(1),(4),(7),(8),(15),(17)). "Effects only now fully understood" does not restart
   the clock — awareness of the **event** starts it. *Lesson:* the exempt grounds are events
   the PM should have notified; physical conditions are yours to notify, fast. [APC: Contract
   practice]
2. **The 60.1(12) test is not pleaded.** The ground turns on what an experienced contractor
   would have judged at the **Contract Date** to have such a small chance of occurring that
   allowing for it was unreasonable — the draft asserts the conclusion with no site-information
   comparison. [APC: Contract practice]
3. **No early warning notified** — under NEC4 61.5/63.7, if the PM decides an experienced
   contractor could have given one, the event is assessed **as if** it had been — quantum
   exposure even where entitlement survives. [APC: Contract practice]
4. **Quantum unparticularised** — "£74,000, details to follow" with no Defined Cost build-up,
   and 3 weeks' delay with no programme evidence. [APC: Quantification and costing]
5. **No records cited** — the diary entry exists and is never referenced. [APC: Data
   management]

## Scenario 2 — "The Wrong Door" (theme: wrong clause ground)

**Records summary (fictional):** RFI-041 (bearing detail) submitted 6 Jan 2026; period for
reply 2 weeks; PM replied 27 Jan. Diaries 20–27 Jan: "deck gang stood down am awaiting
RFI-041 answer" (4 half-days). Possession records show all access windows given on time.

### Draft

> **TRAINING SCENARIO — FICTIONAL PROJECT — Marsh Lane Underpass**
>
> To the Project Manager: we notify a compensation event under clause 60.1(2) — access to the
> deck works was effectively denied between 20 and 27 January 2026 because your late answer to
> RFI-041 prevented us from working. We were stood down for a full week. An early warning was
> raised at the time.

### Answer Key
1. **Wrong ground.** Access was given on time — the records contradict 60.1(2). The facts fit
   **60.1(6)**: the PM did not reply to a communication within the period for reply (due
   20 Jan, replied 27 Jan). Right facts, wrong door — and the receiving PM will simply reject
   the ground pleaded. *Lesson:* plead the ground the records support, per the event-to-clause
   mapping. [APC: Contract practice]
2. **"A full week" overstates the records** — diaries show 4 half-days (am stand-downs), not
   a full week's stand-down. [APC: Quantification and costing]
3. **"An early warning was raised"** — wrong verb (early warnings are **notified**), and no
   EWN reference is cited; if none exists, the 61.5/63.7 as-if assessment risk applies.
   [APC: Contract practice]
4. **Timing language asserts causation** ("prevented us from working") where records show
   coincidence — the diary says stood down *awaiting* the answer; whether other work was
   available is unaddressed. [APC: Construction technology and environmental services]

## Scenario 3 — "The Crystal Ball" (theme: quantum method / dividing date)

**Records summary (fictional):** Instructed variation (PM instruction changing the Scope,
14 Jan 2026, ground 60.1(1)) added a drainage run. The added work was done 19–30 Jan; all
labour/plant recorded. Quotation submitted 20 Feb 2026.

### Draft (a quotation, not a notification)

> **TRAINING SCENARIO — FICTIONAL PROJECT — Marsh Lane Underpass**
>
> Quotation for CE-007 (drainage variation, instructed 14 January 2026): we assess the change
> to the Prices as £41,200, being our forecast of the Defined Cost of the additional drainage
> works plus the fee percentage. We have included a further £6,000 risk allowance for possible
> obstructions that may be encountered. Programme effect: nil.

### Answer Key
1. **Wrong side of the dividing date.** The dividing date (63.1) for a 60.1(1) event is when
   the instruction was communicated (14 Jan); work done 19–30 Jan is **past and recorded** by
   the 20 Feb quotation — yet it is priced as *forecast*. Actual Defined Cost applies to work
   done; forecast only for work not yet done. The recorded labour/plant should be the build-up.
   *Lesson:* the actual/forecast split is drawn at the dividing date, not at whichever is
   easier. [APC: Quantification and costing]
2. **Risk allowance on a completed event.** £6,000 for "possible obstructions" on work already
   done — risk allowances (63.8) cover matters with a significant chance of occurring that are
   at the Contractor's risk; nothing remains to occur. [APC: Quantification and costing]
3. **"Programme effect: nil" is asserted, not shown** — no reference to planned Completion or
   the Accepted Programme; even a nil effect should state its basis. [APC: Programming and
   planning]
4. **No records cited** for the build-up despite full labour/plant records existing.
   [APC: Data management]

## Scenario 4 — "The Polite Chaser" (theme: missed deemed-acceptance lever)

**Records summary (fictional):** CE notified by Contractor 2 Mar 2026 (valid, in time,
ground 60.1(2) — a possession given late, evidenced). PM has not replied. Drafted today,
14 Apr 2026. No failure notification has been sent.

### Draft (a chasing letter)

> **TRAINING SCENARIO — FICTIONAL PROJECT — Marsh Lane Underpass**
>
> Dear Project Manager, we refer to our compensation event notification of 2 March 2026, to
> which we have not yet received your response. We should be grateful for your decision at
> your earliest convenience, and remain happy to discuss. We reserve all our rights.

### Answer Key
1. **The lever is available and unused — the headline flaw.** The PM's one-week reply period
   (61.4) expired 9 Mar. The Contractor may **notify the PM's failure to reply**; if the
   failure continues for a further two weeks, the notification is **treated as acceptance**
   that the event is a compensation event. Five weeks of "earliest convenience" have already
   been given away. *Lesson:* the deemed-acceptance family (61.4, 62.6, 64.4; NEC4 also 31.3,
   50.9) only starts running when you notify the failure — silence is a choice. [APC: Contract
   practice]
2. **"We reserve all our rights" reserves nothing specific** — the right that matters here is
   a defined mechanism with a defined trigger; invoke it. [APC: Contract practice]
3. **No quotation preparation in the meantime** — the letter neither submits nor mentions the
   quotation, which the Contractor could be assembling from the possession records while the
   clock runs. [APC: Quantification and costing]

## Scenario 5 — "The Kitchen Sink" (theme: global claim, final account)

**Records summary (fictional):** Three distinct matters over Feb–May 2026: a late possession
(notified in time, CE agreed at £18k), a two-week utility-diversion delay (never notified),
and low productivity in April the diaries attribute to the Contractor's own plant breakdowns.
Drafted 15 July 2026.

### Draft (a final-account head)

> **TRAINING SCENARIO — FICTIONAL PROJECT — Marsh Lane Underpass**
>
> Final account — delay and disruption: the cumulative effect of the employer's actions
> throughout the works (late possessions, utility diversions, and general disruption) caused
> 9 weeks of delay and £310,000 of additional cost, which we claim as a composite sum. It is
> not practicable to separate the individual effects, which in any event compounded each other.

### Answer Key
1. **Global claim.** Three causes rolled into one composite sum with causation asserted
   "cumulatively" — one cause is already **agreed and paid** (£18k possession CE, double
   recovery risk), one is **time-barred** (utility diversion: aware ~Feb–Mar, never notified,
   60.1-Contractor-notifiable, 8 weeks long gone), and one is **the Contractor's own risk**
   (diaries attribute April productivity to own plant breakdowns). "Not practicable to
   separate" is contradicted by the records, which separate the causes themselves. *Lesson:*
   under NEC each event is notified, quoted, and assessed **separately** — a composite sum
   invites the whole head's rejection. [APC: Contract practice]
2. **Double recovery** — the agreed CE cannot reappear inside a composite claim.
   [APC: Quantification and costing]
3. **Time bar on the diversion** — fatal to that strand, and burying it in a composite doesn't
   cure it. [APC: Contract practice]
4. **Own-culpability strand included** — the records actively contradict entitlement for the
   April strand. [APC: Data management]
5. **9 weeks / £310,000 with no build-up per strand** — no programme evidence, no Defined Cost
   trail. [APC: Quantification and costing]

## Using the pack

- Rotate scenarios via `routine-state/red-team-drill/scores.md` (the log records which were
  used); re-runs of a used scenario are fine for new trainees.
- Score FOUND / MISSED / BONUS per the agent's Sparring Mode steps; the key's numbering makes
  the tally auditable.
- After the reveal, the natural round two: the trainee redrafts (Scenario 1–3), drafts the
  failure notification (Scenario 4), or splits the head into its strands (Scenario 5) — and
  the agent reviews the redraft in Review Mode.
