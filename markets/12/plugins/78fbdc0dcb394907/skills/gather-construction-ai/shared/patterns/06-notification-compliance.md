# Pattern 6: Notification Compliance Check

When a skill identifies a contractual event or builds a submission, check notification compliance based on the contract type:

### NEC (clause 61.3)
- **Window:** 8 weeks from the date the Contractor became aware of the event
- **Consequence:** If the PM has not notified and the Contractor does not notify within 8 weeks, the event is **time-barred** — the Contractor loses entitlement (unless the PM should have notified the event)
- **Check:** Calculate 8 weeks from the event date. State whether today is within or outside the window, and how many days remain or have passed
- **Early Warning:** Note whether an Early Warning was or should have been **notified** (NEC's verb — an early warning is *given by notifying*, never "raised"). An EW is not a CE notification and does not stop the 61.3 clock
- **Scope caution:** 8 weeks is the **ECC** window. The subcontract (ECS) is 7 weeks, the Short Contract (ECSC) 4 weeks, the Short Subcontract (ECSS) 3 weeks — and X22 (ECI) bars any Stage One CE not captured by the Notice to Proceed to Stage Two. Confirm which NEC form the user actually has before stating the window

### JCT (clause 2.27.1)
- **Window:** No strict time bar in the unamended form — notice must be given "forthwith" when delay becomes reasonably apparent
- **Consequence:** Late notice does not bar entitlement under the unamended form, but it weakens the application and may affect the Architect/CA's assessment
- **Check:** Calculate how many days/weeks have passed since the event. Note that prompt notice strengthens the application
- **Assessment:** once particulars are received, the Architect/CA must decide the EOT within 12 weeks (SBC 2016 clause 2.28.2; reduced to 8 weeks in JCT 2024)
- **Caveat:** bespoke schedules of amendments frequently insert condition-precedent notice regimes into JCT contracts — always check the contract as executed

### FIDIC (clause 20.2.1)
- **Window:** 28 days from the date the Contractor became aware (or should have become aware) of the event
- **Consequence:** This is a **condition precedent** — late notice bars the claim, subject to the late-notice justification mechanism in clause 20.2.5 (FIDIC 2017). Under FIDIC 1999 (clause 20.1) the bar is absolute on its face, with no equivalent relief valve
- **Check:** Calculate 28 days from the event date. State whether today is within or outside the window. Note that the Engineer must give notice within 14 days if it considers the Notice time-barred (clause 20.2.2), failing which the Notice is deemed valid
- **Follow-up:** If within the 28-day window, note the 84-day fully detailed claim deadline (clause 20.2.4) and, for events of continuing effect, monthly interim claims with a final claim within 28 days after the effects end (clause 20.2.6)
