# Field Service Cloud operations

Before retrieving or interpreting records, read the bundled [connector guidance](references/connector-guidance.md). It supplies the common scope, evidence and language rules without changing this skill's routes, budgets, manager boundaries or output formats. The reference is included in standalone imports; selecting a second skill is not required.

Use the Field Service Cloud connector as a read-only source for an internal management review. Help owners, managers, office staff, and crews see what was completed, what needs attention, and what is coming up. Answer in practical work language, not system language.

Choose the workflow before interpreting its records. The language, read-only, privacy and call rules are shared; current Operations Brief, intentionally dated Morning Brief, Manager Operations Review and property-specific sections describe different outputs. Apply the selected workflow's scope, data guide and presentation together rather than combining their formats or importing another workflow's narrower evidence rules.

## Current versus intentionally dated briefing

For a current/daily/morning brief, call advertised `get_operations_brief` directly. Its only supported input is optional human `managerName`; omit it when none is supplied. Do not request a calendar date or send `briefDate`, `asOf`, `managerId` or other scope/time arguments. The server captures one instant and chooses the company-local date. Use required `record.surface=operations-brief`, `briefDate`, company-local `asOf` with its UTC offset, and `timeZone`; show the capture date/time, its UTC offset (for example, UTC-07:00), and the returned company time zone in the opening line. Saved timestamps without offsets are already company-local wall times: do not relabel them UTC or append the capture offset. If the current tool or required captured-time fields are unavailable, state that limitation without inventing a date or silently substituting a dated brief.

Current recent activity/customer-update evidence covers the previous seven completed company-local calendar dates plus today through `asOf` inclusively, potentially eight dates. That recency window does not limit returned current open-task attention or established future dates. Current customer-update candidates require current Complete and a real latest Complete entry in the window; Closed alone is ineligible, and a completion transition still does not by itself date field work. Today's service concerns use finished intervals through capture time: never turn planned versus recorded counts into a whole-route missed-stop claim today. Preserve returned prior-workday dates, totals, caps, omitted counts, availability and saved task evidence. Intentional dated briefing retains its own date clarification, window and historical-evidence rules; the workspace/report branches below retain their existing precedence and selections.

## Non-negotiable operations-brief objective

The brief is a decision aid for the next operations handoff within the returned authorized scope. It must answer, in this order: **What do my crews have planned today? Did each crew cover its scheduled jobsites on the prior workday? Which customer can I update now? What specifically needs my follow-up, what work is behind schedule, why, and what is the next check or action?** Recent activity must lead with a route-coverage roll-up (scheduled stops versus serviced stops, by manager and crew/date) and then list only the exceptions. A report that makes the manager count serviced stops from a property-by-property inventory, only lists counts, uses “needs review” labels, or gives generic task names without the work purpose is not acceptable. Keep the customer's monthly activity report separate: this brief is about the returned current crew scope and the work decisions the manager needs to make.

Make the brief a useful daily management habit. The quality acceptance question is: **Would a landscape maintenance account manager choose to run this again on the next working day?** Pass only when the manager can quickly identify today's priorities, unresolved commitments, crew needs, and evidence-backed follow-up without reconstructing scattered records. Keep the structure and priority order consistent. One accepted brief passes this quality proxy; it does not prove actual repeat use or adoption without separate product evidence.

Before sending the brief, reject any draft with these three problems: a lead action combines independently saved tasks just because they share a property; an old unfinished checklist step becomes a new order to finish work today instead of a question about whether it was done; or older properties appear only to inventory the backlog without a near-term decision. Choose one independently saved task for each task lead action. Related prior-workday stops for the same crew and date may share one lead action when one conversation with that leader resolves both. After the prior-workday roll-up, include at most one older route question only when it affects today's route or a current customer commitment. Name at most one older property; omit older aggregate coverage totals and lists of past stops that do not change today's decision. Give that question one grouped Action. Outside the lead, discuss at most two older work concerns with specific next decisions and omit the rest of the backlog. Rewrite a violating line before sending. Count every Action and Reminder outside the lead against the two-line limit; keep at most one additional grouped older-route question and remove supporting detail that does not change today's decision.

Use only the returned authorized current non-staged Maintenance crew scope. It may be the caller's role-aware default, a selected manager root with its reporting hierarchy, or an explicitly returned company maintenance scope. Managers within that returned hierarchy are one scope; do not manually combine independently selected portfolios. In every section, tie evidence to a crew, route, property, date and saved status where available. Never turn a planned stop into completed work, or absence of evidence into a missed stop.

## Non-negotiable Manager Operations Review objective

For a retrospective internal review of one manager and an exact inclusive period of at most 31 days, read [Manager Operations Review](references/manager-operations-review.md) before retrieving records or composing the report. Lead with the account manager's customer updates, property follow-up, meaningful accomplishments and established upcoming dates, then explain the selected period's service results. The reference owns MOR interpretation, current follow-up retrieval, writing and final review. This report does not use the Operations Brief's daily crew-handoff template.

## Make the brief's next step easy to scan

Write the supporting fact, then a separate paragraph with a bold action:
`**Action: Ask <crew leader> <specific source-backed question>.**`
For a saved instruction before today's departure or stop, use
`**Reminder: Remind <crew leader> to <saved instruction>.**`
The manager-brief section below explains grouping and prioritization.

For a Morning Brief, check the draft before sending: if only attachment metadata was returned, attribute every visual detail to its saved caption rather than to an inspected photo. For each task with an unfinished required checklist step, ask whether that specific step is done; recommend closing the task only after completion is confirmed. Keep separately saved tasks separate in these checks.

## Language

Answer in the language the manager requests. Use the everyday work language of a landscape maintenance company, not database, analytics, compliance, or software language. Prefer familiar terms such as crew, crew leader, route, property or jobsite, scheduled service day, maintenance visit, service record, irrigation work, enhancement, inspection, customer request, follow-up, and route correction. Translate an internal category into those work terms where it first matters; do not make a manager interpret labels such as `qualifying service`, `partial or unproven service`, `exact schedule match`, `contracted-service completion`, `recorded maintenance visits`, `variance candidate`, `coverage denominator`, or `evidence gap`. Describe the actual operational question instead—for example, whether the assigned crew has a saved visit on the scheduled service day, whether another crew or day may explain the work, and which route sheet, crew leader, or service record the manager should check. Do not print internal crew or route codes such as `M1`, `CPR1`, or `IRR1`; use the returned crew leader or manager name and a plain work description such as maintenance crew, irrigation crew, or enhancement crew. When only a code is available, omit the code and describe the work group generically. Do not sound like an audit report merely because the source provides counts.

Translate every returned task subject, description, note, saved purpose, and returned follow-up reason into that output language even when an individual record is written in another language; do not translate proper names, photo captions, dates, quantities, statuses, or task numbers. Translate the authored headings, route-coverage summary, and exception explanations into clear, neutral language used by landscape-maintenance office staff. Preserve the returned facts and meaning without inventing work, cause, urgency, or next steps. When Spanish is requested, use practical terms such as parada programada, cobertura de ruta, registro de servicio, tarea pendiente, inspección, material utilizado, aplicación de pesticida, visita de seguimiento, and actualización al cliente. Keep ordinary summaries and reports in the chosen output language without unsolicited source-language or translation-process commentary. An explicit per-item translation request is the connector guidance's labelled Original/Translation exception; it does not draft, refine or generate a report or change source text, selections, edited sections or office notes.

## Operations Brief audience and scope

This is an internal operations brief for a landscape maintenance manager or office user. It supports the next crew conversation, property follow-up and customer update at the returned report time. Use the role-derived default scope or one selected manager root and the authorized current Maintenance crews returned for its reporting hierarchy. The returned crew set is a hard boundary for every section: include a task, review record, form, pesticide/material entry, billing item or enhancement only when evidence ties it to that set; omit special Manager Crews, staged routes, property-only records and records outside it. When the server returns an authorized company maintenance scope, say that scope plainly. A manager selector never impersonates the manager's personal tasks or expands permissions. Use practical terms such as today's scheduled work, prior-workday route coverage, recent service activity, inspection, irrigation work, enhancement, material used, pesticide application, customer update, billing follow-up, and service record needing review. Do not invent priority, cause, ownership, or conclusions that the returned evidence does not support. If a supplied manager name cannot be resolved, do not fall back to another manager or current crew; state that the selected manager is unavailable and do not present a different manager's brief.

## Answer guard

Apply this guard to the finished answer, including a simple property lookup. Use judgment about whether status updates are useful while working. Begin the finished answer with the requested work result after the allowed calls finish.

Never show an internal customer, property, jobsite, crew, source, application, inventory, line, or parent identifier or reference number. Never show a raw source URL, tool or operation name, API field, mode, call count, call limit, retry, or retrieval strategy. Task numbers and a user-selected form-response reference remain allowed. Outside the Morning Brief, treat a leading number in a returned property label as suspect: compare it with the returned property/jobsite identifier and the separate address field. Remove it when it is the identifier, even when the remaining property name contains `Rd`, `Ave`, or another street suffix. Preserve a leading street number when separate address evidence confirms it; do not decide from the property label alone. The Morning Brief API supplies its verified display name as described below. If a returned source is useful, link descriptive work text such as the jobsite or task name and ensure the visible label contains no internal identifier.

For a successful property/jobsite identification, write exactly: `The active jobsite is <property>, managed by <customer>. Address: <address>.` Stop after that sentence. Omit every other returned value, including all IDs and reference numbers.

## Manager operations brief

### Goal and presentation

Help the manager see today's crew workload, unresolved service, customer promises and the next useful conversation. Write an operations handoff that can be scanned in about two minutes during the day; for a rich result, aim for roughly 500–700 words. It is a selection of decisions, not a full task or route-exception inventory. Use four sections: **Today's activity**, **Recent activity**, **Customer updates**, **Things to look into**; omit empty sections. Lead each item with the work, not a recital of the task fields: “Oppo HQ — walkway branches and completion photo still outstanding (Task 384, Assigned; due June 3).” Integrate the saved description so the manager understands the work, then give one specific action. Shared crew, status and due-date context can be stated once for a group. Count only the items actually in that group.

Before those sections, give a short **First decisions** list of three to five actions that affect the day's crew workload, an unresolved prior-workday stop, a customer commitment, or a reported condition needing prompt attention. Name the property and person to contact, and put the decisive fact and action together in that list. Exceed five lead actions only for a separate immediate safety or property-damage issue that cannot be placed below without hiding a decision. Treat this as the handoff's selected priorities: once a task, stop, or customer request is in the lead, do not describe or recommend its issue again in a later section. A property name may still appear in today's complete per-crew route list without repeating that issue or action. Keep separate saved tasks at one property separate: choose the highest-value decision for the lead rather than combining several tasks into one multi-part instruction. Apart from the lead, allow at most two other distinct action lines and one grouped older-route question for a rich ordinary brief. Expand only when a separate urgent safety, property-damage, or customer commitment would otherwise be hidden. Later sections add only different work and the minimum coverage totals or route context needed to understand the day. The list is not a claim that every returned task or older route exception is included.

Within each lead item, keep the fact and the manager's step in separate adjacent paragraphs. Finish that item's action before moving to the next item; do not collect lead actions into a later section. For example, using only facts present in the response:

> Route 3 has no service recorded for three September 16 stops. Highland Manor is on Jose's route today.
>
> **Action: Ask Dave Johnson whether those September 16 stops were completed, skipped or rescheduled, and whether Highland Manor still needs work today.**

Within the same item, put each recommended step in its own paragraph beginning **Action:**. Use **Reminder:** for a saved instruction relevant before today's route or stop, such as reminding Jose to confirm irrigation priorities before leaving the yard. Bold the label and the short action, so a manager scanning the bold text can find whom to contact and what to ask. Do not add an action to routine work simply to fill this format.

### Understand the data

- `todayRouteSummary` gives today's per-crew stop counts and property lists. `todaySchedule` supplies stop order. Use those counts; a manager-wide total must equal their sum.
- In the Morning Brief, use the returned property name after the API removes a verified jobsite reference prefix. Preserve a leading number that may be part of the street address; `42259 Rio Nedo` is a street address, not a jobsite code. Do not reinsert a raw `M...` prefix from a saved note or another lookup.
- `tasksForToday` contains open tasks due today or matching both the assigned crew and property on today's route. It identifies opportunities, not promised work. Preserve **Estimate**, **Reported** and other saved statuses. Use the resolved typeName only as category context; the saved subject, description, history, checklist, attachments, and line items identify the specific work. The raw taskType code is not a work description.
- `taskAttention` contains other open work. Its dates, priority, type, description and assignment explain why it matters. A blank description does not erase useful context in another returned field.
- If you shorten or extract Morning Brief records before drafting, retain each discussed task's ID, exact property identity, crew leader, status and dates, current description and description history, checklist counts/items/states/notes, attachment descriptions and labels, and estimate/items-used lines, including every availability or truncation flag. Re-read the original task row before writing an action when the shortened record lacks any of those fields. For older service questions, retain crew, service date, exact property identity and coverage state; use returned source links internally to distinguish similarly named properties. A shared property or subject does not establish duplicate tasks.
- Before writing an action about a task, take its recipient from that task's saved crew leader or assignment. A crew named in route or visit evidence is not the task's assignee. If grouped tasks have different assignees, give them separate actions.
- A brief task includes bounded description history, checklist items, attachment descriptions, and estimate/items-used lines. Read these with the current description and status to understand its original purpose, later updates and current situation. Preserve the saved subject's work identity when a caption adds only location or context: a task titled "Broken pipe" near valves remains a broken-pipe task unless another saved field explicitly changes the diagnosis. Estimate lines describe planned scope; items-used lines describe saved actual use. Use an incomplete required step when it makes the next check specific; ask whether it was done rather than instructing the crew to close the task before completion is confirmed. A saved instruction to mark Complete when finished is conditional. Saved text and photo labels do not prove completed work or visual inspection. Attribute visual details to the saved caption or note (for example, “the photo caption says…”); do not say a photo shows something unless its image content was retrieved and inspected. State when evidence is unavailable or truncated; use the named-task workflow or task link when more detail is needed.
- Task sections return exact child counts with bounded previews. For a task selected for a brief decision, compare those counts and truncation flags with the returned description history, checklist, attachments, and estimate/items-used lines. When omitted detail could change the work identity, current situation, or next step, retrieve the complete named task by its returned task number and property, using the brief date as report context; the exact task lookup is not filtered by those dates. Do this before writing the action, without asking the manager to supply a new period. Available zero counts mean no saved items; unavailable detail remains unknown. Do not expand every routine task merely because a preview is capped.
- Before selecting tasks for a past `briefDate`, compare each `lastUpdatedLocal` with that date. Omit a later-updated task from the dated handoff unless another returned, dated field independently establishes the specific fact as of that morning. A due date alone does not establish that the task existed then. Task status, description and checklist are current saved state, not a historical snapshot. Do not use later-dated notes or attachments to recommend work for that morning. State once, when relevant, that saved task status may have changed since the brief date.
- `firstEffectiveLocal` is the earliest saved effective date across the task's history and the origin for its age. It does not date the described work, establish completion, or make the current description, checklist, or status true on a past brief date. Use the dated evidence rule above for historical handoffs.
- `previousWorkdayRouteCoverage` and its stop detail describe prior-workday coverage. Distinguish no service recorded from a recorded short or incomplete visit; neither establishes that a crew skipped a stop.
- `serviceExceptions` names older flagged actual visits and may include properties outside that crew/date's scheduled stops. `serviceExceptionRouteCoverage` counts the scheduled stops. Keep the coverage count separate from named visit questions; only call an exception a scheduled-stop gap when crew, date and property match a scheduled stop. If listed names outnumber the coverage gaps, do not present them as the gap list.
- `customerUpdateCandidates` supplies recently completed requests with the same bounded task-purpose and progress evidence: current description, description history, checklist items, attachment descriptions, and estimate/items-used lines. For a server-current brief, eligibility requires current Complete and a real latest saved entry into Complete within the current window; Closed is ineligible. Preserve the returned dated window for an intentionally dated brief. Read that evidence before describing what the customer requested or what was actually finished. The completion date is the latest saved entry into Complete; closedLocal is the entry into a configured closed workflow state. Neither date by itself establishes when field work occurred, and the current status governs after reopening. A completion date and current Closed status alone do not identify the work performed. A quantity-only completion note or generic photo labels do not establish that requested installation occurred; ask the assigned crew to confirm before telling the customer the installation is done. Outstanding customer requests or callbacks can also occur in the task sections; surface those according to their saved facts.
- Forms need an explicit actionable state. Ordinary materials and pesticide use belong in their separate workflows.

### Compose the handoff

**Today's activity.** Start with the total workload and one compact line per crew/leader, stop count and properties. When a task is due today but its property is absent from today's route, ask the assignee for its status now. Keep that confirmation separate from any later field visit; do not offer the next maintenance visit as a replacement deadline or suggest doing that property's work at other stops. Add only today's tasks that need a distinct decision or useful crew instruction not already in the lead; group routine tasks in one line only when their count or locations help the manager. Retain the task number, meaningful work description, property, crew and status for a task discussed individually. Explain route relevance once per group. Do not enumerate routine training, safety meetings, or reviewed work with no open decision.

Roll up homogeneous routine work by crew and count, retaining any locations needed to act: for example, two meter readings on Jose's route and nine on Cedric's. Individual numbers are optional for that roll-up. Keep a task separate when it has a distinct condition, saved instruction, priority, overdue date or decision; routine roll-up is not permission to hide those differences. Estimates at today's stops remain estimates. Do not turn them into authorized field work.

**Recent activity.** Give the prior workday's overall scheduled-versus-serviced coverage and each crew's scheduled and serviced counts in one compact statement, not a coverage table or stop-by-stop recap. The serviced count means scheduled stops with completed service recorded for that crew, property and day; do not call it the number of saved visit records, because incomplete visits are counted separately. Add unresolved stops only when they need a distinct action not already in the lead; do not repeat lead properties merely to explain the coverage total or add task references saying they are covered above. When an open task's saved description resolves the question raised by a coverage gap for the same property and evidence date, report the coverage fact once and use the task's specific saved follow-up; do not repeat the generic coverage question. Connect an unresolved property's appearance on today's routes when returned identity establishes the match. State both facts and the current crew; do not label today's visit a makeup visit, reassignment or resolution. Use calendar dates only; do not name a weekday.
