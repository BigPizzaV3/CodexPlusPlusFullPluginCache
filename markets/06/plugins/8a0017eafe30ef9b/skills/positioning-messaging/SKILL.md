## Step 1: Positioning inputs
- Competitive alternatives (what customers would use instead).
- Unique attributes the user has that alternatives lack.
- The value those attributes create for customers.
- The customers who care most about that value.
- The market category that makes the value obvious.

## Step 2: Positioning statement
"For [target customer] who [need], [brand] is the [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternative], it [main differentiator]."

## Step 3: Messaging hierarchy
1. **Core message** (one sentence).
2. **Three pillars**, each with a benefit, supporting features and proof (numbers, testimonials, guarantees, awards).
3. **Segment variations**: what to stress for each persona.
4. **Objection handling** lines.

## Step 4: Test the words
Use customer language from reviews, interviews and search queries. Check clarity with the five-second test: can a stranger say what it is, who it is for and why it is better?

## Step 5: Output
Positioning statement, messaging table, three tagline options and headline ideas for a homepage. Only use claims the user can prove.
