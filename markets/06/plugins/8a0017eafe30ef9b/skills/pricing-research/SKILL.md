## Step 1: Map the market price bands
| Competitor | Model | Entry price | Mid tier | Top tier | What is included |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
Group into budget, mid-market and premium bands. Ask the user for competitor prices or label estimates clearly.

## Step 2: Three price floors and ceilings
- **Cost floor**: unit cost plus target margin.
- **Competitor reference**: where similar offers sit.
- **Value ceiling**: the value or savings the customer gets.
The right price usually sits between the competitor reference and the value ceiling.

## Step 3: Measure willingness to pay
- **Van Westendorp**: four questions (too cheap, bargain, expensive, too expensive) to find an acceptable range.
- **Gabor-Granger**: purchase likelihood at set price points.
- **Conjoint** for trade-offs between features and price.
- **Live tests**: A/B price pages or offers, where legal and fair.

## Step 4: Pricing models
One-time, subscription, usage-based, tiered, freemium, per seat, bundles. Pick the model that grows with the value customers get.

## Step 5: Tier design
Three tiers often work well: a clear entry tier, a recommended middle tier and a premium anchor. Name tiers by customer outcome.

## Step 6: Output
Price band table, recommended price range with reasoning, tier structure and a test plan. Avoid illegal practices such as price fixing with competitors or misleading discounts.
