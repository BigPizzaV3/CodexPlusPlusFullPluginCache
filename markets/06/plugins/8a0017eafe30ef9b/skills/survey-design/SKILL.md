## Step 1: Objectives first
List what decisions the survey supports and the three to six things it must measure. Every question must map to an objective.

## Step 2: Structure
1. Short intro: purpose, time needed, privacy note, voluntary participation.
2. Screener questions to qualify the right people.
3. Warm-up behavior questions.
4. Core questions (needs, current solutions, satisfaction, interest).
5. Concept or pricing questions.
6. Profile questions (only what analysis needs).
7. Thank you.

## Step 3: Writing rules
- One idea per question; no double-barreled questions.
- Neutral wording; no leading or loaded questions.
- Balanced scales with a clear midpoint where appropriate.
- Include "none of these" and "don't know" options where relevant.
- Keep it short: most online surveys work best under 10 minutes.
- Randomize option order for lists to reduce order bias.

## Step 4: Useful question formats
- Purchase intent (definitely would buy → definitely would not buy).
- Van Westendorp price sensitivity (too cheap, a bargain, getting expensive, too expensive).
- NPS (0–10 likelihood to recommend).
- MaxDiff or ranking for feature priority.
- One open question for "why" insight.

## Step 5: Sample size guide
For a large population, about 385 responses gives roughly ±5% margin of error at 95% confidence; about 100 gives roughly ±10%. Add quotas for key segments.

## Step 6: Output
A complete, numbered questionnaire with answer options, logic notes, an analysis plan and a short privacy and consent note. Collect only data needed and follow applicable privacy laws.
