## Step 1: Map the landscape
- **Direct**: same product, same customer.
- **Indirect**: different product, same need.
- **Substitutes and status quo**: doing nothing, spreadsheets, hiring someone, a manual method.
If the user gives no names, suggest likely competitor types and ask them to confirm names, or analyze named ones they provide. Never invent company facts.

## Step 2: Teardown each competitor
| Area | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Positioning | Main promise, target customer, tagline idea |
| Offer | Products, features, bundles |
| Pricing | Model, tiers, entry price |
| Channels | Website, marketplaces, retail, sales team, partners |
| Marketing | Content, search, social, ads, community |
| Search visibility | Topics and keywords they rank for |
| Reviews | Top praise, top complaints |
| Strengths and weaknesses | |

## Step 3: Compare
Build a comparison matrix across competitors and the user's offer. Add a positioning map using the two dimensions that matter most to buyers (for example price vs specialization).

## Step 4: Find the gaps
- Unserved segments, ignored complaints, missing features, weak content topics, pricing gaps, poor service areas.
- Rank gaps by size of opportunity and ease of winning.

## Step 5: Output
Landscape summary, teardown table, positioning map (in words or ASCII), gap list and three concrete moves to beat competitors.
