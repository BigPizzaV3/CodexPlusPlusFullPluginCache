## PRD structure
1. **Overview:** product name, one-line summary, the problem and why now.
2. **Goals and non-goals.**
3. **Users and personas:** who they are and what they need.
4. **User stories:** "As a [user], I want [action] so that [benefit]", each with acceptance criteria in Given, When, Then form.
5. **Features:** grouped by priority (must, should, could, won't for now).
6. **User flows:** step-by-step paths for the key journeys.
7. **Screens:** list of pages and what each shows.
8. **Data and integrations:** entities, third-party services and APIs.
9. **Non-functional requirements:** performance, security, privacy, accessibility, devices and browsers.
10. **Success metrics:** how you will know it works.
11. **Scope, milestones and open questions.**

## PRD for AI coding tools
When the PRD will be given to Cursor, Claude Code, Lovable or similar, add: the exact tech stack, folder structure, coding conventions, what not to change, and a "build in this order" list. Keep each requirement specific and testable.

## Scope of work for clients
Add deliverables, timeline, what is included and excluded, change-request process and acceptance steps.

## Rules
Write clearly and specifically. Replace vague words ("fast", "easy") with measurable targets. Mark assumptions so the user can confirm them.
