## Step 1: Understand the existing building
Age, construction type, condition, heritage status (if listed or protected, special permission is usually needed), and the proposed new use.

## Step 2: Principles
- Keep what gives the building its character: facades, structure, roof forms, historic materials.
- New interventions can be clearly modern but respectful in scale and material.
- Repair before replacing; use compatible materials (for example, breathable lime-based finishes on old masonry where suitable, as advised by a conservation specialist).
- Plan new services with minimal damage.

## Step 3: Feasibility
- Does the structure suit the new loads? (structural engineer survey needed)
- Fire, access and code upgrades for the new use.
- Daylight and ventilation for new room types.
- Cost of repair vs new build.

## Step 4: Output
Conversion concept, what to keep and what to change, new layout, upgrade list, risks and a professional team checklist (architect, structural engineer, conservation specialist if heritage).
