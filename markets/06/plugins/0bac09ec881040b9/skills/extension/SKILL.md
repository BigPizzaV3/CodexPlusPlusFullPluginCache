## Step 1: Existing house
Current plan or description, structure type, roof type, plot space available, orientation, budget and what new space is needed.

## Step 2: Choose the extension type
| Type | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Rear extension | Bigger kitchen-dining-family room | Daylight to middle rooms |
| Side extension | Wider plan, utility, garage | Boundary rules |
| Upper floor addition | More bedrooms without losing garden | Existing structure capacity (engineer check required) |
| Loft / attic | Bedroom or office | Head height, stair space, insulation |
| Garage conversion | Office, bedroom, ADU | Parking loss, floor levels |
| Basement | Media, storage, gym | Waterproofing, cost, light wells |
| Sunroom / porch | Indoor-outdoor living | Overheating, glazing type |

## Step 3: Design rules
- Connect the new space to the existing plan naturally; avoid dark middle rooms (add rooflights or light wells).
- Match or deliberately contrast the existing style; do not half-match.
- Align floor levels and plan the joint between old and new.

## Step 4: Deliver
Before and after plans, room schedule, exterior appearance, materials, rough cost, and a checklist: structural engineer review, permits, neighbor or boundary rules.
