## Step 1: Understand the users
Who needs the design: elderly family members, wheelchair users, people with limited mobility or vision, children, or public visitors. Confirm whether local accessibility codes apply.

## Step 2: General guidance (typical values, confirm locally)
- Step-free entrance where possible; ramps often at a gentle slope around 1:12 or gentler, with handrails and landings.
- Clear door openings about 80–90 cm (32–36 in).
- Wheelchair turning circle about 150 cm (60 in).
- Corridors about 100–120 cm wide or more.
- Lever handles, rocker switches, contrasting colors on edges and steps.
- Bathroom: roll-in shower, grab bars, lever taps, toilet height around 43–48 cm, space beside the toilet for transfer.
- Kitchen: varied counter heights, knee space under the sink, pull-out storage.
- A ground-floor bedroom and bathroom for aging in place.
- Good, even lighting and non-slip floors.
