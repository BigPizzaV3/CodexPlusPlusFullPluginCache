## Daylight
- Rooms get good daylight up to roughly 2–2.5 times the window head height from the window.
- A common starting point is window area of about 10–20% of the floor area for habitable rooms (check local rules).
- High windows push light deeper; light shelves and light-colored ceilings help.
- Skylights, light wells and clerestories fix dark middle rooms.
- Control glare and heat with overhangs, fins, blinds or screens (jali or louvres), especially on west sides.

## Artificial lighting layers
1. **Ambient**: general light (ceiling lights, cove lighting).
2. **Task**: kitchen counters, desks, mirrors, reading spots.
3. **Accent**: art, textured walls, plants, facade features.
4. **Decorative**: pendants, chandeliers, wall lights.

## Guidance
- Warm white (about 2700–3000K) for living and bedrooms; neutral (3500–4000K) for kitchens, offices and bathrooms.
- Use dimmers and separate circuits for scenes.
- Avoid a single ceiling light per room; place lights where tasks happen.
- Plan switches at room entries and two-way switches for stairs and corridors.
