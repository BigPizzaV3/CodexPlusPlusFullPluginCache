## Step 1: Identify the climate
Ask for the city or region if not given. Classify: hot-dry, hot-humid, composite (hot summers, cool winters, monsoon), temperate, or cold.

## Step 2: Strategies by climate
| Climate | Key strategies |
|---|---|
| Hot-dry | Courtyards, thick walls (thermal mass), small west windows, shaded glazing, light roof colors, night ventilation, evaporative cooling, roof insulation |
| Hot-humid | Lightweight open plans, cross-ventilation, raised floors, big overhangs, ceiling fans, avoid heavy mass |
| Composite | Shade in summer, allow winter sun (proper overhang depth), insulation, operable windows, courtyards and verandas |
| Temperate | Good insulation, south glazing (north in Southern Hemisphere), balanced daylight |
| Cold | Compact form, high insulation, airtightness, sun-facing glazing, entry vestibules |

## Step 3: Specific tools
- Roof insulation and reflective roof finishes cut heat gain strongly in hot climates.
- Horizontal overhangs work on the sun-facing side; vertical fins or screens on east and west.
- Stack ventilation: high vents or a stair tower pull hot air out.
- Planting deciduous trees on the sun side gives summer shade and winter sun.
- Double glazing and shaded windows reduce loads in both heat and cold.

## Step 4: Output
A climate summary, the strategies applied to the user's design (room placement, window sizes, shading devices, materials), and the expected comfort and energy benefits in plain terms.
