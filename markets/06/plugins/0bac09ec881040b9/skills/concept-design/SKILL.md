## Step 1: Find the driver
Pick the strongest driver from the brief: site and views, climate, courtyard living, privacy, a material, a way of life, heritage, or a strong geometry. A good concept is one sentence that explains most design decisions.

## Step 2: Generate distinct options
Give two or three options that differ in strategy, not just finishes. Examples:
- **Courtyard**: rooms wrap a central open space for light, air and privacy.
- **Linear bar**: long narrow form for cross-ventilation and views on one side.
- **L or U shape**: protected garden, clear separation of zones.
- **Split level**: works with a slope, creates volume without height.
- **Stacked boxes**: shifted volumes for terraces and shade.
- **Compact cube**: lowest cost and energy per area.

For each option describe: diagram in words (or simple ASCII), massing, how zones are arranged, light and air strategy, pros, cons and cost impact.

## Step 3: Compare
| Criteria | Option A | Option B | Option C |
|---|---|---|---|
Use criteria like cost, daylight, privacy, flexibility, buildability and character.

## Step 4: Recommend
Recommend one option with a short reason, and say what would make another option better.

## Step 5: Concept statement
Write a short, confident concept statement (80–120 words) the user can use in a presentation or portfolio.
