## Step 1: Understand the project
- Identify the project type (new house, apartment, extension, renovation, commercial, interior, landscape, student project) and who the user is (homeowner, designer, architect, student, builder, agent, investor).
- Collect the essentials, but never block progress: plot size and shape, orientation (which side faces north or the road), number of floors, rooms needed, budget level, style, climate or city, and any fixed constraints.
- If key details are missing, state clear assumptions (for example "assumed 10 m × 20 m plot, road on the south") and design a first version. Offer to refine after.

## Step 2: Deliver a complete concept
Use this structure unless the user asks for one specific part:
1. **Design summary**: the big idea in two or three sentences.
2. **Site strategy**: setbacks, entry, parking, sun and wind, views, garden.
3. **Zoning**: public (living, dining, guest), private (bedrooms), service (kitchen, laundry, store, utility) and how they connect.
4. **Floor plan**: a clear text or ASCII plan per floor with approximate dimensions in the user's units (metric by default, feet if the user uses feet).
5. **Room schedule table**: room, size, area, key notes.
6. **Elevations and form**: massing, roof type, windows, style.
7. **Materials and finishes**: structure, walls, roof, floors, facade.
8. **Performance**: daylight, ventilation, shading, insulation for the climate.
9. **Rough cost range** by quality level, clearly marked as an estimate.
10. **Next steps**: what to confirm, and which professional to involve.

## Step 3: Design quality rules
- Keep circulation short and logical; avoid long corridors and rooms you must pass through to reach others.
- Give every habitable room natural light and cross-ventilation where possible.
- Check typical minimum sizes and clearances (see the room layout skill) and flag anything tight.
- Keep wet areas (kitchen, bathrooms, laundry) stacked or grouped to simplify plumbing.
- Keep structure simple: regular grids, aligned walls and columns between floors, reasonable spans.
- Match style, materials and budget; never propose luxury finishes for a tight budget without saying so.

## Step 4: Professional safety note
Give this plainly when the design will be built: the concept is for planning and visualization. Structural design, local building codes, zoning rules and permit drawings must be checked and prepared by a licensed architect or engineer in the user's area.

## Step 5: Offer the next move
Offer one or two useful next steps, such as a second floor plan option, an elevation description, a 3D render prompt, a cost breakdown, or seeing the idea on a real photo.
