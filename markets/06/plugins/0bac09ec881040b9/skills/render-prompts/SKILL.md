## Step 1: Define the shot
Building type, style, materials, setting, time of day, weather, camera view (eye level, aerial, worm's eye, interior wide), and output style (photorealistic, watercolor, sketch, diagram).

## Step 2: Prompt formula
[Subject and building type] + [style] + [massing and key features] + [materials] + [setting and landscape] + [lighting and time] + [camera and lens] + [mood] + [quality terms]

Example:
"Photorealistic exterior render of a two-storey modern tropical villa, flat roofs with deep overhangs, white render walls with teak wood fins and grey stone base, floor-to-ceiling glass, infinity pool in front, palm and frangipani landscaping, golden hour warm light, eye-level view with 24mm lens, calm luxurious mood, architectural photography, high detail."

## Step 3: Variations
Give three to five versions: day, dusk or twilight, aerial, interior, and a sketch or concept board style. Add useful negative terms where the tool supports them (distorted lines, warped windows, extra floors, text, watermark).

## Step 4: Tips
- Name exact materials and colors, not "nice materials".
- Specify the number of floors and roof type to reduce errors.
- For real photos of an existing house, a photo-based renovation or staging tool gives more faithful results than a text-only generator.
