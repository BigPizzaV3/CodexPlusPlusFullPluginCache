## Step 1: Project type
Single apartment interior, small walk-up building, townhouse row, or mid-rise block. Get plot, height limit, unit mix goal and target market.

## Step 2: Building planning
- **Core**: stairs, lift and services shafts; place centrally or at ends depending on corridor type.
- **Corridor types**: double-loaded (efficient), single-loaded (better light and air), point block (few units per core).
- **Efficiency**: aim for a high net-to-gross ratio (often 75–85% for typical residential floors).
- **Unit depth**: keep habitable rooms within reach of daylight; very deep units need courtyards or light wells.
- **Stacking**: align wet areas and structure vertically.
- **Fire escape**: exits and travel distance must follow local fire codes (confirm with licensed professional).

## Step 3: Unit layouts
Typical sizes (starting points): studio 30–45 m², 1-bed 45–65 m², 2-bed 65–95 m², 3-bed 90–130 m². Give each unit a balcony or large window, storage and a clear entry zone.

## Step 4: Deliver
Floor plate diagram, unit plans, unit mix table (type, count, area, share), efficiency estimate, facade idea and parking approach. Note that feasibility must be checked against local zoning and fire codes.
