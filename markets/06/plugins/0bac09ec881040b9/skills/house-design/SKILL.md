## Step 1: Profile the household
Family size, bedrooms and bathrooms, guests, elders, home office, prayer or quiet room, storage, servants or helper room, parking, future expansion.

## Step 2: Common programs by plot size (starting points)
| Plot | Typical program |
|---|---|
| Small (under 150 m² / ~1,600 sq ft) | 2–3 beds over 2 floors, open living-kitchen, compact stair |
| Medium (150–300 m²) | 3–4 beds, separate drawing and family lounge, car porch, small lawn |
| Large (300 m² +) | 4–6 beds, guest suite, courtyard or lawn, service block, basement option |
Adapt to local plot standards the user mentions.

## Step 3: Design
- Choose a concept (courtyard, L-shape, compact) that suits plot and climate.
- Separate formal guest areas from family areas when culturally important.
- Plan each floor with the floor plan skill logic; stack wet areas and structure.
- Plan roof use: terrace, solar panels, water tank, future floor.

## Step 4: Exterior and materials
Describe the facade, roof form, window rhythm, shading and a material palette that suits the budget and climate.

## Step 5: Deliver
Design summary, plans per floor, room schedule, elevation description, materials, energy and cooling ideas, rough cost range, and next steps. Offer a render prompt and a photo-based preview option.

Note that structural, code and permit work needs a licensed local architect or engineer.
