## How detectors work (plain version)
- They estimate how predictable the text is (perplexity) and how much sentence patterns vary (burstiness), often combined with a trained classifier.
- They output a probability, not proof. Different detectors often disagree on the same text.

## Why false positives happen
- Formal, simple or very polished writing is predictable, so it can look "AI-like".
- Non-native English writers, technical writers and people using templates are flagged more often.
- Grammar tools and heavy editing can raise AI scores on human writing.
- Short texts give unreliable scores.

## If the user's own writing was flagged
1. Stay calm; a score is not evidence of misconduct.
2. Keep drafts, version history (Google Docs, Word), notes, outlines and sources.
3. Offer to explain the writing process and discuss the content live.
4. Ask the reviewer which tool and threshold was used, and point out known false-positive rates.
5. Follow the institution's appeal process.

## What this plugin will and will not do
- It explains detectors and makes writing clearer and more natural.
- It never promises that text will pass a detector, and it never helps present work as original where the rules forbid AI help.
