# Blocking and Staging

64 cards. Also in the other stage: a shot has a frame too. These decide a frame: what is in it, how it is lit, and what it is made of.

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, what to avoid, and the prompt written out in full.

---

### The Floor Before the Frame

**Also called:** the blocking selector, the floor before the frame, where the bodies stand
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `block.` shelf. It does not describe a staging. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **which axis of the room the arrangement uses, what is between the bodies, and whether the arrangement holds or the change of position is the shot** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were read off the twenty `Yields to:` conditions the cards carry, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. **`block.` decides the floor. `comp.` decides the rectangle.** The test is mechanical: if the answer changes when somebody moves and the camera does not, it is a `block.` question; if it changes when the camera moves and nobody else does, it is a `comp.` one.
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so the staging that does reach the screen was built against the room, the head count and the turn in the scene, rather than assembled out of whatever positions were remembered.
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each**, and expect to leave holding several ids rather than one — **a staging is built, not picked.** Ask them of the bodies, not of the frame: how the arrangement reads once it is cropped is the other shelf's question, and answering it here produces a frame with nobody standing in it. **And before the first question, ask a prior one: has the script already chosen?** If the scene is at a table, in a car, on a phone, in a fight or in a kiss, the shelf has a card with that name and the three questions have nothing left to decide.
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all
**Avoid when:** **More than half of this shelf sits on no pointer at all, and this map is honest only about the part that forks.** The three questions place every card, but placement is not a choice: where two cards in one shortlist are named after different furniture, the map is describing your scene back to you rather than offering an alternative, and treating that shortlist as a menu is how a staging gets picked instead of built. Also avoid it in **observational documentary and reportage**, where the bodies were not placed by you and the whole shelf becomes a vocabulary for describing what happened rather than a set of decisions. And **do not use it to fix a frame** — if the complaint is about crop, margin, balance or what the lens sees, the answer is on `comp.`, and reaching for a staging card instead moves people around a room to solve a problem that was in the rectangle.
**Prompt:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

---

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — Which axis of the room does this arrangement use: across the frame, into it, up and down it, or is it the turn?**
Asked first because the shelf names it itself: `block.lateral_staging`, `block.depth_staging` and `block.vertical_staging` are three cards written as the three axes of the same decision, and the conditions confirm it — *"Long lens flattens it, or the scene needs intimacy"* (`block.lateral_staging`, which names two different reasons in one line), *"In a vertical frame, where the size relationship breaks"* (`block.depth_staging`), *"The scene needs intimacy, not formal distance"* (`block.tableau`), *"The background event must carry meaning"* (`block.background_life`) and its exact reverse in `block.fg_bg_counterpoint`. Answer *into it* and you have committed to depth of field and separation light before you have cast anybody.

**Axis 2 — Between the bodies: are they with each other, against each other, is a thing between them, or is the room the other party?**
Six conditions turn on this and they are stated as facts about the scene, not as feelings — *"The scene needs confrontation, not closeness"* (`block.shoulder_to_shoulder`), *"A solid barrier would hide bodies and hands"* (`block.barrier_between`), *"Real fear between them makes closeness a lie"* (`block.kneel_to_level`), *"A dramatic scene that needs relationships"* (`block.the_line`), *"The scene has not earned the glorification"* (`block.hero_group_walk`), *"Deep emotional dialogue needs stillness"* (`block.walk_and_talk`). **The fourth value is not a leftover.** A large part of this shelf stages one body against a space rather than two bodies against each other, and separating those out is what stops the map from inventing a second person.

**Axis 3 — Does the arrangement hold, or is the change of position the shot?**
Seven conditions are about duration and about who supplies the movement — *"The actor cannot or should not move"* (`block.actor_moves_camera_static`), *"Quiet scene where the performance carries the change"* (`block.camera_moves_actor_static`), *"The block must be sustained, not fast"* (`block.walk_past_reveal`), *"The reveal should be fast, not sustained"* (`block.body_occlusion_reveal`), *"The empty space is not worth holding"* (`block.lead_into_frame`), *"The exit should resolve rather than hang"* (`block.walk_away_stop`). It is asked last because it is the axis that splits the shelf almost exactly in half and therefore disqualifies least — and because its third value holds only two cards, which is itself the finding recorded below.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `block.master_plan` drawn first, with `block.depth_staging` as the arrangement inside it — and the reasoning has to be different here, because the usual measurement is unavailable.** On most shelves the default is argued from in-degree. On `block.` **no card is the target of more than one pointer.** Every inbound edge lands on a different card, and the shelf is therefore silent about what it falls back on. In-degree cannot name a default where in-degree is flat, and a selector that reported the joint-first card as the default would be reporting an accident of ordering.

So the default is argued from the cards themselves. `block.master_plan` is the only card on the shelf that states its own position in the sequence — it is the first step in any scene with more than two people or more than one turning point, and everything else is drawn on top of it. `block.depth_staging` is the only one of the three axis cards that anything falls back to: `block.lateral_staging` sends you there on **two distinct conditions in a single line** — a long lens that flattens the lateral arrangement, and a scene that needs intimacy — and `block.depth_staging` in turn hands off to `vertical.stacked_two_shot` when the frame goes vertical. Of the three axes the shelf names, that is the one it argues about.

**The one against it is `block.same_frame_two_worlds`.** It is the target of nothing and the source of nothing, and it inverts the shelf's working assumption. Every other card here arranges bodies so that the frame states **one** relationship — with, against, or separated by a thing. `block.same_frame_two_worlds` puts two people in one frame and stages them so that they are not in the same scene at all: different light, different distance, no shared geometry, and the two-shot stops being a two-shot while remaining one. Its only stated cost is that the difference has to be strong, and a weak version collapses into an ordinary two-shot — which a single decisive separation pays for. Its cousin against the grain is `block.back_of_room`, likewise on no edge, which puts the person who matters where nobody in the scene is looking.

**The unasked**

**Forty-one of the reachable cards are the target of no `Yields to:` field anywhere in the package, and thirty-three of them sit on no pointer at all — neither reaching out nor being reached.** More than half this shelf is disconnected. That is not a gap in the audit: every card was read and every one is accounted for. It is a fact about what the pair field can ask.

**The shape of the list is the finding, and it has two parts.**

**First, sixteen of the isolated cards are named after a piece of furniture or a scripted event:** `block.dinner_table` · `block.car_geometry` · `block.phone_call` · `block.kiss_geometry` · `block.interrogation_geometry` · `block.fight_geography` · `block.chase_geography` · `block.doorway_threshold` · `block.window_staging` · `block.procession` · `block.surrounded_circle` · `block.push_through_crowd` · `block.static_in_moving_crowd` · `block.back_of_room` · `block.circling_standoff` · `block.empty_half_bed`. **Nothing routes to them because there is nothing to route.** The pair field asks *in situation X, not this card — that one*, and no situation makes a dinner table into a car. The script chose, not the shelf. **A card named after the room cannot have an alternative on its own shelf**, and a reader looking for one writes a craft warning instead — *keep them readable*, *vary it with the mirrors*, *do not cut on the contact* — which is precisely the shape the audit files as `execution-note`.

**Second, the rest of the isolated block is additive:** `block.cross` · `block.counter` · `block.reach_withdraw` · `block.step_into_light` · `block.turn_to_camera` · `block.back_then_turn` · `block.sit_stand_shift` · `block.hands_insert` · `block.approach_camera` · `block.pacing` · `block.depth_swap` · `block.eyeline_levels` · `block.side_reversal` · `block.mirror_pair` · `block.clear_frame_and_hold` · `block.same_frame_two_worlds` · `block.master_plan`. These are single moves, and one scene uses four of them in sequence. Asked *instead of this, which?* the honest answer is *all of them, in order*, and a field with one slot cannot hold it.

**So the thing this shelf is missing is not a card. It is a field.** Nothing here stages a scene as a **sequence** of positions. `block.master_plan` gestures at it — draw the scene from above, mark where each actor stands and when they move — and then the shelf hands you its remaining single positions with no card that says how one becomes the next. The nearest thing that exists, `block.ensemble_oner`, records the failure in its own `Avoid when:` — a take that is only a display of skill makes the viewer watch the technique and forget the story — without anything routing a reader there to read it. **The new card this shelf wants is the one that turns a list of positions into a path**, and the map above cannot produce it, because all three of its questions are asked of one moment.

**And one more thing the graph shows: not a single pointer crosses between this shelf and `comp.`, in either direction.** Two shelves that decide the same picture, adjacent in the same phase, and no reader on either side ever reached across. Nine subjects are held twice, once here and once there — `block.three_actor_triangle` against `comp.triangle`, `block.the_line` against `comp.grouping`, `block.shoulder_to_shoulder` against `comp.two_faces_one_away`, `block.body_occlusion_reveal` against `comp.dirty_frame`, `block.depth_staging` against `comp.depth_layers`, `block.behind_glass` against `comp.shooting_through`, `block.doorway_threshold` against `comp.frame_within_frame`, `block.barrier_between` against `comp.vertical_bars`, `block.empty_half_bed` against `comp.empty_chair`. `COLLISIONS.md` records no ruling on any of them. The line holds — `block.` is where the body is, `comp.` is what the rectangle does with it — and since September 2026 `SHELVES.md` §2b writes it down and pairs all nine twins: stage it here, then frame it there.

### The Master Blocking Plan

**Also called:** blocking plan, staging map  
**What it is:** Before shooting you draw the scene from above: where each actor stands, when they move, where the camera sits, and every cut in the scene comes out of that map.  
**Effect on the audience:** The scene feels built rather than merely covered. The viewer understands the space unconsciously and believes everything that follows.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The first step in any scene with more than two people or more than one turning point. Essential in features and series, and essential in AI work because you write the positions into the prompt and they have to stay consistent from shot to shot.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Docudrama / Reenactment, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Soap Opera / Telenovela | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** A rigid plan kills what an actor brings in the moment. Draw it as a foundation and leave room for it to change in rehearsal.  
**Prompt:** `two characters start on opposite sides of the room, she crosses to the window at the midpoint, he stays seated, camera holds`  
`block.master_plan`

### Staging in Depth (Z Axis)

**Also called:** depth blocking, staging on the Z axis  
**What it is:** You place the actors one behind the other along the lens axis instead of lining them up left to right, so the near one is large and the far one small.  
**Effect on the audience:** A power relationship readable in one frame: the near figure dominates, the far one is weakened or excluded, and the shot feels deep with no camera move.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The boss and the subordinate, the father and the son, the detective and the suspect, the character receiving news from across a room. It lets you play a whole scene without a cut, and it is the first answer when one shot has to carry a scene in anime or in generated video.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Short Film, Limited Series, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Docudrama / Reenactment, Stop Motion | genres: Drama, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Historical / Biopic, War, Tragedy, Noir / Neo-Noir, Mystery / Whodunit, Seinen (mature anime), Political Thriller  
**Avoid when:** It needs enough depth of field and separation lighting or the far figure melts away. In a vertical frame the space is tight, so watch the size relationship.  
**Prompt:** `staged in depth, the man large in the foreground facing away, the woman small and sharp in the doorway behind him`  
**Yields to:** `vertical.stacked_two_shot` — In a vertical frame, where the size relationship breaks.  
`block.depth_staging`

### Lateral Staging (X Axis)

**Also called:** sideways staging, profile staging  
**What it is:** The actors move left and right on a single plane parallel to the camera, which either tracks with them laterally or stays locked while they cross the frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** It plays like theatre or a wall relief: the relationship reads through the distance between them rather than through size, and the image feels ordered and deliberate.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The walk side by side, the argument that moves around a room, the corridor fight. Essential in heritage work because it echoes the reliefs, and essential in anime and side scrolling game language.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Short Film, Music Video, Drama Series Episode, AI-Generated Clip, Stop Motion | genres: Drama, Action, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Martial Arts / Wuxia, Samurai / Chanbara, Shonen Battle (anime), Comedy, Western, Mythology / Ancient Epic  
**Avoid when:** On a long lens the depth disappears entirely and the image flattens into a world with no space. In intimate scenes lateral movement across the plane pushes the viewer away; two people held still side by side, looking the same way, is a different staging, `block.shoulder_to_shoulder`, and that one is built for the hard confession.  
**Prompt:** `lateral staging, both men moving left to right along one plane, camera tracking sideways with them, flat background`  
**Example:** Oldboy, the corridor fight staged side on in a single plane.  
**Yields to:** `block.depth_staging` — Long lens flattens it, or the scene needs intimacy.  
`block.lateral_staging`

### Vertical Staging (Y Axis)

**Also called:** staging on stairs, height staging  
**What it is:** You distribute characters at different heights: stairs, a balcony, a roof, a ladder, a platform, a stairwell, so the height itself carries the information.  
**Effect on the audience:** Social or moral hierarchy without dialogue. Whoever is above has power or safety, and whoever is below is asking or losing.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Negotiation between two classes, the meeting on the stairs, the leader on the balcony with the crowd below, the family living below street level. Essential in social drama and in heritage work when the ziggurat stair or the throne dais is in the scene.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Short Film, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Music Video | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Political Thriller, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Crime / Procedural, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Thriller / Suspense, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Seinen (mature anime)  
**Avoid when:** If the height does not match the power relationship in the scene, you create a silent contradiction and the viewer senses something is off.  
**Prompt:** `she stands three steps above him on the staircase, he looks up from the landing, both in the same frame`  
**Example:** Parasite, the stairs used to separate the two households.  
`block.vertical_staging`

### The Depth Swap

**Also called:** position swap, exchanging planes  
**What it is:** Inside the scene and without a cut, the person in the foreground moves back and the one behind comes forward.  
**Effect on the audience:** A power shift you feel physically. The viewer watches control pass from one person to the other with nobody saying a word.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The confession that flips the scene, the interrogator who gets exposed, the son who finally stands up to his father. The best use of a long single take, and it works in anime and generated video because it is one clear physical event inside one shot.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Docudrama / Reenactment | genres: Drama, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Tragedy, Political Thriller, Noir / Neo-Noir, Psychological / Arthouse, Mystery / Whodunit, Seinen (mature anime)  
**Avoid when:** A swap with no dramatic cause reads as empty actor movement. It has to land on the line that turns the scene.  
**Prompt:** `he steps back into the shadow while she walks forward into the foreground, one continuous take, they exchange positions`  
`block.depth_swap`

### Actor Moves, Camera Holds

**Also called:** static camera blocking, camera locked  
**What it is:** The camera never moves, and every change in the frame comes from the actor moving inside the composition: coming closer, pulling back, crossing, sitting.  
**Effect on the audience:** Confidence and calm. The viewer observes instead of being led, and when the actor comes closer the intimacy hits harder than any camera push.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Long dialogue scenes, comedy, observational documentary, and any scene where the performance should be the event. Ideal in low budget work and in generated video, because actor movement is easier to control than camera movement.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Mockumentary, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Stop Motion, Experimental / Art Film | genres: Drama, Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Verite / Observational, Tragedy, Slice of Life, Psychological / Arthouse, Horror, Family  
**Avoid when:** If the actor does not move, a locked frame goes dead. Stillness in the camera requires movement inside the frame.  
**Prompt:** `locked off camera, she walks from the back of the room into a close-up and stops, the frame never moves`  
**Yields to:** `block.camera_moves_actor_static` — The actor cannot or should not move.  
`block.actor_moves_camera_static`

### Camera Moves, Actor Holds

**Also called:** moving camera on a still subject  
**What it is:** The actor stays exactly where they are and the camera is the thing that circles, pushes in or slides past.  
**Effect on the audience:** It turns the focus inward. The actor's stillness reads as paralysis or resolve, while the camera movement says the world around them is changing.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The moment of shock, the moment of decision, the character who hears the news and does not move. Strong in suspense and music video, and in documentary around an artefact or a statue, which cannot move anyway.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Music Video, Trailer / Teaser, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Anime (Japanese 2D) | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Horror, Psychological / Arthouse, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mystery / Whodunit, Action, Tragedy  
**Avoid when:** Move the actor and the camera at once in a quiet scene and the meaning dissolves; the movement just feels unmotivated.  
**Prompt:** `he stands completely still in the centre while the camera arcs a slow half circle around him`  
**Yields to:** `block.actor_moves_camera_static` — Quiet scene where the performance carries the change.  
`block.camera_moves_actor_static`

### The Choreographed Ensemble Oner

**Also called:** ensemble long take, choreographed one-shot  
**What it is:** A whole scene in one unbroken take where camera and cast are choreographed together: one person leaves, another arrives, and the camera hands off from group to group.  
**Effect on the audience:** Real time and real space. The viewer cannot suspect an edit trick, and the tension builds because no cut ever relieves it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The party, the kitchen, the hospital, the market, the moment of organised chaos. Spectacular in openings and in series when you must introduce a place and its people at the same time.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Concert / Live Event | genres: Drama, Crime / Procedural, War, Comedy, Action, Political Thriller, Medical Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Musical, Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** If the take is only a display of skill with no dramatic reason, the viewer watches the technique and forgets the story.  
**Prompt:** `continuous single take, the camera leaves the couple at the bar, follows a waiter through the kitchen and finds the chef`  
**Yields to:** `scene.master_to_coverage_rhythm` — The take has no dramatic reason.  
`block.ensemble_oner`

### The Walk and Talk

**Also called:** walking dialogue, corridor walk, pedeconference, corridor two-hander  
**What it is:** Two or more characters talk while walking, a Steadicam moving with them either facing them or behind, and other people join and drop out of the conversation as they go, handing over short pieces of information. The West Wing team established it as a complete series language.  
**Effect on the audience:** Energy and urgency. Administrative dialogue comes alive, and the viewer feels the world is busy and the clock is running.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Corridor scenes in hospitals, ministries, police stations and studios, the explanation of a plan before it is executed, and any place you must deliver a lot of information without the viewer feeling the weight. Essential in political, medical and legal series, and excellent in corporate video and vlogs because it makes exposition feel alive rather than lectured.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Feature Film, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Corporate / Training, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Explainer, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Docudrama / Reenactment, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Political Thriller, Medical Drama, Legal Drama, Drama, Crime / Procedural, Comedy, Thriller / Suspense, Mystery / Whodunit, Dark Comedy / Satire  
**Avoid when:** With no reason to be walking it reads as movement for its own sake, an empty corridor where nobody passes turns it into a long lifeless walk, and the corridor has to be long and wide enough or the camera ends up cramped and bouncing. Do not use it for deep emotional dialogue either; the motion stops the scene from settling.  
**Prompt:** `tracking backwards in front of two people walking fast down a hospital corridor, talking, others joining and peeling off`  
**Example:** The West Wing, the corridor walk and talks written by Aaron Sorkin.  
**Yields to:** `block.shoulder_to_shoulder` — Deep emotional dialogue needs stillness.  
`block.walk_and_talk`

### The Cross

**Also called:** crossing the frame, actor cross  
**What it is:** An actor moves from one side of the frame to the other, or crosses in front of another actor and briefly cuts the line of sight.  
**Effect on the audience:** It changes the composition without a cut and works as punctuation: the cross says one idea has ended and another is starting.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Your basic tool for keeping a long dialogue scene alive with no camera movement. Land it exactly on the pivotal line, and in theatrical staging and sitcoms it solves the problem of actors standing frozen.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Stop Motion, Limited Series | genres: Drama, Comedy, Romance, Family, Crime / Procedural, Legal Drama, Melodrama, Slice of Life, Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** A cross with no motive distracts, and crossing in front of a face on the performance beat steals the shot.  
**Prompt:** `he crosses the frame from right to left behind her on the key line, she stays seated and still`  
`block.cross`

### The Counter

**Also called:** countering, counter-move  
**What it is:** When one actor moves, the other shifts a little in the opposite direction so they stay visible to camera and the composition stays balanced.  
**Effect on the audience:** The scene feels natural and both remain legible. Without it one actor disappears behind the other and the frame breaks.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every scene with two people moving inside a room, and any single take that carries a full conversation. Essential in daily drama and sitcoms, where time is short and the shot must work on the first take.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Feature Film, Short Film, Limited Series, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Stop Motion | genres: Drama, Comedy, Romance, Melodrama, Family, Legal Drama, Medical Drama, Slice of Life  
**Avoid when:** If the counter becomes obvious the viewer feels the actors are dancing for the camera. Keep it small and motivated.  
**Prompt:** `as he steps left she shifts slightly right to stay clear of him, both faces kept visible in the same frame`  
`block.counter`

### Entrances and Exits

**Also called:** entering frame, exiting frame  
**What it is:** You let a character enter or leave the frame instead of already being there when the shot starts.  
**Effect on the audience:** An entrance gives you an event and a beginning, an exit gives you a conclusion and a void. Both give the scene rhythm without editing.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Introducing a new character, tipping the balance of a scene by having someone arrive, and closing a scene on a slow exit. Very useful in generated video, because entering frame is a clear action the model understands and it yields a living shot.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** If every shot begins with an entrance and ends with an exit the scene turns into a stage play and drags.  
**Prompt:** `the frame starts empty on the kitchen, she walks in from the left, stops at the sink, and the shot holds`  
`block.entrance_exit`

### Clear the Frame and Hold

**Also called:** empty frame hold, hold on the empty space  
**What it is:** The character leaves the shot and the camera stays on the empty space for a beat or two before the cut.  
**Effect on the audience:** The residue of absence. The empty space still holds the person who left, the viewer notices the place itself, and a sense of ending or loneliness usually arrives.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of a farewell, a dismissal or a death, and the last shot of an episode. Strong in drama, documentary and anime, where the empty place shot sits between scenes.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Anime (Japanese 2D), Heritage / Historical Documentary, Music Video, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Melodrama, Slice of Life, Psychological / Arthouse, War, Romance, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Horror  
**Avoid when:** Held too long in fast content (reels, ads) the viewer scrolls away. Shorten it for fast channels.  
**Prompt:** `she walks out of frame left and the camera holds on the empty room for three seconds before the cut`  
`block.clear_frame_and_hold`

### Lead the Character Into Frame

**Also called:** reveal by movement, frame the empty space first  
**What it is:** You frame the space first and the character walks in and settles exactly into the composed position, so the composition waits for the actor rather than chasing them.  
**Effect on the audience:** A sense of design and inevitability. The place was ready for this person, which gives the shot weight and intention.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The hero entering a new place, the character stepping into a temple, an office or a grave, and the start of big scenes. Excellent in heritage documentary, advertising and generated video, because a move to a defined mark is easy to describe in a prompt.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Commercial / TVC, Trailer / Teaser, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Music Video, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Fantasy / Epic, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Thriller / Suspense, Faith / Biblical Epic, Western, Sci-Fi  
**Avoid when:** If the space is not worth waiting on, the empty beat before the entrance just reads as slack.  
**Prompt:** `the frame is composed on the empty temple doorway, she walks in from the right and stops exactly on the centre line`  
**Yields to:** `block.entrance_exit` — The empty space is not worth holding.  
`block.lead_into_frame`

### The Approach to Camera

**Also called:** walking into the lens, approach to close-up  
**What it is:** The character walks from the depth of the scene toward the lens until they are very close, with the camera locked, so the shot size changes through movement rather than through a cut.  
**Effect on the audience:** Threat or resolution. The viewer cannot retreat, the proximity grows on them physically, and the figure doubles in size until it fills the screen.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The villain arriving, the beat of decision before a fight, the end of a scene landing on a face. Essential in trailers and reels because the scale change holds attention with no cut, and excellent in generated video because it is one clear action.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Feature Film, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Commercial / TVC, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Action, Thriller / Suspense, Horror, Crime / Procedural, Fantasy / Epic, Shonen Battle (anime), Superhero, War, Sports Drama  
**Avoid when:** Stop too close and the focus breaks while a wide lens distorts the face. Set a clear mark for the stop.  
**Prompt:** `he walks from deep in the corridor straight toward the lens and stops in a tight close-up, camera locked off`  
`block.approach_camera`

### The Walk-Past Reveal

**Also called:** pass by reveal, body wipe reveal  
**What it is:** A person or object passes close to the lens and blanks the frame for a moment, and when it clears a new situation is revealed: another person, a different place, or something that has changed.  
**Effect on the audience:** A clean surprise with no effects. The viewer feels time jumped or that something appeared out of nowhere.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Revealing someone who was standing behind, changing the time of day inside one shot, and switching between two states in a commercial. Very useful in reels and music video because it is an in camera transition that saves you a cut.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Trailer / Teaser, Feature Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Meme / Comedy Edit, Fashion Film | genres: Comedy, Action, Thriller / Suspense, Romance, Musical, Horror, Fantasy / Epic, Crime / Procedural  
**Avoid when:** A slow pass or a visible gap looks cheap. It must black out the whole frame and move fast enough.  
**Prompt:** `a man in a dark coat passes right across the lens filling the frame, and when he clears she is standing where the room was empty`  
**Yields to:** `block.body_occlusion_reveal` — The block must be sustained, not fast.  
`block.walk_past_reveal`

### The Turn to Camera

**Also called:** direct address, look to lens  
**What it is:** The character turns and looks straight down the lens, either with a brief glance or with a line addressed to the viewer.  
**Effect on the audience:** The wall breaks. The viewer shifts from observer to participant, and the feeling is intense: complicity in comedy, threat in horror.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Comedy that confides in the audience, documentary and direct interview, and an episode ending on a look that expects no answer. Extremely strong in reels, because looking into the lens is the native language of the platform.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Interview / Video Podcast, Mockumentary, Feature Film, Commercial / TVC, Meme / Comedy Edit, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Drama, Horror, Psychological Horror, Crime / Procedural, Rom-Com, Verite / Observational, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** In naturalistic drama a look to lens breaks the illusion and becomes a mistake. Use it once in the whole film or not at all.  
**Prompt:** `mid conversation she turns her head and looks straight into the lens, holds the look for two seconds, then turns back`  
**Example:** Fleabag, the direct looks and asides to camera.  
`block.turn_to_camera`

### Back to Camera, Then the Turn

**Also called:** reveal turn, back of the head reveal  
**What it is:** The shot opens on the character's back or the back of their head, the face stays hidden for a few seconds, and then they turn and the face or the expression is revealed.  
**Effect on the audience:** Anticipation, cheap to stage and highly effective. The viewer wants the face, and the turn itself becomes an event.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Introducing an important character, the reaction beat after news, and presenting the villain. Essential in trailers, music video and reels because it creates suspense in the first two seconds, and excellent in heritage work because it keeps the face hidden until the chosen moment.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video, Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Anime (Japanese 2D), Vertical Micro-Drama, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Title Sequence / Opening Credits | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Action, Crime / Procedural, Horror, Drama, Fantasy / Epic, Shonen Battle (anime), Superhero, Mystery / Whodunit, Heritage (civilisation-focused)  
**Avoid when:** If the face is not worth the wait, the turn lands as an anticlimax. Repeat it inside the same episode and the trick is exposed.  
**Prompt:** `opens on the back of his head as he faces the window, he turns slowly to camera revealing the scar, medium close-up`  
`block.back_then_turn`

### The Doorway Threshold

**Also called:** threshold staging, standing in the door  
**What it is:** The character stops in the doorway itself, neither inside nor out, framed by the door, and the scene turns on whether they step through or step back.  
**Effect on the audience:** A decision made physical. The threshold is the border between two worlds, standing on it means hesitation, and crossing it means there is no way back.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Entering the room of the dead, coming home after years, stepping into a temple or an operating theatre. Powerful in drama, horror and heritage work, and ending a scene on someone standing in a doorway carries meaning with no dialogue at all.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Docudrama / Reenactment, Anime (Japanese 2D), Limited Series, AI-Generated Clip, Music Video | genres: Drama, Horror, Tragedy, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Gothic / Vampire, Thriller / Suspense, Family, Faith / Biblical Epic, Romance  
**Avoid when:** If a character stops in every doorway it becomes a tic. And watch the light: a doorway gives hard contrast and blows out the background.  
**Prompt:** `she stops exactly in the doorway, half in the dark hall and half in the lit room, hand still on the frame`  
**Example:** The Godfather, the door closing on Kay at the end.  
`block.doorway_threshold`

### Staging at the Window

**Also called:** at the window, looking out  
**What it is:** The character stands or sits at the window looking out, with the camera either behind them in the room or outside, through the glass.  
**Effect on the audience:** Longing, waiting and confinement. The window promises another world and the character is inside this one, not in it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Waiting for the absent, thinking before a decision, watching the street, the beat of illness or quarantine. Essential in melodrama and period drama, and in heritage work a mud brick window or lattice gives a shape and a light you cannot fake.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Docudrama / Reenactment, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Limited Series | genres: Melodrama, Drama, Romance, Tragedy, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), War, Gothic / Vampire, Psychological / Arthouse, Shojo (anime romance)  
**Avoid when:** A cliche when staged for no reason. Either there is something outside worth looking at, or the emptiness itself is the point.  
**Prompt:** `she stands at the window with her back to camera looking down at the street, soft daylight on her shoulders, room dark behind`  
`block.window_staging`

### The Mirror Scene

**Also called:** playing to the mirror, mirror staging  
**What it is:** You stage the actors so we see one directly and the other in the mirror, or both at once: a real face and a reflected face inside the same frame. It differs from the structural Mirror Scene, which repeats an entire scene in the same composition at a later point in the story: here the mirror is a real object in the frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** It gives you two faces in one shot with no cut, and it carries the sense that one of them is not quite present or is playing a part.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The truth scene in a bathroom or bedroom, getting ready for a confrontation, the couple where one talks to the mirror instead of to the other. Strong in drama, crime and psychological thriller.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Music Video, Vertical Micro-Drama, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Fashion Film | genres: Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Crime / Procedural, Noir / Neo-Noir, Thriller / Suspense, Romance, Psychological Horror, Melodrama, Gothic / Vampire  
**Avoid when:** It needs careful angle planning so the camera and crew do not appear, and any alignment error puts the lens in the shot.  
**Prompt:** `he stands at the mirror talking, we see his real profile on the left and her reflected face watching him on the right`  
**Yields to:** `scene.mirror_scene` — The mirroring is structural, across the story.  
`block.mirror_scene`

### The Mirror Pair

**Also called:** mirrored blocking, matched posture  
**What it is:** You stage two characters in matched and opposed postures: the same angle, the same height, the same hand, as though each is the reflection of the other.  
**Effect on the audience:** It says the two are versions of the same thing: an antagonist who mirrors the hero, or a son becoming his father, with no dialogue at all.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The confrontation between hero and rival, and the generational scene where father and son hold the same posture. Powerful in anime, epics and heritage work when you want to tie a king to his predecessor, or a statue to a living person.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Trailer / Teaser, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Shonen Battle (anime), Action, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Drama, Fantasy / Epic, Tragedy, Superhero, Martial Arts / Wuxia  
**Avoid when:** Matched too exactly it looks artificial, almost like a dance. Leave a small difference so it stays human.  
**Prompt:** `the two men stand facing each other in identical stance, same angle and same raised hand, symmetrical two shot`  
`block.mirror_pair`

### Staging Behind Glass

**Also called:** glass partition staging, through the window  
**What it is:** A glass barrier stands between two characters, or between the character and the camera: an office partition, a car window, a prison visiting window, a shop front, a hospital incubator.  
**Effect on the audience:** Impossible closeness. The viewer sees everything and hears less, and the reflections mix the two worlds so the intrusion is visual and emotional at once.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The prison visit, the parent and the sick child, the employee watching the meeting that decides their fate, watching from the rain. Very strong in drama and crime, and it lets one shot carry an entire scene.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Vertical Micro-Drama, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Drama, Crime / Procedural, Tragedy, Medical Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Melodrama, Romance, Noir / Neo-Noir, Political Thriller  
**Avoid when:** Reflections can bury the faces completely. You must control the light on both sides or you lose the performance.  
**Prompt:** `they face each other through a scratched glass partition, her reflection overlapping his face, both hands on the glass`  
`block.behind_glass`

### The Barrier Between Them

**Also called:** obstacle staging, table between  
**What it is:** You place something physical between the two characters inside the frame: a table, a desk, a car, a fire, a wall, a sleeping child, and you keep the barrier in every setup.  
**Effect on the audience:** Psychological distance becomes visible. The viewer measures the relationship by the barrier, and any attempt to cross it becomes a major event.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The negotiation, the couple after the fight, the interrogation, the father and son who have stopped speaking. Plan the moment the barrier is crossed, a hand reaching over the table, as the peak of the scene.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Limited Series, Short Film, Docudrama / Reenactment, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Drama, Crime / Procedural, Melodrama, Legal Drama, Romance, Tragedy, Thriller / Suspense, Family, Political Thriller  
**Avoid when:** If the barrier covers the actors' bodies or crops the hands, you lose the physical language the scene depends on.  
**Prompt:** `the wide table between them in every angle, neither leaning across it, the empty tabletop dominant in the frame`  
**Yields to:** `block.behind_glass` — A solid barrier would hide bodies and hands.  
`block.barrier_between`

### Business With Props

**Also called:** stage business, handling props  
**What it is:** You give the actor a manual task during the dialogue: pouring tea, cleaning a weapon, peeling an apple, sorting papers, sewing, rolling a cigarette.  
**Effect on the audience:** Truth and subtext. The character feels real because they are occupied, the occupation hides the emotion, and the viewer starts hunting for it and works with you.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Any long seated dialogue, and the confession scene where neither can look the other in the eye. Essential in docudrama and heritage work, because a manual craft (clay, wool, bread) establishes the period without a word of explanation.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Docudrama / Reenactment, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Limited Series, AI-Generated Clip, Kids Content | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Family, Crime / Procedural, Romance, Slice of Life, Tragedy, War, Comedy  
**Avoid when:** If the business is stronger than the dialogue it steals attention. And if it does not match the emotional state it feels manufactured.  
**Prompt:** `she keeps grinding wheat in a stone bowl through the whole conversation, never looking up, he talks from behind her`  
`block.prop_business`

### The Hands Business Insert

**Also called:** insert on hands, hand detail  
**What it is:** A close insert on the hands doing something during the scene: shaking, twisting a handkerchief, counting money, touching a ring, steadying a pistol.  
**Effect on the audience:** It tells the truth the face is hiding. The face lies and the hands do not, and the viewer believes the hands.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The lying scene, the tension before a decision, the grief that never reaches the face. An excellent rescue shot in the edit for bridging time, and essential in heritage documentary for documenting craft.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Short Film, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | genres: Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Crime / Procedural, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Romance, Tragedy, True Crime, Psychological / Arthouse, Noir / Neo-Noir  
**Avoid when:** Dropped in with no reason it is just edit filler. It must carry information the wide shot does not have.  
**Prompt:** `tight insert on her hands in her lap, fingers twisting the ring, slight tremble, shallow focus, dialogue continuing off screen`  
`block.hands_insert`

### The Object Handoff

**Also called:** the pass, handing over  
**What it is:** An object passes from hand to hand inside the shot: a key, an envelope, a weapon, a child, bread, a dagger, a document. It differs from the structural Object Handoff, where one object travels from scene to scene as the link: here the whole exchange happens inside a single shot.  
**Effect on the audience:** A turning point made physical. The viewer understands that power or responsibility has changed hands, and the shot becomes the hinge of the scene.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat of a deal, a betrayal, an inheritance or a sacrifice. Essential in crime and heritage work (handing over the tablet, the crown, the weapon), and in advertising handing the product to the customer is the same move.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Docudrama / Reenactment, Commercial / TVC, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Trailer / Teaser, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: Crime / Procedural, Gangster, Heist, Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Mythology / Ancient Epic, Spy / Espionage, War, Faith / Biblical Epic  
**Avoid when:** Done fast and far from camera, nobody reads what changed hands. Shoot it close, or make the object large and clear.  
**Prompt:** `the key passes from her hand to his in a tight two shot, both hands sharp in the centre of the frame`  
**Yields to:** `scene.object_handoff` — The object travels between scenes, not hands.  
`block.object_handoff`

### The Sit-Down and Stand-Up Power Shift

**Also called:** rising and sitting, status move  
**What it is:** A change of body level inside the scene: the one standing sits, or the one sitting rises, exactly on a chosen line.  
**Effect on the audience:** A status change that reads instantly. Standing means taking power or refusing, sitting means yielding, exhaustion or opening up.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Getting fired, the interrogation, asking for help, announcing a decision. A cheap and powerful tool in series and theatrical staging, and in generated video it is a clear physical action the model reads easily.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Limited Series, Docudrama / Reenactment, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Drama, Legal Drama, Political Thriller, Crime / Procedural, Comedy, Melodrama, Family, Tragedy, Medical Drama  
**Avoid when:** Standing and sitting several times in one scene with no cause turns into aimless fidgeting.  
**Prompt:** `he stays seated through the argument and stands up exactly on the last line, she stays standing and then sits`  
`block.sit_stand_shift`

### Standing Over the Seated

**Also called:** standing dominance, towering staging  
**What it is:** One character stands while the other sits or is on the floor, and the camera preserves the height difference in the composition instead of equalising them.  
**Effect on the audience:** Naked authority and threat. The viewer reads who is in control in an instant and feels unsafe alongside the seated one.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The interrogation, the father's dressing down, the doctor and the patient, the captor and the prisoner. Essential in crime, war and heritage scenes of a king and a supplicant, and pairing a low angle on the standing figure with a high angle on the seated one doubles the effect.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Docudrama / Reenactment, Limited Series, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: Crime / Procedural, War, Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Mythology / Ancient Epic, Political Thriller, Medical Drama, Tragedy  
**Avoid when:** Holding the same arrangement for the whole scene makes it static. Let the change of level arrive as the climax.  
**Prompt:** `he stands directly over her chair looking down, she stays seated looking up, the height difference kept in every frame`  
`block.standing_over_seated`

### Kneeling Down to Their Level

**Also called:** getting down to eye level, kneel to a child  
**What it is:** A character lowers their body (kneeling, crouching, sitting) so their eyes come level with the other person's, usually a child, a wounded person or someone broken.  
**Effect on the audience:** Empathy and a surrender of power. The move itself says respect and safety without a single line of dialogue.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The adult with the child, the medic with the patient, the commander with the wounded, the apology. Essential in kids content, family drama and humanitarian advertising, and the fastest way to turn a hard character human.  
**Best in:** formats: Kids Content, Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Commercial / TVC, Docudrama / Reenactment, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), 3D CG Animation, Short Film, AI-Generated Clip, Corporate / Training | genres: Family, Drama, Preschool / Early Learning, Coming of Age, War, Medical Drama, Melodrama, Faith / Biblical Epic, Romance  
**Avoid when:** In a scene with real fear between the two it lies to the viewer. The physical closeness has to be earned by the story.  
**Prompt:** `he kneels down so his eyes are level with the child's, both faces at the same height in a two shot`  
**Yields to:** `block.standing_over_seated` — Real fear between them makes closeness a lie.  
`block.kneel_to_level`

### Eyeline Levels: Who Looks Up and Who Looks Down

**Also called:** status eyelines, looking up and down  
**What it is:** You decide who looks up and who looks down inside the scene, and you keep it consistent across every setup even when the angles change.  
**Effect on the audience:** The map of power is built across the whole scene, not in one shot. The viewer knows who is above and who is below even when they never share a frame.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The courtroom, the classroom, the throne, the meeting, the investigation. Essential in series because it holds the relationship across dozens of setups, and essential in anime, where status is built almost entirely on the direction of a look.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Feature Film, Limited Series, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Docudrama / Reenactment, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Soap Opera / Telenovela | genres: Legal Drama, Political Thriller, Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Mythology / Ancient Epic, Shonen Battle (anime), Crime / Procedural, War, Seinen (mature anime)  
**Avoid when:** If the eyeline level flips with no dramatic reason, the sense of space breaks and the viewer stops believing they are in the same room.  
**Prompt:** `the king seated above looks down, the messenger below looks up, the up and down eyelines matched in every reverse`  
`block.eyeline_levels`

### The Three-Actor Triangle

**Also called:** three hander staging, triangle blocking  
**What it is:** Three actors arranged as a triangle rather than a line: two in the foreground and one deep, or one in front and two behind.  
**Effect on the audience:** It marks the third as the withholder, the observer or the excluded, and the viewer tracks their silent reaction, so the silence becomes a fourth performer.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The scene of secrets among three, the parents and the child, two detectives and a witness, the betrayal before it surfaces. It lets you play the scene in one shot and holds the tension without a cut.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Anime (Japanese 2D), Docudrama / Reenactment, AI-Generated Clip, Stop Motion | genres: Drama, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Family, Melodrama, Mystery / Whodunit, Tragedy, Legal Drama, Romance  
**Avoid when:** Line all three up parallel to the camera and the scene flattens and dies. The triangle is what opens it up.  
**Prompt:** `two of them seated facing each other in the foreground, the third standing deeper and off to the side, all three in frame`  
`block.three_actor_triangle`

### Dinner Table Geometry

**Also called:** table staging, family table blocking  
**What it is:** Placing people around the table by relationship rather than convenience: who sits at the head, who sits opposite whom, who is on the side, and whose back is to camera.  
**Effect on the audience:** The whole family or group reads in one frame. The viewer understands power and alliances before the first line is spoken.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The family dinner, the meeting, the negotiation, the king's table in heritage work. The strongest approach is to keep the camera near table height and use depth so the faces do not flatten into one row.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Limited Series, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Docudrama / Reenactment, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Drama, Family, Melodrama, Crime / Procedural, Gangster, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Comedy, Tragedy, Political Thriller  
**Avoid when:** Lining everyone up in a single row facing camera gives you a dead formal dinner. Break the row with depth and height. A banquet composed on purpose as a carved relief is the exception: figures in profile on parallel planes rather than a row facing camera, which is `meso_stage.bas_relief_frieze_horizontal_staging`.  
**Prompt:** `the father at the head of the table in the centre, two children in profile on the sides, the empty chair opposite him`  
**Example:** 12 Angry Men, the jury table and the shifting seats around it.  
`block.dinner_table`

### Interrogation Geometry

**Also called:** two sides of the table, interview room staging  
**What it is:** A table in the middle of a room, one person on each side, and sometimes a third standing in the corner or circling the table.  
**Effect on the audience:** A confrontation with a fixed shape and an open outcome. The one standing owns the space, the one seated owns the information, and the tension comes from that trade.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Crime, investigation, legal drama and captivity scenes. It works because the shape is familiar to the viewer, so any violation of it, the detective sitting beside the suspect, becomes a major event.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Feature Film, Limited Series, Docudrama / Reenactment, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: Crime / Procedural, True Crime, Thriller / Suspense, Legal Drama, Political Thriller, Noir / Neo-Noir, Mystery / Whodunit, War, Spy / Espionage, Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** Playing the whole scene as plain cuts between two faces with no change of position gets dull and predictable fast.  
**Prompt:** `suspect seated alone on the far side of a metal table, detective standing behind his own empty chair, single overhead light`  
`block.interrogation_geometry`

### Shoulder to Shoulder

**Also called:** side by side staging, parallel bodies  
**What it is:** The two sit or stand side by side looking the same way instead of facing each other: on a step, a wall, in a car, on a shore.  
**Effect on the audience:** Closeness without confrontation. Not looking makes the words easier and the confession truer, and the viewer feels they are on the same side rather than opposed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The hard confession, the reconciliation after a fight, the father and son, two friends after a loss. Essential in quiet drama and coming of age films, and it plays in one shot with no cutting.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Limited Series, Anime (Japanese 2D), Documentary Feature, AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Drama, Coming of Age, Romance, Family, Slice of Life, Tragedy, War, Road Movie, Shojo (anime romance), Melodrama  
**Avoid when:** If the scene needs confrontation and conflict, side by side weakens it and leaves it tepid.  
**Prompt:** `the two sit side by side on the step looking out at the street, never turning to each other, one wide two shot held`  
**Yields to:** `block.barrier_between` — The scene needs confrontation, not closeness.  
`block.shoulder_to_shoulder`

### Car Geometry

**Also called:** driving scene blocking, car two shot  
**What it is:** The scene inside a car: driver and passenger side by side facing forward, or one in the back seat, with the glass and mirrors offering extra angles.  
**Effect on the audience:** A moving cage. Neither can leave nor avoid the other, and facing forward lets the words come out without a confrontation.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The honest conversation on the road, the family argument, the stakeout. Strong in road movies and crime, and excellent for small productions because the space is tiny and the light is limited yet the scene plays big.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Vertical Micro-Drama, Documentary Feature, Music Video, AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Limited Series | genres: Road Movie, Crime / Procedural, Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Comedy, Coming of Age, Noir / Neo-Noir, Family, Spy / Espionage  
**Avoid when:** Shooting only from the bonnet gives you a flat frame. Vary it with the mirrors, the back seat and the passing exterior.  
**Prompt:** `two shot from the bonnet through the windscreen, both facing forward, neither looking at the other, street lights passing`  
`block.car_geometry`

### Phone Call Blocking

**Also called:** split conversation staging, phone scene  
**What it is:** A scene between two people who are not in the same place. You stage it in opposition: one moving and one still, one in warm light and one in cold, with opposed looking directions so the viewer feels them connected.  
**Effect on the audience:** An invented proximity. The viewer assembles the two places into one and feels both people are inside the same moment despite the distance.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The bad news call, the apology from far away, the coordination before an operation. Essential in series and vertical micro drama, where half the scenes are calls, and it is solved by looking direction rather than shot size.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Vertical Micro-Drama, Feature Film, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Limited Series, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Short Film | genres: Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Crime / Procedural, Romance, Melodrama, Spy / Espionage, Political Thriller, Comedy, Family  
**Avoid when:** If both look the same way in the same composition, the viewer stops believing they are talking to each other. Opposing the directions is the safe default, because opposed eyelines build the invisible line between the two rooms; the exception is when you want them alone in the same emptiness rather than connected.  
**Prompt:** `she stands still facing frame right in warm lamplight, he paces facing frame left in cold blue street light, same phone call`  
`block.phone_call`

### Side Reversal / Betrayal Blocking

**Also called:** switching sides, crossing to the other camp  
**What it is:** A character who was standing with one group physically moves across the frame to the other side and stays there in every setup that follows.  
**Effect on the audience:** A betrayal or a change of loyalty reads physically before it is spoken. The viewer feels the switch even before they understand why.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The traitor exposed, the witness who changes their testimony, the brother who goes over to the other camp. Powerful in crime, political drama and historical epic, and powerful because it removes the need for a line of explanation.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Docudrama / Reenactment, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Anime (Japanese 2D), Trailer / Teaser, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: Crime / Procedural, Political Thriller, War, Historical / Biopic, Drama, Spy / Espionage, Gangster, Thriller / Suspense, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Fantasy / Epic  
**Avoid when:** If the move happens off screen or in a quick cut the meaning is lost. Make the crossing visible and do it in a wide.  
**Prompt:** `he was standing with the two on the left, he walks across the frame and takes his place beside the man on the right`  
`block.side_reversal`

### The Listener in the Foreground

**Also called:** reaction in frame, foreground listener  
**What it is:** You put the speaker deep in the shot and the listener close to the lens (often a shoulder or half a face), so the reaction becomes the subject rather than the words.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer listens and watches at once and feels stationed beside the listener. The silence ends up louder than the dialogue.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The news, the confession, the verdict, the accusation. It matters most when the reaction is the event, and it is the cheapest way to get depth and tension out of one shot without cutting.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Docudrama / Reenactment, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Soap Opera / Telenovela, AI-Generated Clip, Interview / Video Podcast | genres: Drama, Crime / Procedural, Tragedy, Thriller / Suspense, Melodrama, Legal Drama, Medical Drama, Family, Mystery / Whodunit  
**Avoid when:** If the listener is completely out of focus and unreadable, the idea is gone. Keep them legible even at half a face.  
**Prompt:** `her face large and soft in the left foreground listening, he stands sharp and small in the background delivering the news`  
`block.listener_foreground`

### Same Frame, Different Worlds

**Also called:** two worlds in one shot, divided frame staging  
**What it is:** You stage two characters in one shot with each inside their own environment: one inside the house and one outside the window, one in light and one in shadow, one near and one far beyond a barrier.  
**Effect on the audience:** The separation reads even while they share a frame. The viewer senses the shot does not unite them, and the physical closeness only deepens the sadness.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of a relationship, migration and farewell, the father and son who cannot reach each other, the patient and the family. Strong in drama and social documentary, and in heritage work between the present and a ruin in the same frame.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Feature, Documentary Series, Short Film, Limited Series, Music Video, Heritage / Historical Documentary, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Melodrama, Romance, War, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Medical Drama, Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** If the difference between the two worlds is weak, the same light and the same distance, the separation does not read and you just have an ordinary two shot.  
**Prompt:** `he stands inside in warm lamplight, she stands outside the window in cold rain, both sharp in the same frame, glass between them`  
`block.same_frame_two_worlds`

### Back-of-the-Room Staging

**Also called:** watching from the back, observer at the rear  
**What it is:** You place a character at the back of the space, away from the centre of the action, watching and not taking part, kept in the frame in depth.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer notices them precisely because they are quiet and far, and starts asking what they are thinking, so they shift from background to threat or to hope.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The funeral, the wedding, the meeting, the trial, and the arrival of someone nobody expected. Essential in crime and family drama because it plants information with no dialogue, and excellent in documentary when someone is watching the filming.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Documentary Feature, Docudrama / Reenactment, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Heritage / Historical Documentary, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Crime / Procedural, Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Mystery / Whodunit, Family, Tragedy, Political Thriller, Historical / Biopic, War, Noir / Neo-Noir  
**Avoid when:** If the lighting ignores that person they vanish into shadow and the whole point is lost. Keep them readable, even faintly.  
**Prompt:** `the crowd fills the front of the hall, one man stands alone at the back by the door watching, small but clearly lit`  
`block.back_of_room`

### The Circling Two-Person Standoff

**Also called:** circling each other, orbit standoff  
**What it is:** Two characters orbit each other around a single centre while the camera circles with them or against them, so the centre stays fixed and the background is what changes.  
**Effect on the audience:** Tension that rises with no cut. The viewer feels the collision is inevitable and that time is slowing down.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Before a fight, before a dangerous deal, the negotiation between two enemies. Essential in action, wuxia, battle anime and westerns, and it works well in generated video because an orbit is one clean movement.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Drama Series Episode, AI-Generated Clip, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film | genres: Action, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Samurai / Chanbara, Western, Thriller / Suspense, Shonen Battle (anime), Crime / Procedural, Gangster, Fantasy / Epic, Sports Drama  
**Avoid when:** Circling that runs long with no escalation turns into a dance. Limit it to a couple of orbits and then break it with the event.  
**Prompt:** `the two circle each other slowly around a fixed centre, camera orbiting with them, the background sweeping behind`  
`block.circling_standoff`

### Fight Geography

**Also called:** fight staging, action geography  
**What it is:** You lay the fight out in four legible stages: establish the space and the distance, the exchange, the impact, the reaction. Every shot knows where both bodies are in relation to each other.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer knows who is winning and who is losing at every moment, so the fight becomes a story rather than noise.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every fight in action, epic and battle anime. The keys are a wide establishing frame at the start, consistent screen direction through the exchange, and a reaction shot after every significant blow so it lands.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Trailer / Teaser, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, AI-Generated Clip, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film | genres: Action, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Samurai / Chanbara, War, Shonen Battle (anime), Superhero, Fantasy / Epic, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Crime / Procedural, Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** Fast cutting with no establishing shot turns the fight into chaos nobody remembers. And breaking screen direction mid exchange usually loses the viewer, because they are still holding who faces whom; the exception is when the fight has collapsed into confusion and you want them lost with it.  
**Prompt:** `wide establishing shot of both fighters and the distance between them, then the exchange with consistent screen direction`  
`block.fight_geography`

### Chase Geography

**Also called:** pursuit staging, chase direction  
**What it is:** The pursued moves in a fixed screen direction, the pursuer follows in the same direction, and every shot tells the viewer whether the gap has closed or widened.  
**Effect on the audience:** Readable tension. The viewer feels the distance physically, and any change of direction becomes a deliberate event rather than a mistake.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Chases in action, sports and war, even a pursuit across a market or a rooftop. You must plan at least one shot that holds both in the same frame so the viewer never gets lost.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, AI-Generated Clip, Music Video | genres: Action, Thriller / Suspense, Crime / Procedural, Heist, Spy / Espionage, War, Adventure, Shonen Battle (anime), Superhero, Survival  
**Avoid when:** Flipping the direction shot to shot makes the viewer feel they are running at each other. And do not repeat the same running angle; it goes monotonous.  
**Prompt:** `the runner moving consistently left to right across every shot, the pursuer entering the same frame from the left behind him`  
`block.chase_geography`

### Push Through the Crowd

**Also called:** forcing through, moving against the crowd  
**What it is:** The character forces a way through people while the camera moves with them or ahead of them, and the crowd collides with them and slows them down.  
**Effect on the audience:** Urgency and helplessness at the same time. The viewer feels the physical resistance, and every second gets heavier.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Searching for a lost child, catching up with someone, escaping a public place, the moment bad news arrives in the middle of a celebration. Strong in suspense and drama, and it gives high energy in reels because the movement never stops and the face stays close.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), Documentary Feature, AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Micro-Drama, Concert / Live Event | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Action, Crime / Procedural, War, Disaster, Survival, Romance, Political Thriller  
**Avoid when:** It needs enough extras and a planned path. A thin crowd makes the shot feel empty and the effort wasted.  
**Prompt:** `she pushes forward through a dense crowd moving the other way, camera facing her and retreating, shoulders knocking past`  
`block.push_through_crowd`

### The Crowd Parts to Reveal

**Also called:** crowd opening, parting reveal  
**What it is:** People stand blocking someone or something, then open up or are pushed aside, revealing what was behind them.  
**Effect on the audience:** A ceremonial reveal. The delay creates anticipation, and the opening gives weight to whatever appears, even something simple.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Revealing the body, the king's entrance, the hero's appearance, the product in an ad. Essential in heritage work, epics and trailers, and it plays in one shot with no effects at all.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Commercial / TVC, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Music Video, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Concert / Live Event | genres: Mythology / Ancient Epic, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Fantasy / Epic, Action, Crime / Procedural, Superhero, War, Musical, Shonen Battle (anime)  
**Avoid when:** If what is revealed is weaker than the build up, it deflates. And if the move is over choreographed it looks like a school play.  
**Prompt:** `the wall of soldiers steps aside one by one revealing the king seated behind them, camera pushing slowly forward`  
`block.crowd_parts`

### The Still Figure in a Moving Crowd

**Also called:** standing still while they flow, stillness in motion  
**What it is:** Everyone in the frame is moving while one character stands completely still, with the camera locked or pushing in slowly.  
**Effect on the audience:** The eye goes straight to the still one because the motion around them becomes background. The stillness reads as shock, resolve or estrangement.  
**Used for and where it works best:** After the news in the street, the stranger in the city, the character who has decided to stop. Excellent in urban documentary and music video, and it works nicely with slow motion but does not need it.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Music Video, Documentary Feature, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, News Report / Reportage | genres: Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Tragedy, Romance, Coming of Age, Thriller / Suspense, Slice of Life, War, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** If the crowd is not moving enough there is no contrast and the shot reads as ordinary. You need real density of motion.  
**Prompt:** `commuters streaming past in both directions, one woman standing completely still in the middle of the frame`  
`block.static_in_moving_crowd`

### The Slow-Motion Group Walk

**Also called:** hero walk, team walk  
**What it is:** A group walks in a line toward the camera or alongside it, in step, usually in slow motion with music over it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Glorification and confidence. The viewer reads them as a team and senses something big is coming, and the feeling is deliberately larger than life.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Introducing the team, the moment before the job or the match, the title sequence. Essential in trailers, music video, sports and advertising, and it works in heritage work to introduce a band of warriors or priests.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Feature Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Action, Heist, Crime / Procedural, Sports Drama, Superhero, War, Comedy, Gangster, Shonen Battle (anime), Musical  
**Avoid when:** A very familiar cliche, and used seriously in a scene that has not earned it, it turns comic. Comedy uses it for exactly that reason.  
**Prompt:** `five of them walking abreast straight toward the lens in slow motion, matched stride, dust rising behind them`  
**Example:** Reservoir Dogs, the slow motion walk in the opening.  
**Yields to:** `block.the_line` — The scene has not earned the glorification.  
`block.hero_group_walk`

### The Procession

**Also called:** parade staging, ceremonial line  
**What it is:** A long ordered line of people moving through the space: a royal procession, a funeral, a wedding parade, a column of prisoners, pilgrims, an army entering a city.  
**Effect on the audience:** Order and inevitability. One long continuous movement feels historical, and the viewer senses this event is larger than any individual inside it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Essential in heritage documentary and historical epic: the king's entry, the moving of a statue, the procession to the temple. Shoot it from three positions: a distant side view, from inside the line, and from above so the length reads.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Feature Film, Documentary Feature, Docudrama / Reenactment, 3D CG Animation, Trailer / Teaser, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Wedding / Event Film, News Report / Reportage | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Faith / Biblical Epic, War, Tragedy, Political Thriller, Fantasy / Epic, Drama  
**Avoid when:** Too few bodies exposes the production. Shoot closer and use depth and repetition instead of showing where the line ends.  
**Prompt:** `a long procession moving through the temple gate, seen from the side in a wide shot, the line continuing past both frame edges`  
`block.procession`

### Surrounded: The Circle Closing

**Also called:** encircled staging, ring of figures  
**What it is:** A group forms a ring around one person at the centre, and the ring either tightens or holds, with the camera either at the centre with them or overhead.  
**Effect on the audience:** Total entrapment. There is no empty side to escape to, and the viewer feels the threat arriving from every direction.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The bullying scene, the mob judgement, the ritual, the arena fight. Essential in folk horror, school drama and heritage ritual scenes, and an overhead shot shows the shape whole.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Short Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Trailer / Teaser, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Folk Horror, Horror, Drama, Coming of Age, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Martial Arts / Wuxia, Thriller / Suspense, Mythology / Ancient Epic, War  
**Avoid when:** A broken or scattered ring loses the entrapment. Every side must be closed, even if only with bodies near the lens.  
**Prompt:** `a ring of standing figures closing around one kneeling man in the centre, overhead shot showing the full circle`  
`block.surrounded_circle`

### The Line-Up

**Also called:** the row, facing line, row shot, band lineup, team line  
**What it is:** A group forms a single line facing the camera or facing another group, at roughly equal spacing inside a symmetrical frame: a team, a family, soldiers, suspects.  
**Effect on the audience:** A formal presentation. The viewer reads them one by one in a moment, the line says these people are a single unit, and the differences in stance give each one a personality.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The team photo before the match, the band in a music video, the character introduction in a title sequence, the rank of soldiers, the family portrait. Essential in sports, advertising and trailers, and in heritage work a rank of guards or priests plays like a living relief. Shoot it on a slightly longer lens so the edges do not bend, and let one person break the symmetry when you want to say there is a crack in the group.  
**Best in:** formats: Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Commercial / TVC, Trailer / Teaser, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Wedding / Event Film, Concert / Live Event, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Sports Drama, Action, War, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Comedy, Crime / Procedural, Musical, Superhero, Family, Drama  
**Avoid when:** A line kills depth and flattens the shot, so do not use it for a dramatic scene that needs relationships; keep it for presentation. And with a large group in a vertical frame the row compresses into mush.  
**Prompt:** `the whole team standing in one line facing the lens, evenly spaced, wide shot, matched height and posture`  
**Yields to:** `block.three_actor_triangle` — A dramatic scene that needs relationships.  
`block.the_line`

### Kiss Geometry

**Also called:** staging the kiss, romantic blocking  
**What it is:** You arrange the moment: the height difference, which way each head tilts, where the hands go, who moves first, and a camera angle that keeps both faces rather than burying one.  
**Effect on the audience:** With the geometry right the moment feels true and it stays with people. With a face buried or an angle blocked, the emotion collapses instantly.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The romantic peak in features, series, music video and advertising. The usual answer is a three quarter angle rather than a full profile, a slight height difference, and the short hesitation before the kiss, which is what actually carries the scene.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Vertical Micro-Drama, AI-Generated Clip, Wedding / Event Film | genres: Romance, Rom-Com, Melodrama, Drama, Shojo (anime romance), Coming of Age, Fantasy / Epic, Musical, Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** A flat profile hides the expressions and leaves the moment cold. And do not cut on the contact; let the shot carry through.  
**Prompt:** `three quarter angle two shot, he is slightly taller, both faces visible, a held pause before they move together`  
`block.kiss_geometry`

### The Empty Half of the Bed

**Also called:** empty side, the space beside them  
**What it is:** You frame a person lying or sitting on one side with the other side clearly empty inside the frame, the sheets either smooth or still creased.  
**Effect on the audience:** An explicit absence with no dialogue. The empty side holds the person who is gone, and the viewer understands the whole story in one shot.  
**Used for and where it works best:** After a separation, a death or a departure, and the morning after. Essential in drama, melodrama and the sad music video, and it works as an episode opener that explains the situation without a word.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Music Video, Short Film, Limited Series, Vertical Micro-Drama, AI-Generated Clip, Anime (Japanese 2D) | genres: Melodrama, Drama, Romance, Tragedy, War, Psychological / Arthouse, Shojo (anime romance), Family, Gothic / Vampire

**Prompt:** `she lies on the left side of the bed, the right half empty with the sheet still creased, high angle, dawn light`  
`block.empty_half_bed`

### Walk Away and Stop

**Also called:** the pause on the exit, stopping mid exit  
**What it is:** The character leaves the scene walking away, then suddenly stops halfway and stays standing without turning round.  
**Effect on the audience:** Everything hangs. The viewer cannot tell whether they will come back or keep going, and the silence gets heavier than any line.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of the fight, the last line of the relationship, the doubt right after the decision. Strong in drama and romance, and an excellent scene or episode ending because it leaves a question.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Music Video, Vertical Micro-Drama, Limited Series, AI-Generated Clip, Anime (Japanese 2D) | genres: Drama, Romance, Melodrama, Tragedy, Family, Coming of Age, Shojo (anime romance), Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** If the character turns round quickly the suspension is gone. The stop without the turn is the whole device.  
**Prompt:** `she walks away down the corridor, stops halfway, stands with her back to us and does not turn, long lens, held`  
**Yields to:** `block.look_back` — The exit should resolve rather than hang.  
`block.walk_away_stop`

### The Look Back

**Also called:** glance back, turning at the door  
**What it is:** As the character leaves, they turn once for a short glance at what is behind them, and then go.  
**Effect on the audience:** It says the thing being left behind still matters, and it gives a scene or an act an emotional close.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Leaving the house, the end of the visit, the goodbye at the station, leaving the city in a heritage story. It has to happen once in the scene, because repeating it cancels the meaning.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Music Video, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Soap Opera / Telenovela, AI-Generated Clip, Anime (Japanese 2D), Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Drama, Romance, Tragedy, War, Road Movie, Coming of Age, Melodrama, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Family  
**Avoid when:** Two or three looks back make the scene rubbery and the moment loses its weight.  
**Prompt:** `at the door she turns her head once, looks back into the room for two seconds, then walks out without stopping`  
`block.look_back`

### Reach and Withdraw

**Also called:** the almost touch, withdrawn hand  
**What it is:** A character reaches toward another (a shoulder, a hand, a face), stops just short of contact, and pulls back.  
**Effect on the audience:** Stronger than the touch itself. The viewer completes the movement in their head and feels the distance that was never crossed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The father who does not know how to hold his son, unspoken love, the apology that never gets said. Essential in melodrama, family drama and romance anime, and it plays as a tight hands shot with no face at all.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Music Video, Limited Series, AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Melodrama, Drama, Romance, Family, Tragedy, Shojo (anime romance), Coming of Age, War, Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** Done fast it does not read. You need a clear hold in the air before the withdrawal so the viewer catches it.  
**Prompt:** `his hand rises toward her shoulder, stops a few centimetres short, hangs there, then drops back to his side`  
`block.reach_withdraw`

### One Steps Into the Light

**Also called:** entering the light, stepping out of shadow  
**What it is:** The character stands in shadow or outside the pool of light, and takes a step or two forward until the light falls on them and their face is revealed.  
**Effect on the audience:** A reveal from the simplest possible move, and it means crossing from hiding to confrontation, or from neutrality to admission.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Revealing whoever was standing in the corner, the suspect's admission, the appearance of the mysterious figure, the witness stepping up. Essential in noir, horror and heritage scenes lit by fire or an oil lamp, and easy to execute in generated video because it is one clear action.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Short Film, AI-Generated Clip, Music Video | genres: Noir / Neo-Noir, Crime / Procedural, Horror, Thriller / Suspense, Mystery / Whodunit, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Gothic / Vampire, Psychological Horror, War  
**Avoid when:** If the face was already readable in the shadow, the move gives you nothing. The gap between shadow and light has to be real and strong.  
**Prompt:** `he stands hidden in shadow at the back, takes two steps forward into the lamp light and his face is revealed`  
`block.step_into_light`

### Pacing the Room

**Also called:** walking back and forth, restless blocking  
**What it is:** The character walks back and forth on the same path inside the room during the dialogue, with the camera either panning with them or letting them move in and out of frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** Visible anxiety. The viewer feels the internal pressure, and the repetition itself suggests there is no way out.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Waiting for news, working out a solution, the hospital waiting room, the beat before a dangerous decision. Strong in drama and suspense, and the key moment is when the character suddenly stops, because that is where the decision lands.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Docudrama / Reenactment, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Medical Drama, Legal Drama, Crime / Procedural, Political Thriller, Tragedy, Psychological / Arthouse, War  
**Avoid when:** Pacing that never stops across the whole scene tires the eye and buries the dialogue. Stop it on the line that matters.  
**Prompt:** `he paces back and forth across the room while she sits still, camera holds, he passes in and out of frame twice then stops`  
`block.pacing`

### Background Life

**Also called:** background action, life behind the scene  
**What it is:** You plan the movement behind the scene: workers, customers, children, traffic, housework, so that life continues behind the main action with its own logic and rhythm.  
**Effect on the audience:** The world feels real and ongoing, and the main scene feels like part of a place rather than something erected on a set.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every scene in a public place: the cafe, the market, the hospital, the workshop, the station. Essential in docudrama and heritage work, because background movement is what builds the period, and essential in prompts, because naming it prevents a dead background.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Docudrama / Reenactment, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Documentary Feature, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Crime / Procedural, Comedy, Slice of Life, Family, War, Political Thriller, Romance  
**Avoid when:** If the background movement is stronger than the scene it pulls the eye and buries the line. Keep it regular, further back, and slower than the foreground.  
**Prompt:** `two men talking in the foreground while behind them workers carry baskets, a child runs past and a cart crosses slowly`  
**Yields to:** `block.fg_bg_counterpoint` — The background event must carry meaning.  
`block.background_life`

### The Tableau

**Also called:** tableau staging, static wide staging, painterly wide, frontal staged wide  
**What it is:** A wide locked shot with no cuts, the actors placed at deliberate positions inside the architecture like a painting, and the entire scene plays out within that single frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer chooses where to look and time feels real, and it delivers formality and distance, because the relationships are read by the eye instead of being announced by the cut. Irony or dread follows, depending on the content, because the camera refuses to intervene.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Deadpan comedy, the group scene, the ritual, the formal scene (the court, the trial, the tense family dinner, the council, the verdict) and the disaster beat that needs no comment. Let movement inside the frame do the editing: one stands, one enters, one withdraws. It suits small productions, since you only need one well judged setup, and it suits heritage work because it echoes a wall relief.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Limited Series, Drama Series Episode, Experimental / Art Film, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Anime (Japanese 2D), Stop Motion, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Dark Comedy / Satire, Drama, Surreal / Absurdist, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Tragedy, Comedy, Psychological / Arthouse, War, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Faith / Biblical Epic, Political Thriller, Gothic / Vampire  
**Avoid when:** It demands exact composition and staging, and any dead area inside the frame is exposed, because there is no cut to cover it. And when the scene needs intimacy, a tableau pushes the viewer away.  
**Prompt:** `one static wide shot, the whole scene playing inside the frame, figures spread across foreground and depth, no cuts`  
**Example:** The films of Roy Andersson, built from long static staged tableaux.  
**Yields to:** `block.listener_foreground` — The scene needs intimacy, not formal distance.  
`block.tableau`

### Power Blocking

**Also called:** status staging, who stands and who sits, power geography  
**What it is:** The principle behind the small staging decisions: who stands and who sits, who moves and who stays, and who closes the distance first. Every move shifts the balance of power in the moment, and the individual beats in this category, such as standing over the seated or the sit down power shift, are how this principle is executed.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer reads power from the bodies before the dialogue says anything. Whoever moves freely through the space looks like its owner, and whoever waits looks like they were summoned.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Designing any scene with unequal status: an interrogation, a job interview, a family council, a royal court. Read the scene first and decide who holds power in each quarter of it, then stage the bodies accordingly and change them at the instant the power moves.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Recorded Theatre, Docudrama / Reenactment, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy | genres: Drama, Crime / Procedural, Legal Drama, Political Thriller, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Family Drama, Gangster, Tragedy  
**Avoid when:** The principle is usually applied literally, with the powerful always standing and the weak always seated, and the scene becomes predictable. The exception is when the reverse is stronger: the calm seated figure who does not need to rise is the most dangerous person in the room.  
**Prompt:** `staging note: the interrogator stays seated and still while the suspect paces, then the interrogator rises on the line that turns the scene and the suspect sits`  
`block.power_blocking`

### Body Occlusion and Reveal

**Also called:** sustained body block, hidden by a person, shift to reveal  
**What it is:** A person standing in the foreground so that an important element behind them is hidden for a long stretch of the scene, then revealed with a small movement: a step to the side, a turn, or a shift of weight. It differs from the fast walk-past reveal because here the blocking is sustained and deliberate, and the reveal is timed to the dialogue.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer senses something is being kept from them before knowing what it is, so they stay alert. The reveal lands as a blow because it came from a human movement rather than from a cut.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A scene where the viewer must discover a body, a letter or a third person in the room, the product reveal in a commercial, and the turn in a horror beat. It is very useful in a long single take because it saves a cut and keeps the tension unbroken.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Trailer / Teaser, Limited Series | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Horror, Crime / Procedural, Mystery / Whodunit, Drama, Psychological Thriller, Noir / Neo-Noir, Gangster  
**Avoid when:** The viewer usually feels the blocker is standing there for no reason when they have no business inside the scene. The exception is when the standing is dramatically motivated, a guard, a nurse, or someone who is hiding the thing on purpose.  
**Prompt:** `staging note: the standing nurse hides the bed from camera for the whole first half of the scene, then steps left on the doctor's question and the empty bed is revealed behind her`  
**Yields to:** `block.walk_past_reveal` — The reveal should be fast, not sustained.  
`block.body_occlusion_reveal`

### Foreground and Background Counterpoint

**Also called:** contrapuntal staging, two actions one frame, background contradiction  
**What it is:** Two events running in the same frame at different depths and saying opposite things: a serious conversation in front and something absurd behind, or a smile in the foreground while a disaster approaches in the depth. Both are sharp enough to be read together.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer catches a third meaning that exists in neither event alone. This is the machinery of dramatic irony: we know what the character does not.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Comedy where the joke arrives in the background and nobody in the scene turns, thrillers where the danger approaches while the characters talk, and social drama where the place contradicts the words. It needs deep focus and a lens that is not too long so the depth stays legible.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Comics / Webtoon / Photo Essay, Anime (Japanese 2D), Commercial / TVC, Recorded Theatre, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Dark Comedy / Satire, Comedy, Thriller / Suspense, Social Drama, Deadpan Comedy, Horror, Drama, Tragedy  
**Avoid when:** The viewer's attention usually splits and they miss the dialogue and the background together. The exception is when the dialogue carries no information at that moment and is deliberately left as an audio bed while the picture carries the meaning.  
**Prompt:** `two plane staging, the two men arguing in sharp foreground at the table while a small figure in the deep background carries the coffin past the window, both planes in focus, static wide frame`  
**Yields to:** `block.background_life` — The dialogue carries information that must land.  
`block.fg_bg_counterpoint`
