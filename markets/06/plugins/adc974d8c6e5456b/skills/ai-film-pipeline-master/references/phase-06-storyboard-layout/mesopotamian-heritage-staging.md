# Mesopotamian & Near Eastern Heritage Staging

22 cards. Ancient visual grammar, palace bas-relief frieze staging, monumental architecture scale, royal profile dignity, cuneiform tablet framing, and authentic Near Eastern staging rules for heritage productions.

---

### Name What the Frame Is Holding

**Also called:** the heritage staging selector, subject before staging, what is in this frame  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `meso_stage.` shelf. It does not describe a look. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **what is the frame holding, does anything cross it, and whose ground is this** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were read off the shelf's `Yields to:` conditions and cross-checked against every `Avoid when:` on the shelf; the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id.  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so that the card that does reach the screen was chosen against the subject, the beat and the ground, rather than picked because a relief wall is the first thing anyone pictures when they hear the word Assyrian.  
**Used for and where it works best:** **On a history piece the user wants realistic, the heritage rule comes before axis one:** reference-first for every historical element — a reference that exists is used, none in the skill means a web search, none found there means the closest thing, and the choice goes under `decisions` (root `SKILL.md` §0). It is never a stop. On any other piece this map is staging craft only. Eight cards on this shelf carried historical claims as settled fact, and on 2026-09-23 the owner ruled them moved to attributed form: `meso_stage.sacred_tree_ritual_symmetry`, `meso_stage.babylonian_processional_way_staging`, `meso_stage.mesopotamian_chariot_charge_vector`, `meso_stage.stepped_ziggurat_low_angle_perspective`, `meso_stage.ancient_marsh_reed_boat_silhouette`, `meso_stage.royal_profile_dignity_framing`, `meso_stage.water_basin_temple_reflection`, `meso_stage.zagros_mountain_fortress_scale`. Each now names what has to be **taken from the reference** - the count of the lions, the bow, the crown, what stood on the summit, which boat - instead of supplying it. **This selector does not supply any of it either.** On a realistic history piece, reaching one of these cards means those details come from a reference — found, searched, or the closest documented thing, noted — and then the prompt is written. Then answer the three questions in order, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf. Where two answers fight for one frame, that is two shots.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage Film, Historical Documentary, Epic | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Epic, History  
**Avoid when:** **The shelf's pair graph is a list of near-duplicates, not a map of choices, and this card inherits that.** Every reciprocal pair on it — the gate twice, the temple interior twice, the relief wall twice, the procession twice — is two cards pointing at *the same physical thing*, and no pair on it ever crosses between two different subjects. So the map can tell you which of two treatments of one gate to use and **cannot tell you whether the heritage is the subject of the shot or the background to it**, which is the question actually being asked most of the time. **Distrust it entirely whenever your honest answer to axis three is the last value** — a card with no ground is a card this shelf never placed. Also avoid it where the scene is fast, casual or comic: the `Avoid when:` fields here refuse that scene over and over — *"Fast outdoor action chases"*, *"Fast comedy sequences"*, *"Fast comedic slapstick"*, *"Casual conversational banter"* — and a shelf that refuses a whole tempo is not a shelf you should be choosing from at that tempo. And it is **not a fact-checker**: see the standing rule above.  
**Example:** `[Selector — produces no staging line. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the staging line.]`

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — What is the frame holding: a place, an object, or a body?**
Asked first, because it is the only condition that ever moves a reader between two different kinds of card rather than between two versions of one. `meso_stage.ancient_armour_bronze_scale_detail` — an insert of a cuirass — hands off with *"Civilian palace banquets, where armour is absent"* and lands on a whole staged room. `meso_stage.cylinder_seal_unrolling_narrative` and `meso_stage.nineveh_palace_wall_frieze_panning` trade the same beat between a carved object in the hand and a carved wall in a corridor: *"The transition must enter the live scene"* against *"The artifact stays an artifact, no dissolve."* And the `Avoid when:` fields split the same way without being asked to — every place card on this shelf refuses a small room (*"Low-budget domestic kitchen scenes"*, *"Small intimate village bedrooms"*, *"Tight indoor interior dialogue scenes"*), and every object card refuses speed (*"Fast outdoor action chases"*).

**Axis 2 — Does anything cross the frame, or does the frame itself move?**
The condition the shelf states most often, and it is stated as a fact about the beat rather than a feeling: *"Same gate, when the beat is the crossing"* against *"Same gate, when the beat is scale and awe"* (`meso_stage.winged_bull_gatekeeper_threshold` and `meso_stage.lamassu_colossal_scale_ratio`); *"A solitary prayer held still, not drawn inward"* against *"The sacred interior read as depth, not reflection"* (`meso_stage.water_basin_temple_reflection` and `meso_stage.temple_courtyard_deep_perspective`); *"Active battlefield skirmishes, which that card owns"* (`meso_stage.royal_banquet_horizontal_hierarchy`). Two of the shelf's cards are a third answer on their own — the thing that moves is the camera or the image itself, not anything inside it.

**Axis 3 — Whose ground is this, and does the card own it?**
Four of the shelf's conditions are pure handoffs of territory — *not this card, that one owns that material*: *"Mountain fortress material, which that card owns"* (`meso_stage.ancient_marsh_reed_boat_silhouette`), *"Active battlefield skirmishes, which that card owns"* (`meso_stage.royal_banquet_horizontal_hierarchy`), *"Civilian palace banquets, where armour is absent"* (`meso_stage.ancient_armour_bronze_scale_detail`), and the Assyrian-against-Babylonian trade between `meso_stage.bas_relief_frieze_horizontal_staging` and `meso_stage.babylonian_processional_way_staging`. The `Avoid when:` fields carry the same boundary in the opposite direction — *"Mountain fortress battles"*, *"Lowland mudbrick agricultural sequences"*, *"Daytime city markets"*, *"Peaceful treaty negotiations inside closed council chambers"*. Asked last, because it is the only axis with a value that holds a single card, and that card is the finding rather than the answer.

**The default, and the one against it**

**This shelf cannot name a default by in-degree, and that is the first thing to know about it.** Every inbound edge on it is singular: no card is the target of more than one `Yields to:`, and the shelf is six disconnected pairs and handoffs rather than a graph with a centre. Counting conditions behind inbound edges — which is the only honest count — gives every reached card exactly one, so the measurement that names a default elsewhere returns a flat line here. **The default below is therefore argued, and labelled as argued.**

**The default is `meso_stage.bas_relief_frieze_horizontal_staging`, and it is the default because the rest of the shelf is defined against it.** `meso_stage.babylonian_processional_way_staging` describes itself by negation — *"deep avenue, not flat frieze"* — and hands back on *"An Assyrian procession, or no avenue to shoot down."* `meso_stage.nineveh_palace_wall_frieze_panning` is the same wall with the camera moving. `meso_stage.cylinder_seal_unrolling_narrative` is the same carved surface made to dissolve. The frieze convention is the thing this shelf keeps re-describing, which is what a default is. **It is also the most worn image the subject has**, and that is the reason for the next paragraph rather than an objection to this one.

**The one against it is `meso_stage.desert_camp_firelight_caravanserai`.** Nothing on this shelf routes to it, it names no alternative of its own, and it is the only card here where the frame holds **no monument, no relief, no palace and no king** — a circle of people, a fire, and darkness past them. Every other card on the shelf proves the civilisation by showing something it built; this one proves it by showing someone talking in it, which is the register a heritage piece almost never spends a shot on and the register an audience actually sits still for. Its stated cost is narrow and payable — its own `Avoid when:` gives up only *"Daytime city markets"* — and unlike most of the shelf it carries no flagged historical claim, so it is the cheapest card here to clear. Its near cousin against the grain is `meso_stage.ancient_marsh_reed_boat_silhouette`, which likewise holds no architecture, but which does carry a flagged claim and therefore owes the reference pass first.

### Continuous Bas-Relief Horizontal Frieze Staging

**Also called:** Ashurbanipal frieze staging, palace relief composition, linear narrative frieze  
**What it is:** Composing a widescreen storyboard panel as a continuous horizontal stone relief, with figures arranged in parallel planes against an incised architectural or natural background.  
**Effect on the audience:** Evokes majestic historical authenticity, ancient regal dignity, and the tactile feeling of walking along the palace corridors of Nineveh.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Royal processions, military marches, banquet celebrations, and historical heritage documentaries.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage Film, Historical Documentary, Epic | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Epic, History  
**Avoid when:** Modern casual vlog framing or tight diagonal shaky-cam footage.  
**Example:** `staging: Continuous horizontal frieze composition, Assyrian spearmen and royal archers marching in the sequence's already established screen direction in formal profile against carved limestone wall; do not reverse direction merely to imitate a different relief panel.`  
**Yields to:** `meso_stage.babylonian_processional_way_staging` — A Babylonian procession: deep avenue, not flat frieze.  
`meso_stage.bas_relief_frieze_horizontal_staging`

### Colossal Lamassu Architectural Scale Ratio

**Also called:** winged bull scale framing, portal guardian ratio, monumental threshold  
**What it is:** Framing human characters against the colossal carved winged bulls (Lamassu) guarding palace gateway arches at the statues' real scale: the surviving ones stand about 3 to 5 meters tall (see Source), so the awe comes from the object's own size, not from a ratio chosen for effect.  
**Effect on the audience:** Instills awe, divine intimidation, and the overwhelming majesty of imperial Assyrian architecture.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Characters entering royal palace gates, foreign ambassadors arriving at Dur-Sharrukin or Nimrud, and ceremonial entrances.  
**Best in:** formats: Epic Feature, Historical Documentary | genres: Heritage, Epic, Fantasy  
**Avoid when:** Low-budget domestic kitchen scenes.  
**Example:** `composition: Low-angle extreme wide shot, human royal guard standing small at lower left frame, dwarfed by a carved alabaster Lamassu gatekeeper roughly 3 to 5 meters (10 to 16 feet) tall.`  
**Source:** reference — surviving lamassu run from about 3.1 m / 10 ft 3 in (Nimrud pair, The Metropolitan Museum of Art, acc. 32.143.1, https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/322608) to about 4.2 m / 13 ft 9 in (Khorsabad pair, Musée du Louvre, AO 19857, https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl010122557) to about 5 m / 16 ft (the largest Khorsabad bull, Institute for the Study of Ancient Cultures, University of Chicago, https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/collex/exhibits/discovery-collection-memory-oriental-institute-100/human-headed-winged-bull/), all checked 2026-09-23; no single figure fits every surviving example.  
**Yields to:** `meso_stage.winged_bull_gatekeeper_threshold` — Same gate, when the beat is the crossing.  
`meso_stage.lamassu_colossal_scale_ratio`

### Royal Profile Dignity Framing

**Also called:** monarch profile convention, formal royal eye-line, imperial silhouette  
**What it is:** Staging the Assyrian or Babylonian emperor strictly in clean, unbroken profile view, wearing the royal crown as the reliefs of the period show it (take it from the reference, not from this card), with curled beard and dignified level eyeline.  
**Effect on the audience:** Communicates unshakeable sovereign authority, a divinely appointed mandate to rule, and historical reverence; echoes the profile convention of the royal reliefs - reference the reliefs rather than trusting this line.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Royal throne rooms, formal decrees, receiving tributes, and ceremonial hunting sequences.  
**Best in:** formats: Historical Epic, Biopic, Heritage Documentary | genres: Heritage, Drama, Epic  
**Avoid when:** Humorous, goofy, or disheveled modern character treatments.  
**Example:** `framing: Medium close-up strictly in right profile, King Ashurbanipal wearing tasseled royal tunic and the banded polos crown (a truncated cone with a smaller cone at its crest, not a pointed cone; J. E. Reade, Studia Orientalia 106, 2009), sharp aristocratic profile under directional lighting.`  
**Source:** reference — ORACC (University of Pennsylvania), "Nimrud: Materialities of Assyrian Knowledge Production — Aššur, divine embodiment of Assyria," https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/nimrud/ancientkalhu/thepeople/ashur/, checked 2026-09-23; and N. Brisch, "Of Gods and Kings: Divine Kingship in Ancient Mesopotamia," Religion Compass 7/2 (2013), https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/rec3.12031, checked 2026-09-23; Neo-Assyrian kings styled themselves iššiakku (vice-regent) of the god Aššur, chosen and named by the god, not born of divine descent.  
`meso_stage.royal_profile_dignity_framing`

### Stepped Ziggurat Low-Angle Perspective

**Also called:** ziggurat ascension angle, cosmic stair framing, Sumerian pyramid scale  
**What it is:** Placing the camera at the base of a documented stair or ramp — at Ur, one of three staircases (a central flight and two flanking flights that converge at a gateway on the second stage) — looking upward at a 35-to-45 degree angle toward the summit of a stepped ziggurat. Whether a shrine stood at the top, and what it looked like, is a reconstruction - take it from the reference, not from this card, and label a conjectural summit as a reconstruction.  
**Effect on the audience:** Visualizes the connection between heaven and earth; elevates the high priest or king into a cosmic mediator standing against the sun or stars.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Religious ceremonies, astronomical observations, solar eclipses, and sacrificial processions. On a history piece the user wants realistic, astronomical observation or sacrifice at the ziggurat is searched and attributed on its own, because the building form does not establish it; on any other piece it is staged as the story wants.  
**Best in:** formats: Historical Epic, Religious Drama, Heritage Film | genres: Heritage, Faith, Epic  
**Avoid when:** Tight indoor interior dialogue scenes.  
**Example:** `camera_angle: Low angle worm's-eye perspective looking up the monumental brick stairs of the Ziggurat of Ur, sunset golden flare crowning the summit.`  
**Source:** reference — Penn Museum, "The Ziggurat of Ur," The Museum Journal, https://www.penn.museum/sites/journal/1235/, checked 2026-09-23; three flights of about a hundred steps each — a central flight and two flanking flights running up against the wall face — converge at the top in a gateway through the parapet of the second stage, per L. Woolley's excavation for the Penn Museum/British Museum expedition.  
`meso_stage.stepped_ziggurat_low_angle_perspective`

### Ishtar Gate Glazed Lapis and Ochre Color Staging

**Also called:** Babylonian glazed brick palette, lapis-ochre royal contrast  
**What it is:** Framing sequences using the authentic vibrant color contrast of Babylon's Ishtar Gate: deep lapis lazuli glazed blue walls (#0A2240) contrasted with raised golden-yellow ochre reliefs of Sirrush dragons and bulls.  
**Effect on the audience:** Shocks modern audiences who mistakenly believe the ancient world was monochromatic dusty grey; radiates opulent imperial wealth and sacred splendor.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Triumphal processions into Babylon, royal coronation scenes, and military returns.  
**Best in:** formats: Historical Epic, Visual Lookbook | genres: Heritage, Epic, Historical  
**Avoid when:** Somber desolate ruin scenes where glazed blue ruins would look anachronistically intact.  
**Example:** `color_staging: Vibrant glazed lapis lazuli cobalt blue brick backdrop, highlighted by golden ochre relief carvings of marching Sirrush dragons and lions.`  
`meso_stage.ishtar_gate_lapis_lazuli_color_staging`

### Cuneiform Clay Tablet Macro Insert

**Also called:** stylus impression insert, cuneiform library shot, wedge script close-up  
**What it is:** An extreme close-up macro shot with shallow depth of field, focusing on moist clay receiving triangular reed stylus impressions under soft raking sidelight.  
**Effect on the audience:** Bridges millenia of intellectual history; conveys scholarly precision, sacred knowledge, and the birth of written civilization.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Scribe school scenes (*Edubba*), royal library of Ashurbanipal, astronomical omen recordings, and legal contracts.  
**Best in:** formats: Historical Documentary, Memoir, Drama | genres: Heritage, Biography, History  
**Avoid when:** Fast outdoor action chases.  
**Example:** `macro_shot: Extreme close-up on damp clay tablet, warm raking sidelight casting deep shadows into wedge-shaped cuneiform impressions, reed stylus in soft focus.`  
`meso_stage.cuneiform_tablet_macro_insert`

### Mesopotamian Chariot Charge Vector

**Also called:** royal hunting vector, two-wheeled war chariot framing  
**What it is:** Staging royal war chariots moving aggressively across the frame in the sequence's one screen direction — left to right unless a frieze march (`meso_stage.bas_relief_frieze_horizontal_staging`) or a relief pan (`meso_stage.nineveh_palace_wall_frieze_panning`) cut beside it already runs right to left, in which case the charge runs with them (`cut.screen_direction`) — with spoked wheels throwing up desert dust, horses at full gallop, and royal archers drawing bows (the bow's type, and the chariot, team and crew: take them from a named relief or archaeological source for the period, not from this card).  
**Effect on the audience:** Delivers relentless momentum, kinetic battlefield supremacy, and ancient tactical lethality.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Royal lion hunt recreations, open-field chariot charges, and imperial conquest scenes.  
**Best in:** formats: Action Epic, War Film, Trailer | genres: Action, Heritage, War  
**Avoid when:** Peaceful treaty negotiations inside closed council chambers.  
**Example:** `action_vector: Left-to-right tracking shot at wheel height (right-to-left if the sequence already runs that way), royal two-wheeled Assyrian chariot galloping through dry scrubland, archer drawing bow aimed off-screen.`  
`meso_stage.mesopotamian_chariot_charge_vector`

### Temple Courtyard Deep Perspective Staging

**Also called:** Mesopotamian axial symmetry, temple door frame, portal alignment  
**What it is:** Aligning three or four successive arched palace portals in a straight linear axis, creating extreme one-point perspective from the outer sunny courtyard into the dim inner holy of holies.  
**Effect on the audience:** Guides the viewer into ancient mystery and architectural secrecy; establishes spatial depth and sacred hierarchy.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Secret royal conspiracies, priestly initiations, and solemn solo prayers to the gods (Ashur, Ishtar, Marduk).  
**Best in:** formats: Historical Feature, Mystery, Religious Drama | genres: Heritage, Mystery, Drama  
**Avoid when:** Modern handheld documentary interviews.  
**Example:** `one_point_perspective: Symmetrical frame down a succession of three arched stone doorways, sunlight fading into flickering oil lamp glow in the inner temple.`  
**Yields to:** `meso_stage.water_basin_temple_reflection` — A solitary prayer held still, not drawn inward.

### Sacred Tree Ritual Symmetry

**Also called:** Ashur tree framing, apkallu bilateral symmetry, winged genie ritual  
**What it is:** Staging two winged protective spirits (*apkallu*) or kings facing each other in perfect bilateral symmetry, holding the cone and the bucket the reliefs show, flanking the stylized sacred tree. What the cone and bucket were for, and what the tree meant, are interpretations - on a realistic history piece, narration attributes them rather than stating them as settled.  
**Effect on the audience:** Creates mystical sacred harmony, eternal ritual cosmic order, and religious stillness.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Temple rites, royal blessing ceremonies, and mythical dream visions. On a realistic history piece, a literal purification or coronation scene takes its detail from a searched source beyond the motif itself, or the closest documented rite, noted under `decisions`.  
**Best in:** formats: Mythology, Religious Epic, Heritage Documentary | genres: Heritage, Mythology, Fantasy  
**Avoid when:** Casual conversational banter.
