# Composition

69 cards. Also in the other stage: a shot has a frame too. These decide a frame: what is in it, how it is lit, and what it is made of.

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, what to avoid, and the prompt written out in full.

---

### What Holds the Frame

**Also called:** the composition selector, what holds the frame, the frame before the subject
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `comp.` shelf. It does not describe a look. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **what is supplying the composition, what will defeat it, and does it settle the scene or press on it** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were not invented: they were read off the forty-eight `Yields to:` conditions the cards carry, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id. **`comp.` decides the rectangle. `block.` decides the floor.** The test is mechanical: if the answer changes when you move the camera and nobody else moves, it is a `comp.` question; if it changes when somebody moves and the camera does not, it is a `block.` one.
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so the card that does reach the screen was chosen against the location, the schedule and the delivery surface, rather than picked because it was remembered.
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf.** The first eliminates most of the file, the second cuts it again against the day you actually have, and the third is only asked of the cards that come in reversing pairs. Ask question one of the frame, not of the people in it — where the bodies stand and where they look is the other shelf's question, and asking it here produces a staging plan with no frame in it.
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all
**Avoid when:** **You are matching an existing board panel, a reference still or a graded frame** — there the composition is measured off the picture, not chosen from a shelf. Also **distrust axis one whenever your honest answer is its second value**: most of the shelf lives there, so on a location you did not design and cannot dress, this map has two questions left and is doing two thirds of the work it does on a built set. And **never read a margin number off this map.** `comp.safe_areas` keeps the principle and the broadcast figures; `vertical.safe_zones` is the single carrier of the vertical ones, which `COLLISIONS.md` settled — one number, one place, so the board and the still are composed to the same frame. Finally, **this map says nothing about the cut.** Every question here is asked of one frame standing alone, which is exactly the blind spot named under *the unasked*.
**Prompt:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

---

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — What is supplying this composition: the rectangle, the contents, or the border?**
Asked first, because it is the condition the shelf repeats most. Roughly a dozen `Yields to:` lines turn on which of the three is doing the work — *"The scene is built from straight lines"* (`comp.golden_ratio`), *"No time to work out an armature"* (`comp.dynamic_symmetry`), *"The shot needs real depth in the space"* (`comp.planimetric`), *"Tight interior with no room for a curve"* (`comp.s_curve`), *"Indoors, with no haze to separate planes"* (`comp.aerial_perspective`), *"The enclosure is landscape, not architecture"* (`comp.frame_within_frame`), *"Phone viewing, where the edge crowds the border"* (`comp.frame_edge_tension`), *"Converting existing horizontal material rather than composing new"* (`comp.vertical_stack`). A composition supplied by the empty rectangle can be drawn on paper before anyone is cast. One supplied by the contents cannot be chosen at all until you have seen the room. One supplied by the border is not really yours — the delivery surface set it.

**Axis 2 — What will defeat this frame: nothing much, the place and the clock, or the screen it plays on?**
A dozen conditions are about feasibility, not look, and they are stated as facts — *"Handheld or run-and-gun, no control of set"* (`comp.fincher_low_offcenter`), *"The camera cannot be squared to the millimetre"* (`comp.one_point_perspective`), *"Architecture not symmetrical enough for a centred one-point"* (`comp.kubrick_center`), *"Location cannot land exact symmetry"* (`comp.symmetry`), *"Doorways not on one axis, or dim"* (`comp.layered_doorway`), *"The shot has to move and breaks the illusion"* (`comp.forced_perspective`), *"Branches would block the action or move"* (`comp.natural_frame`). Eight more name the surface instead — *"A vertical reel, where a void loses the face"* (`comp.negative_space`), *"Phone viewing in daylight, where shadows collapse"* (`comp.light_contrast`), *"Broadcast and interview treat it as technical error"* (`comp.excess_headroom`), *"Commercial or instructional work, where a crop reads careless"* (`comp.breaking_frame_edge`), *"A vertical frame, where the band becomes an odd crop"* (`comp.leone_eyes_strip`), and the kids carve-out on `comp.no_headroom`. **This is the axis that decides whether you get the frame you drew**, and it is the reason the shelf's graph runs downhill: nearly every elaborate card names a condition under which you fall back to a plain one.

**Axis 3 — Does the frame settle the scene, or press on it?**
Ten conditions turn on this and every one of them names a fact in the script rather than a feeling in the viewer — *"Authority or confrontation wanted, not comfort"* (`comp.rule_of_thirds`), *"The scene is built on conflict"* (`comp.balance`), *"The scene must carry real danger"* (`comp.wes_anderson_center`), *"No dramatic reason for the lean"* (`comp.imbalance`), *"A calm head-on frame with no slant"* (`comp.golden_triangles`), *"Comfortable or comic scene, where offset reads as error"* (`comp.off_center_unease`), *"Calm or formal scene, or interview background"* (`comp.diagonals`), *"You want constriction rather than comfort"* (`comp.lead_room`), *"Weakness or loss must be stated"* (`comp.headroom`). **The proof that this is a fork and not praise in disguise is that the shelf carries reversing pairs on it** — `comp.balance` against `comp.imbalance`, `comp.headroom` against `comp.excess_headroom`, `comp.lead_room` against `comp.short_siding`, `comp.high_horizon` against `comp.low_horizon`, `comp.minimalism` against `comp.maximalism`. A field that cannot discriminate does not produce cards that point at each other and mean opposite things. It is asked last because it is the only axis most of the shelf declines to answer.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `comp.rule_of_thirds` holding the placement with `comp.depth_layers` holding the planes — and it is measured, not asserted.** `comp.rule_of_thirds` is the most-yielded-to card on the shelf, and it survives the test that matters: the inbound edges fire on **four different conditions**, not four repetitions of one. `comp.golden_triangles` sends you there for *"A calm head-on frame with no slant"*, `comp.dynamic_symmetry` for *"No time to work out an armature"*, `comp.fincher_low_offcenter` for *"Handheld or run-and-gun, no control of set"*, `comp.off_center_unease` for *"Comfortable or comic scene, where offset reads as error"*, and `sig.center_punch_framing` reaches in from outside the shelf as well. Four unrelated failures, one address. That is a fallback. `comp.depth_layers` earns the second half on the same test from two conditions — *"The shot needs real depth in the space"* (`comp.planimetric`) and *"Indoors, with no haze to separate planes"* (`comp.aerial_perspective`) — and it is one of only three cards here that read `Best in: formats: all | genres: all`.

**And the counter-example is on this shelf, which is why the test is worth running.** `comp.two_faces_one_away` takes two inbound edges and looks like a small default. It is not. `comp.rule_of_odds` sends you there for *"The even count is the meaning: two facing off"* and `comp.triangle` for *"Intimate two-hander with no real third element"* — **two edges, one condition: there are exactly two people.** It is a door out of the arrangement region, not something the shelf falls back on. `comp.imbalance` fails the same test: both its inbound edges say the scene has conflict.

**The one against it is `comp.over_the_void`.** Nothing routes to it, nothing routes out of it, and it is the only card here that puts the emptiness **between the lens and the subject** rather than around them or behind them. `comp.negative_space`, `comp.isolated_figure` and `comp.scale_contrast` all leave the camera in the room and make the subject small inside it; `comp.over_the_void` puts a courtyard, a stairwell or a valley in the way, so the distance is the audience's and not the character's — the viewer is the one who cannot cross. It inverts the shelf's unstated assumption that the camera is standing on the same floor as the scene. Its only stated cost is the lens: on a long lens the compression removes the gap and you are left with an ordinary distant shot. Its cousin against the grain is `comp.empty_chair`, which composes the person who is not there, and which is likewise the target of nothing.

The graph runs downhill.** Every elaborate composition on this shelf yields outward and almost nothing yields inward: `comp.golden_ratio`, `comp.dynamic_symmetry`, `comp.symmetry`, `comp.kubrick_center` and `comp.wes_anderson_center` are all on the list, `comp.golden_triangles` and `comp.fincher_low_offcenter` are reached exactly once each and only because a location failed, and the plain card takes four inbound conditions. **The pair field was filled in by readers asking *what do I do when this one does not work*, and the answer to that question is always the simpler card.** Nothing anywhere on the shelf says *your film has gone flat — go and build the armature.* The graph therefore cannot be used to raise ambition, only to recover from it, and a map that trusted in-degree alone would drive every project toward the same comfortable frame.

The second thing the list shows: **the whole continuity region is unasked** — `comp.screen_direction`, `comp.eyeline_height`, `comp.open_closed_frame`, `comp.tilted_horizon`, `comp.tangent_avoid`, `comp.vertical_stack`. These are the cards that govern how one frame relates to the **next** one, and the pair field cannot express them, because a reader comparing two compositions was holding one frame at a time. **A frame composed against the frame before it is not an alternative to a frame composed alone — it is a different kind of answer.** That is the new card this shelf wants: nothing here says *this composition exists to be the reverse of the last one*, and the nearest thing that exists, `comp.screen_direction`, records the failure in its own `Avoid when:` — *flipping the direction for no reason makes two characters feel like they are not facing each other* — without anything routing a reader there to read it.

The third: **not one pointer crosses between this shelf and `block.`, in either direction.** Two shelves that decide the same picture, adjacent in the same phase, and no reader on either side ever reached across. Nine subjects are held twice, once here and once there — `comp.triangle` against `block.three_actor_triangle`, `comp.grouping` against `block.the_line`, `comp.two_faces_one_away` against `block.shoulder_to_shoulder`, `comp.dirty_frame` against `block.body_occlusion_reveal`, `comp.depth_layers` against `block.depth_staging`, `comp.shooting_through` against `block.behind_glass`, `comp.frame_within_frame` against `block.doorway_threshold`, `comp.vertical_bars` against `block.barrier_between`, `comp.empty_chair` against `block.empty_half_bed` — and the boundary between them is written down in exactly one place, about vertical margins. ### Rule of Thirds

**Also called:** thirds grid, third lines  
**What it is:** The frame is split by two vertical and two horizontal lines, and the subject, or the character's eye, sits on a line or on an intersection instead of dead centre.  
**Effect on the audience:** Comfortable balance with a little energy. The frame reads as neither static nor chaotic, and the background still gets room to speak.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The default for most shots: dialogue, interview, documentary, a walking shot in a reel. It is the right call when you want the character and the place in one frame, because the remaining two thirds work as context rather than wasted space.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** When you want authority, unease or entrapment. Thirds deliver comfort, and comfort kills tension. If every shot in the project sits on the thirds, the look becomes a catalogue with no voice.  
**Prompt:** `composed on the rule of thirds, his eyes on the upper third line, body on the left third, open space to the right`  
**Yields to:** `comp.centered` — Authority or confrontation wanted, not comfort.  
`comp.rule_of_thirds`

### Golden Ratio / Golden Spiral

**Also called:** phi grid, Fibonacci spiral  
**What it is:** An uneven division that puts the subject line slightly closer to centre than the thirds do, with a spiral curling in from the frame edge toward one small point.  
**Effect on the audience:** An organic, flowing feel. The eye enters from the edges and slides inward until it lands on the thing that matters, without feeling steered.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Scenes that already carry a natural curve: a spiral staircase, a winding road, a reclining body, an architectural arch, moving hair or cloth. Strong in fashion, heritage documentary and product video when you want the frame to feel painted rather than stacked.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Fashion Film, Product Video / Motion Design, Commercial / TVC, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Romance, Drama, Fantasy / Epic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Nature / Wildlife, Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** Do not force it onto a scene built from straight lines; you will bend the composition for nothing. Remember that much of what is written about the golden ratio in film is after the fact analysis, not a plan the cinematographer followed.  
**Prompt:** `golden spiral composition, the winding road curling from the bottom left edge inward and ending on the small figure at the spiral centre`  
**Yields to:** `comp.golden_triangles` — The scene is built from straight lines.  
`comp.golden_ratio`

### Golden Triangles

**Also called:** diagonal method, triangle grid  
**What it is:** You draw a diagonal from corner to corner, then two perpendiculars onto it from the other two corners. The frame splits into triangles, and elements sit on those lines and on their intersections.  
**Effect on the audience:** Order inside movement. A tilted composition stays balanced instead of collapsing to one side.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Any scene built on a slant: a staircase, a sloping street, an outstretched arm, a drawn sword, a wing, a long shadow across the floor. Useful in action and in heritage work when a pyramid, a ziggurat or a temple stair is in the frame.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Action, Adventure, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Mythology / Ancient Epic, Fantasy / Epic, War, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Samurai / Chanbara  
**Avoid when:** It does not suit calm, head on frames; it makes a still scene feel restless for no reason.  
**Prompt:** `golden triangle composition, the stair rail cutting the frame corner to corner, the figure placed where the perpendicular meets the diagonal`  
**Yields to:** `comp.rule_of_thirds` — A calm head-on frame with no slant.  
`comp.golden_triangles`

### Dynamic Symmetry

**Also called:** armature of the rectangle, root rectangle grid  
**What it is:** A grid derived from the diagonals of the frame itself rather than from a fixed division, so every aspect ratio gets its own armature, and elements land on those diagonals and their crossings.  
**Effect on the audience:** The image feels designed and locked because the compositional lines follow the shape of the frame itself instead of an imported grid.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The hard ratios: wide 2.39 scope and vertical 9:16, where thirds give a flat result. Use it for posters, title sequences, fashion, and whenever you rebuild a horizontal composition as a vertical one.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Fashion Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Experimental / Art Film, 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Noir / Neo-Noir, Fantasy / Epic, Historical / Biopic, Surreal / Absurdist, Action  
**Avoid when:** There is no time for geometry on set. Work it out in the storyboard, and do not let it turn into maths that strangles the shot.  
**Prompt:** `composition built on the frame diagonals, subject placed on a diagonal crossing point, background lines echoing the same armature`  
**Yields to:** `comp.rule_of_thirds` — No time to work out an armature.  
`comp.dynamic_symmetry`

### Centred Composition

**Also called:** dead centre framing, centre punch  
**What it is:** The subject sits exactly on the vertical centre line, with the background usually arranged around it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Confrontation and authority. The viewer is not allowed to choose where to look; they are placed in front of the subject. If the space around it is not symmetrical, a low grade discomfort creeps in.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat of power, judgement or confession, and the face that meets the viewer in documentary and interview. Essential in reels and shorts for the face held to the viewer, where the frame is narrow and an offset loses it (a walking shot in a reel, where the place shares the frame, is `comp.rule_of_thirds`), and essential in fast action, because the eye never has to hunt between cuts. On a frame that will be animated with lateral or forward movement, leave the subject off centre into the direction of travel (`kf.motion_headroom`); centre only locked-off frames and the settled final frame of a move, and mark on the board which panels are start frames so the distinction survives the handoff.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Vertical Micro-Drama, Interview / Video Podcast, Documentary Feature, Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, Anime (Japanese 2D), Music Video, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Horror, Action, Crime / Procedural, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Psychological / Arthouse, Shonen Battle (anime)  
**Avoid when:** In a quiet two hander it severs the relationship and strands each actor on an island. Centre everything and you lose eye movement across the whole film.  
**Prompt:** `dead centre composition, subject on the vertical centre line facing the lens, symmetrical background, locked off camera`  
**Example:** Mad Max: Fury Road, the point of interest held in the centre of the frame so the eye never hunts between cuts.  
**Yields to:** `kf.motion_headroom` — on a start frame that will be animated with lateral or forward movement. Centre only locked-off frames and the settled final frame of a move.  
`comp.centered`

### Symmetry / Mirror Composition

**Also called:** bilateral symmetry, mirrored frame  
**What it is:** One half of the frame mirrors the other, either vertically (left and right) or horizontally (a reflection in water or polished floor).  
**Effect on the audience:** Imposed order. Beautiful and threatening at once, because nature is never this exact, so symmetry means somebody arranged this place.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Temples, palaces, halls and institutions, and the moment a character enters an ordered world bigger than they are. In heritage work it is the facade and the gateway shot; in horror it makes the place feel like it is watching.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, 3D CG Animation, Anime (Japanese 2D), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Horror, Thriller / Suspense, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Mythology / Ancient Epic, Faith / Biblical Epic, Sci-Fi, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** Symmetry that is off by a little reads as a mistake, not a choice. Either land it exactly or break it clearly.  
**Prompt:** `perfectly symmetrical composition, the hall mirrored left and right around the centre line, camera square to the back wall`  
**Yields to:** `comp.pattern_break` — Location cannot land exact symmetry; break it clearly.  
`comp.symmetry`

### Planimetric Composition / Flat Frontal Staging

**Also called:** frontal staging, flat space  
**What it is:** The camera sits exactly perpendicular to the wall or plane behind the subject, every line runs parallel to the frame edges, depth flattens out and the image reads like a technical drawing.  
**Effect on the audience:** Cold irony and order. People become elements inside a design instead of people inside a place.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Deadpan comedy, dystopia, bureaucracy, and any scene where you want the viewer to step back and observe from outside. Excellent for ads and teasers because the frame reads in a quarter of a second, and excellent for information cards inside a documentary.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Explainer, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), 3D CG Animation, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Experimental / Art Film, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Dark Comedy / Satire, Comedy, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Sci-Fi, Surreal / Absurdist, Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Science / Educational  
**Avoid when:** In close emotional scenes it chills the feeling and pushes the viewer away. Do not use it when you need depth and distance inside the space.

### The Kubrick Centre / One-Point Symmetry

**Also called:** Kubrick stare framing, symmetrical one-point  
**What it is:** Full centring married to one-point perspective: the corridor or room recedes to a vanishing point in the middle of the frame, the figure stands on that same axis, camera height locked and the lens wide.  
**Effect on the audience:** Terrifying order. The architecture feels designed against the character, and the viewer slides from empathy into cold witness.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Institutional corridors, hotels, hospitals, temples and long halls, and the beat where a character comes apart inside a system larger than them. Strongest in psychological horror and sci-fi, and in a teaser that must say from frame one that this world is not normal.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Trailer / Teaser, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Drama Series Episode, 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Experimental / Art Film, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Psychological Horror, Horror, Sci-Fi, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Thriller / Suspense, Surreal / Absurdist, Mystery / Whodunit, Drama  
**Avoid when:** It needs genuinely symmetrical architecture; a location that cannot deliver gives you a lopsided centre. Used in every scene it turns into visible imitation and the dread evaporates.  
**Prompt:** `Kubrick style one-point symmetry, long corridor receding to a central vanishing point, the figure dead centre, wide lens, locked off`  
**Example:** The Shining, the corridors of the Overlook Hotel.  
**Yields to:** `comp.fincher_low_offcenter` — Architecture not symmetrical enough for a centred one-point.

### The Wes Anderson Centre / Dollhouse Framing

**Also called:** storybook symmetry, dollhouse frame  
**What it is:** Strict centring and symmetry with the camera square to the background, flat ordered colour, and set dressing lined up on the axis like a dollhouse.  
**Effect on the audience:** Whimsy with sadness underneath. The story feels read aloud from a book, and the emotion stays trapped inside a tidy shape.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Melancholy comedy, a childhood story, a playful ad, a laid out product video. It works in kids content and motion design because clear axes make movement and transitions easy to build.

**Avoid when:** In any scene that needs real chaos or danger, the tidiness contradicts the drama. It is also a very recognisable signature, so using it without a reason reads as imitation.  
**Prompt:** `perfectly centred dollhouse framing, camera square to the back wall, symmetrical set dressing, flat pastel palette, subject dead centre`

**Yields to:** `comp.imbalance` — The scene must carry real danger.  
`comp.wes_anderson_center`

### The Low Off-Centre Frame

**Also called:** precision framing, cold geometric frame  
**What it is:** The figure sits slightly low in the frame and off the centre axis, the camera is absolutely locked, the background lines are squared off, and the air above and beside the subject is measured to the millimetre. A style associated with David Fincher.  
**Effect on the audience:** Cold surveillance. It feels like someone is watching the scene through glass and logging it, rather than the camera living alongside the character.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Crime, investigation, interrogation, and office meetings where somebody is lying. It fits a crime series and a legal deposition scene, and any moment where you want the viewer reading detail instead of feeling.

**Avoid when:** It needs total control of set and light. Handheld or in run and gun documentary it just looks like a badly placed frame.

### Off-Centre for Unease

**Also called:** wrong placement, uncomfortable offset  
**What it is:** The subject lands between the third line and the frame edge, neither centred nor on the thirds, in a spot the eye registers as wrong.  
**Effect on the audience:** Anxiety with no named cause. The viewer senses that something in the frame is off and cannot say what, which is exactly the point.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat of doubt, the lie, the character hiding something, and the first sign that the world is tilting in psychological horror. Useful in a trailer when you want the feeling to change before the events do.

`comp.off_center_unease`

### Visual Balance

**Also called:** balanced frame, equilibrium  
**What it is:** Spreading elements across the frame so it does not lean to one side: a figure on one side answered by a window, a lamp or a shadow on the other.  
**Effect on the audience:** Stability and calm. The viewer settles into the scene and attends to words and faces rather than to the frame.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Long dialogue scenes, the family scene, the interview, the explainer. Essential in educational video and kids content, because a balanced frame lowers fatigue and keeps attention on the information.

**Yields to:** `comp.imbalance` — The scene is built on conflict.  
`comp.balance`

### Deliberate Imbalance

**Also called:** weighted frame, lopsided composition  
**What it is:** All the visual weight is packed into one side while the other is left empty or light, and it is deliberate, not an error.  
**Effect on the audience:** The frame feels about to tip. A physical tension that precedes the event, and the viewer starts waiting for something to come from the empty side.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Before the blow, before someone enters, on the bad news beat, and in the scene where a person is quietly collapsing. Very strong in suspense and horror, and in vertical micro drama because it builds expectation in a single second.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Vertical Micro-Drama, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Trailer / Teaser, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Music Video, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Horror, Drama, Psychological Horror, Crime / Procedural, Tragedy, Noir / Neo-Noir, Mystery / Whodunit  
**Avoid when:** With no dramatic reason it reads as careless framing. And if the shot runs long and nothing ever arrives from the empty side, the viewer feels cheated.  
**Prompt:** `deliberately unbalanced composition, all the weight crowded into the left third, the right side empty and waiting`  
**Yields to:** `comp.balance` — No dramatic reason for the lean.  
`comp.imbalance`

### Visual Weight

**Also called:** compositional weight, eye pull  
**What it is:** Every element in the frame pulls the eye with a different force: the face is strongest, then movement, then the brightest light, then saturated colour, then size and sharpness.  
**Effect on the audience:** It decides where the viewer looks within the first third of a second, and that single fact decides whether the shot lands or gets lost.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The design tool that comes before all the others: you plan the weight so the eye travels to the actor who matters, to the weapon, to the door, to the product. Critical in ads and reels where you have one second, and in any frame with more than one person.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** Two equal weights in one frame make the eye oscillate and the beat is lost. Decide which one is strongest and dial the others down.  
**Prompt:** `the brightest and sharpest element is her face, everything else darker, softer and less saturated so the eye goes straight to her`  
`comp.visual_weight`

### The Rule of Odds

**Also called:** odd numbers rule, three subjects  
**What it is:** An odd number of elements in the frame (one, three, five) instead of an even number, so one of them holds the middle and the picture does not split in two.  
**Effect on the audience:** The eye settles on one and circles the rest. An even count splits attention evenly and the frame feels undecided.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Group compositions: three people standing, three candles, three ships on the horizon. Useful in heritage documentary when arranging objects and statues, and in advertising when arranging products.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Kids Content, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), 3D CG Animation, Wedding / Event Film, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Drama, Family, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Nature / Wildlife, Fairy Tale, Science / Educational, Comedy  
**Avoid when:** When the even number is the meaning itself (two facing off, a duality, a confrontation) the rule hurts you. And in observational documentary you do not rearrange how many people are there.  
**Prompt:** `three figures arranged in the frame, one centre and two flanking, odd numbered grouping`  
**Yields to:** `comp.two_faces_one_away` — The even count is the meaning: two facing off.  
`comp.rule_of_odds`

### Grouping and Clustering

**Also called:** clustering, group shapes  
**What it is:** You arrange people as masses rather than individuals: a cluster of two and a cluster of one, or a block of four and an outsider. The gap between the clusters carries the message.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer reads the alliances instantly and without dialogue: who is with whom, and who is outside.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The family scene with a rift, the company meeting, the courtroom, the team before the match, any scene with group conflict. Strong in drama and series because it explains the relationships in one frame before a word is spoken.

**Avoid when:** If the grouping does not match the story of the scene it misleads: the viewer reads an alliance that does not exist.

`comp.grouping`

### Triangular Composition

**Also called:** triangle staging, pyramid composition  
**What it is:** Elements or faces arrange into a triangle inside the frame: two low and one high, or the reverse.  
**Effect on the audience:** Stability and conflict at once. The base steadies the picture while the apex pulls the eye, so tension lives inside a stable shape.

### Diagonals

**Also called:** diagonal lines, oblique composition

**Used for and where it works best:** Action, chases, climbs and descents, and the scene built on a fast decision. Very useful in reels, because a diagonal fills a vertical frame and breaks the feeling of a narrow column.

**Yields to:** `comp.balance` — Calm or formal scene, or interview background.  
`comp.diagonals`

### The S-Curve

**What it is:** A curving line that snakes into the picture in an S shape: a river, a road, a line of people, a caravan, a fabric, a reclining body.  
**Effect on the audience:** It draws the eye slowly into the depth of the image and creates a sense of journey, distance and elapsed time.

### Leading Lines

**Also called:** guide lines, converging lines  
**What it is:** Lines inside the scene (rails, a street, a fence, a row of columns, a roof edge) that run from the frame edge toward the subject.  
**Effect on the audience:** They pull the viewer's eye to the chosen point before they think. The strongest attention tool you have without movement or a cut.  
**Used for and where it works best:** When the subject is small inside a large space: a person at the end of a corridor, a door at the end of a hall, a statue at the end of a temple colonnade. Vital in heritage documentary and architecture, and in advertising to steer the eye to the product.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Trailer / Teaser, 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mystery / Whodunit, Thriller / Suspense, Sci-Fi, Drama, Noir / Neo-Noir, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Science / Educational  
**Avoid when:** If the lines lead to an empty spot with no subject you have cheated the eye. Strong lines in a dialogue scene steal attention from the faces.  
**Prompt:** `strong leading lines, the colonnade running from both frame edges toward the small figure standing at the far end`  
**Yields to:** `comp.visual_weight` — Dialogue scene where lines would steal the faces.  
`comp.leading_lines`

### Vanishing Point

**Also called:** convergence point  
**What it is:** The point where all the depth lines of the image converge. Where you put it inside the frame decides the feel of the entire shot.  
**Effect on the audience:** A central vanishing point reads as order and inevitability, one pushed to the side reads as movement and escape, and one outside the frame reads as openness with no end.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Roads, corridors, halls, tunnels and platforms. Put it dead centre for fate and confrontation, off to one side for escape, and in documentary put it on the artefact that matters so the eye finds it on its own.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Trailer / Teaser, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Sci-Fi, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mystery / Whodunit, Western, Noir / Neo-Noir  
**Avoid when:** Two competing vanishing points in one frame confuse the eye. And on a long lens the depth compresses and the idea disappears anyway.  
**Prompt:** `deep perspective, all lines converging on a single vanishing point placed dead centre at the end of the tunnel`  
**Yields to:** `comp.two_point_perspective` — Two competing sets of receding lines in frame.  
`comp.vanishing_point`

### One-Point Perspective

**Also called:** single point perspective, frontal perspective  
**What it is:** The camera faces the space square on, so every line converges to a single point while the wall faces stay parallel to the frame edges.  
**Effect on the audience:** Powerful depth plus order. The space feels logical and manufactured, and the viewer is pulled into the picture.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Corridors, halls, temples, tunnels, straight streets and server rooms. A staple for the shot that enters a monumental space in heritage documentary, in sci-fi and in title sequences.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Trailer / Teaser, 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, 360 / VR, AI-Generated Clip, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Sci-Fi, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Horror, Thriller / Suspense, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Psychological / Arthouse, Faith / Biblical Epic  
**Avoid when:** It demands a camera position accurate to the millimetre. Two degrees off and the shot looks crooked and the error is obvious.  
**Prompt:** `one-point perspective, camera square to the corridor, all lines converging to a single central point, wide lens, locked off`  
**Example:** 2001: A Space Odyssey, the centrifuge corridor.  
**Yields to:** `comp.two_point_perspective` — The camera cannot be squared to the millimetre.  
`comp.one_point_perspective`

### Two-Point Perspective

**Also called:** corner perspective, oblique perspective  
**What it is:** The camera stands at the corner of the object rather than facing it, so two sets of lines run off to two vanishing points near the frame edges.  
**Effect on the audience:** Realism and mass. The space feels three dimensional and natural rather than designed, which is what one-point perspective always feels like.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Presenting a building, a temple or a city from the corner so mass and depth read together. The default for architectural documentary, city shots and real estate video, and for any frame where the place must feel real rather than staged.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Product Video / Motion Design, AI-Generated Clip, News Report / Reportage, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Drama, Crime / Procedural, Noir / Neo-Noir, Sci-Fi, Western, Science / Educational, Nature / Wildlife  
**Avoid when:** When you want imposed order or formal awe, two-point perspective softens the formality and makes the place ordinary.  
**Prompt:** `two-point perspective on the temple corner, both wall faces receding to opposite vanishing points, wide lens, low camera`  
**Yields to:** `comp.one_point_perspective` — You want imposed order and formal awe.  
`comp.two_point_perspective`

### Aerial Perspective / Atmospheric Depth

**Also called:** atmospheric perspective, haze depth  
**What it is:** Distant planes come out lighter, cooler and lower in contrast because of dust, mist or heat, so depth reads through tonal layers instead of lines.  
**Effect on the audience:** Genuine distance and awe. The viewer feels the place extends past the frame, and the shot gains geographic weight.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Desert, mountain, river and big city wides, and the shot of an army approaching from far off. Essential in heritage documentary and epics, and in a prompt you add light dust or mist to force the planes apart.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Feature Film, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Trailer / Teaser, 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Music Video, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mythology / Ancient Epic, War, Fantasy / Epic, Western, Nature / Wildlife, Adventure, Survival  
**Avoid when:** It means nothing indoors unless there is smoke. Push the haze too far and you lose the very detail the shot exists to show.  
**Prompt:** `atmospheric depth, three receding ridges each lighter and cooler than the one in front, fine dust haze between the planes`  
**Yields to:** `comp.depth_layers` — Indoors, with no haze to separate planes.  
`comp.aerial_perspective`

### Depth Layers: Foreground, Midground, Background

**Also called:** layering, three planes  
**What it is:** You build the shot on three planes: something close to the lens, the subject in the middle, and a world behind. Each plane carries information.  
**Effect on the audience:** The image feels three dimensional and rich, and the viewer feels inside the space rather than looking at a wall.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The fastest way to lift any shot: put a plant, a shoulder, a column or smoke in the foreground. Fundamental in drama and documentary, and in every AI generated shot, because naming the planes in the prompt is what stops the image from coming out flat.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** A meaningless foreground plane steals attention and covers the performance. In a formal interview keep the foreground light so it does not distract.  
**Prompt:** `strong three layer depth, out of focus branches in the foreground, the man in the midground, the lit city behind him`  
**Yields to:** `comp.overlap_depth` — Formal interview where a foreground plane would distract.  
`comp.depth_layers`

### Overlap as a Depth Cue

**Also called:** occlusion cue, overlapping planes  
**What it is:** You let one element cover part of another, so the eye instantly knows which is in front and which is behind, even when light and colour are flat.  
**Effect on the audience:** A clear spatial order with no need for shallow depth of field or camera movement.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Crowded scenes: the market, the battle, the crowd, the dinner table. Critical in 2D animation and anime, where all depth is built on overlap, and critical in generated shots because it stops shapes from fusing into each other.  
**Best in:** formats: 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Kids Content, Feature Film, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Stop Motion, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Adventure, Shonen Battle (anime), Fantasy / Epic, Action, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Family, Slice of Life, Comedy  
**Avoid when:** An overlap that lands exactly edge to edge creates an ugly visual tangent. Make the occlusion decisive instead of letting one edge kiss another.  
**Prompt:** `clear overlapping planes, the near soldier partly covering the one behind him, three ranks reading as three depths`  
`comp.overlap_depth`

### Dirty Frame / Foreground Occlusion

**Also called:** foreground obstruction, dirty foreground  
**What it is:** Something close to the lens covers part of the frame: a shoulder, a chair back, a branch, a bottle, a door edge, a passing back. Usually out of focus and sometimes fully dark.  
**Effect on the audience:** A sense of surveillance and secrecy. The viewer feels hidden, watching something they were not meant to see.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Surveillance, stakeouts and tailing, the character being watched, and the conversation someone overhears. Strong in crime and horror, and in documentary it sells the feeling that you walked into the place rather than had it arranged for you.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Feature, News Report / Reportage, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Trailer / Teaser, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Vertical Micro-Drama, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Noir / Neo-Noir, Spy / Espionage, Horror, Mystery / Whodunit, True Crime, Drama, Political Thriller  
**Avoid when:** If it covers the face on the performance beat you have lost the scene. Do not confuse it with the dirty single, which is a relational framing choice, not a foreground obstruction.  
**Prompt:** `dirty frame, a dark out of focus shoulder filling the left third, the couple visible in the clear gap beyond it`  
`comp.dirty_frame`

### Shooting Through

**What it is:** You shoot from behind something with openings or transparency: a fence, a window, a curtain, dusty glass, foliage, a row of chairs, the balusters of a staircase.  
**Effect on the audience:** A psychological barrier. The character is caged or out of the viewer's reach, and intimacy is broken on purpose.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Prison, hospital, quarantine and surveillance, and the beat where a character is beyond reach. Strong in heavy drama and crime, and in heritage documentary shooting through a lattice window or a crack in a mud wall so the place itself becomes the lens.

### Frame Within a Frame

**Also called:** nested frame, inner frame, internal framing  
**What it is:** You use something inside the scene to build a second frame around the subject: a door, a window, a mirror, an arch, a screen, a gap in a wall.  
**Effect on the audience:** It concentrates the eye hard on whoever is enclosed, and at the same time says this person is trapped, watched, or in another world.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat of isolation inside a full house, the character observed from another room, and the end of a scene where one person stays inside while the rest leave. A staple in drama and heritage work, because old architecture is already made of arches and doorways that buy you depth and period in the same shot. In psychological drama tighten the inner frame as the film advances so the viewer feels the walls closing.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Limited Series, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Music Video, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Thriller / Suspense, Romance, Mystery / Whodunit, Psychological / Arthouse, Western, Gothic / Vampire, Noir / Neo-Noir  
**Avoid when:** A frame inside a frame inside a frame in the same shot becomes decoration. And when the inner frame clips the face or kills the eye light, style has beaten meaning.  
**Prompt:** `frame within a frame, the woman framed by a lit doorway seen from a dark hallway, the door edges boxing her in`  
**Example:** The Searchers, the closing shot framed in the cabin doorway.  
**Yields to:** `comp.natural_frame` — The enclosure is landscape, not architecture.  
`comp.frame_within_frame`

### Natural Framing

**Also called:** organic framing, environmental frame  
**What it is:** The same idea as a frame within a frame but built from natural elements rather than architecture: branches, rocks, a cave mouth, an arch of trees, a gap between two tents.  
**Effect on the audience:** It softens the edge and makes the camera feel inside the world rather than watching it from outside, much warmer than an architectural frame.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Nature, the journey, the camp, the village, and the shot of a hero arriving somewhere new. Essential in wildlife documentary, travel and fantasy, and in kids content it gives the frame a storybook feel.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Feature Film, Kids Content, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), 3D CG Animation, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Music Video, AI-Generated Clip, Wedding / Event Film, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Nature / Wildlife, Adventure, Fantasy / Epic, Fairy Tale, Family, Romance, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Survival, Road Movie, Slice of Life  
**Avoid when:** If the branches block the action or dance across the face in the wind, the frame turns into noise.  
**Prompt:** `framed naturally by overhanging branches on both sides, the rider passing through the gap in the centre`  
**Yields to:** `comp.frame_within_frame` — Branches would block the action or move.  
`comp.natural_frame`

### The Layered Doorway / Enfilade

**Also called:** nested doorways, room beyond room  
**What it is:** You shoot through a line of doorways or arches set on the same axis, so you see room beyond room beyond room.  
**Effect on the audience:** Depth and secrets. Every doorway says there is more beyond, and the viewer feels the house or palace is hiding something at the end.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The palace, the old house, the temple, the hotel, and the shot of a small figure at the far end of the chain. Very strong in horror, in large family drama, and in heritage documentary when one frame must convey the length of a building.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Trailer / Teaser, 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Horror, Gothic / Vampire, Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Mystery / Whodunit, Thriller / Suspense, Melodrama, Fantasy / Epic  
**Avoid when:** It needs exact alignment; doorways that are not on one axis give you a messy frame. And do not stage it in a dim space or the far rooms vanish.  
**Prompt:** `shot through three aligned doorways, room beyond room, a small figure standing in the farthest lit room`  
**Yields to:** `comp.frame_within_frame` — Doorways not on one axis, or dim.  
`comp.layered_doorway`

### Window Light Composition

**Also called:** window frame composition, light from the window  
**What it is:** The window works as light source, frame and graphic shape at once: the light enters from one side, the figure stands at its edge, and the lit rectangle prints itself onto the wall or floor.  
**Effect on the audience:** Stillness, reflection and waiting. The figure feels held inside looking out, and the light itself becomes a promise or a memory.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Waiting, remembering, mourning and prayer, and the classic shot of the mother, the soldier or the prisoner. Strong in heritage and period drama, because a mud brick window or a wooden lattice throws a light shape you cannot fake.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Docudrama / Reenactment, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, AI-Generated Clip, Anime (Japanese 2D), Fashion Film, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Tragedy, Romance, Faith / Biblical Epic, War, Melodrama, Gothic / Vampire, Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** Do not let it become the default for every interior; it turns into a visual cliche. And watch the window blowing out and taking every exterior detail with it.  
**Prompt:** `standing at the window, soft directional daylight from frame left, the bright window shape printed on the floor behind her`  
`comp.window_light`

### Silhouette as a Graphic Shape

**Also called:** silhouette composition, black shape against light  
**What it is:** The figure reads as a solid black shape against a bright background, so the composition becomes a play of shapes rather than detail. This record is about how the shape reads; the lighting setup itself belongs to its own category.  
**Effect on the audience:** Hidden identity and symbol. The viewer reads posture and movement instead of a face, the person becomes anyone, and the symbolism strengthens.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Essential in heritage work when we must show ancient people without committing to a face, and for endings, title sequences, and the villain before the reveal. Strong in music video and trailers because the shape reads in an instant.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Music Video, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mythology / Ancient Epic, War, Thriller / Suspense, Horror, Noir / Neo-Noir, Fantasy / Epic, Faith / Biblical Epic, Western  
**Avoid when:** If the scene needs a facial performance the silhouette kills it. And watch for a shape that no longer reads: the posture must be legible from the outline alone.  
**Prompt:** `full silhouette, the figure completely black against a bright dusty sky, clean readable outline, no fill light`  
**Example:** Skyfall, the fight staged in silhouette against the neon glass in Shanghai.  
**Yields to:** `comp.figure_ground` — The scene needs a facial performance.  
`comp.silhouette_shape`

### Figure to Ground Separation

**Also called:** subject separation, figure ground contrast  
**What it is:** You separate the subject from the background with a difference in colour, brightness, sharpness or motion, so the eye grabs it at once.  
**Effect on the audience:** Instant clarity. Without it the viewer spends a second or two hunting for the subject, and in short form that second is the whole video.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every shot with people against a busy background: the market, the party, the stadium, the battle. Decisive in reels, advertising and sports, and in a prompt you ask for it explicitly so the model does not fuse the subject into the background.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Commercial / TVC, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Product Video / Motion Design, Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, News Report / Reportage, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Action, Drama, Comedy, Sports Drama, Crime / Procedural, War, Adventure, Shonen Battle (anime), Family  
**Avoid when:** Over separation, a glowing figure against pure black, makes the shot look composited if the scene is meant to be real.  
**Prompt:** `strong figure to ground separation, a bright rim of light along her shoulders, the crowd behind her dark and soft`  
`comp.figure_ground`

### Scale Contrast

**Also called:** scale juxtaposition, size contrast  
**What it is:** You place a small element of known size (a person, a door, a car) beside something massive, so the scale becomes readable instead of merely claimed.  
**Effect on the audience:** Awe. The viewer measures the huge thing against the human and feels the difference in their body rather than their head.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The ziggurat, the wall, the colossus, the mountain, the ship, the monster, the tower. Indispensable in heritage documentary, because without a human in frame the scale is only a claim. The same applies in fantasy, kaiju and sci-fi.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, AI-Generated Clip, Anime (Japanese 2D), Music Video, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Mythology / Ancient Epic, Fantasy / Epic, Sci-Fi, Monster / Kaiju, Historical / Biopic, Adventure, Disaster, War, Nature / Wildlife  
**Avoid when:** If the small element does not read, too far away or lost in shadow, you lose the measure entirely. And a long lens compresses the distance into a lie.  
**Prompt:** `enormous ziggurat filling the frame with two tiny human figures at its base for scale, wide lens, clear separation`  
`comp.scale_contrast`

### Negative Space

**Also called:** empty space, breathing space  
**What it is:** A large empty area inside the frame: sky, wall, water, desert, fog, with the subject small in a corner or near an edge.  
**Effect on the audience:** It cuts two ways: loneliness, loss and contemplation, or dread and the expectation that something will appear in the emptiness.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat of solitude after a loss, the character standing before a large decision, and the opening that defines a harsh world. Essential in arthouse drama and sci-fi, and in horror the same emptiness makes the viewer wait for something to step into it.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Trailer / Teaser, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Music Video, Experimental / Art Film, AI-Generated Clip, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Sci-Fi, Horror, Tragedy, Survival, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Noir / Neo-Noir, Western  
**Avoid when:** In reels a large void loses the face on a phone screen. And when the emptiness carries no meaning the shot just looks unfinished.  
**Prompt:** `tiny figure in the lower right corner, the rest of the frame empty grey sky and fog, vast negative space`  
**Example:** Blade Runner 2049, the small figure against enormous empty environments.  
**Yields to:** `comp.fill_frame` — A vertical reel, where a void loses the face.  
`comp.negative_space`

### The Isolated Figure

**Also called:** lone subject, single figure in space  
**What it is:** One person inside a large empty space, or inside a crowd they do not belong to, with everything around them saying they are alone.  
**Effect on the audience:** Empathy and pity. The viewer reads loneliness with no dialogue, and any small move the person makes becomes an event.  
**Used for and where it works best:** After the funeral, after being fired, the first day somewhere foreign, the migrant at the station. Strong in drama, social documentary and a sad music video, and in heritage work the survivor after a city falls.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Documentary Series, Music Video, Drama Series Episode, Heritage / Historical Documentary, AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Melodrama, Psychological / Arthouse, War, Survival, Coming of Age, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Romance, Slice of Life  
**Avoid when:** It needs space and time. A short shot in a tight frame builds no isolation and reads as a throwaway.  
**Prompt:** `one man standing alone in the centre of a huge empty hall, wide shot, cold light, nobody else in frame`  
`comp.isolated_figure`

### Minimalist Composition

**Also called:** minimalism, reduction  
**What it is:** You strip out everything with no function: one or two elements only, a limited palette, a clean background.  
**Effect on the audience:** Calm, focus and a premium feel. Everything left in frame becomes important precisely because it survived the cut.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Luxury advertising, product video and fashion, and the single artefact on black in a heritage documentary. It also suits cold drama and the mourning scene.  
**Best in:** formats: Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Fashion Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Experimental / Art Film, Feature Film, AI-Generated Clip, Explainer, Corporate / Training, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Sci-Fi, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Surreal / Absurdist, Science / Educational, Noir / Neo-Noir, Tragedy  
**Avoid when:** In scenes that need a world and context, the stripping leaves the place feeling empty and history free.  
**Prompt:** `minimalist composition, one clay tablet on a clean dark surface, single soft light, nothing else in frame`  
**Yields to:** `comp.maximalism` — The scene needs world and context.  
`comp.minimalism`

### Maximalism / Controlled Chaos

**Also called:** dense frame, controlled clutter  
**What it is:** You pack the frame with deliberate detail: people, objects, signs, colour, but with a clear path for the eye and calculated visual weight.  
**Effect on the audience:** Life, noise and richness. The viewer feels the world is bigger than the story and wants to watch the shot again.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The market, the festival, the bazaar, a crowded kitchen, a cluttered room that describes its owner. Essential in social documentary, fantasy and comedy, and useful in the first second of a reel because density stops the thumb.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Feature Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Commercial / TVC, Music Video, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Comedy, Fantasy / Epic, Adventure, Slice of Life, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Family, Surreal / Absurdist, Crime / Procedural, Isekai (anime)  
**Avoid when:** With no clear eye path it collapses into clutter and the subject is lost. Do not use it on a decisive dialogue beat, because the detail steals the line.  
**Prompt:** `densely packed market frame, hanging fabrics, stacked baskets and passing bodies, a clear bright path leading to the boy in the middle`  
**Yields to:** `comp.minimalism` — Decisive dialogue beat where detail steals the line.  
`comp.maximalism`

### Fill the Frame

**Also called:** frame filling, no air  
**What it is:** The subject fills the whole frame and almost no surroundings remain: a face, a hand, fabric, a wall surface, carved stone. This is a composition rule and the fill is total, to the point where the texture itself becomes the subject, unlike the vertical Fill the Frame, which is a discipline about shot sizes on a phone screen.  
**Effect on the audience:** Intimacy and pressure. The viewer has nowhere to escape to, and the texture itself becomes the subject.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Texture shots in heritage documentary (cuneiform, clay, wool, stone), the peak emotional beat in drama, and the product shot in advertising. Essential in reels because the vertical frame is small and filling it guarantees legibility.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Feature Film, Fashion Film, Music Video, AI-Generated Clip, Kids Content, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Drama, Romance, Horror, Thriller / Suspense, Science / Educational, Nature / Wildlife, Melodrama, Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** Used before the space has been established, the viewer gets lost and does not know where we are. Establish first, then fill.  
**Prompt:** `extreme close framing, the carved cuneiform tablet filling the entire frame edge to edge, raking light across the texture`  
`comp.fill_frame`

### The Crowd as Texture

**Also called:** crowd as pattern, human texture  
**What it is:** You shoot the crowd as a surface rather than as individuals: repeating heads, shields, umbrellas, candles, so that no single face reads.  
**Effect on the audience:** Power and number. The viewer feels the quantity rather than the people, which makes any individual who later emerges from it matter enormously.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The army, the demonstration, the pilgrimage, the stadium, the market from above. Essential in heritage, war and sports, and it works as the establishing frame just before you drop down onto one face.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Feature Film, News Report / Reportage, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Trailer / Teaser, 3D CG Animation, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Concert / Live Event, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: War, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Political Thriller, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Sports Drama, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Disaster, Fantasy / Epic, Action  
**Avoid when:** If you stay on the crowd and never come down to a face, nobody empathises with a number. The texture is a beginning, not an ending.  
**Prompt:** `high angle on a packed crowd reading as pure texture, repeating heads and raised shields, no single face legible`  
`comp.crowd_texture`

### Pattern and Repetition

**Also called:** repetition, rhythm in frame  
**What it is:** One shape repeated across the frame: columns, windows, queues, tiles, arches, stairs, rows of seats.  
**Effect on the audience:** Order and rhythm. The image feels designed, and the repetition reads either as discipline or as monotony depending on the content.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Architecture, institutions, factories, schools, armies, and the temple shot in heritage work. Very useful in title sequences and motion design, because a visual rhythm locks onto a musical one.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Feature Film, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, 3D CG Animation, Music Video, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Experimental / Art Film, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Sci-Fi, Drama, Science / Educational, War, Psychological / Arthouse, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** Repetition with no break turns into tedium and looks like an architectural catalogue plate. Make it a setup for something.  
**Prompt:** `rows of identical columns repeating across the frame, even spacing, strong graphic rhythm, static wide shot`  
**Yields to:** `comp.pattern_break` — The repetition has no break and tires.  
`comp.pattern`

### The Break in the Pattern

**Also called:** odd one out, pattern interrupt  
**What it is:** You build a repeating pattern and then let one element break it: one person turning, an empty chair, a different colour, one open door in a row of closed ones. It differs from the vertical Pattern Interrupt, which breaks the monotony of the feed to stop the scroll: here both the pattern and its break live inside one frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** The eye goes to the exception immediately and involuntarily. The fastest and strongest way to direct attention with composition alone, and it creates meaning with no dialogue.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat of the one who does not belong: the soldier who will not clap, the student who turns, the one lit room in a dark tower. Powerful in dystopia, in suspense, and in advertising to single out the product.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, Drama Series Episode, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), AI-Generated Clip, Music Video, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Sci-Fi, War, Political Thriller, Dark Comedy / Satire, Mystery / Whodunit, Coming of Age  
**Avoid when:** The pattern must be established first for the break to work. Reveal the exception in the first frame and there is nothing left to discover.  
**Prompt:** `rows of identical seated figures facing forward, one single figure turned the other way, the eye pulled straight to the break`  
`comp.pattern_break`

### Correct Headroom

**Also called:** head room, top spacing  
**What it is:** The distance between the top of the head and the top frame line. Set correctly, the eyes sit near the upper third line and the space above the head is small and proportional to the shot size.  
**Effect on the audience:** Invisibility. The viewer never notices the framing and attends to the face and the words, which is exactly what an ordinary scene needs.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The default for every dialogue, interview, news piece and explainer. The first thing you set before you play with anything, because any deviation from it later carries meaning.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** When you want to express weakness, pressure or imbalance, correct headroom says nothing. And remember the tighter the shot, the less headroom it should carry.  
**Prompt:** `correct headroom, eyes on the upper third line, a small even gap above the head, medium close-up`  
**Yields to:** `comp.excess_headroom` — Weakness or loss must be stated.  
`comp.headroom`

### Excess Headroom

**Also called:** too much headroom, sunken subject  
**What it is:** The subject drops down in the frame and a large empty area sits above them: ceiling, sky, bare wall.  
**Effect on the audience:** Smallness, weakness and loss. The subject feels weighed down from above, as though the place is bigger than them and time heavier.  
**Used for and where it works best:** After the bad news, after the defeat, a person inside an institutional space (courtroom, hospital, interview room), a child inside an adult world. Strong in drama, tragedy and psychological thriller.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Limited Series, Documentary Feature, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Micro-Drama, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Melodrama, Psychological / Arthouse, Legal Drama, Medical Drama, Coming of Age, War, Psychological Horror  
**Avoid when:** If it is not deliberate it reads as a framing mistake, and in television and interviews it is treated as a plain technical error.  
**Prompt:** `deliberately excessive headroom, the man small in the lower half of the frame with an empty ceiling above him`  
**Yields to:** `comp.headroom` — Broadcast and interview treat it as technical error.  
`comp.excess_headroom`

### No Headroom / Cropped Crown

**Also called:** cropped forehead, tight top frame  
**What it is:** The top frame line cuts across the crown or forehead, and the eyes sit above the upper third line.  
**Effect on the audience:** Pressure, proximity and force. The face spreads across the whole frame, the viewer feels very close, and tension arrives because the person is bigger than the frame can hold.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The tight close-up on the beat of rage, confession or tears, and the interrogation face off. Essential in reels, where the vertical frame wants the face high and full, and common in modern advertising and trailers.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Vertical Micro-Drama, Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Meme / Comedy Edit, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Crime / Procedural, Horror, Action, Psychological Horror, Romance, Shonen Battle (anime), Sports Drama  
**Avoid when:** On a medium or wide it reads as an error; this belongs to close framing. And do not crop the chin and crown at once or the face falls apart. Never apply it in kids content at all: a crop that cuts the crown frightens the youngest viewers, so hold the generous headroom of `kids.safe_framing` and frame the joy close-up (`kids.big_smile_cu`) with the whole head in frame.  
**Prompt:** `tight close-up with no headroom, the top of the frame cutting across his forehead, eyes high in the frame`  
**Yields to:** `kids.safe_framing`, `kids.big_smile_cu` — in kids content, where a crop that cuts the crown frightens the youngest viewers.  
`comp.no_headroom`

### Look Room / Lead Room

**Also called:** nose room, looking room  
**What it is:** The space you leave on the side the subject looks toward or walks toward. Looking right means space on the right.  
**Effect on the audience:** Comfort and logic. The eye completes the line of sight in the empty space, so the subject feels like they are seeing something and the world feels continuous beyond the frame.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The default for every profile shot and every walking, running or driving shot. In a documentary interview it is what makes the subject feel like they are talking to someone rather than to a wall.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** When you want constriction, leaving it kills the tension. And if there is too much of it the subject looks like they are about to slide out of frame.  
**Prompt:** `profile shot with generous look room, she sits on the left third looking right into open space`  
**Yields to:** `comp.short_siding` — You want constriction rather than comfort.  
`comp.lead_room`

### Short-Siding

**Also called:** short-sided framing, no look room  
**What it is:** The exact reverse of look room: the subject faces the near frame edge while all the empty space sits behind their back.  
**Effect on the audience:** Instant constriction and a sense of a wall. The viewer feels boxed in without knowing why, and a suspicion forms that something is behind the character.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat of being trapped, of lying, of quietly breaking, and the character with no way out. The single strongest compositional secret in psychological drama and suspense, and a good fit for vertical micro drama because it builds tension in one shot.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Feature Film, Vertical Micro-Drama, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Short Film, Trailer / Teaser, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Psychological / Arthouse, Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Psychological Horror, Crime / Procedural, Noir / Neo-Noir, Mystery / Whodunit, Tragedy, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic  
**Avoid when:** In comfortable or comic scenes it reads as a framing error. Used on every shot it becomes a style that tires the eye instead of creating tension.  
**Prompt:** `short-sided framing, she is pushed to the right edge looking off right with no room, all the empty space behind her`  
**Example:** Mr. Robot, characters pressed into the corners with the space left behind them.  
**Yields to:** `comp.lead_room` — A comfortable or comic scene.  
`comp.short_siding`

### Breaking the Frame Edge

**Also called:** deliberate cropping, cutting on the edge  
**What it is:** You crop the subject at the edge in an unusual place: half a face outside, a body cut through the middle, a hand or back entering the foreground and sliced off.  
**Effect on the audience:** Anxiety and incompleteness. The viewer senses the frame failed to contain everything, that part of the truth is outside it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Scenes of breakdown, separation and incomplete memory, and the fractured flashback. Strong in arthouse and experimental work and in music video, and in documentary it sells the sense that the camera caught the event rather than arranged it.  
**Best in:** formats: Experimental / Art Film, Music Video, Short Film, Feature Film, Documentary Feature, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Title Sequence / Opening Credits, AI-Generated Clip, Fashion Film, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Psychological / Arthouse, Surreal / Absurdist, Drama, Tragedy, Psychological Horror, Romance, Coming of Age, Noir / Neo-Noir  
**Avoid when:** In commercial and instructional work it reads as carelessness. And if you crop the eye or the mouth on the key line, you have lost the information.  
**Prompt:** `deliberately cropped framing, half her face cut off by the right frame edge, the rest of the room empty`  
**Yields to:** `comp.frame_edge_tension` — Commercial or instructional work, where a crop reads careless.  
`comp.breaking_frame_edge`

### The Frame Edge as Tension

**Also called:** edge pressure, crowding the edge  
**What it is:** You place the subject right against the frame edge without cropping them, so the edge works as a wall pressing on them.  
**Effect on the audience:** Silent pressure. The viewer feels the subject is cornered or about to leave, and any small movement becomes threatening.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Before the escape, before being thrown out, and when a character decides to withdraw. Useful in suspense and family drama, and in reels because the vertical edge is already close so the pressure arrives fast.

### Open Frame and Closed Frame

**Also called:** open form, closed form  
**What it is:** An open frame points at a world that continues beyond its borders (glances out, sounds, people entering and leaving), while a closed frame keeps everything inside and refuses to admit anything exists outside.  
**Effect on the audience:** Open reads as freedom and realism; closed reads as entrapment and fate, because there is no escape when there is no outside.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A stylistic decision for a whole act rather than one shot: documentary and naturalistic drama run open, while horror, prison and tragedy run closed. Shift from open to closed as the trap tightens in the third act.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Experimental / Art Film, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Horror, Tragedy, Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Thriller / Suspense, Verite / Observational, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Gothic / Vampire, Slice of Life, Crime / Procedural

### Eyeline Height in the Frame

**Also called:** eye placement, eye line position  
**What it is:** How high the subject's eyes sit inside the frame. The default is near the upper third line, and moving it up or down changes the feel of the whole shot.  
**Effect on the audience:** High eyes read as closeness, strength and presence. Low eyes read as smallness, weight and isolation. Eyes exactly on the middle line read as cold formality.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The key decision on any face shot, and the critical one in reels: keep the eyes in the upper third so they do not fall below centre and disappear behind captions and interface. Use a low eyeline deliberately on the beat of collapse.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Vertical Micro-Drama, Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Interview / Video Podcast, Commercial / TVC, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: all

### Screen Direction

**Also called:** left to right, direction of travel  
**What it is:** The direction a subject moves or looks inside the frame, and whether it stays consistent from shot to shot.  
**Effect on the audience:** In left to right reading cultures, moving right reads as progress and future, while moving left reads as return, past and resistance. The viewer processes it unconsciously.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Plan the direction of travel across the whole film: out to the right, back to the left, and keep a chase running one way so the viewer never gets lost. Essential in chases, in journey documentaries, and in heritage work when you trace a migration or a campaign across a map.

### Tilted Horizon

### Vertical Stacking for 9:16

**Also called:** stacked vertical composition, vertical layering  
**What it is:** In a vertical frame you build the composition top to bottom in bands: sky or ceiling on top, the face in the upper third, hands or the key object in the middle, ground or a caption low in the frame, above the band the platform interface covers.  
**Effect on the audience:** The eye travels vertically instead of horizontally, and the information reads in the order you chose in under a second.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The first rule of reels, shorts, TikTok and vertical micro drama. ### Title-Safe and Action-Safe Areas

**Also called:** safe zones, safe margins  
**What it is:** Margins inside the frame: the action-safe area sits near the edge and the tighter title-safe area lives inside it, and nothing important goes outside them.  
**Effect on the audience:** It guarantees the face, the caption and the logo are not cropped or hidden behind platform interface or a screen bezel.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Essential in broadcast, advertising and social. It is also critical whenever you recut the same material for several ratios.  
**The broadcast and horizontal numbers, which this card owns.** Two agents in one round needed them and found only the vertical card, which does not apply to a 16:9 film. The broadcast convention is **action-safe at 93% of the frame and title-safe at 90%** — so on a 1920x1080 master, action-safe is about 67 px in from each edge and title-safe about 96 px, and nothing that must be read goes outside the inner box. **On a horizontal piece for the web there is no interface to avoid**, so the safe area is a typographic margin rather than a platform constraint: keep type off the outer 5% and off any band a player puts its controls in. **What the platforms themselves publish for 16:9, read 2026-09-24:** neither YouTube nor Meta publishes an overlay-free safe area for a 16:9 video. Google's safe-zone reference for YouTube is drawn for vertical ads only, and says overlays, calls to action and buttons "may appear in different places depending on the format, campaign type, and screen" (Google Ads Help, https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9128498). YouTube's end screens take up to four elements on a 16:9 video, in its last 5 to 20 seconds, on a video at least 25 seconds long, placed where the creator drags them, so leave room for them in the closing frames (YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789). Meta's safe-zone page covers 9:16 ads, and 1:1 or 4:5 ads in Instagram Feed (keep the bottom and side edges clear); it gives nothing for 16:9 (Meta Business Help Center, https://www.facebook.com/business/help/980593475366490). Facebook Feed accepts 16:9 but recommends 4:5, and on the computer version of Facebook Feed the video is shown at 1:1, which may put black bars on either side of it (https://www.facebook.com/business/help/682655495435254). A film delivered to both takes the tighter of the two, once, rather than two masters. **A horizontal feed video** — a 16:9 piece in a Facebook or YouTube feed, as common as a vertical one — keeps the web margin above and also keeps type out of the player's control band along the bottom. No platform publishes that band's height, so take a working band (about the bottom tenth of the frame), record it under `decisions`, and continue.
**Yields to:** `vertical.safe_zones` for every vertical figure. **No number is repeated here** — this line used to restate all three and carried a wrong one for as long as the card did, which is the argument for not restating it. One number, one place, so the storyboard and the keyframe are composed to the same frame.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Commercial / TVC, News Report / Reportage, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Product Video / Motion Design, Explainer, Corporate / Training, Lyric Video, Kids Content, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** Do not let safe areas dictate the whole composition or every shot will end up loose and distant. They are limits, not a design.  
**Shelf life:** dated — the YouTube and Meta pages as read on 2026-09-24. Re-check them before delivery and at least every 90 days.  
**Prompt:** `composed for social safe zones, key action kept inside the central safe area, with the top band, the bottom band and the right edge left clear`  
*(the figures for those bands come from `vertical.safe_zones`, which owns them; a prompt is not a place to keep a platform measurement)*  
`comp.safe_areas`

### Colour Contrast as Composition

**Also called:** colour separation, colour as subject  
**What it is:** You use colour itself to build the composition: one element in an opposing hue inside a unified palette, or one half of the frame in one temperature and the other half in another.  
**Effect on the audience:** The eye goes to the differing colour before it reads the shape, so the colour becomes the pointer and a colour split becomes meaning.  
**Used for and where it works best:** To pick a character out of a crowd (a red coat in a grey street), and to split two worlds inside one frame (warmth indoors, cold beyond the window). ### Light Contrast as Composition

### Forced Perspective

**Also called:** in camera scale trick, perspective illusion  
**What it is:** Placing one object near the lens and another far away so that they appear to sit on the same plane, which completely changes their relative size. It needs deep focus so both stay sharp, and there is only one angle from which it works.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer believes a scale that does not exist, because the eye trusts perspective more than it trusts logic. The trick keeps working even after it is understood, which is where its charm lives.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A visual joke in comedy and family content, showing a giant or a dwarf with no effects, and the tourist frame where someone pinches the top of a tower. In heritage work it serves the scale of a colossus or a gate by putting a person in the far ground.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Kids Content, Commercial / TVC, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Comics / Webtoon / Photo Essay, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Heritage / Historical Documentary, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Landscape Photography, Street Photography, Still Life Photography | genres: Comedy, Family, Fantasy / Epic, Physical Comedy / Slapstick, Fairy Tale, Adventure, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Preschool / Early Learning  
**Avoid when:** The illusion usually breaks with any camera move because only one angle sells it. The exception is when the move is designed to expose the trick on purpose, as a second joke after the first one.  
**Prompt:** `forced perspective shot, locked camera, a small figurine held close to the lens aligned so it appears to stand as tall as the distant tower, deep focus, both planes sharp`  
**Yields to:** `comp.scale_contrast` — The shot has to move and breaks the illusion.  
`comp.forced_perspective`
