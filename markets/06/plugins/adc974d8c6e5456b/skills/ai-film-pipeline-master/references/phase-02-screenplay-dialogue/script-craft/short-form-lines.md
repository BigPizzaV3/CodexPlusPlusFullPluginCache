# Short-Form Scripting Lines

4 cards. Reels, shorts and vertical video, where the first line decides whether the rest is read.

**The examples show the shape of a line, not facts.** A date, a figure or a historical claim inside an example is illustrative and unsourced; a piece that uses one takes it from the brief or, on a history piece the user wants realistic, searches for it and uses the closest figure found (root [`SKILL.md`](../../../SKILL.md) §0); nothing waits on it.

> **These four are the line-writing half of four rules whose story half lives in Phase 1.**
> `sf.first_line_both_ways`, `sf.front_load`, `sf.one_point` and `sf.every_word_earns` in
> [`../../phase-01-story-generation/short-form-craft.md`](../../phase-01-story-generation/short-form-craft.md)
> decide **whether the piece obeys the rule** — they are structural and they are the authority.
> The `sfl.` cards here decide **how the sentence is built** once that decision is made: the word
> order, where the verb lands, what a muted reader sees first.
>
> They are not two versions of one rule and they must never be treated as competing. If they ever
> appear to disagree, the `sf.` card wins — it is upstream, and this is the only place that says so.
> (`canon-rules.md` is right that two live copies of one rule is a defect; this is the split that
> stops these four from being one.)

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, and what to avoid.
Each carries `**Yields to:**` its Phase 1 twin.

---

### The First Line Works Twice

**Also called:** sound-off first line, the dual hook  
**What it is:** The opening line is built so its promise lands both as spoken audio and in the muted frame, because most of the audience meets it muted. The two channels carry the same promise, not the same words in the same seconds.  
**Effect on the audience:** A spoken hook with the sound off is no hook at all, and the viewer is gone before the sound question is ever settled.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every reel, short and vertical piece. Essential on a feed where autoplay is muted.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The piece is delivered where sound is on by default, such as a cinema, an exhibition or a subscribed podcast feed — which excludes the cinema and broadcast release of the feature, series and documentary formats in Best in; only their cut for a muted feed takes this card, the scope `sf.first_line_both_ways` sets.  
**Example:**
> On-screen hook, 0:00-0:01.5: *"Babylon, 539 BC."*  
> Voiceover from 0:01.5: "Babylon didn't fall to an army. It fell to a night nobody wrote down."  
> (The text reads instantly on mute and adds the place and date; the line lands when unmuted, in its own seconds.)

**Yields to:** `sf.first_line_both_ways` — on whether the piece needs a dual hook at all; this card only builds the sentence.  
`sfl.first_line_both_ways`

### Front-Load the Payoff

**Also called:** no saving the ending  
**What it is:** The thing the piece promises is delivered early rather than held for the end.  
**Effect on the audience:** On short form the end may never arrive. A payoff behind forty seconds of build is a payoff most of the audience never receives.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Reels, shorts and vertical video, particularly on a feed where the first seconds decide distribution.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The piece is built on a single withheld reveal and the withholding is the whole structure. Then the hook must promise the withholding itself.  
**Example:**
> ❌ "Deep in the sands of southern Iraq, archaeologists spent thirty years excavating before they discovered what destroyed the city..."  
> ✅ "This bronze helmet stopped an iron arrow in 612 BC. Here is why the soldier wearing it still lost."  
**Yields to:** `sf.front_load` — on whether the payoff moves; this card only builds the sentence.  
`sfl.front_load`

### One Point, Not Three

**Also called:** the single-idea rule  
**What it is:** A short piece carries one idea. Three ideas become three pieces with three hooks.  
**Effect on the audience:** Three ideas in forty-five seconds means none of them lands, and the viewer remembers the feeling of being talked at rather than any of the three.  
**Used for and where it works best:** All short form. The discipline also produces more pieces from the same research, which is the opposite of a loss.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The three points are one argument that genuinely does not divide, which is rarer than it feels while writing.  
**Example:**
> ❌ "Today we cover Assyrian iron metallurgy, palace relief carving, and their irrigation canals."  
> ✅ "One reason Assyrian iron won its wars: [the single reason, from the source]."  
**Yields to:** `sf.one_point` — on what the single point is; this card only builds the sentence.  
`sfl.one_point`

### Every Word Earns Its Place

**Also called:** the cut test  
**What it is:** If a word can be removed without losing meaning, it is removed. Applied to the whole script, line by line, after the draft.  
**Effect on the audience:** Short form has no room for a slow opener or an extended explanation, and the cost of a wasted word is paid in viewers rather than in seconds.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every short-form script, as a separate pass after writing rather than during.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The extra words are rhythm rather than information, and cutting them would flatten the delivery.  
**Example:**
> ❌ "In this video, I am actually going to basically show you the reason why the great city of Nineveh was abandoned."  
> ✅ "Nineveh burned. Within two generations, nobody remembered where it stood."  
**Yields to:** `sf.every_word_earns` — on what stays in the piece; this card only trims the line.  
`sfl.every_word_earns`
