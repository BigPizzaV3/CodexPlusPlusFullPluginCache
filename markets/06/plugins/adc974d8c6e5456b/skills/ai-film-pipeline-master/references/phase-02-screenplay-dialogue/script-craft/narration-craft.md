# Narration and Voiceover

9 cards. Writing a line that sits against a picture without competing with it. Documentary craft, and the trade with the most named failures, because every one of them has shipped.

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, and what to avoid.

---

---
### Say Cow, See Cow

**Also called:** the redundant line, narrating the picture  
**What it is:** Narration that states what the viewer is already looking at, so the same information arrives twice in the same second.  
**Effect on the audience:** The picture stops carrying meaning and becomes an illustration of the sentence. The viewer disengages from looking, because looking has been made unnecessary.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The repair is to write the line the picture cannot say: where this is, when it was, what it cost, what happened next — and where that line is a date, a quantity or a statistic, the number goes on screen and the voice keeps what it means (`nar.numbers_to_text`), unless the number is itself the emotional beat. Picture and narration each carry half.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** Never deliberately, except where the image is genuinely ambiguous and misreading it would break the sequence, and then name the thing once and move on.  
**Example:** > Picture: a ruined stone wall.
> ❌ "This is a ruined stone wall."
> ✅ "Students studied behind it." — the count, if the viewer needs it, on screen (`nar.numbers_to_text`).  
`nar.say_cow`

### Title Before Name

**Also called:** the descriptive lead-in  
**What it is:** A person is introduced by what they are before they are introduced by who they are: the Assyrian king Ashurbanipal, not Ashurbanipal.  
**Effect on the audience:** The listener has nowhere to put a bare name and it passes them. The title gives the name a shelf to sit on, and they keep it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every first mention of a person, and every re-mention after a long gap. Essential on a heritage subject whose audience does not already hold the names.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The name is more famous than any title you could give it, and the title would read as condescension.  
**Example:** > ❌ "Ashurbanipal built the library."
> ✅ "The Assyrian king Ashurbanipal built the library."  
`nar.title_first`

### Need to Know

**Also called:** NTK, the encyclopaedia test  
**What it is:** Every sentence is tested against one question: does the viewer need this to understand what follows? What fails is cut, however true and however hard it was to find.  
**Effect on the audience:** Attention is finite and spends down. A fact the viewer does not need is paid for with attention they would have spent on the fact they do.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Heritage and factual work, where research produces far more than the piece can hold and the temptation is to prove the research.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The piece is explicitly an archive or a record, where completeness is the point rather than comprehension.  
**Example:**
> ❌ "The mudbrick rampart was built with bitumen mined from Hit, forty miles north, during the reign of Ashurbanipal who had twelve siblings and collected clay tablets."
> ✅ "The wall was mudbrick and bitumen. In the third year of the siege, it held."  
**Yields to:** `inter.archive_way_in` — The piece is an archive or record, where completeness is the point.  
`nar.need_to_know`

### Numbers to the Screen

**Also called:** the text card handoff  
**What it is:** Dates, quantities and statistics are given to on-screen text, and narration keeps what only a voice can carry: perspective, connection, consequence.  
**Effect on the audience:** A spoken number passes without landing; a written one can be read twice and held. Splitting them lets the voice sound like a person rather than a report.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Any factual piece carrying dates. On a heritage documentary, where a century or a reign is a number the viewer needs to hold while the voice keeps moving.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The number is the emotional beat itself and the voice should land it, or the delivery is sound-off, where on-screen text is already carrying the whole load.  
**Example:**
> ❌ "In seven hundred and twenty-one BC, twenty-seven thousand two hundred and ninety people were deported to the mountains."
> ✅ [On Screen: 721 BC · 27,290 EXILED]  
> Narration: "They marched them into the mountains. None of them returned."  
**Yields to:** `nar.pause_written` — The number is the emotional beat and the voice must land it.  
`nar.numbers_to_text`

### The Bridge

**Also called:** the transition line  
**What it is:** Narration used to carry the viewer between two things that do not otherwise join: two topics, two interviews, two periods.  
**Effect on the audience:** Without it the cut reads as a mistake. With it the two halves become one argument.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Interview-led and archive-led work, where the material was not shot to run in sequence and the join is the writer's job.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The two things join on their own through image or sound. A bridge over an existing join is narration explaining an edit that already worked.  
**Example:**
> ❌ "Now let us turn our attention to what was happening in Babylon during this exact same period."
> ✅ "Three hundred miles south, Babylon was watching."  
**Yields to:** `cut.sound_match` — The two scenes already join through image or sound, with no narration.  
`nar.bridge`

### The Written Pause

**Also called:** PAUSE, the ellipsis mark  
**What it is:** Silence is written into the script as an instruction rather than left to the read. The script carries `PAUSE` or an ellipsis at the point the voice must stop.  
**Effect on the audience:** A pause before a hard fact makes the viewer lean in. A pause after one gives them room to feel it. An unmarked script gets read at an even pace and both are lost.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every script with a voice. Essential where the piece has a moment that should land: a death, a number, a reveal.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The pace is already carried by picture and music, and a written pause would stack silence on silence.  
**Example:**
> ❌ "The messengers reached Nineveh at dawn with news that the northern front had collapsed completely."
> ✅ "At dawn, the messenger reached the gate. [PAUSE: 2.0s] Nineveh was alone."  
**Yields to:** `sound_realize.event_silence_map` — Picture and music already carry the pace; the silence is placed in the audio map.  
`nar.pause_written`

### Active Voice

**Also called:** the acting subject  
**What it is:** The sentence names who did the thing. The Assyrians built the wall, not the wall was built.  
**Effect on the audience:** Passive construction removes the actor, and a documentary with no actors becomes a description of conditions. Active voice restores cause.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Nearly always. The exception is where the actor is genuinely unknown or deliberately withheld.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** Naming the actor would be a claim the research does not support. Then the passive is honest and the alternative is invention.  
**Example:**
> ❌ "A decision was made that the temple treasuries should be emptied and tributes sent to the Medes."
> ✅ "The king stripped the gold from the temple doors and sent it to the Medes."  
`nar.active_voice`

### Written for the Mouth

**Also called:** read it aloud, the contraction test  
**What it is:** The line is written the way a person speaks: contractions, short clauses, no participial phrase before the comma. It is read aloud before it is accepted.  
**Effect on the audience:** Written-not-spoken prose makes a narrator sound like they are reading, and the viewer hears the paper.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every narration line, in every register, including formal ones. Formal speech is still speech.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The register is deliberately archaic or ceremonial and the stiffness is the effect.  
**Example:** > ❌ "Having ruled for forty years, the king died in the winter."
> ✅ "The king ruled forty years. He died that winter."  
**Yields to:** `pronounce.archaic_diction_rhythm` — The register is deliberately archaic or ceremonial.  
`nar.conversational`

### No Undefined Term

**Also called:** the specialist word test  
**What it is:** A specialist term is defined in the line that uses it, or replaced. Nothing is left for the viewer to look up.  
**Effect on the audience:** An undefined term does not just lose that sentence; the viewer spends the next two sentences wondering, and loses those too.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Heritage, scientific and technical work, where the writer knows the field and has stopped hearing which words are specialist.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Action, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** The audience is the field itself, and defining the term would insult them.  
**Example:**
> ❌ "The king commissioned an orthostat programme depicting apotropaic figures along the dromos."
> ✅ "Carved stone slabs lined the hallway—each one showing winged bulls meant to turn back evil."  
`nar.no_jargon`
