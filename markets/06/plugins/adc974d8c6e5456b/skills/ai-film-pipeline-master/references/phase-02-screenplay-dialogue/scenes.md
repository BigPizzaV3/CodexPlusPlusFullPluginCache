# Scene types

A beat plan per dramatic scene type, with the card ids it calls.

This is the direct counterpart to `scenes.md` in the Visual Creativity Database. That file gives a **shot skeleton** — how to cover a scene. This one gives a **beat skeleton** — what must happen in it dramatically, and what it is actually about under the surface. Where a scene type appears in both sets, the two cards are designed to be read together: this one first, then that one.

Each entry names the genres it belongs to, what the scene is really about, **what must turn**, the beats in order, where to come in, where to leave, and the honest failure modes.

---

### The Confession

**genres:** Drama, Family Drama, Crime, Romance, Tragedy, Psychological
**The scene:** One character finally says the thing. The trap is that the confession itself is not the drama — it is the release of drama built elsewhere. The scene belongs to the **listener**, because the audience is watching a relationship be re-decided in real time on a face that is not speaking.
**What must turn:** Knowledge, from hidden to shared — and underneath it, the relationship, from one arrangement to another. If only the first turns, the confession is exposition with feeling attached.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The confessor circles, offering an unrelated topic `dial.strategic_withholding` · `sub.talk_to_avoid`
2. The listener gives an opening they did not intend to give `beat.beat_change`
3. The first partial admission — true but incomplete `dial.half_truth`
4. The listener's response makes full admission unavoidable `dial.question_control`
5. The confession, stated plainly and too fast `emo.understate_at_peak`
6. Silence. The listener does nothing, and the weight transfers `dial.silence_as_reply` · `beat.held_beat`
7. The listener's response, which is never the one the confessor rehearsed `scene.reversal_of_expectation`
8. The new arrangement tested by one small ordinary action `scene.value_turn`

**Enter:** After the decision to confess has been made. The audience should meet someone who has decided and is failing to begin.
**Exit:** On the ordinary action, not on the emotional peak. Someone picks up a cup, or does not. `end.quiet_ending`
**Avoid:** Writing the confession as a speech — the truth should arrive shorter than the evasion did. Cutting away from the listener at the moment of impact. Having the listener answer immediately; the pause is the scene. An absolving response, which costs the confession its price and retroactively removes the reason it was hidden.

### The Refused Request

**genres:** Social Drama, Family Drama, Political, Crime, Migration, Legal
**The scene:** Someone asks for something they need from someone who can give it and will not. The drama is almost never in the asking — it is in what the asker is prepared to spend, and what the refusal costs the refuser.
**What must turn:** Dignity, usually downward for the asker and sideways for the refuser, who does not escape it either.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The asker arrives having rehearsed, and does not use the rehearsal `char.stress_tell`
2. The request made too casually, to leave a retreat `dial.deflection`
3. The refusal, delivered politely and immovably `dial.polite_aggression` · `anta.institutional`
4. The asker names the cost of refusal `scene.tactic_change`
5. The refuser's one moment of genuine difficulty — which changes nothing `anta.right_about_something`
6. The asker drops the retreat and asks plainly `dial.enter_late_leave_early`
7. The second refusal, which is the one that lands `tens.shrinking_options`
8. The asker manages their exit `emo.emotion_through_task`

**Enter:** After the greeting, on the first line of business.
**Exit:** On the exit itself — how someone leaves a room they have lost something in is the whole characterisation. `end.held_final_beat`
**Avoid:** Making the refuser corrupt, which converts a structural scene into a moral one and lets the audience off. Giving the asker a speech. A refusal that is reversed later in the same story without a new cost.

### The Escalating Argument

**genres:** Drama, Family Drama, Social Drama, Political, Tragedy, Melodrama
**The scene:** A disagreement breaks into open conflict. The real escalation is not volume — it is the tactic ladder, and the moment one of them says the thing they had agreed never to say.
**What must turn:** The rules of the relationship, from intact to broken. After this scene they cannot speak the way they spoke before it.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The disagreement about the stated subject `scene.goal_conflict_disaster`
2. First tactic — reason — fails `scene.tactic_change`
3. Second tactic — appeal to history — fails `rel.the_debt` · `sub.shared_history`
4. Someone changes the subject to the real one `dial.topic_change`
5. Third tactic — the old grievance, produced as evidence `dial.exposition_accusation`
6. The unsaid thing is said `arc.moral_line` · `sub.circled_word`
7. Silence, or the wrong response `emo.emotion_against_type`
8. One of them leaves, or refuses to `scene.trap_scene`

**Enter:** Two lines into the argument, after the subject is clear and before anyone has raised their voice.
**Exit:** On the beat after the unsaid thing, not on the door. `beat.beat_after_line`
**Avoid:** Escalating by volume rather than by tactic, which produces two pages of the same beat. Letting both characters be equally articulate at the peak — under pressure one of them should become worse at speaking. Resolving it in the same scene.

### The Interrupted Ritual

**genres:** Family Drama, Heritage, Historical, Faith, Tragedy, Social Drama, War
**The scene:** A ritual with known steps — a meal, a prayer, a wedding, a funeral, a shift handover — broken partway through by something that does not belong.
**What must turn:** Belonging. Whoever breaks the form is outside it afterwards, and everyone in the room knows.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The ritual shown working correctly, for longer than feels necessary `time.iterative_habit`
2. The audience learns the form well enough to predict the next step `tens.rule_demonstration`
3. A small deviation nobody remarks on `tens.wrongness_detail`
4. The deviation is absorbed — the ritual is stronger than it `rel.group_hierarchy_display`
5. The break, from a participant rather than an outsider `scene.interrupted_ritual`
6. The room's response, which is procedural before it is emotional `rel.who_defends_whom`
7. Someone attempts to resume the form `emo.emotion_through_task`
8. Whether it resumes is the scene's answer `scene.value_turn`

**Enter:** At the ritual's start, not before — the audience needs the form, not the arrival.
**Exit:** On whoever is left performing the form alone.
**Avoid:** A break from an outsider, which is an intrusion rather than a rupture. A ritual the audience does not know — teach it earlier or choose one they hold. Explaining the significance of the form to the audience through a character.

### The Threshold Return

**genres:** Family Drama, Drama, Migration, Heritage, Crime, Social Drama
**The scene:** Someone comes back after a long absence and is met at the door. The physical arrangement — one inside, one outside, no invitation — states the question the dialogue can then avoid.
**What must turn:** Admission, from withheld to granted or refused. Whether the door opens wider is the entire scene.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The knock, and the delay before the door `tens.waiting_as_weapon`
2. Recognition, and the decision not to show it `sub.behaviour_contradicts`
3. Small talk conducted at the boundary `dial.trivial_surface`
4. The returner states a practical reason for coming `dial.deflection`
5. The one at the door asks the question that is not about the reason `dial.question_control`
6. The returner's answer costs them something `emo.understate_at_peak`
7. The door moves — wider, or closed one degree `scene.threshold_scene`
8. Neither of them crosses `beat.exit_beat`

**Enter:** On the door opening, never on the journey.
**Exit:** Before the outcome is settled, if possible — this scene is stronger unresolved. `scene.early_exit`
**Avoid:** Letting them come in, unless the coming in is the turn. A reunion embrace, which spends in one beat what the scene was built to withhold. Explaining the absence.

### The Diagnosis

**genres:** Drama, Medical, Family Drama, Tragedy, Social Drama
**The scene:** Information that changes a life is delivered by someone for whom it is routine. The gap between those two states is the scene.
**What must turn:** The future, from open to fixed. Everything after this scene happens in a different story from everything before it.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The professional's ordinary preliminaries — a form, a chair, a name confirmed `expo.procedural_exposition`
2. The recipient performing calm `sub.behaviour_contradicts`
3. The information, delivered in the professional's own vocabulary, too fast `dial.expert_explains_badly`
4. The recipient asks the wrong question — practical, small `emo.emotion_against_type`
5. The professional answers it seriously, which is the kindness `char.competence_cost`
6. The real question, asked or visibly not asked `sub.circled_word`
7. The answer, or the refusal of one `dial.silence_as_reply`
8. The recipient collects their belongings `emo.emotion_through_task`

**Enter:** Mid-sentence, on the professional's second explanation.
**Exit:** In the corridor or the car park, not the room. The reaction belongs elsewhere. `emo.delayed_reaction`
**Avoid:** A cruel or careless professional, which converts the scene into a complaint about a person rather than an encounter with a fact. Tears in the room. The patient repeating the diagnosis back for the audience's benefit.

### The Negotiation

**genres:** Crime, Legal, Political, Workplace, Gangster, Drama, Heist
**The scene:** Two parties want something the other holds. The pleasure is watching competence, and the drama is which one first reveals how much they need it.
**What must turn:** Power, measured by who is asking the questions at the end versus the beginning.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The pleasantries, which are the first move `dial.polite_aggression`
2. Opening positions, both false `dial.half_truth`
3. The first real concession, offered as a gift `dial.concession_that_wins`
4. A third element arrives — information, a person, a deadline `scene.barrier_mid_scene` · `anta.time`
5. One party's need becomes visible `char.stress_tell`
6. The other party does not take it, which is more frightening than taking it `tens.polite_threat`
7. The terms, which nobody states in full `sub.iceberg_omission`
8. Something changes hands `scene.transaction_scene`

**Enter:** After both parties have sat down.
**Exit:** On the object moving, or on the refusal to move it.
**Avoid:** Making one party obviously stronger — a negotiation with a foregone outcome is a scene about compliance. Stating the terms plainly, which removes the subtext the format runs on. A handshake.

### The Public Humiliation

**genres:** Social Drama, Melodrama, Comedy, Coming of Age, Family Drama, Vertical Micro-Drama
**The scene:** Someone is diminished in front of people whose opinion matters to them. This is the engine of an entire commercial format and it works on precise mechanics.
**What must turn:** Standing, publicly and irreversibly. A humiliation nobody witnessed is a private disappointment.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The claim — the character asserts a version of themselves `char.likeable_vs_interesting`
2. The witnesses established, by name or by look `scene.public_scene`
3. Something small does not match the claim `tens.wrongness_detail`
4. A first rescue attempt, which extends the exposure `scene.tactic_change`
5. The gap between claim and reality made undeniable `emo.shame`
6. The room's reaction, which is silence before it is laughter `tens.social_dread`
7. The character's exit, or their refusal to leave `emo.emotion_against_type`
8. One witness does something unexpected `rel.who_defends_whom`

**Enter:** On the claim, so the audience holds it before it breaks.
**Exit:** On the witness beat, not on the humiliation. That is what makes the scene worth returning to.
**Avoid:** Humiliation without witnesses. Extending it past the point of usefulness — sustained humiliation of a sympathetic character becomes unwatchable. A perpetrator who is simply cruel, which lets the audience off.

### The Reunion That Goes Wrong

**genres:** Drama, Family Drama, Romance, War, Migration, Heritage
**The scene:** Two people who have wanted to meet finally do, and the meeting fails — not through conflict, but because the version each carried has not survived.
**What must turn:** Hope, from held to released. This is usually the saddest scene in a work and it contains no argument.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The approach — one of them sees the other first `emo.physical_register`
2. The greeting, which both have rehearsed differently `dial.talking_past`
3. A stretch of successful ordinary conversation `dial.trivial_surface`
4. The first reference to the shared past, which does not land `sub.shared_history`
5. A correction — one of them remembers it differently `time.unreliable_memory`
6. The attempt to return to the ordinary conversation `scene.tactic_change`
7. Something practical intervenes and is welcomed by both `scene.interrupted_scene`
8. The parting, warmer than the meeting deserved `sub.behaviour_contradicts`

**Enter:** On the approach, from the viewpoint of whoever wanted it more.
**Exit:** On the walk away, and on which of them looks back. `end.final_image`
**Avoid:** An argument — this scene is ruined by conflict, which would give both parties something to do. A stated realisation. Making either of them at fault.

### The Decision Alone

**genres:** Drama, Crime, Tragedy, Thriller, War, Psychological
**The scene:** One character, no dialogue, choosing. This is the sequel unit at full length and it is the scene most often cut and most often missed.
**What must turn:** Commitment, from undecided to decided, and the audience must see the price register.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The physical aftermath of the previous scene `emo.delayed_reaction`
2. A practical task begun and abandoned `emo.emotion_through_task`
3. The options laid out in behaviour — a phone picked up, a bag half packed `scene.reaction_dilemma_decision`
4. The option they want, rejected `arc.the_want`
5. The option they fear, approached `arc.the_need`
6. An object consulted — a photograph, a letter, a key `emo.object_carrier`
7. The decision, shown as a small unambiguous action `beat.physical_beat`
8. They do not look back at what they have decided against `sub.behaviour_contradicts`

**Enter:** Late, after the character is already alone and already failing to do something else.
**Exit:** On the action, immediately. No reflection afterwards. `scene.early_exit`
**Avoid:** Interior monologue explaining the choice. A phone call that turns it into dialogue. Making the right option obvious — then there is no dilemma, only a slow character.

### The Lie Discovered

**genres:** Crime, Family Drama, Thriller, Drama, Noir, Social Drama
**The scene:** One character learns another has been lying. The craft decision is whether the liar knows they have been found out, and when.
**What must turn:** Trust, irreversibly. This scene cannot be undone by a later explanation.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The discoverer holds the knowledge and behaves normally `info.audience_only_secret`
2. An ordinary conversation in which the liar repeats the lie `dial.known_information`
3. The discoverer offers one chance, disguised as a question `dial.question_control`
4. The liar takes the chance and lies again `info.dramatic_irony`
5. The evidence produced — an object, not an accusation `scene.transaction_scene`
6. The liar's first response, which is defence of the lie `dial.deflection`
7. The second response, which is defence of the reason `anta.right_about_something`
8. The discoverer's silence, and what they do with the object `dial.silence_as_reply`

**Enter:** After the discovery, so the audience watches the discoverer perform normality.
**Exit:** On the object — kept, returned, or destroyed.
**Avoid:** The confrontation opening with the accusation, which spends the whole scene in beat five. A liar who confesses immediately. An explanation the audience is asked to accept.

### The Teaching Scene

**genres:** Drama, Sports, Coming of Age, Heritage, Historical, Redemption
**The scene:** One character teaches another something practical. The subject is never the subject — the scene is about whether the student will accept the teacher's authority, and what the teacher gets from being needed.
**What must turn:** The relationship's hierarchy, usually by one degree in either direction.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The student's first attempt, competent enough to be embarrassing `char.incompetence_zone`
2. The teacher corrects the wrong thing — technique, when the problem is attention `dial.exposition_correction`
3. The student resists the correction `scene.tactic_change`
4. The teacher demonstrates rather than explains `expo.rule_by_demonstration`
5. A moment where the student sees what the teacher can actually do `char.competence_first`
6. The real correction, which is not about the craft `theme.stated_rejected`
7. The second attempt, better but not right `beat.escalation_step`
8. Something the teacher says that will return much later `info.buried_plant`

**Enter:** On the first attempt, never on the introduction.
**Exit:** On the teacher alone after the student has gone. `end.held_final_beat`
**Avoid:** Success in the scene — the student should not get it right here. A teacher who is patient throughout. The lesson stated as a maxim, which converts the scene into a fortune cookie.

### The Meal

**genres:** Family Drama, Heritage, Social Drama, Comedy, Drama, Historical
**The scene:** A table, food, and everything that cannot be said at it. The most efficient scene in drama: it seats the whole cast, gives every character something to do with their hands, and enforces politeness.
**What must turn:** One relationship inside a group, while the group pretends not to notice.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The seating, which states the hierarchy without a word `rel.group_hierarchy_display`
2. A ritual opening — a blessing, a toast, the first serving `scene.interrupted_ritual`
3. Ordinary conversation with one person not participating `rel.pair_off_the_room`
4. A remark aimed at one person and addressed to another `dial.talking_to_the_room`
5. The target does not reply, and serves someone `emo.emotion_through_task`
6. A second remark, which the table cannot ignore `sub.social_prohibition`
7. Someone changes the subject, successfully `dial.topic_change`
8. One person leaves the table for a practical reason `beat.exit_beat`

**Enter:** After everyone is seated and the first plate has moved.
**Exit:** On whoever is left at the table, or on the person who left.
**Avoid:** An argument that involves everyone — the scene works because most of the table is maintaining the surface. Anyone naming what is happening. More than one turn.

### The Handover

**genres:** Crime, War, Family Drama, Heritage, Drama, Thriller
**The scene:** Something passes from one person to another — keys, money, a child, a body, a responsibility, a name. The object's movement is the structure and the meaning both.
**What must turn:** Responsibility, from one party to the other, irreversibly.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The object present and not yet touched `emo.object_carrier`
2. Business conducted around it as though it were not there `sub.second_subject`
3. The giver states a condition `dial.strategic_withholding`
4. The receiver accepts a condition they do not intend to keep `info.dramatic_irony`
5. A moment where the giver could stop and does not `scene.reversal_of_power`
6. The object moves `scene.transaction_scene`
7. Neither of them looks at it afterwards `sub.behaviour_contradicts`
8. The giver leaves first `beat.exit_beat`

**Enter:** With the object already on the table.
**Exit:** On whoever is left with it. `end.final_image`
**Avoid:** Discussing what the object means. A ceremonial handover, which removes the discomfort. The receiver refusing — that is a different scene.

### The Last Normal Day

**genres:** War, Tragedy, Heritage, Historical, Drama, Disaster
**The scene:** Ordinary life, shown in full, immediately before it ends. Its entire power comes from the audience knowing and the characters not.
**What must turn:** Nothing, deliberately — and that is the point. It is the only scene type in this file where the absence of a turn is correct.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. A routine, performed competently and without comment `time.iterative_habit`
2. A small irritation treated as significant `emo.emotion_against_type`
3. A plan made for later `emo.hope`
4. An unremarkable kindness `char.sympathy_beat`
5. Someone says they will do something tomorrow `tens.known_outcome_dread`
6. A minor argument that is not resolved because there is time `dial.unanswered_line`
7. A door closed, a light switched off, a routine completed `emo.emotion_through_task`
8. Nothing happens `emo.contrast_sets_level`

**Enter:** In the middle of the routine.
**Exit:** On the most ordinary available action. `end.quiet_ending`
**Avoid:** Foreboding of any kind — no lingering looks, no ominous music cue written into the action. Any character sensing something. A scene that announces itself as the last normal day, which converts dread into sentimentality.

### The Interrogation

**genres:** Crime, Legal, Political, Thriller, War, Psychological
**The scene:** One party extracts, the other withholds. Power is tracked by a single visible instrument — who is asking.
**What must turn:** Control of the frame, which is more interesting than whether the information is obtained.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The procedural opening, which establishes the asymmetry `expo.procedural_exposition`
2. Easy questions with known answers, to set a rhythm `dial.question_control`
3. The subject answers a question that was not asked `dial.refusing_the_frame`
4. The interrogator accepts it, which is a concession `dial.concession_that_wins`
5. A piece of evidence produced too early or too late `info.staged_reveal`
6. The subject asks their first question `scene.reversal_of_power`
7. A silence neither fills `dial.silence_as_reply`
8. One of them gives something away that was not the subject of the scene `sub.want_statement_gap`

**Enter:** Several questions in, with the rhythm established.
**Exit:** On whichever of them is asking at the end.
**Avoid:** Physical threat, which ends the scene's real contest. An interrogator who loses their temper without it costing them. The information being the point — it almost never is.

### The Apology That Fails

**genres:** Drama, Family Drama, Romance, Social Drama, Comedy
**The scene:** Someone apologises and is not forgiven, usually because they are apologising for the wrong thing.
**What must turn:** The gap between the two versions of the injury, from hidden to visible.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The apology, prepared and delivered `dial.on_the_nose`
2. Accepted formally and not actually `sub.behaviour_contradicts`
3. The apologiser presses for the real acceptance `scene.tactic_change`
4. The injured party names something else `dial.topic_change`
5. The apologiser defends themselves, which undoes the apology `emo.emotion_against_type`
6. The real injury stated, and it is smaller and older `arc.the_ghost`
7. The apologiser does not recognise it `dial.talking_past`
8. Someone concedes the surface in order to end the conversation `dial.concession_that_wins`

**Enter:** On the apology's first sentence.
**Exit:** On the false peace, not on a resolution. `dial.unanswered_line`
**Avoid:** Forgiveness. An apologiser who is simply insincere — the scene is stronger when they mean it and are still wrong. The injured party explaining the real injury clearly.

### The Discovery of an Object

**genres:** Mystery, Crime, Heritage, Historical, Drama, Horror
**The scene:** Someone finds a physical thing that reframes what they knew. It is the cheapest reveal available and the most durable.
**What must turn:** Understanding of the past, which changes the meaning of the present.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. A practical reason to be searching that is not this `scene.give_a_want`
2. The object found and not recognised as significant `info.buried_plant`
3. A detail on it that does not fit `tens.wrongness_detail`
4. The character's first explanation, which is wrong and reasonable `info.lesser_guilt`
5. A second detail that removes the explanation `info.double_meaning_clue`
6. The reframing lands `info.recontextualising_reveal`
7. The character conceals the object, or does not `beat.physical_beat`
8. They return to the original task, badly `emo.emotion_through_task`

**Enter:** Mid-search, on the wrong task.
**Exit:** On what they do with the object.
**Avoid:** The character narrating the realisation. An object whose significance is obvious to the audience long before the character. Finding it while looking for it.

### The Goodbye Nobody Calls a Goodbye

**genres:** Drama, War, Family Drama, Migration, Heritage, Tragedy
**The scene:** Two people part, one or both knowing it may be permanent, and neither acknowledges it.
**What must turn:** Acceptance, from refused to granted — usually in one of them only.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. Logistics discussed at length `expo.procedural_exposition`
2. A practical gift or instruction handed over `scene.transaction_scene`
3. A promise about the future, made lightly `emo.hope`
4. One of them nearly says it `sub.circled_word`
5. The other changes the subject, kindly `dial.topic_change`
6. A physical gesture that is not the one either intended `emo.physical_register`
7. The departure, conducted at normal speed `emo.understate_at_peak`
8. One of them stays where they are afterwards `end.held_final_beat`

**Enter:** On the logistics, deep in.
**Exit:** On the one who stayed.
**Avoid:** Anyone saying goodbye. A last look — or if there is one, it must be missed by the other. Slowing down, which announces the scene to the audience.

### The Room After

**genres:** Drama, War, Crime, Tragedy, Heritage, Literary
**The scene:** The space where something happened, some time later, with one person in it. Almost no dialogue. It is the aftermath scene and it does more than any speech about consequence.
**What must turn:** The event, from recent to permanent.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The room in its ordinary state, with one thing not put back `tens.wrongness_detail`
2. The character enters on a practical errand `scene.give_a_want`
3. They perform the errand `emo.emotion_through_task`
4. They touch nothing that matters `sub.behaviour_contradicts`
5. A small physical failure — something does not work, does not fit, will not close `emo.emotion_in_the_gap`
6. They stop `beat.held_beat`
7. They put the one thing back, or move it further `emo.object_carrier`
8. They complete the errand and leave `end.quiet_ending`

**Enter:** On the empty room, before the character.
**Exit:** On the room again, after they have gone. `end.final_image`
**Avoid:** Crying. A flashback. Any voiceover. Music that tells the audience what the scene is.

### The First Meeting

**genres:** Romance, Drama, Crime, Comedy, Coming of Age, Thriller
**The scene:** Two people who will matter to each other meet. The audience knows this scene is important and the characters do not, which is the whole tension available.
**What must turn:** Attention. One of them stops treating the other as furniture.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. Both present, neither aware the scene has begun `open.open_mid_situation`
2. A practical transaction between them — a question, an obstruction, a queue `scene.transaction_scene`
3. One of them is slightly rude, or slightly too honest `char.entrance_against_type`
4. The other responds out of character for a stranger `char.the_exception`
5. A shared observation about a third thing `comedy.observational_truth`
6. Names exchanged late, or not at all `dial.name_weapon`
7. One of them says something they will be quoted on much later `info.buried_plant`
8. They separate before either wants to `scene.early_exit`

**Enter:** After the transaction has started.
**Exit:** On whichever of them looks back. If neither does, that is also an answer.
**Avoid:** Chemistry announced by the narration. A meeting engineered by coincidence — let a shared ordinary situation do it. Anyone being charmed on the page; charm is the audience's verdict, not the writer's.

### The Job Interview

**genres:** Drama, Social Drama, Comedy, Workplace, Crime, Migration
**The scene:** A ritualised power exchange in which both parties are performing and both know it. The subject is competence; the content is class, deference and who gets to define what counts.
**What must turn:** Self-estimation. The applicant leaves valuing themselves differently, whatever the outcome.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. Waiting, and what they do with their hands `char.stress_tell`
2. The opening question with a rehearsed answer `dial.on_the_nose`
3. A question the rehearsal did not cover `scene.reversal_of_expectation`
4. The applicant tells a true thing by accident `sub.want_statement_gap`
5. The interviewer's interest changes register `beat.beat_change`
6. A question that is really about background, asked as though it is not `dial.polite_aggression` · `anta.institutional`
7. The applicant chooses how much to concede `theme.moral_test`
8. The formal close, which tells them nothing `dial.unanswered_line`

**Enter:** In the waiting room, one beat before the door.
**Exit:** Outside, on the applicant recalculating.
**Avoid:** A cruel interviewer. A triumphant answer. Telling the audience the outcome in the scene.

### The Deathbed

**genres:** Drama, Family Drama, Tragedy, War, Heritage, Historical
**The scene:** Someone is dying and someone is present. The failure mode is enormous and universal: writing the scene the survivors wish they had had.
**What must turn:** What is left unsaid, from postponable to permanent.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The room's logistics — a chair, a machine, a window `expo.environmental_detail`
2. Ordinary conversation, badly `dial.talking_past`
3. The dying person is concerned with something small and practical `emo.emotion_against_type`
4. The visitor tries to open the large subject `scene.tactic_change`
5. It is deflected, or missed, or the timing is wrong `dial.deflection`
6. A physical act of care replaces the conversation `emo.emotion_through_task`
7. Something is said that is not the thing, and is enough `emo.understate_at_peak`
8. The visitor leaves the room for an ordinary reason `beat.exit_beat`

**Enter:** Mid-visit, not on arrival.
**Exit:** In the corridor. `emo.delayed_reaction`
**Avoid:** A final speech. Forgiveness granted on schedule. The dying person made wise by dying. A last word that resolves the work's argument.

### The Testimony

**genres:** Courtroom, Legal, Social Drama, War, Historical, Documentary
**The scene:** Someone recounts what happened, to a room with the power to decide what it means. The account and the event are never the same thing, and that gap is the drama.
**What must turn:** Credibility, which is separate from truth and does not always move with it.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The formal preliminaries, which are also the framing `expo.procedural_exposition`
2. The account given in the witness's own words, unhelpfully `voice.register_setting`
3. The first correction of their vocabulary by the room `anta.institutional`
4. A detail the witness insists on that nobody else finds relevant `emo.specific_detail`
5. Cross-examination reframes the account `dial.refusing_the_frame`
6. The witness is made to doubt something they know `info.unreliable_narrator`
7. They repeat the irrelevant detail `dial.repetition_variation`
8. The room's verdict on them arrives before any formal one `rel.group_hierarchy_display`

**Enter:** Several questions in.
**Exit:** On the witness leaving the stand, and who looks at them.
**Avoid:** A lawyer's speech. The witness winning. Making the truth legible to the room — the point is usually that it is not.

---

### The Border

**genres:** Migration, Social Drama, War, Thriller, Historical, Heritage
**The scene:** A person and an official at a point of control. One holds a life, the other holds a procedure, and neither can see the other's constraints.
**What must turn:** Status, from provisional to decided — or worse, left provisional.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The queue, and the watching of others being processed `tens.rule_demonstration`
2. Documents produced and examined in silence `tens.waiting_as_weapon`
3. A question with no good answer `scene.goal_conflict_disaster`
4. The traveller adjusts their story slightly `dial.half_truth`
5. The official notices and does not say so `info.dramatic_irony`
6. A second official is consulted out of earshot `tens.shrinking_options`
7. A decision delivered without explanation `anta.institutional`
8. The traveller thanks them `emo.shame`

**Enter:** At the counter.
**Exit:** On whichever side of the line they end up, held one beat too long. `end.held_final_beat`
**Avoid:** A sadistic official. A speech about rights. Resolution by a sympathetic bystander.

---

### The Intervention

**genres:** Family Drama, Drama, Social Drama, Addiction, Comedy
**The scene:** A group confronts one person about their behaviour, having agreed on a plan the person immediately breaks.
**What must turn:** The group's unity, which almost always fractures before the subject does.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The subject arrives expecting something else `open.enter_on_the_answer`
2. The prepared opening, delivered by the wrong person `rel.who_defends_whom`
3. The subject's first response is charm `char.likeable_vs_interesting`
4. One member of the group laughs, or agrees `rel.shifting_coalition`
5. The plan collapses and someone improvises an accusation `dial.exposition_accusation`
6. The subject names something true about a group member `anta.right_about_something`
7. The group turns on itself briefly `rel.everyone_agrees`
8. The subject leaves, and the group has to sit with each other `end.quiet_ending`

**Enter:** On the subject's arrival.
**Exit:** On the group, after. The scene is about them.
**Avoid:** Success. A subject who breaks down. A group that stays united — if they do, the scene has no second act.

---

### The Phone Call Across Two Places

**genres:** Drama, Thriller, Family Drama, Migration, War, Crime
**The scene:** Two people in different locations, each visible to the audience and not to each other. The audience sees two rooms; the characters see one voice.
**What must turn:** The distance, from bridged to confirmed.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. Both rooms established before the call `pov.same_event_twice`
2. The call answered while doing something else `emo.emotion_through_task`
3. Ordinary business, conducted in parallel `dial.talking_past`
4. One of them lies about where they are or what they are doing `dial.half_truth`
5. The audience sees the lie; the other does not `info.dramatic_irony`
6. A sound in one room that the other asks about `tens.repeating_sound`
7. The real subject approached and abandoned `dial.topic_change`
8. One of them holds the phone after the call ends `beat.held_beat`

**Enter:** On the ring, in the room that did not expect it.
**Exit:** On the room where the call meant more.
**Avoid:** Cutting only to whoever is speaking. A dropped connection as the turn. A confession that would not be made by phone.

### The Scene the Child Is In

**genres:** Family Drama, Drama, Social Drama, War, Heritage, Comedy
**The scene:** Adults conduct an adult conflict while a child is present. The child is the instrument: what the adults will and will not say in front of them defines them entirely.
**What must turn:** The adults' self-image, measured by what they allow the child to witness.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The child occupied with something of their own `char.what_they_notice`
2. The adults begin in code `sub.high_context_indirection`
3. The code slips once `dial.interruption`
4. One adult checks the child; the other does not `char.the_exception`
5. The child asks a question about something adjacent `comedy.literal_answer`
6. An adult answers it honestly, which is the scene's turn `scene.value_turn`
7. The conflict resumes at lower volume and higher damage `dial.polite_aggression`
8. The child returns to their own activity, changed `sub.kid_readable_subtext`

**Enter:** With the child already in place.
**Exit:** On the child.
**Avoid:** A precocious child who understands everything. A child used as a device to stop the argument. Sending the child out of the room, which is a different and lesser scene.

### The Return to the Childhood Home

**genres:** Family Drama, Drama, Heritage, Migration, Literary, Coming of Age
**The scene:** An adult in a house that formed them, usually to clear it, sell it or attend something.
**What must turn:** Their account of their own childhood, from settled to disturbed.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The house's scale is wrong — everything smaller `emo.physical_register`
2. An object exactly where it was `emo.object_carrier`
3. A practical task begun `emo.emotion_through_task`
4. Evidence that contradicts a story they tell about themselves `time.unreliable_memory`
5. A sibling or parent offers a different version `info.character_asymmetry`
6. The disagreement is not resolved and not pursued `dial.unanswered_line`
7. They take something small and worthless `emo.object_carrier`
8. They leave a light on, or turn one off `end.final_image`

**Enter:** Inside the house, already.
**Exit:** At the door, on what they are carrying.
**Avoid:** A flashback. A revelation about the past. Weeping in a bedroom.

### The Crowd

**genres:** War, Social Drama, Political, Historical, Heritage, Thriller
**The scene:** Many people and one viewpoint. The craft problem is legibility: a crowd is only dramatic if the audience can hold one person inside it.
**What must turn:** The crowd's temperature, and the viewpoint character's position relative to it.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. One named person established before the crowd is `pov.scene_ownership`
2. The crowd's mood shown through three specific individuals `emo.specific_detail`
3. A sound or signal moves through it `tens.repeating_sound`
4. The viewpoint character is carried somewhere they did not choose `tens.shrinking_options`
5. They lose sight of whoever they came with `tens.closing_space`
6. A single act by one person changes the whole `rel.group_under_pressure`
7. The crowd becomes something other than the sum of its people `anta.ambient_pressure`
8. The viewpoint character gets out, or does not `scene.trap_scene`

**Enter:** With the viewpoint character, on the edge.
**Exit:** On what the crowd leaves behind.
**Avoid:** Describing the crowd instead of a person in it. A leader who explains it. Treating the crowd as a single mind from the first line.

### The Rehearsal

**genres:** Drama, Sports, Coming of Age, Comedy, Heritage, Artist
**The scene:** A group practising something they will later perform. It is a gift of a scene: it supplies repetition, a visible standard, a legitimate reason for criticism, and stakes that are not yet real.
**What must turn:** Belief in the group's chance, up or down.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The run-through, with one visible weakness `char.incompetence_zone`
2. A correction that is accepted `expo.rule_by_demonstration`
3. A correction that is not `scene.tactic_change`
4. The weakest member's moment, and who covers for them `rel.who_defends_whom`
5. Something works better than expected `emo.hope`
6. A remark about the real event intrudes `anta.time`
7. The leader chooses what to fix and what to abandon `theme.moral_test`
8. They run it once more, differently `beat.beat_rhyme`

**Enter:** Mid-run.
**Exit:** On the person who has not yet been corrected.
**Avoid:** A perfect run. A motivational speech. Making the rehearsal about the leader.

### The Scene in a Second Language

**genres:** Migration, Social Drama, War, Drama, Heritage, Comedy
**The scene:** At least one character is operating in a language they do not fully control. Fluency is not a fixed trait — it drops under stress and rises with confidence, and that movement is the scene's instrument.
**What must turn:** Standing, which in this scene is measured in vocabulary.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The character managing well, with visible effort `voice.second_language_voice`
2. A word they cannot find, worked around successfully `char.competence_cost`
3. The other party adjusts — slows down, or does not `dial.polite_aggression`
4. A moment of full fluency in their own language, to a third person `voice.code_switching`
5. Pressure rises and the fluency drops `char.stress_tell`
6. Something important said badly, and misread `dial.misheard_line`
7. They repeat it, worse `tens.escalating_humiliation`
8. They stop trying, and the meaning transfers anyway `sub.behaviour_contradicts`

**Enter:** With the conversation already competent, so the fall registers.
**Exit:** On whichever language they use last.
**Avoid:** Broken-English spelling — carry it in syntax and word choice `voice.eye_dialect`. A character whose fluency is constant. Comedy at their expense unless they are the one making it.

### The Handover of a Child

**genres:** Family Drama, Social Drama, Drama, Migration, War
**The scene:** A child moves from one adult's care to another's — a custody exchange, a boarding, a sending away. The adults perform civility for the child and conduct a war under it.
**What must turn:** Which adult the child is protecting, which is not the same as which they prefer.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. Logistics discussed with excessive politeness `dial.polite_aggression`
2. A bag checked, an item missing `scene.transaction_scene`
3. The child is spoken about in the third person while present `dial.talking_to_the_room`
4. One adult breaks the civility by a degree `beat.beat_change`
5. The child intervenes practically, not emotionally `sub.kid_readable_subtext`
6. The adults reunite briefly against the child's intervention `rel.shifting_coalition`
7. The goodbye, conducted at normal speed `emo.understate_at_peak`
8. The remaining adult stays where they are `end.held_final_beat`

**Enter:** On the doorstep or the platform, mid-logistics.
**Exit:** On the adult who is left.
**Avoid:** A crying child. An adult who says what they actually think. Resolution.

### The Shift Handover

**genres:** Medical, Crime, Procedural, War, Social Drama, Drama
**The scene:** One team ends and another begins, and the work continues without either. It is the most efficient exposition scene in the professional genres, because the information is genuinely being transferred.
**What must turn:** Responsibility, and often a small piece of it is deliberately not transferred.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The formal list, delivered fast in professional shorthand `expo.procedural_exposition`
2. One item summarised in a way that flattens it `expo.jargon_threshold`
3. A question from the incoming shift `dial.exposition_correction`
4. An answer that is technically complete `dial.half_truth`
5. Something left off the list, deliberately `info.audience_only_secret`
6. Informal conversation as the formal part ends `dial.enter_late_leave_early`
7. The outgoing shift leaves too quickly `char.stress_tell`
8. The incoming one finds the omitted item `info.staged_reveal`

**Enter:** Mid-list.
**Exit:** On the omission being found, or on the door.
**Avoid:** Explaining the shorthand to the audience. A handover with no omission. Making the outgoing shift incompetent rather than tired.

### The Scene Where Nothing Is Said

**genres:** Drama, Tragedy, War, Heritage, Literary, Family Drama
**The scene:** Two people, no dialogue, doing something together or beside each other. It is the hardest scene to write and the one most often remembered.
**What must turn:** Proximity, emotional rather than physical.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. A shared task begun without discussion `emo.emotion_through_task`
2. A division of labour that reveals the relationship `rel.unspoken_agreement`
3. One of them does something the other's way `beat.physical_beat`
4. A pause in which speech is possible and not taken `sub.silence_as_content`
5. An object passed between them `beat.object_beat`
6. One of them makes a small mistake `char.stress_tell`
7. The other corrects it without comment `rel.who_defends_whom`
8. The task is finished `end.quiet_ending`

**Enter:** With the task already begun.
**Exit:** On the finished task, or on the one who stays.
**Avoid:** Voiceover. A look that carries the scene. Any character speaking — if one must, the scene is a different one.

---

### The Last Day at Work

**genres:** Drama, Social Drama, Comedy, Workplace, Heritage
**The scene:** Someone finishes for the last time, in a place that is not going to notice for long.
**What must turn:** Their sense of having mattered, usually downward, and the audience's estimation of them upward.

**Beat skeleton:**
1. The routine performed for the last time, competently `time.iterative_habit`
2. A colleague asks them something work-related, forgetting `rel.rotating_spotlight`
3. They answer it fully anyway `char.competence_cost`
4. The card, the collection, the brief speech `scene.interrupted_ritual`
5. Someone says the wrong thing kindly `emo.emotion_against_type`
6. They clear a drawer and find something old `emo.object_carrier`
7. They do one small piece of work nobody asked for `theme.object_carrier`
8. They hand over the keys and are not walked out `end.quiet_ending`

**Enter:** On the routine.
**Exit:** Outside, on the building.
**Avoid:** A tribute. Bitterness stated. A replacement already at the desk, which is a different and cheaper scene.
