# Score Composition Dynamics, Leitmotifs & Underscore

21 cards. Film score architecture, recurring leitmotif design, civilizational scales (Maqam Bayati), tempo-to-visual-ASL synchronization, and dynamic tension underscore.

---

### The Span Before the Sound

**Also called:** the score selector, span before sound, how long does this decision have to hold  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `score.` shelf. It names no instrument and no mood. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **how long must this decision hold, what is the scene doing to the character, and does the audience hear the score arrive** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were read off the sixteen `Yields to:` conditions the cards carry and the `Avoid when:` boundary on every one of them; the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id.  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so the cue that does reach the screen was chosen against the length of the thing it has to survive, rather than against the adjective in the brief.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer question one **before you open the shelf**, because a decision that has to hold for one frame and a decision that has to hold for four hours are not competing and this shelf files them side by side. Question two narrows to a handful; question three is only asked when the score is entering or leaving. Where two answers fight for one moment, that is two cues, and one of them is `score.subconscious_score_sneak`.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Limited Series, Heritage Documentary, Period Epic, Trailer / Teaser | genres: Epic, Drama, Tragedy, Heritage, Thriller, History  
**Avoid when:** **The score is being licensed or tracked rather than composed.** Choosing from a library is a search against a catalogue, not a fork between two ideas, and this map will have you designing a cue you are not going to write. Also avoid it on **children's content**, where `kids.no_fast_cuts` outranks the tempo lock on this shelf outright and the shelf says so in its own field. And **distrust question one whenever your honest answer is the whole work** — five of the nineteen live there and four of those govern every scene equally, so on a single scene this map has two questions left and is doing less than half the work it does on a film. **A last warning about the shelf rather than the map:** several cards here carry an Example that disagrees with the card's own body about a figure. Take values from the body, never from the Example, and never from this card — it carries no figures on purpose.  
**Example:** `[Selector — produces no cue and no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the Example.]`  

---

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — How long does this decision have to hold: one instant, one scene, or the whole work?**
Asked first because it is the only question that separates cards which otherwise look like rivals and are not. `score.revelation_stinger_impact` and `score.minimalist_suspense_drone` are not two answers to one question — one detonates on a frame, the other has to survive an entire scene without a melody. The conditions state it plainly and repeatedly: *"Overusing every thirty seconds for trivial information"* (`score.revelation_stinger_impact`), *"an unbroken drone across an entire scene"* (`score.minimalist_suspense_drone`), *"Recurring figures across long-form films and series"* (`score.character_leitmotif_binding`), *"the number of treatments comes from the declared act map"* (`score.thematic_variation_acts`), *"After the whole of a minor-key struggle"* (`score.philosophical_tonal_resolution`).

**Axis 2 — What is the scene doing to the character at this moment?**
Stated as facts about the scene, not as feelings about the music, which is exactly how the boundaries are written: *"A fast kinetic chase, not suspended time"* (`score.minimalist_suspense_drone`), *"A quiet negotiation, not an advance"* (`score.war_drum_acceleration_pulse`), *"The turn is a decision, not a shock"* (`score.revelation_stinger_impact`) and its exact reverse in `score.harmonic_modulation_pivot`, *"An optimistic turn: lift the key, drop the fate figure"* (`score.bass_ostinato_inevitable_doom`), *"A cliffhanger that must stay unresolved"* (`score.philosophical_tonal_resolution`), *"A fight scene that needs aggression, not beauty"* (`score.tragic_melodic_counterpoint`), *"A fast kinetic chase sequence"* (`score.solo_vocalise_monolithic_space`). Seven conditions, four scene states, and one value for the cards that are not answering a scene question at all.

**Axis 3 — Does the audience hear the score arrive, or is it already running?**
Three conditions turn on ingress and egress rather than on content: *"No instrument in the scene to hand over from"* (`score.diegetic_to_score_transmutation`), *"A shock cut that needs an immediate hit"* (`score.subconscious_score_sneak`), *"An intimate conversation or character study"* (`score.trailer_riser_detonation`). It is asked last because for most of the shelf the question does not arise — and the cards for which it does arise are the ones the shelf argues about hardest, which is why it is asked at all.

**One row of Axis 1 is a warning rather than a shortlist.** `score.four_way_score_stems` sits alone under the fourth value because it answers a different question from every other card here — not *what does the music do* but *what shape does the file leave in* — and that is exactly where the package's worst numeric disagreement lives. **Stem delivery is specified four different ways:** this card names four sub-mixes and its **Used for** binds the handoff *from an AI music engine* to that number; `sync.sync_stems_deliverables_package` names six groups; `song_handoff.stem_separation_protocol` names two; `lyria.native_multitrack_stems` names another figure again. Whether an engine's output can be split four ways is a vendor fact, recorded in its adapter in ENGINE-CHECK.md §7, so the binding written here may ask for something the engine in use cannot produce. **No number is corrected on this card and no winner is picked.** The decision is owned by **`sync.sync_stems_deliverables_package`**, because it is the only one of the four whose own `Yields to:` branches on the fact that settles it — *no multitrack session to render from* — and routes you out of a count you cannot render. Take the question there, and read the destination's own written spec before anything is exported.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `score.revelation_stinger_impact`, and it is measured rather than asserted — but the measure names a punctuation mark rather than a bed, and that is worth saying out loud.**

It is reached three times, on **three distinct conditions, from three different families**: `score.harmonic_modulation_pivot` sends a reader here when the turn is a shock and not a decision; `score.subconscious_score_sneak` sends one here when the cut is sudden and needs an immediate hit; and `foley.cinematic_braam_hit` sends one here when the piece is naturalist history and a trailer braam would read cheap. Nothing else on this shelf is reached on more than two distinct conditions. **The shelf, asked where to fall back to, falls back to the treatment of the turn** — which is itself a reading of what this shelf is: eleven of its sixteen edges are arguments about what happens at a moment of change, and the bed underneath is chosen afterwards, if at all.

**The measure had to be counted the hard way, and this shelf is the clean demonstration of why.** `score.pre_climax_score_vacuum` is also reached three times — from `foley.acoustic_vacuum_drop`, from `hook_psych.sudden_silence_punch_drop` and from `sensory_sonic.silence_as_reply`. Counting edges, it ties for first. Counting **conditions**, all three fire on the same one: *you wanted everything taken out, and everything is too much.* It is a door out of the total-silence family, not a fallback within this shelf. In-degree would have made it the default and it would have been wrong.

**The one against it is that same card, `score.pre_climax_score_vacuum`** — and the opposition is exact, because it answers the default's question by refusing the default's instrument. Where the shelf's own answer to a turn is to hit it, this one's answer is to take the score away and let the room carry the moment. Its body is explicit that it is not its two neighbours: `foley.acoustic_vacuum_drop` removes everything, `sound.silence_before` puts the gap ahead of a cut rather than ahead of an impact. **Nothing on this shelf ever proposes it positively.** All three of its inbound edges are retreats — somebody who had already decided on silence, being talked down to less of it. A card that is only ever arrived at by climbing down is a card nobody has chosen on purpose, and on the one shelf where the loudest available gesture is the measured default, that is the choice most worth making deliberately. Its cost is stated and is the honest one: the length is found on the timeline, not on the card, and too much of it reads as a playback fault rather than as dread.

**The unasked**

**Eight of the nineteen mapped cards are the target of no `Yields to:` field anywhere in the package.** They can be chosen but nothing ever sends you to them. By id:

`score.character_leitmotif_binding` · `score.bpm_to_asl_synchronization` · `score.diegetic_to_score_transmutation` · `score.tragic_melodic_counterpoint` · `score.microtonal_quarter_tone_tension` · `score.solo_vocalise_monolithic_space` · `score.philosophical_tonal_resolution` · `score.trailer_riser_detonation`

**And the shape of that list is the finding, not its length.** Every card on it binds the score to something that is not music — a character (`score.character_leitmotif_binding`), the cut (`score.bpm_to_asl_synchronization`), an object inside the frame (`score.diegetic_to_score_transmutation`), what the picture is showing (`score.tragic_melodic_counterpoint`), a place and a culture (`score.microtonal_quarter_tone_tension`, `score.solo_vocalise_monolithic_space`), the end of the film (`score.philosophical_tonal_resolution`), a title card (`score.trailer_riser_detonation`). Every card the shelf *does* reach is a card about the music's own behaviour: a shock, a modulation, a drone, a drum, a gap, a sneak, a doom figure. **The pair graph compares cues to cues and never compares a cue to the thing the cue is attached to.** The readers who wrote these fields were each holding one cue, not one film, and the pair field cannot express an attachment — it can only express a substitution.

The second thing the list shows is sharper and it names the card this shelf is missing. **`score.character_leitmotif_binding` is unreached, while `score.thematic_variation_acts` — which is nothing but that motif developed across the acts — is reached, and only from outside the shelf.** The shelf holds one idea in two cards and has never once routed between them. Nothing here says *you have bound a motif; now vary it, and the number of variations comes from the act map you declared.* They are one decision made twice, months apart, and the pair field recorded neither as an alternative to the other because neither is — which is precisely why they needed a pointer instead and never got one.

And a third, quieter one. The whole vocabulary region of this shelf — what the music is physically made of — is reached by exactly one internal edge, `score.microtonal_quarter_tone_tension` → `score.near_eastern_maqam_scales`. Nothing ever routes **from a move to a vocabulary**: you can be sent from one drone to another drone, but nothing on this shelf ever tells you that the drone should be a ney rather than a cello. The shelf treats the palette as settled before you arrive, and on a heritage project it is the thing least likely to be settled and most expensive to get wrong.

### Character Leitmotif Binding

**Also called:** thematic sonic tag, signature melody, hero motif  
**What it is:** Composing a distinct, memorable 3-to-5 note melodic motif bound to a specific character, kingdom, or dramatic destiny.  
**Effect on the audience:** Triggers instant emotional recognition; announces a character's presence or psychological influence before they appear on screen.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Recurring figures (e.g. Gilgamesh, Enkidu, Ishtar, Ashurbanipal) across long-form films and series.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Limited Series, Epic Animation | genres: Epic, Fantasy, Drama  
**Avoid when:** Generic non-thematic background wallpaper music with no identifiable melody.  
**Example:** `motif: Gilgamesh Theme: Solitary Duduk playing rising fourth (D - G - A - F#) over low contrabass drone.`  
`score.character_leitmotif_binding`

### Ancient Near Eastern Maqam Scale System

**Also called:** Mesopotamian modal scale, Maqam Bayati / Rast, authentic microtones  
**What it is:** Scoring using traditional regional musical modes featuring quarter-tone intervals (such as Maqam Bayati, Hijaz, and Saba) rather than Western European major/minor scales.  
**Effect on the audience:** Transports the listener directly into the authentic antiquity of the Near East; prevents Hollywood orientalist stereotyping.  
**Used for and where it works best:** All historical documentaries, films, and heritage projects focused on Mesopotamia, Babylon, and Assyria.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage Documentary, Historical Feature | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), History, World  
**Avoid when:** Western orchestral pop chords (e.g. C major to G major) in an ancient Mesopotamian temple scene.  
**Example:** `scale: Maqam Bayati on D: D, E-half-flat (quarter tone), F, G, A, B-flat, C, D.`  
`score.near_eastern_maqam_scales`

### Musical BPM to Visual ASL Synchronization

**Also called:** tempo-cut pacing lock, rhythmic shot editing  
**What it is:** Synchronizing the musical tempo (Beats Per Minute) with the Average Shot Length (ASL) calculated in Phase 3 (Shooting Script).  
**Effect on the audience:** Creates an organic visual-auditory dance where camera cuts land naturally on downbeats or syncopated off-beats.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Action montages, trailer cuts, and rhythmic documentary sequences. On children's content the 1.5-second cut floor (`kids.no_fast_cuts`) outranks this formula: at 120 BPM the ASL is four beats (2.0 s / 48 frames), never the one-beat-pair cut.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Short-Form Reel | genres: Action, Trailer, Modern Social  
**Avoid when:** Cuts drifting aimlessly a few frames ahead or behind the musical beat, creating visual-auditory friction.  
**Example:** `sync_formula: 120 BPM = exactly 2 beats per second (cut on every beat pair = 1.0s / 24 frames; cut every 2 bars of 4/4 = 4.0s / 96 frames).`  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; the frame counts are arithmetic at 120 BPM and 24 fps; set the cut rate against the edit.  
**Yields to:** `kids.no_fast_cuts` — on children's content, where the 1.5-second cut floor governs and the beat multiple is taken at four beats or eight, never one pair.  
`score.bpm_to_asl_synchronization`

### Minimalist Suspense Drone Architecture

**Also called:** tension bed, harmonic drone, subterranean score, meditation underscore, atmospheric river bed, drone tapestry (this card was two until September 2026; the second one's id was lyria.lyrical_ambient_drone)  
**What it is:** An unbroken, shifting drone (bowed cello, analog modular synth, or Tibetan singing bowl) holding tension across an entire scene without a distracting melody. Its lyrical shape is soft, evolving harmonic drones featuring delicate acoustic instrumental accents (a solitary Ney note every 8 seconds, water bells, gentle harp).  
**Effect on the audience:** Suspends dramatic time; keeps the audience on edge while leaving 100% frequency room for dialogue comprehension. The lyrical shape induces deep contemplative stillness without inducing boredom; holds attention effortlessly beneath spoken narration.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Detective investigations, archaeological decipherment, and tense negotiations. Nature sequences along the Euphrates and Tigris rivers, archaeological digs, and starry desert skies.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Documentary Feature, True Crime, Nature Special, Meditation Audio | genres: Thriller, Mystery, History, Nature, Heritage, Ambient  
**Avoid when:** Fast kinetic chase scenes or joyous celebrations. Fast-paced informational reels requiring high tempo.  
**Example:** `score_bed: Low A drone (55Hz) with slow flanger and gentle filter sweep (200Hz-800Hz) over 3 minutes.` — or, the lyrical shape as a Lyria 3.5 prompt (key and "Instrumental only" written in the prompt, syntax on `lyria.semantic_natural_prompting`): `prompt: "Ethereal acoustic drone in D minor with subtle reed flute flutter notes, a solitary ney note every 8 seconds, and gentle river water acoustics, continuous and meditative. Instrumental only."`  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; set it by ear against the rendered score.  
**Yields to:** `sync.high_octane_action_chase_percussion` — A fast kinetic chase, not suspended time; and `lyria.stylistic_hybridization` — Fast high-impact reel needing tempo.  
`score.minimalist_suspense_drone`

### Sudden Revelation Stinger Impact

**Also called:** musical stinger, shock chord, discovery hit  
**What it is:** A sudden, dissonant orchestral or percussive chord detonating at the exact frame a plot twist or terrifying discovery is revealed.  
**Effect on the audience:** Spikes the viewer's heart rate; punctuates the revelation as an irreversible historic turning point.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Unmasking a killer, opening the tomb to find it empty, or discovering the enemy has breached the gates.  
**Best in:** formats: Thriller, Mystery, Historical Drama | genres: Mystery, Thriller, Horror  
**Avoid when:** Overusing every 30 seconds for trivial information, which numbs the audience.  
**Example:** `cue: Sudden fortissimo cluster chord (violins screeching in minor seconds + timpani slam) on frame 0.`  
**Yields to:** `score.harmonic_modulation_pivot` — The turn is a decision, not a shock.  
`score.revelation_stinger_impact`

### Authentic Ethnic Acoustic Instrumentation

**Also called:** period instruments, indigenous organology  
**What it is:** Composing exclusively with instruments authentic to the region: Oud (lute), Ney (reed flute), Qanun (zither), Daf/Riq (frame drums), and Sistrum.  
**Effect on the audience:** Instills deep cultural reverence and historical truth that synthetic MIDI strings cannot replicate.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Hero themes for ancient empires, temple ceremonies, and desert crossing montages.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage Documentary, Period Epic | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), World  
**Avoid when:** Cheap synthesized General-MIDI oriental synth presets.  
**Example:** `instrumentation: Solo acoustic wooden Oud playing intricate Maqam Saba melody, accompanied by soft ceramic darbuka.`  
`score.ethnic_acoustic_instruments`

### Harmonic Modulation on Narrative Turning Points

**Also called:** key change turning point, emotional pivot modulation, triumph modulation, heroic key shift, the golden landing (this card was two until September 2026; the second one's id was lyria.climax_modulation_release)  
**What it is:** Shifting the harmonic key center (e.g. from D minor to G minor, or minor to major) at the exact moment a character makes an irreversible decision. At the dramatic climax it takes a second shape: modulating upward by a whole step or into parallel major at the exact dramatic climax, releasing all built-up tension.  
**Effect on the audience:** The audience feels the emotional ground shift beneath their feet; subconscious cues announce that the world has changed forever. At the climax it triggers an overwhelming emotional flood of relief, triumph, and awe; brings tears of catharsis.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The moment the hero accepts the quest, decides on betrayal, or achieves spiritual enlightenment. Victory celebrations, completing the monument, or achieving spiritual redemption. On Lyria 3.5, direct the modulation in the prompt at its timestamp. Google documents timestamped sections (`lyria.syllable_timing_sync`) and a key written in words (`lyria.semantic_natural_prompting`), but not a key change at a set time, so check the take; where it misses, generate the passages before and after in their own keys and change at the join (`song_handoff.extended_suite_linking`).  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Feature Film Finale, Epic Documentary Climax | genres: Drama, Epic, Adventure, Inspirational  
**Avoid when:** Modulating randomly in the middle of a mundane expository sentence. Cynical noir endings or tragic unresolved mysteries.  
**Example:** `modulation: Scene shifts from D minor (despair) to D major (divine revelation) as the sun hits the ziggurat apex.` — or, at the climax of a Lyria 3.5 cue (Gemini app, model Pro, length Full): `prompt: "Create a full-length epic orchestral piece in D minor. At 2:45 modulate smoothly from D minor into triumphant D major, full brass and choir soaring to a magnificent final resolution."`  
**Source:** reference — the Lyria 3.5 facts this card uses are on `lyria.syllable_timing_sync` and `lyria.semantic_natural_prompting`, each with its official URL, read 2026-09-23.  
**Yields to:** `score.revelation_stinger_impact` — The turn is a shock discovery, not a decision.  
`score.harmonic_modulation_pivot`

### Ancient War Drum Acceleration Pulse

**Also called:** battle cadence, advancing percussion, tribal war pulse  
**What it is:** Starting with a slow, ritualistic 54 BPM frame drum heartbeat and progressively accelerating and layering percussive complexity to 120 BPM.  
**Effect on the audience:** Drives involuntary physical adrenaline; primes the viewer's nervous system for violent physical combat.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Armies marching into battle lines, siege towers rolling toward walls, and impending confrontations.  
**Best in:** formats: War Epic, Action Short | genres: War, Action, Epic  
**Avoid when:** Quiet diplomatic negotiations or bedroom romance.  
**Example:** `score: 54 BPM solitary frame drum -> add bronze sistrum rattle @ 80 BPM -> add thunderous daf drums @ 116 BPM -> full layered pulse @ 120 BPM.`  
**Yields to:** `score.minimalist_suspense_drone` — A quiet negotiation, not an advance.  
`score.war_drum_acceleration_pulse`

### Subconscious Underscore Ingress (The Sneak)

**Also called:** music sneak, imperceptible entry, ambient score merge  
**What it is:** Bringing the score in *underneath the ambience* — quieter than the room tone, so it is not heard arriving — and letting it swell so slowly that the listener never catches the moment music started. The cue is written and generated to support that: it enters on a single sustained note, tuned to something already in the scene, with no attack to give it away. How quietly it enters and how long the swell takes are ride decisions made in the edit, against the scene's own noise floor.  
**Effect on the audience:** Manipulates emotion covertly; the audience suddenly finds themselves deeply moved without realizing music has begun playing.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Transitioning from calm factual discussion into deep tragic emotion.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Serious Drama | genres: Drama, History, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** Sudden shock cuts where an immediate hit is required.  
**Example:** `handover: solitary cello enters in unison with the pitch of the desert wind, from below the ambience, and rises so slowly the entry is never heard. Ride it up in the edit — if the audience notices music starting, it came up too fast.`  
**Yields to:** `score.revelation_stinger_impact` — A shock cut that needs an immediate hit.  
`score.subconscious_score_sneak`

### Pre-Climax Score Vacuum

**Also called:** total music drop, silence before drop, cue cut-off  
**What it is:** Cutting the *score alone* to nothing before the catastrophic beat, leaving the scene's own sound — breathing, wind, a footstep — naked underneath it. The score goes; the world stays. This is the composed version of the move: the cue is written so it can be cut mid-bar without sounding unfinished. **How long the score stays out is a length found on the timeline, in the edit** — long enough that the protection is gone, short enough that the audience has not started wondering whether something broke.  
**Effect on the audience:** Deprives the viewer of emotional protection; exposes raw, unadorned reality right before the fatal blow falls.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The moment before the king is assassinated, or the second before the dam breaks. Distinguish it from its two neighbours: `foley.acoustic_vacuum_drop` takes *everything* out, not just the score, and `sound.silence_before` puts the silence ahead of a **cut** rather than ahead of an impact. Three different moves; one length each, found in the edit.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Action Short, Thriller | genres: Thriller, Tragedy, War  
**Avoid when:** Upbeat commercial montages.  
**Example:** `handover: orchestra stops mid-bar here; only the character's breathing carries into the impact. Hold the score out as long as it stays frightening — set the length on the timeline.`  
**Yields to:** `foley.acoustic_vacuum_drop` — Everything drops, not the score alone.  
`score.pre_climax_score_vacuum`

### Thematic Variation Across Narrative Acts

**Also called:** theme transformation, dynamic variation, film score leitmotif variation, thematic adaptation (this card was two until September 2026; the second one's id was lyria.thematic_variation_generation)  
**What it is:** Evolving the central theme across the 3 acts: played tenderly on solo flute in Act 1, played aggressively on low brass in Act 2, played mournfully on solo cello in Act 3. Read this as one treatment per act the project actually declared, not a fixed three: a four-act film (`struct.four_act`) gets four, with the new treatment entering at the midpoint. Across scenes rather than acts it takes a second shape: directing Lyria to take a single approved thematic melody and generate 4 distinct emotional variations (Action, Tragedy, Romance, Mystery), the melody recognisable in each.  
**Effect on the audience:** Communicates character growth, corruption, or tragedy through musical evolution; unifies the entire multi-hour work. Unifies an entire feature film or multi-episode documentary series under a single coherent musical identity.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The main theme of an epic protagonist (e.g. Gilgamesh as proud young king, fierce warrior, broken mortal seeker). Scoring feature films and long-form historical series with recurring characters. On Lyria 3.5 in the Gemini app or the API, generation is single-turn and takes no audio (`lyria.conditioning_audio_input`), so write the theme once as notes and rhythm and paste that same text into every variation's prompt. ### Microtonal Quarter-Tone Tension Inflection

**Also called:** microtonal pitch bend, sikah quarter tone, supernatural interval, microtonal fidelity, Maqam tuning precision (this card was two until September 2026; the second one's id was lyria.quarter_tone_purity)  
**What it is:** Intentionally bending notes by a quarter-tone (50 cents) to create subtle, eerie microtonal dissonance outside Western 12-tone equal temperament. Its second shape is the opposite intent: holding a maqam's true intervals through a generated track — the maqam's neutral (three-quarter-tone) intervals -- theorized in modern Arab music theory as a 24-tone equal-tempered grid, but not fixed at those exact quarter-tone (50-cent) values in actual performance, where the neutral pitch shifts with maqam and melodic context -- without drifting onto 12-tone equal temperament — and checking that they are there.  
**Effect on the audience:** Evokes supernatural mystery, ancient divination, astrological dread, and non-Western cultural depths. Holding the intervals keeps out the out-of-tune, sour-sounding pitch artifacts that Eastern scales suffer when they are pulled onto Western-biased tuning. When a musician approves the take, the modal colour survives; failed intonation is rejected rather than described as authentic.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Temple divination rituals, dream sequences, and encounters with Mesopotamian deities (Inanna, Tiamat). Mesopotamian, Arabic, Persian, Turkish, and Mediterranean traditional music requiring exact microtonal intervals. No provider name is evidence of authentic maqam intonation or drift-free quarter tones. Google documents no pitch or tuning setting for Lyria 3.5, and none of its Lyria pages checked mentions microtones or maqam; the only scale control in any Lyria model is Lyria RealTime's 12-value `scale` field (`lyria.foundational_architecture`). So write the tuning directive into the prompt, audition whether the model holds the intervals in this prompt and take, and check the pitches in the result by ear or pitch detection without "correcting" the quarter tones (`song_handoff.vocal_tuning_audit`); where they are missing, record the line with a player (`audio_exec.music_tracking`).  
**Best in:** formats: Mythological Epic, Dark Fantasy, Heritage Documentary, Traditional Folk, World Music | genres: Mythology, Fantasy, Mystery, Heritage (civilisation-focused), World  
**Avoid when:** Contemporary corporate or upbeat commercial music. Standard Western pop music where strict 12-tone chromatic tuning is required.  
**Example:** `cue: Solo Ney flute slowly bending the neutral third down by 45 cents, hovering between minor and major.` — or, holding a maqam through a generated track: `prompt: "Enforce precise quarter-tone microtonal tuning on E-half-flat and B-half-flat in Maqam Bayati mode" — then check those pitches in the result.` That reading of Maqam Bayati differs from the one in `score.near_eastern_maqam_scales` and `maqam.bayati_river_mode`, `scale: Maqam Bayati on D: D, E-half-flat (quarter tone), F, G, A, B-flat, C, D.`, which has one quarter tone and a plain B-flat; both are attested, named forms of Bayati rather than a right-and-wrong pair -- the plain-B-flat form takes jins Nahawand on G (G, A, B-flat, C) as its upper tetrachord, the B-half-flat form takes jins Rast on G (G, A, B-half-flat, C).  
**Source:** vendor — Lyria prompt guide https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/lyria-prompt-guide and Lyria RealTime https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/realtime-music-generation , read 2026-09-23 (no microtone or maqam control documented). On the two Bayati readings: The Open University, OpenLearn, "Introduction to music theory 2: pitch and notation," section 8.3, "Intervals in Maqam bayati" (open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/introduction-music-theory-2-pitch-and-notation/content-section-8.3), checked 2026-09-23, gives both the B-flat and the B-half-flat readings as valid; MaqamWorld.com (maqamworld.com/en/maqam/bayati.php, checked 2026-09-23), built by Johnny Farraj, co-author with Sami Abu Shumays of *Inside Arabic Music*, Oxford University Press, 2019 -- names jins Nahawand on G (B-flat) and jins Rast on G (B-half-flat) as the two attested upper-jins options for Bayati's upper tetrachord. On the neutral intervals themselves: H. H. Touma, *The Music of the Arabs* (Amadeus Press, 1996), pp. 17-18 and 23, and the same OpenLearn section: the 24-tone division is a theoretical grid, and in performance the neutral pitch is not fixed at an exact quarter tone.  
**Shelf life:** provider capability snapshot recorded 2026-09-23; re-test microtonal output on the selected model before execution and at least every 30 days.  
**Yields to:** `score.near_eastern_maqam_scales` — The quarter tone must read authentic, not eerie.  
`score.microtonal_quarter_tone_tension`
