# Foley & Historical Cinematic Sound Design

26 cards. Tactile historical foley, acoustic material signatures of ancient civilisations, cinematic impact hits, and environmental sound effects.

---

### The Bed Before the Spot

**Also called:** the foley selector, bed before spot, is the place already carrying it  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `foley.` shelf. It does not describe a sound. It asks the three questions this shelf's own `Avoid when:` lines and pair graph keep asking — **what gives this sound away if it is wrong, is a larger soundscape card already carrying it, and does it follow a body, run on its own or land once** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. **The second question matters most and the pair field cannot ask it.** Most of this shelf's pairs point at a soundscape bed whose own text already names the foley card's sound, and a bed is not an alternative to a spot effect — it is the thing a spot effect sits inside. The field reads *instead of* where the reader meant *inside*, and this card says which is which. Every card cited here is cited by id.  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the track. It exists so that a spot effect is added because the camera is on the one thing making it, not because the card existed and the bed already underneath it was forgotten.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, one sentence each, before you open the shelf**. Question one splits the world's sounds from the film's own hits, and those are two decisions: the hits are chosen alongside the stings and risers of the shot-planning shelf and the score, and no world card on this shelf ever cites one. Question two is where most readers should stop: **if a larger soundscape card already names the sound and the camera is on the place, you do not need a card from this shelf — you need the bed**, and the foley card is lifted out of the bed only when the camera goes close on the one source. Question three tells you how the card you keep sits against the bed: a sound that follows a body rides on top of it while the body is in shot, a sound that runs lives inside it, a sound that lands breaks through it and is gone.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** **Your shot has both a place and an object in it.** The map hands you one card where the honest answer is a bed plus a spot effect, and nothing in the package tells you when to lift the spot effect out: the edges run from this shelf up to the beds and never once from a bed back down to an object. **Distrust question one's first value on any period subject.** Every anachronism line on this shelf guards against the modern world — nylon, rubber tyres, motorboats, church bells, plate armour — and not one guards against the wrong ancient century. What this map calls *right* is only *not modern*; the period is the canon's to settle, not the card's. `soundscape.foley_clay_tablets` carries a period gate that its twin `foley.cuneiform_tablet_foley` does not, and a reader who meets the twin first meets no gate at all. And avoid it for the film's own hits taken alone: the same moment is decided by `sound.` and `score.` cards, and a hit chosen here without looking there is chosen from half the candidates.  
**Example:** `[Selector — produces no cue. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the cue.]`  

---

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — What gives it away if it is wrong?**
Asked first because every `Avoid when:` line on this shelf answers it, and answers it one of exactly three ways. A made thing fails as an anachronism — *"Rubber tire rolls or modern engine sounds"* (`foley.war_chariot_creak`), *"Light, silky modern nylon swishes"* (`foley.ceremonial_wool_rustle`), *"Fast motorboat wakes or churning metal propellers"* (`foley.marsh_reed_boat`). A sound of the land or of a trade fails in the wrong setting — *"Lush coastal or forest scenes"* (`foley.desert_dust_devil`), *"Arid mountain fortresses"* (`foley.marsh_night_chorus`), *"Desert battlefields or desolate ruins"* (`foley.coppersmith_hammer`). The film's own hit fails on a moment too small for it — *"Quiet naturalist historical documentaries"* (`foley.cinematic_braam_hit`), *"Minor comedic or transactional transitions"* (`foley.sub_drop_50hz`), *"Casual scene cuts where the silence reads as an encoding error"* (`foley.acoustic_vacuum_drop`). The `Yields to:` field agrees: the setting cards pair with each other on setting — *"Wetland night instead of arid night"*, *"Arid locations, where a frog chorus is wrong"* — and the hits leave on register — *"Naturalist history, where a trailer braam reads cheap"*, *"Period documentary, where a glitch edit breaks authenticity."* The third value is a second decision sharing this file: its six cards never cite a world card, and no world card cites them.

**Axis 2 — Is a larger soundscape card already carrying it?**
The most repeated condition on the shelf, seven times over: *"Massed combat rather than a single blade"*, *"Massed chariot warfare rather than one cart"*, *"The waterway bed rather than one hull"*, *"The whole sanctuary rather than one strike"*, *"Whole-city background, not the artisan quarter"*, *"Full battle bed, which already carries the horns"*, *"The sanctuary bed already carries the brazier."* Read as the pair field reads them, they offer an alternative. Read against the cards they point at, they do not: in every one of the seven, the bed's own `What it is:` already names the source — *"bronze and iron blades"*, *"chariot wheels"*, *"war horns"*, *"mashoof boats"*, *"bronze ceremonial bells"*, *"frankincense coals in bronze braziers"*, *"coppersmith hammers clinking on anvils"*. An eighth goes further: `soundscape.foley_ancient_garments` has as its own `Example:` the wool-and-braided-fringe friction that is `foley.ceremonial_wool_rustle`'s whole content. Three more cards on this shelf say outright that they are the same sound written twice — *"The canon-gated version of the same foley"*, *"The same drop written on the SFX shelf"*, *"The same scroll-and-seal foley, era-gated."* So the question this shelf actually asks is not *which card instead* but **is the camera on the place, or on the one thing in it**.

**Axis 3 — Does it follow a body, run on its own, or land once?**
Asked last, and the fewest conditions carry it, but it is the one that tells you how the card you keep sits against the bed: *"Continuous desert wind rather than a whirlwind event"* (`foley.desert_dust_devil`), *"When the build must actually peak and stop"* (`foley.shepard_tone_riser`), *"Only the score drops; the world keeps running"* (`foley.acoustic_vacuum_drop`), *"Gentle dialogue scenes or continuous unbroken shots"* (`foley.reverse_cymbal_swell`'s own boundary), and from the shot-planning shelf, *"The vacuum precedes an impact, not a cut"* (`sound.silence_before`).

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is not on this shelf: it is the soundscape bed for the place, with `foley.mud_brick_footsteps` lifted out of it — and the first half is measured.** Asked where to fall back, this shelf answers off itself. Fourteen of its twenty `Yields to:` edges leave for `soundscape.`; two stay home, and those two are the night choruses pointing at each other. Eight of the fourteen land on a card that already carries the source's sound. **The shelf, asked twenty different ways where to go when the card is not enough, says *wider* — and wider is the bed.**

The measure had to be counted the hard way, because in-degree names a different card. `foley.acoustic_vacuum_drop` is the most-reached card here — three edges, from three families, on two distinct conditions: *"Everything drops, not the score alone"* and *"The drop to dead silence is the effect"* are one condition, *"The vacuum precedes an impact, not a cut"* is the other. It is still not the default. Every one of those edges comes from another shelf, from a reader who had already decided on silence and was being told to take more away; no foley card ever falls back to it. It is where the silence family enters this shelf, not where this shelf retreats to.

The second half is argued, not counted: `foley.mud_brick_footsteps` is filed as the only card that does its job, no place bed names it, and its own scene list — *"Street scenes, marketplace strolls, and temple approaches"* — walks through three of the places the beds describe. It is the spot effect the beds need most often and carry least.

**The one against it is `foley.boiling_pitch_cauldron`.** Every other war sound on this shelf is the battle happening — the blade striking, the arrow landing, the horn blowing, the chariot rolling, the armour marching — and three of those five are already inside the battle bed. This one is the battle not yet begun: a pot simmering on a wall with nobody touching it, before the picture has shown what it is for. Nothing routes to it, it routes nowhere, no bed carries it, and it is the only war card on the shelf that runs instead of landing. The default puts the audience in the place; this puts them one scene ahead of it. Its cost is written in its own `Avoid when:` — the simmer is a kitchen unless the picture says siege — so it needs one shot that says what the pot is for, and the scene it names is a period claim for the canon to settle, not this card. Its near cousin against the grain is `foley.palace_cedar_door`, also outside the pair graph altogether, which is the sound of a room before the room is seen.

### Bronze vs Steel Blade Clatter

**Also called:** ancient blade impact, bronze weapon timbre  
**What it is:** Synthesizing the distinct acoustic timbre of bronze weapons (lower, heavier, duller thud with brief 800Hz ring) vs modern steel (sharp, high-frequency 4kHz scrape).  
**Effect on the audience:** Subconsciously authenticates Bronze Age warfare, distinguishing genuine ancient history from generic Hollywood medieval clanging.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Sword fights, armor impacts, and weapon handling in Sumerian, Akkadian, and Egyptian battles.  
**Best in:** formats: Historical Epic, Animation, Documentary | genres: War, Historical, Heritage  
**Avoid when:** Medieval knight battles or modern warfare.  
**Example:** `foley: Heavy, thick bronze sickle-sword (khopesh) strikes leather shield: dull low thud (180Hz) with short bronze resonance.`  
**Yields to:** `soundscape.ancient_battlefield` — Massed combat rather than a single blade.

### Sun-Dried Mud-Brick Footsteps

**Also called:** adobe footsteps, ancient street foley, dried clay ground  
**What it is:** The acoustic signature of leather sandals or bare feet moving across dried alluvial clay: dry crunches, light dust puff, and hollow clay tap.  
**Effect on the audience:** Transports the listener directly onto the streets of ancient Ur, Babylon, or Eridu.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Street scenes, marketplace strolls, and temple approaches across Mesopotamia.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage Documentary, Historical Drama | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), History  
**Avoid when:** Wet modern asphalt sounds or hollow wooden floorboards in mud-brick environments.  
**Example:** `foley: Leather sole on baked clay dust: dry, crisp crunch with fine grit scatter (decay 150ms).`  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; set the decay by ear against the recorded footstep.

### Sub-Drop 50Hz Revelation Detonation

**Also called:** bass boom, sub-drop impact, seismic revelation  
**What it is:** A pure sub-bass sine drop sweeping rapidly from 80Hz down to 35Hz, decayed over 1.2 seconds with no high-frequency clutter. The endpoints are a design choice, not a standard: check the drop on the speakers the piece will play on, which may reproduce little of it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Delivers a heavy, non-audible physical punch in the chest; underlines a devastating plot reveal or tomb discovery.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The moment the royal seal is broken, a traitor is unmasked, or the burial chamber is unsealed.  
**Best in:** formats: Mystery, Thriller, Feature Documentary | genres: Mystery, History, Thriller  
**Avoid when:** Minor comedic or transactional transitions.  
**Example:** `sfx: 80Hz to 35Hz sine wave pitch drop, length: 1200ms, nothing above it in the spectrum. It needs headroom to land in — how much the mix gives way for it is set in the edit.`  
**Source:** reference — checked 2026-09-23: a sub drop is a sine that falls in pitch inside the sub-bass band, below about 60 to 70 Hz (Wikipedia, *Sub-bass*; Morphic, *Sub drop sound effects*, which adds that small speakers reproduce little down there); no reference fixes its start or end point, so the figures here are a design choice.  
**Yields to:** `soundscape.sfx_sub_bass_hit` — The same drop written on the SFX shelf.  
`foley.sub_drop_50hz`

### Shepard Tone Infinite Tension Riser

**Also called:** infinite pitch rise, acoustic escalator, anxiety riser  
**What it is:** An auditory illusion consisting of overlapping sine wave scales separated by octaves that appear to rise infinitely in pitch without getting louder.  
**Effect on the audience:** Generates intolerable subconscious panic, claustrophobia, and mounting dread; the brain feels disaster is seconds away.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Countdown sequences, escaping a burning palace, and ticking clocks before enemy siege rams strike.  
**Best in:** formats: Psychological Thriller, Horror Short, Action Climax | genres: Thriller / Suspense, War  
**Avoid when:** Relaxed educational explainers or celebratory celebrations.  
**Example:** `sfx: 15-second Shepard scale rising continuously beneath high-speed horse gallop sound design.`  
**Yields to:** `sound.riser` — When the build must actually peak and stop.  
`foley.shepard_tone_riser`

### Stutter Audio Impact Edit

**Also called:** stutter cut audio, gate chop, adrenaline glitch  
**What it is:** Chopping an impact sound or screaming line into 1/16th or 1/32nd rhythmic slices synchronized with fast visual strobe cuts.  
**Effect on the audience:** Shatters visual continuity and induces disorientation, simulating the concussive shock of a blast or heavy blow.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Physical explosions, collapsing temple columns, or protagonist psychological shocks.  
**Best in:** formats: Action Short, Modern Trailer, Music Video | genres: Action, Sci-Fi, Psychological  
**Avoid when:** Traditional period documentaries aiming for museum authenticity.  
**Example:** `sfx: 32nd-note audio gate chop applied to battle roar sound effect across 12 frames of high-contrast visual flashes.`  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; set the chop length against the picture cut.  
**Yields to:** `sound.subjective` — Period documentary, where a glitch edit breaks authenticity. And `safety.photosensitive_flash` — the high-contrast flash run the audio chops against; that card owns the rate and where it stops.  
`foley.stutter_audio_impact`

### Sudden Acoustic Vacuum Drop

**Also called:** the pre-shock vacuum, silence before blast, air suck drop  
**What it is:** Taking *everything* out — music, ambience and foley together — immediately before a catastrophic explosion or strike, so the detonation lands into a vacuum. The gain in perceived size comes from the contrast, not from the level of the hit: the explosion sounds enormous because the ear was straining a moment earlier. **The length of the vacuum is set on the timeline, in the edit**, against the actual cut and the actual hit.  
**Effect on the audience:** Causes the viewer's ears to strain inward during the vacuum; makes the explosion feel far larger than it measures, with no extra level.  
**Used for and where it works best:** City walls collapsing, assassination strikes, and explosive revelations. Not the same move as `score.pre_climax_score_vacuum`, which takes out the score and leaves the world running, or `sound.silence_before`, where the silence precedes a **cut** rather than an impact.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Cinematic Trailer, Action Short | genres: Action, Thriller, War  
**Avoid when:** Casual scene cuts where the silence reads as an encoding error rather than as tension.  
**Example:** `handover: everything out before the detonation, then the hit on the exact frame. Find the length in the edit — long enough to be felt, short enough that nobody checks their connection.`  
**Yields to:** `score.pre_climax_score_vacuum` — Only the score drops; the world keeps running.  
`foley.acoustic_vacuum_drop`
