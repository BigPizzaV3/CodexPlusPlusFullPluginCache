## `phase-01-story-generation/cat-expo-3.md`

*Lines 334–334:*

**12 cards** in this file. Running total for category `expo`: **72** — 32 + 28 + 12.

*Lines 338–338:*

**Item 10 is already written, three times, and better.** The brief asked for the card that the season-two problem generalises into — a reader or viewer returning after months. The database already holds the whole of it for narrative: `info.between_episode_memory` carries the decay model, and it is a real one (emotional states survive a gap, names decay, plot mechanics collapse, and binge audiences invert the middle two); `expo.season_two_reteach` carries the delivery, including the rule that you re-teach only the rules the new season will bend; `expo.re_entry_exposition` carries the weekly form. A fourth narrative card would have been a duplicate with a different name. What is genuinely absent is the *non-narrative* case — the reader who did half a procedure six months ago — and that is written here as `expo.lapsed_learner_re_entry`, which points at all three rather than competing with them. The useful finding is that the sorting rule inverts between the two: a story audience loses facts and keeps feeling; an instruction reader loses state and keeps the concept.

*Lines 340–340:*

**Item 9 is exposition, not rhythm, and the answer turned out to be sharper than the question.** Writing to a decodable or levelled specification is not primarily a constraint on sentence music — it is a constraint on *subject*. If the phonics sequence you are writing to has not reached long vowels, you cannot write about the sea, and the discovery that your subject is unwriteable arrives at draft three. That is a delivery problem and it belongs here, as `expo.decodability_spec`. The rhythm half of it — the sentence-length ceiling and what the mandated repetition does to the ear — is genuinely a rhythm matter and is flagged here for whoever holds that file; the two cards would not overlap.
