# Mesopotamian & Arabic Maqam Craft & Poetic Forms

25 cards. Traditional modal scales (Bayati, Hijaz, Saba, Rast), Iraqi folk poetic structures (Mawal, Darami, Abudiyya), classical Arabic meters, and authentic historical instruments.

---

### Name the Era Before the Mode

**Also called:** the maqam selector, era before mode, what may this piece claim
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `maqam.` shelf. It does not describe a sound. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **how far back must this sound be able to stand and what may you say about the evidence, does the card set the clock or take it from something else, and how many people are making the noise** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were not invented: they were read off the shelf's `Yields to:` conditions and cross-checked line by line against `Avoid when:`, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id. **This card routes. It does not check a claim, and on a heritage shelf that distinction is the whole risk.**
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so that the card that does reach the screen was chosen against the period, the clock and the room, rather than picked because it is the Iraqi sound everybody already has in their head.
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf.** Answer one first, because on a history piece the user wants realistic it is the only one that can disqualify a card outright — a mode written for a modern screen cannot be made to stand in Bronze Age antiquity by playing it on an old instrument; on any other piece it only ranks the shortlist. Two and three then leave a shortlist of two to five. Where axis one's answer is *deep antiquity*, **read the chosen card's own body first, before you write a word of narration**: on this shelf two cards carry instructions about what may be said that outrank anything a map can tell you. Where two eras fight for one cue, that is two cues.
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all
**Avoid when:** **The subject is Assyrian or Syriac rather than Iraqi-Arabic.** This map will answer anyway and it should not; `assyrian.` and `assyrian_exp.` govern the living folk and liturgical traditions there, and `assyrian.beth_gazo_eight_modes` records in its own words that putting one tradition's material in another's book "is the commonest error on this material and it is invisible to anyone who does not already know." **Ancient Assyrian music is the exception**: neither shelf covers the empire's palace or cult music, so its instruments come from the canon — [`grimoire-16-neo-assyrian.md`](../phase-07-image-prompt-engineering/mesopotamia-canon/grimoire-16-neo-assyrian.md) heading 6, the Music bullet — and its mode from this map's deep-antiquity value. Also **distrust axis one whenever your honest answer is its middle value** — more than two thirds of the shelf lives there, so on a documented-period brief this map has two questions left and is doing a fraction of the work it does on a brief that is genuinely ancient or genuinely modern. **And do not read a route as a ruling.** Several cards on this shelf state a history in their own words; this map hands those cards over exactly as written and settles nothing about them. Where a card's own text says what you may claim — *name whose reconstruction* — the card outranks the map. Finally, avoid it when you are matching an existing recording or a supplied reference track: there the mode is heard off the record rather than chosen from a shelf.
**Example:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — How far back must this sound be able to stand, and what may you say about the evidence?**
Asked first, because it is the condition the shelf repeats most: nine of its `Yields to:` lines turn on period and on what the piece is entitled to claim — *"The track needs European tuning, no neutral second"* (`maqam.bayati_river_mode`), *"Modern screen drama with no augmented second"* (`maqam.hijaz_desert_majesty`), *"Raw indigenous microtonal folk colour wanted"* (`maqam.nahawand_east_west_bridge`), *"A Bronze Age museum reconstruction piece"* (`maqam.kurd_modern_melancholy`), *"A grimy urban street track"* (`maqam.santur_hammered_dulcimer`), *"A smooth, glossy crossover ballad"* (`maqam.joza_spike_fiddle`), *"Futuristic or electronic pop"* (`maqam.chalghi_baghdadi_ensemble`), *"A Bronze Age documentary"* (`maqam.modern_iraqi_hiphop`), and — the sharpest of them, because it is not about taste at all — *"An antiquity prologue that cannot name whose reconstruction"* (`maqam.hurrian_hymn_reconstruction`). **That last condition is an evidence test wearing the clothes of a style note, and it is why this axis has a third value rather than two.**

**Axis 2 — Does this card set the clock, or does it take the clock from something else?**
Seven conditions turn on time-shape and they are stated as facts, not feelings: *"A locked beat is needed from the first second"* (`maqam.iraqi_mawal_improvisation`), *"Grief must be held unbroken"* (`maqam.iraqi_bosta_refrain`), *"The cue must turn to celebration"* (`maqam.saba_profound_sorrow`), *"Short-form where four lines will not fit"* (`maqam.abudiyya_sorrowful_poetry`), *"A long narrative ode, not a ten-second hook"* (`maqam.iraqi_darami_couplet`), *"Modern free verse that resists strict feet"* (`maqam.classical_meter_basit`), *"Courtly banquet demanding strict rhyming couplets"* (`maqam.tafila_modern_epic`). The axis has a fourth value that no condition names and the shelf's shape forces: **half the cards here set a colour and no clock at all**, and take their time-shape from whichever of the other three you also chose.

**Axis 3 — One player, the ensemble, or the orchestra?**
Four conditions are about forces rather than mood: *"The temple scene wants monumental awe, not trance"* (`maqam.sikah_mystical_mode`), *"Claustrophobic horror or chaotic panic"* (`maqam.rast_royal_authority`), *"A fast, aggressive military charge"* (`maqam.ney_reed_flute`), and *"A temple scene needing ambience, not a forty-voice choir"* (`maqam.polyphonic_temple_chant`), which is the only one on the shelf that names the count of bodies as the reason. It is asked last because it never disqualifies — it only narrows.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `maqam.bayati_river_mode`, played by `maqam.acoustic_oud_iraqi_school` — and the in-degree count does not produce it, which is the point.** The most-yielded-to card on this shelf is `maqam.hurrian_hymn_reconstruction`, reached three times — from `maqam.kurd_modern_melancholy` (*"A Bronze Age museum reconstruction piece"*), from `maqam.modern_iraqi_hiphop` (*"A Bronze Age documentary"*) and from a card on the rhyme shelf (*"Monolingual historical purist reconstruction"*). **All three fire on one condition: the piece has gone historical-purist.** That is a period escape hatch, not a fallback, and making it the default would send every ordinary Iraqi cue to a card that spends most of its body telling you what you are not allowed to say. Nothing else on the shelf is reached by more than two distinct conditions. So the default is argued rather than counted, and the argument is the shelf's own text: **Bayati is the mode two other cards reach for inside their own Example lines** — `maqam.santur_hammered_dulcimer` sets its example "in Maqam Bayati", `maqam.iraqi_mawal_improvisation` sets its example "in Maqam Bayati" — and `maqam.acoustic_oud_iraqi_school` is one of only two cards here whose `Best in:` reads *All Near Eastern music, genres All*, with an `Avoid when:` that is a production-quality note rather than a scene condition. It is the thing everything else is already assuming.

**The one against it is `maqam.sikah_mystical_mode`.** Nothing routes to it; it is named by no `Yields to:` anywhere in the package; and it is the only mode here whose tonic is itself a quarter-tone, which makes it the shelf's maximum refusal of the ear the default is quietly flattering. Bayati on an oud is warm, tonal and instantly loved — and "universally beloved" is its own card's phrase, which is another way of saying it is the sound an Iraqi heritage cue reaches for by reflex. Sikah does the opposite in its own words: it "disorients standard Western expectations." Its stated cost is that it is unmetered and gives you no groove, which a short beat simply pays. Its near cousin against the grain is `maqam.abudiyya_sorrowful_poetry`, likewise reached by nothing, whose whole form is a three-way pun the audience either catches or does not.

**The unasked**

**Seven of the reachable cards are the target of no `Yields to:` field anywhere in the package.** They can be chosen but nothing ever sends you to them. By id:

`maqam.rast_royal_authority` · `maqam.sikah_mystical_mode` · `maqam.abudiyya_sorrowful_poetry` · `maqam.acoustic_oud_iraqi_school` · `maqam.santur_hammered_dulcimer` · `maqam.joza_spike_fiddle` · `maqam.chalghi_baghdadi_ensemble`

**And the shape of that list is the finding, not its length.** Four of the seven are the instruments and the ensemble — the oud, the santur, the joza and the Chalghi — and the fifth instrument on the shelf, `maqam.ney_reed_flute`, is reached exactly once, **from `assyrian_exp.circular_breathing_zurna`, which is on a different shelf.** So: **no card on `maqam.` routes a reader to any instrument on `maqam.`, and the instrument layer's only inbound edge comes from outside the family.** The traffic runs the other way and it runs hard — the santur yields to a hip-hop cadence, the joza to a crossover mode, the Chalghi to a hip-hop cadence, the ney to a war-drum card on the score shelf. **The instruments are write-only.** The readers who wrote these fields were each holding a mode or a verse form and asking *what else could carry this feeling*; nobody was holding an instrument and asking *what else could play this line*. A player is not an alternative to a scale, and the pair field cannot say so.

The second thing the list shows: **`maqam.rast_royal_authority` is unreached** — the card the shelf's own text calls "king of maqams" and "the basic reference scale of Near Eastern music". The shelf's named foundation is the shelf's own orphan, which is what happens when every pair is written as an escape from somewhere rather than a route to somewhere.

The new card this shelf wants is the one that sits between the two layers: **given a mode and a room, which instrument should carry the line, and which one will fight it.** Nothing here answers that in either direction. The nearest thing that exists is `maqam.chalghi_baghdadi_ensemble`, which names five players and the order they sit in — and nothing routes a reader to it to read that.

### Maqam Bayati: The Soul of the Twin Rivers

**Also called:** Bayati, مقام البيات, the river mode, homeland melody  
**What it is:** The most iconic musical mode in Iraq and the Near East: begins on D, features a neutral second (E-half-flat), conveying deep serenity, warmth, and nostalgic longing.  
**Effect on the audience:** Evokes the tranquil Euphrates at sunset, ancient wisdom, and deep familial comfort; universally beloved across the Orient.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Heritage documentary themes, folk songs of the river valleys, and wise reflective ballads.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage Documentary, Folk Ballad, Cinematic Score | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Folk, World  
**Avoid when:** Frenetic modern electronic bangers with European tuning.  
**Example:** `maqam_spec: "Maqam Bayati on D (D, E-half-flat, F, G, A, Bb, C, D), 84 BPM, solo acoustic Oud and Ney flute, warm and contemplative".`  
**Source:** assumption — the 84 BPM and the instrumentation in the example are a working illustration for a contemplative theme, not a measured or published figure; the scale is the common notation of Bayati on D and was not checked against a source for this card.  
**Yields to:** `maqam.nahawand_east_west_bridge` — The track needs European tuning, no neutral second.  
`maqam.bayati_river_mode`

### Maqam Hijaz: Desert Majesty & Sacred Passion

**Also called:** Hijaz, مقام الحجاز, desert grandeur, sacred fire  
**What it is:** Highly dramatic mode featuring an augmented second interval between the second and third degrees (D, Eb, F#, G), evoking sacred awe and exotic mystery.  
**Effect on the audience:** Sends shivers of awe down the spine; evokes monumental desert horizons, ancient temples, and intense spiritual passion.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Desert caravan journeys, temple rituals, dramatic revelations, and epic cinematic trailers.  
**Best in:** formats: Cinematic Feature, Trailer, Epic Animation | genres: Epic, Mythology, Adventure  
**Avoid when:** Light, bouncy children's nursery rhymes.  
**Example:** `maqam_spec: "Maqam Hijaz on D (D, Eb, F#, G, A, Bb, C, D), 96 BPM, majestic brass, heavy frame drums, and soaring solo female vocalise".`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.kurd_modern_melancholy` — Modern screen drama with no augmented second.  
`maqam.hijaz_desert_majesty`

### Maqam Saba: The Mode of Profound Sorrow

**Also called:** Saba, مقام الصبا, weeping mode, ultimate lament  
**What it is:** The saddest and most emotionally devastating mode in oriental music theory: features a diminished fourth and weeping microtonal pulls.  
**Effect on the audience:** Triggers uncontrollable tears and deep spiritual catharsis; sounds like an ancient mother weeping over ruins.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Funerals of kings, mourning fallen cities (fall of Nineveh or Babylon), and tragic character deaths.  
**Best in:** formats: Historical Tragedy, Memorial Feature, War Film | genres: Tragedy, History, Drama  
**Avoid when:** Joyous celebrations or upbeat commercial advertisements.  
**Example:** `maqam_spec: "Maqam Saba on D (D, E-half-flat, F, Gb, A, Bb, C, D), 68 BPM, solitary weeping Kamancheh / Joza, heavy heart-breaking pathos".`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.iraqi_bosta_refrain` — The cue must turn to celebration.  
`maqam.saba_profound_sorrow`

### Maqam Rast: Royal Authority & Moral Balance

**Also called:** Rast, مقام الرست, king of maqams, the foundation mode  
**What it is:** The basic reference scale of Near Eastern music: features neutral thirds and sevenths, radiating dignity, justice, royal sovereignty, and calm joy.  
**Effect on the audience:** Communicates undisputed moral authority, regal justice, and civilizational prosperity; feels balanced and grounded.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Royal coronations, lawgiver decrees (Hammurabi), and grand state pageants.  
**Best in:** formats: Historical Epic, State Anthem, Cultural Documentary | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), History  
**Avoid when:** Claustrophobic horror or chaotic panic scenes.  
**Example:** `maqam_spec: "Maqam Rast on C (C, D, E-half-flat, F, G, A, B-half-flat, C), 108 BPM, noble full Arabic orchestra, regal Oud and Qanun unisons".`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.kurd_modern_melancholy` — Claustrophobic horror or chaotic panic.  
`maqam.rast_royal_authority`

### Maqam Nahawand: Bridge Between East & West

**Also called:** Nahawand, مقام النهاوند, the harmonic minor bridge  
**What it is:** Corresponds closely to the Western harmonic minor scale (C, D, Eb, F, G, Ab, B, C), offering romantic elegance and accessible drama.  
**Effect on the audience:** Appeals effortlessly to Western and global ears while maintaining authentic oriental romantic passion.  
**Used for and where it works best:** International film scores, crossover ballads, and cinematic love themes.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Romantic Ballad, Global Documentary | genres: Romance, Drama, Epic  
**Avoid when:** Seeking raw, unapologetic indigenous microtonal folk flavor.  
**Example:** `maqam_spec: "Maqam Nahawand on C (C minor natural/harmonic), 88 BPM, lush romantic cello melody doubled by acoustic Oud and classical harp".`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.bayati_river_mode` — Raw indigenous microtonal folk colour wanted.  
`maqam.nahawand_east_west_bridge`

### Maqam Ajam: The Bright Major Mode for Children's Songs

**Also called:** Ajam, مقام العجم, the major-scale maqam, the sing-along mode  
**What it is:** The maqam built from two identical ajnas, jins Ajam on the first degree and jins Ajam again on the fifth, each one whole tone, whole tone, half tone. On C it runs C, D, E, F, G, A, B, C: the step pattern of the Western major scale, with no quarter-tone degree anywhere in it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Reads as open and bright to ears raised on major-key nursery songs and cartoon themes, so a child can sing it back on first hearing without meeting an interval they have not heard before. The Arabic colour comes from the language, the rhythm and the instruments, not from the scale.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Arabic children's songs meant to be sung back: teaching songs, alphabet and counting songs, celebration refrains, the theme of a kids' series. Because no degree is a quarter tone, the melody can be doubled on a keyboard, a glockenspiel or a toy xylophone without retuning, which matters when the video shows a child playing along. Tempo belongs to `genre.kids_teaching_tempo`; this card only chooses the mode.  
**Best in:** formats: Kids Content, Educational, Animated Series, Music Video | genres: Children's, Educational, Celebration  
**Avoid when:** The song has to sound Iraqi or Near Eastern from its first bar on melody alone. Ajam carries no quarter tone, so without the language, rhythm or instruments to carry the region it is heard as a plain major-key song.  
**Example:** `maqam_spec: "Maqam Ajam on C (C, D, E, F, G, A, B, C), bright children's sing-along, oud and hand claps doubling the tune, riq on the downbeat, refrain identical at every return".`  
**Source:** reference — Al-Ghawanmeh, Jensenius and Smaïli, "Arab music improvisation corpus for research (AMICOR): development and machine translation experiments", Language Resources and Evaluation 60:36 (2026), https://doi.org/10.1007/s10579-026-09905-z, checked 2026-09-24; supports maqam ajam notated with its tonic on C, its two main ajnas both jins ajam on the first and fifth degrees, and the jins ajam intervals whole-whole-half (CDEF and GABC). That this is the major scale's pattern follows from those intervals; using it for children's songs is the package's craft choice, not a finding of the paper.  
**Yields to:** `maqam.bayati_river_mode` — The children's song must sound Iraqi from its first note, on melody alone.  
`maqam.ajam_bright_major_mode`

### Maqam Sikah: Mystical Transcendence

**Also called:** Sikah, مقام السيكاه, the third degree mode, mystical light  
**What it is:** A mode built entirely starting on a quarter-tone note (E-half-flat), radiating spiritual mysticism, philosophical detachment, and ancient prayer.  
**Effect on the audience:** Disorients standard Western expectations; opens the mind into a meditative, dream-like spiritual trance.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Ziggurat summits, astrological stargazing scenes, and ancient priestly divinations.  
**Best in:** formats: Mystical Epic, Religious Documentary, Ambient Score | genres: Mythology, Faith / Biblical Epic  
**Avoid when:** Fast commercial jingles or modern sports hype videos.  
**Example:** `maqam_spec: "Maqam Sikah on E-half-flat, unmetered mystical Ney flute improvisation over low resonant drone bowl, sacred and otherworldly".`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.hijaz_desert_majesty` — The temple scene wants monumental awe, not trance.  
`maqam.sikah_mystical_mode`

### Maqam Kurd: Melancholic Modern Epic

**Also called:** Kurd, مقام الكرد, Phrygian mode, modern melancholy  
**What it is:** Near Eastern mode built with a flat second (D, Eb, F, G, A, Bb, C, D) without quarter-tones, popular in modern Arabic epic drama and cinema.  
**Effect on the audience:** Conveys dark suspense, tragic heroism, and modern cinematic emotional momentum.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Thriller sequences, modern patriotic ballads, and action-drama crossovers.  
**Best in:** formats: Cinematic Action, Thriller Short, Modern Anthem | genres: Action, Thriller, Drama  
**Avoid when:** Bronze Age museum pieces, where the cue is one named scholar's reconstruction rather than a modern mode.  
**Example:** `maqam_spec: "Maqam Kurd on D, 116 BPM, driving cinematic percussion, electric cello and acoustic Oud playing urgent ostinato".`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.hurrian_hymn_reconstruction` — A Bronze Age museum reconstruction piece.  
`maqam.kurd_modern_melancholy`

### The Iraqi Mawal (الموال): Unmetered Vocal Heroics

**Also called:** Mawal, الموال البغدادي, unmetered vocal improvisation  
**What it is:** A traditional solo vocal improvisation delivered in free rhythm (rubato) displaying the singer's maximal vocal range, ornamentation, and poetic grief.  
**Effect on the audience:** Halts all mundane action; the audience sits spellbound by the sheer athletic and emotional power of the human voice.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The prelude (intro) to major epic songs, funeral laments, and solitary night reflections.  
**Best in:** formats: Cultural Feature, Heroic Epic, Traditional Song | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Folk, Drama  
**Avoid when:** Fast dance tracks where a strict locked beat is required from second one.  
**Example:** `style: "Iraqi Mawal in Maqam Bayati, unmetered solo male tenor singing passionate classical poetry, acoustic Oud providing soft harmonic chords".`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.iraqi_bosta_refrain` — A locked beat is needed from the first second.  
`maqam.iraqi_mawal_improvisation`

### The Iraqi Bosta (البستة): Upbeat Folk Refrain

**Also called:** Bosta, البستة, post-mawal dance, communal chorus  
**What it is:** The lively, rhythmic, metric folk song (usually 2/4 or 4/4) that immediately follows a heavy Mawal to release tension and invite group clapping.  
**Effect on the audience:** Releases built-up emotional pressure with joyful communal relief and rhythmic hand-clapping.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Transitioning from sad historical reflection into festive community celebration.  
**Best in:** formats: Folk Single, Cultural Music Video | genres: Folk, Upbeat  
**Avoid when:** Tragic scenes where grief must be maintained unbroken.  
**Example:** `sequence: "[Mawal finishes on sustained high note] -> [Instant transition to lively 120 BPM Iraqi Bosta, wooden spoons, clay drums, upbeat chorus]".`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.saba_profound_sorrow` — Grief must be held unbroken.

### The Al-Ataba & Abudiyya (الأبوذية والعتابة) Poetic Form

**Also called:** Abudiyya, الأبوذية, 4-line sorrowful couplet, homonym rhyme  
**What it is:** A traditional four-line Iraqi folk poem where the first three lines end with the identical sounding word having three different meanings (Tajnis), ending in '-yeh'.  
**Effect on the audience:** Evokes immense admiration for poetic wit and overwhelming southern Mesopotamian marshland pathos.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Songs of heartbreak, exile, betrayal, and mourning the death of a hero.  
**Best in:** formats: Folk Ballad, Southern Iraqi Heritage | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Tragedy, Poetry  
**Avoid when:** Generic Western pop songs.  
**Example:** `lyric_spec: 4-line Abudiyya structure in Southern Iraqi dialect, sung with deep raspy vibrato in Maqam Saba.`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.iraqi_darami_couplet` — Short-form where four lines will not fit.

### The Iraqi Darami (الدارمي): 2-Line Condensed Epigram

**Also called:** Darami, الدارمي, marshland couplet, two-line spear  
**What it is:** A two-line rhyming folk poem (16 syllables per line) capable of expressing devastating romantic longing, satirical fury, or existential wit.  
**Effect on the audience:** Hits like a laser; delivers maximal emotional or comedic impact in fewer than 10 seconds of singing.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Fast social media reels, short-form musical hooks, and quick romantic banter songs.

### Free Verse & Taf'ila in Modern Epic Songcraft

**Also called:** Taf'ila verse, شعر التفعيلة, Sayyab cadence

### The Acoustic Oud: The Iraqi School

**Also called:** Al-Oud, العود العربي العراقي, floating bridge lute  
**What it is:** The pear-shaped fretless wooden lute, here in its Iraqi school — founded and taught by Sharif Muhyiddin Haidar at Baghdad's Institute of Fine Arts (its music department head, 1937–1948) and carried to international fame by his students, the brothers Munir Bashir and Jamil Bashir — featuring rich floating-bridge harmonics.  
**Effect on the audience:** Carries a sense of deep heritage - the popular claim that the oud reaches back 5,000 years is an attribution, not something a narration should state; evokes refined contemplation and melancholy warmth.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Solo intros, virtuosic Taqsim improvisations, and melodic song backbones.  
**Best in:** formats: All Near Eastern music | genres: All  
**Avoid when:** Cheap synth MIDI approximations that lack natural wood resonance.  
**Example:** `instrument_spec: "Solo acoustic Iraqi wooden Oud, tuned in C-F-A-D-G-C, virtuosic tremolo on high strings, warm resonant bass body".`

### The Santur: Iraqi Hammered Dulcimer

**Also called:** Santur, السنطور العراقي, trapezoid hammered zither  
**What it is:** Trapezoidal hammered dulcimer traditionally described with 72 to 100 steel and bronze strings — Encyclopaedia Iranica gives 72 strings for the (Persian) santur, and the higher end of this range reflects other descriptions of the larger Iraqi instrument that this card has not independently verified against a named academic or museum source — struck with delicate wooden mallets (Mizrab). Check stringing, range and construction against the actual instrument or player being recorded, and do not project a modern Iraqi santur specification unchanged into ancient Mesopotamia.  
**Effect on the audience:** Produces a shimmering, crystalline, cascading shower of celestial acoustic harmonics; sounds like sunlight dancing on water.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The classical Iraqi Maqam Chalghi ensemble, palace court scenes, and mythical dreamscapes.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage Documentary, Historical Drama, Classical Chalghi | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Classical  
**Avoid when:** Grimy urban street hip-hop tracks.  
**Example:** `instrument_spec: "Traditional Iraqi Santur, delicate walnut mallets striking bronze strings, crystalline sustained overtones in Maqam Bayati".`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.modern_iraqi_hiphop` — A grimy urban street track.

### The Mosul Maqam: What the Sources Support

**Also called:** Maslawi maqam, المقام الموصلي, the Mosul reader, maqam in Mosul  
**What it is:** Mosul is one of the cities of the Iraqi maqam, the urban, semi-improvised vocal art of a lead reader (qari') answered by the chalghi ensemble, found primarily in Baghdad, Mosul, Kirkuk and Basra. What a source opened today supports about Mosul in particular is short, and this card is that list. The maqam was heard across the city's daily life (vendors, kitchens, coffee houses, weddings, the public baths) and in calls to prayer and church choirs. Its living readers include Ramiz al-Rawi (also written Al-Rawwi), whom the University of Exeter's Mosul Maqam project calls the city's iconic maqam reader, and Fakhri Fadhil. Its surviving masters are few and mostly elderly, and after the ISIS attack of 2014 musicians were killed and imprisoned and the city's Maqam house was left in ruins.  
**Effect on the audience:** A Mosul scene that uses a named reader, the city's own speech and its real places reads as Mosul; the same scene with a generic "oriental" cue reads as anywhere.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Mosul-set drama and documentary, a Maslawi elder who sings, a coffee-house or wedding scene in the old city, a church-choir or call-to-prayer moment where the maqam crosses communities. The instruments are those of the Iraqi maqam (santur, the jawza spike fiddle, the dumbak goblet drum, the daff or riq), and none of the sources consulted names a mode or instrument that belongs to Mosul and not to Baghdad, so a Mosul scene takes its ensemble from `maqam.chalghi_baghdadi_ensemble` and carries the city through its reader, its dialect (`voice.cast_regional_iraqi_dialect`) and its places. On dating, say the origin is unknown: the Exeter release dates Mosul's maqam to 750, while the Cleveland Museum of Art essay says the beginning of the Iraqi maqam is unknown, with theories running from the Ottomans to the Abbasids to the ancient civilisations. On historical names, check which man is meant: the TDV Encyclopedia of Islam separates Osman Dede Efendi, a Mosul-born singer and composer of Istanbul's Mevlevi circles who died in 1920 (1918 in some sources), from the Osman al-Mawsili of an Arabic encyclopedia entry, and says the two are confused in the sources.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Drama Series, Feature Film, Music Video | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Drama, Music  
**Avoid when:** The scene hangs on a Mosul-only mode, instrument or song that none of these sources supplies; the Mosul Maqam project's archive or a living Mosul reader is the source for that, not this card.  
**Example:** `ensemble_spec: "Iraqi maqam as sung in Mosul: solo male reader in free rhythm on colloquial and classical verse, santur and jawza answering, dumbak and daff entering for the closing rhythmic song, coffee-house room"`

### The Hurrian Hymn & Ancient Sumerian Reconstructions

**Also called:** Hurrian Hymn No. 6, oldest known melody, Hymn to Nikkal  
**What it is:** Musical reconstruction of the cuneiform musical tablets discovered in Ugarit (circa 1400 BC), utilizing ancient diatonic modal tunings.  
**Effect on the audience:** Stuns the listener with the realization that they are hearing one scholar's reconstruction of the oldest written music we have.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Bronze Age archaeological documentaries, museum VR exhibits, and opening prologues of deep antiquity. **Say "a reconstruction", never "the melody".** What survives is a text with notation-like annotations naming intervals and numbers; how those annotations become pitches, which note of an interval is sung, whether the line is melody-with-accompaniment or something else, and the rhythm — none of that is given, and the published reconstructions differ from each other so much that they are audibly different pieces of music. There are several, by Kilmer, by Duchesne-Guillemin, by West and by others, and no consensus among them. So the honest framing, and the one this package's evidence rules require everywhere else, is *"one scholar's reconstruction of the oldest written music we have"* — which loses nothing, because the astonishing fact is the notation, not the tune. Name whose reconstruction you used, in the narration or at least in the credits. A film that says "this is what it sounded like" is making a claim no scholar makes.  
**Best in:** formats: Museum Audio, Archaeological Film, Epic Prologue | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Ancient History  
**Avoid when:** Commercial radio singles.  
**Example:** `reconstruction: "Hurrian Hymn No. 6, played on replica bronze age gut-string lyre in Nid Qabli mode, accompanying archaic sacred chant".`  
**Yields to:** `maqam.polyphonic_temple_chant` — An antiquity prologue that cannot name whose reconstruction.  
`maqam.hurrian_hymn_reconstruction`
