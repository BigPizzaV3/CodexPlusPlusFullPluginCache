# Assyrian & Syriac Songcraft, Rhythms & Liturgical Heritage

22 cards. Traditional Assyrian folk dance rhythms (Khigga, Bagiyeh, Sheikhani), Syriac liturgical chant traditions of both churches (the East Syriac Ḥudra, the West Syriac Beth Gazo, Madroshe), authentic instruments (Zurna, Davula), and Sureth phonetic directing for a song engine.

---

### Ask Whose Before What

**Also called:** the Assyrian selector, whose book is this, tradition before rhythm
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `assyrian.` shelf. It does not describe a sound. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **whose material is this and can the card name the source, what is the room doing, and how many people are making the noise** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were not invented: they were read off the shelf's `Yields to:` conditions and cross-checked line by line against `Avoid when:`, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. The first axis is not a courtesy. It is the fork the shelf's two most explicit cards were written to force, and they force it in both directions. Every card cited here is cited by id. **This card routes. It does not check a claim.**
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so that the card that does reach the screen was chosen against the community the piece is about, rather than against a mood board — and so that the commonest invisible error on this material gets caught before a choir is booked rather than after.
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf.** Answer one first and answer it about the subject, not about the sound: *which community, and which church if any.* If the brief does not say, **take it from the subject** — an Assyrian subject is the Church of the East — or search and choose, and record it under `decisions`; `assyrian.hudra_east_syriac` says in its own body that the answer changes the repertoire, which is why it is settled first. Two and three then leave a shortlist of one to four. Where two answers to axis one fight for one piece, that is two pieces: make the one the brief leans towards and record the choice under `decisions`.
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all
**Avoid when:** **The material is Iraqi-Arabic rather than Assyrian or Syriac** — `maqam.` governs there, and this map will answer anyway, which is exactly the failure axis one exists to prevent, pointed at itself. **And distrust this map whenever your honest answer to axis one is its fourth value**, the village and the diaspora stage: two thirds of the shelf lives there, so on an ordinary folk brief this card has two questions left and is doing a fraction of the work it does on a liturgical one. **Do not read a route as a ruling.** More than a third of the cards here state a history in their own words — a date, a lineage, an unbroken span of centuries — and this map hands those cards over exactly as written and settles nothing about them. Where a card's own text says what may be said, as `assyrian.beth_gazo_eight_modes` does when it requires a narration line to read *traditionally attributed to* rather than *composed by*, **the card outranks this map.** And avoid it entirely when you are matching an existing recording or working to a community's own supplied reference: there the repertoire is heard off the record and matched to it, not chosen from a shelf.
**Example:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — Whose material is this, and can the card name whose?**
Asked first, because it is the only condition on the shelf that can make a technically perfect choice wrong. Four `Yields to:` lines turn on it and two of them are the sharpest pair in the package, pointing at each other: *"The subject is the Assyrian Church of the East"* (`assyrian.beth_gazo_eight_modes`) and *"The subject is Syriac Orthodox or Maronite"* (`assyrian.hudra_east_syriac`). Two more split the same question along the sacred/secular line: *"Secular diaspora grief, not liturgical penitence"* (`assyrian.bauta_ninwaye_supplication`) and *"A church or liturgical mourning subject"* (`assyrian.ninevite_homeland_lament`). The `Avoid when:` fields carry it harder than the pair fields do — `assyrian.beth_gazo_eight_modes` spends half of its on the carve-out, and calls the error "the commonest error on this material and it is invisible to anyone who does not already know." **The axis has a third value because some cards name a tradition and no church, and that gap is the answer, not a missing answer.**

**Axis 2 — What is the room doing?**
Eight conditions turn on it and they are stated as facts about the scene, not feelings about the audience: *"A solemn funeral lament, not a circle dance"* (`assyrian.khigga_dance_rhythm`), *"Solemn documentary or contemplative moment"* (`assyrian.gubareh_high_speed`), *"Winter funeral or defeat scene"* (`assyrian.akitu_festival_anthem`), *"Harsh battle sequences"* (`assyrian.bagiyeh_graceful_dance`), *"War march or political mobilisation"* (`assyrian.belaty_romantic_cadence`), *"Delicate indoor courtly romance"* (`assyrian.sheikhani_martial_rhythm`), *"Slow reflective passage, not athletic footwork"* (`assyrian.siskani_footwork_rhythm`), *"Two opposing voices rather than a taught monologue"* (`assyrian.madroshe_teaching_hymn`).

**Axis 3 — How many people, and how loud may they be?**
Five conditions are about forces and the size of the room rather than mood: *"Quiet indoor or intimate chamber scene"* (`assyrian.zurna_davula_acoustic`), *"A small naturalist domestic scene"* (`assyrian.epic_choral_anthem`), *"A modern urban nightclub setting"* (`assyrian.kochari_mountain_stomp`), *"A busy crowded scene with loud instruments"* (`assyrian_exp.rawe_mountain_recitative`, reaching in from the neighbouring shelf) and *"High-speed reel transitions and rapid montage"* (`assyrian.khigga_yaqoora_heavy`). It is asked last because it never disqualifies — it only narrows.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `assyrian.khigga_dance_rhythm` — and it is argued, because on this shelf the count refuses to produce one.** Three cards tie for most-yielded-to at four inbound edges each, and all three collapse when you count conditions instead of edges. `assyrian.diaspora_folk_pop_fusion` is reached from `assyrian.kochari_mountain_stomp` (*"A modern urban nightclub setting"*), `assyrian.ancient_mesopotamian_harp` (*"Modern pop or synthesised dance track"*), `assyrian_exp.evin_agassi_storytelling` (*"A dance track with no story lyric"*) and `assyrian_exp.khabour_village_suite` (*"A modern urban club scene"*) — **four edges, one condition: the piece has gone modern and produced.** A format escape hatch. `assyrian.ninevite_homeland_lament` takes four edges of which three are the same condition — the scene has turned to defeat. `assyrian.ancient_mesopotamian_harp` takes four of which three are the same condition — the room got small. **Not one card on this shelf is reached by three different reasons**, so in-degree names three doors out and no floor. The argument for Khigga is the shelf's own text instead: its card calls it "the foundational" and "universal community rhythm"; **it is what the shelf's biggest structure collapses to when there is no room**, since `assyrian_exp.wedding_grand_finale_suite` yields to it on *"A 30-second cut: one rhythm, not a suite"*; and three other cards build their own Example lines on top of it by name — `assyrian.diaspora_folk_pop_fusion` at a Khigga rhythm, `assyrian_exp.george_chaharbakhshi_anthem` at a Khigga beat, `assyrian_exp.circular_breathing_zurna` over a Khigga Davula beat. It is what everything else is already standing on.

**The one against it is `assyrian.hudra_east_syriac`.** It inverts the default at every joint: the default is a crowd, a drum and a rhythm anyone recognises in one bar; this is an unaccompanied choir with no drum at all, in a repertoire most of the audience cannot name. And it inverts something more useful than texture — **it is the only card on the shelf that needs one decision before it gives a sound.** Its own body says: name the tradition before you name a mode, and where a brief says only *Syriac chant*, settle which church from the subject or a search. Every other card here will give you a sound on demand. This one asks for the church first, which the agent settles and records, and the work goes on. It also carries the package's own record of why it exists — "The package had no entry for it at all while carrying a full card for the other one" — which makes it the shelf correcting itself in public, and a default's proper opposite is the thing the default was hiding.

**The unasked**

**Five of the reachable cards are the target of no `Yields to:` field anywhere in the package.** They can be chosen but nothing ever sends you to them. By id:

`assyrian.kochari_mountain_stomp` · `assyrian.siskani_footwork_rhythm` · `assyrian.akitu_festival_anthem` · `assyrian.epic_choral_anthem` · `assyrian.sureth_phonetic_directing`

**And the shape of that list is the finding, not its length.** Two things are in it and they are unrelated to each other, which is why the list is worth reading rather than counting.

**First: both cards under Axis 2's *declaring* value are unreached, and they are the only two.** The shelf will route you out of a celebration — into a lament, into a small room, into a modern production — and it will route you between dances. It will not route you into a ceremony. `assyrian.akitu_festival_anthem` and `assyrian.epic_choral_anthem` yield outward and nothing yields inward, so the crowd, the anthem and the orchestra are reachable only by someone who already decided to go there. The pair graph was written by readers comparing two ways to feel something; a declaration is not a feeling, it is a function, and the pair field cannot express it.

**Second, and sharper: `assyrian.sureth_phonetic_directing` is reached by nothing.** It is the card that governs the language every vocal card on this shelf is sung in — and which script that language is written in, a point its own body has to make because the obvious answer is the wrong one. Every lament, every dance chorus, every liturgical line here presupposes it, and no card on the shelf sends a reader to it. **A card everything depends on and nothing points at is the exact shape of a rule that will be discovered late, on the day a model sings the wrong script back.**

The third thing the list shows: `assyrian.kochari_mountain_stomp` and `assyrian.siskani_footwork_rhythm` are both unreached while `assyrian.khigga_dance_rhythm`, `assyrian.khigga_yaqoora_heavy`, `assyrian.gubareh_high_speed`, `assyrian.sheikhani_martial_rhythm` and `assyrian.bagiyeh_graceful_dance` are all reached. **The pair graph knows tempo and it knows mood, and it does not know the step.** The new card this shelf wants is the one that chooses between two dances by what the feet are doing — which is the thing the community itself recognises first and the only thing none of these cards sorts on.

### Khigga Folk Dance Rhythm & Tempo

**Also called:** Khigga, ܚܓܐ, the Assyrian circle dance, universal community rhythm  
**What it is:** The foundational 2/4 or 6/8 driving rhythmic circle dance of the Assyrian people, characterized by a heavy downbeat and syncopated hand-clapping cadence.  
**Effect on the audience:** Triggers involuntary physical celebration, foot-tapping, and joyful communal unity; instantly recognizable to any Near Eastern audience.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Wedding scenes, harvest festivals, village celebrations, and high-energy cultural anthems.  
**Best in:** formats: Cultural Music Video, Folk Anthem, Wedding Suite | genres: Folk, Heritage (civilisation-focused), World  
**Avoid when:** Solemn funeral laments or intimate slow ballads.  
**Example:** `style: "Traditional Assyrian Khigga dance, 128 BPM 2/4 meter, driving Davula bass drum, piercing acoustic Zurna lead, lively handclaps, festive crowd chants".`  
**Yields to:** `assyrian.ninevite_homeland_lament` — A solemn funeral lament, not a circle dance.  
`assyrian.khigga_dance_rhythm`

### Khigga Yaqoora (The Heavy Dignified Khigga)

**Also called:** Khigga Yaqoora, ܚܓܐ ܝܩܘܪܐ, slow heavy khigga, noble step  
**What it is:** A slower, stately variation of the Khigga (88 to 96 BPM) with deep, resonant bass drum strokes and elongated melodic phrases.  
**Effect on the audience:** Imparts noble dignity, generational pride, and ancient mountain endurance rather than frantic speed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Opening village processions, elders entering celebrations, and patriotic cultural ballads.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage Film, Cultural Documentary, Folk Ballad | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Folk  
**Avoid when:** High-speed modern social media reel transitions.  
**Example:** `style: "Slow Assyrian Khigga Yaqoora, 92 BPM, deep ceremonial Davula thuds, mournful and proud acoustic Oud and Zurna harmonies, spacious reverb".`  
**Yields to:** `assyrian.gubareh_high_speed` — High-speed reel transitions and rapid montage.

### Sheikhani Mountain Martial Rhythm

**Also called:** Sheikhani, ܫܝܟܢܝ, the warrior dance, mountain cadence  
**What it is:** A 4/4 syncopated marching rhythm featuring distinctive sudden step-pauses and shoulder-shaking accents, originating in the rugged northern mountains.  
**Effect on the audience:** Evokes martial courage, defensive solidarity, and unbreakable mountain resilience.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Warrior mobilization, historical defense ballads, and mountain village festival scenes.  
**Best in:** formats: Epic Folk, Historical Feature, Action Anthem | genres: War, Folk, Heritage  
**Avoid when:** Delicate indoor courtly romance.  
**Example:** `style: "Assyrian Sheikhani folk dance, 114 BPM syncopated 4/4 rhythm, rhythmic wooden percussion, energetic zurna melody, masculine chorus shouts".`  
**Yields to:** `assyrian.bagiyeh_graceful_dance` — Delicate indoor courtly romance.

### Beth Gazo Eight-Mode Liturgical System

**Also called:** Beth Gazo, ܒܝܬ ܓܙܐ, Treasury of Chants, Syriac Octoechos  
**What it is:** The eight-mode liturgical chant treasury of the **West Syriac** tradition — Syriac Orthodox and Maronite — governing sacred hymns across the liturgical seasons. **It is not the book of every Syriac church, and this card used to say it was.** On the attribution: the hymn corpus is traditionally ascribed to Ephrem the Syrian and the eight-mode arrangement traditionally to Severus of Antioch, with the earliest solid descriptions far later — these are inherited attributions, not documented acts of codification, and a narration line should say *traditionally attributed to* rather than *composed by*.  
**Effect on the audience:** Imparts transcendental holiness, timeless antiquity, and cosmic peace; silences modern anxieties completely.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Ancient church sequences, candlelit monastery prayers, and sacred historical memorials.  
**Best in:** formats: Sacred Epic, Heritage Documentary, Historical Drama | genres: Faith / Biblical Epic, Heritage, Religious  
**Avoid when:** Commercial party songs or secular comedic sketches. **And on an Assyrian Church of the East subject** — use `assyrian.hudra_east_syriac` below instead; putting a choir of one tradition in the other's book is the commonest error on this material and it is invisible to anyone who does not already know.  
**Example:** `style: "Sacred Classical Syriac Beth Gazo chant, Qinto 1 mode, unhurried monastic male choir singing in unison, ancient stone cathedral reverb".`  
**Yields to:** `assyrian.hudra_east_syriac` — The subject is the Assyrian Church of the East.  
`assyrian.beth_gazo_eight_modes`

### The Ḥudra — the East Syriac Liturgical Cycle

**Also called:** Ḥudra, Khudra, ܚܘܕܪܐ, "the cycle", the Church of the East liturgical book
**What it is:** **The principal liturgical book of the Church of the East** — the Assyrian tradition — carrying the variable chants of the divine office and the Qurbana across the whole liturgical year. Its compilation is traditionally attributed to Patriarch Išoʿyahb III of Adiabene (648–658) with the monk ʿEnanishoʿ. **This is the book an Assyrian choir actually sings from**, and it is a different repertoire from the West Syriac Beth Gazo above — related tradition, different church, different melodies, different book. The package had no entry for it at all while carrying a full card for the other one.
**Effect on the audience:** For the community it is recognition — the difference between a piece made *about* them and a piece made *with* them, and it is audible in the first phrase. For everyone else it is the same weight of antiquity the other tradition carries.
**Used for and where it works best:** Any Assyrian Church of the East subject: a choir, a feast, a church interior, a diaspora commemoration, a heritage documentary about the Assyrians specifically rather than about Syriac Christianity generally. **Name the tradition before you name a mode.** Where a brief says only "Syriac chant", settle which church before choosing — from the brief's subject (an Assyrian subject is this church), or by a search — and record it under `decisions`, because the answer changes the repertoire.
**Best in:** formats: Heritage Documentary, Sacred Epic, Concert / Live Event, Historical Drama | genres: Faith / Biblical Epic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Religious
**Avoid when:** The subject is Syriac Orthodox or Maronite — `assyrian.beth_gazo_eight_modes` governs there.
**Prompt:** `East Syriac Ḥudra chant, unaccompanied male choir in unison, East Syriac liturgical pronunciation verified against the named church or source recording, stone church acoustic, unhurried`
**Yields to:** `assyrian.beth_gazo_eight_modes` — The subject is Syriac Orthodox or Maronite.  
`assyrian.hudra_east_syriac`

### Madroshe Teaching Hymnody, Traditionally Attributed to St. Ephrem

**Also called:** Madrasha, ܡܕܪܫܐ, Syriac doctrinal ode, theological hymn  
**What it is:** Metrical, strophic teaching poems set to music, featuring profound theological imagery and alternating choral refrains. The form is traditionally attributed to Ephrem the Syrian; a narration line should say *traditionally attributed to*, not *composed by*. *Madrasha* names a genre, so the label alone does not make a given hymn or meter his: attribute an individual text to Ephrem only from a named critical edition or catalogue.  
**Effect on the audience:** Elevates the listener into intellectual contemplation and spiritual awe through poetic theological beauty.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Educational heritage pieces, historical reflections on Late Antiquity, and spiritual documentaries.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Sacred Music Album | genres: History, Religion, Literature  
**Avoid when:** Fast kinetic action trailers.  
**Example:** `prompt: "Ancient Syriac Madroshe hymn in the 7+7 meter traditionally associated with St. Ephrem, solitary reverent vocal with soft harp accompaniment, tranquil and sacred".`  
**Source:** reference — Sebastian P. Brock, An Introduction to Syriac Studies, rev. 3rd edn (Gorgias Press, 2022), checked 2026-09-23; Syriac syllabic meters are traditionally named for their associated poets — 5+5 for Balai, 7+7 for Ephrem, 12+12 for Jacob of Serugh — as a naming convention, not a record of individual authorship of every hymn in that meter.  
**Yields to:** `assyrian.soghyotho_dialogue_hymn` — Two opposing voices rather than a taught monologue.  
`assyrian.madroshe_teaching_hymn`

### Soghyotho Dialogue Song Architecture

**Also called:** Sogyitha, ܣܘܓܝܬܐ, dramatic disputation hymn, dialogue verse  
**What it is:** An ancient Syriac poetic genre composed as a dramatic dialogue between two opposing figures (e.g. Death and the Cherub, Church and Synagogue).
