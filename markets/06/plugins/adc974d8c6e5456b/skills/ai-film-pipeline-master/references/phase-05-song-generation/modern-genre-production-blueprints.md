# Modern Genre Production Blueprints

Production architectures, sound design standards, tempo baselines, rhythmic pockets, and instrumentation formulas for state-of-the-art music styles across AI engines and modern DAWs.

---

### What the Body Does, Before What the Genre Is Called

**Also called:** the blueprint selector, body before taste, what is this music being asked to do  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `genre.` shelf. It does not describe a sound. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **what does the listener's body have to do, may the sound be built or must it read as played, and how big is the space it has to fill** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were not invented and they are not a genre taxonomy: they were read off the `Yields to:` conditions the cards carry and cross-checked against every `Avoid when:` on the shelf, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id.  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches a listener. It exists so the blueprint that does reach one was chosen against the edit, the room and the brief, rather than picked because it is the style the writer likes.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf**. The first answer eliminates roughly two thirds of the file; the second and third leave a shortlist of two to five. If you cannot answer question one — *does the listener's body have to move* — you do not have a music brief yet, you have a mood word, and a mood word is what this shelf is built to refuse. Where two answers fight for one track, that is two sections and the blueprint that owns switching between them is `genre.k_pop_genre_bending_rollercoaster`.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** **The client or the reference track has already named the genre.** Then the name is the answer and the job is execution, not choice, and running this map produces a second opinion nobody asked for. Also avoid it when the brief is eighty percent right and wants one layer swapped — **this shelf routes only to its poles.** Every one of its pair fields sends a reader from a style to that style's opposite corner, never to the neighbour one step away, so asked *"not quite this"* the map answers *"then the reverse of this"*, which is almost never what was meant. And **distrust axis two whenever your honest answer is its third value**: exactly one card lives there, so that value is a note about the shelf rather than a fork, and a map is weaker than it looks the moment one of its answers holds a single card. Finally, this map cannot see the picture. Where the track sits under dialogue or narration the deciding field is the spoken rate, which lives in Phase 4 and is not on this shelf.  
**Example:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen blueprint carries the prompt.]`  

---

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — Does the listener's body have to move, be held still, or neither?**
Asked first, because it is the condition the shelf repeats most: thirteen of the twenty-six `Yields to:` lines turn on it, and they are stated as facts about the project rather than feelings about the sound — *"Competitive sports hype and gym energy"* (`genre.lofi_hiphop_chill_tape`), *"Bass-heavy club dancefloors"* (`genre.indie_folk_acoustic_intimacy`), *"Energetic dance clubs, not slowed liminal drift"* (`genre.vaporwave_slowed_reverb_mall`), *"Energetic daytime workouts"* (`genre.r_and_b_slow_burn_sensual`), *"Dance choreography and catchy pop singalongs"* (`genre.ambient_drone_ethereal_soundscape`), *"Aggressive workout videos and drift-speed adrenaline"* (`genre.bossa_nova_smooth_jazz_nylon`), and its exact reverse in *"Quiet meditation guides and introspective stillness"* (`genre.reggaeton_dembow_perreo`), *"Slow sentimental farewells at low tempo"* (`genre.drum_and_bass_liquid_breakbeat`) and *"Acoustic folk stories and peaceful wedding vows"* (`genre.phonk_drift_cowbell_distorted`).

**Axis 2 — May the sound be built, or must it read as played by people in a place you can date?**
Eight conditions turn on this and none of them mention taste: *"Understated acoustic storytelling instead of sensory maximalism"* (`genre.hyperpop_glitch_maximalism`), *"Period costume drama needing authentic period instruments"* (`genre.trap_triplet_hihat_808`), *"Historical war epics"* (`genre.deep_house_four_on_floor_club`), *"Minimalist folk documentary, not a genre rollercoaster"* (`genre.k_pop_genre_bending_rollercoaster`), *"The piece is modern trap rap, not retro synth"* (`genre.synthwave_outrun_retrofuturism_blueprint`), *"Cyberpunk sci-fi instead of open highway"* (`genre.country_modern_twang_crossover`) and *"High-end luxury fashion and sleek modern polish"* (`genre.midwest_emo_math_twinkle`). This is the axis a period drama, a heritage piece or a wilderness documentary settles first and everything else settles last.

**Axis 3 — How much room does the music get: one room, or a hall?**
Five conditions are about the size of the space and about what else has to be heard in it, not about the style: *"Rushing high-octane battle scenes"* (`genre.amapiano_piano_percussion`), *"Epic war battles and stadium scale"* (`genre.neo_soul_jazzy_chords`), *"Historical war epics"* (`genre.deep_house_four_on_floor_club`) at one end, and *"Casual dialogue, intimate rooms, quiet lifestyle shorts"* (`genre.cinematic_orchestral_hybrid_epic`) and *"Elegant dining commercials and calm sophistication"* (`genre.djent_polyrhythmic_metal`) at the other. It is asked last because it is the only axis that separates the blueprints an editor can talk over from the ones that have to be ducked, and because on a track with no voice over it, it decides nothing.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `genre.deep_house_four_on_floor_club`, and the measurement had to be argued rather than counted.** Two cards tie for most-yielded-to, at four inbound edges each, and one of them is not a default at all. `genre.indie_folk_acoustic_intimacy` takes four, and all four fire on the same condition wearing four coats — *"Understated acoustic storytelling instead of sensory maximalism"*, *"Slow sentimental farewells at low tempo"*, *"Acoustic folk stories and peaceful wedding vows"*, *"Minimalist folk documentary, not a genre rollercoaster"*. Every one of them says *strip the production out and put a person in a room*. **That is the door out of the shelf, not the shelf's fallback**, and in-degree alone would have named it wrongly. `genre.deep_house_four_on_floor_club` takes its four on two unrelated conditions — three readers send you there for a dancefloor (`genre.shoegaze_wall_of_fuzz`, `genre.indie_folk_acoustic_intimacy`, `genre.vaporwave_slowed_reverb_mall`) and one for polish (`genre.midwest_emo_math_twinkle`, *"High-end luxury fashion and sleek modern polish"*). It also lands under the most populated value of all three axes at once, and its own `Avoid when:` names only three narrow exits — war epics, weeping scenes, wilderness documentaries — which is the shape of a card that fits most briefs rather than one that rescues a few.

**The one against it is `genre.ambient_drone_ethereal_soundscape`.** It is the only card on the shelf whose tempo field states that there is no tempo, on a shelf where every other answer hands the edit a grid to cut to. That inversion is the whole argument: choosing it means the picture stops being cut to music and the music starts being cut to picture, which is the one thing the default cannot offer at any tempo. Its cost is stated plainly in its own card — nothing to dance to, nothing to sing — and a short beat simply pays it. Its near cousin against the grain is `genre.vaporwave_slowed_reverb_mall`, the shelf's only subtractive blueprint: every other card here is a recipe for something you build up, and that one is a recipe for taking the engine out of something that already exists. Nothing on the shelf routes to it either.

**The unasked**

**Thirteen of the twenty-six cards are the target of no `Yields to:` field anywhere on this shelf.** They can be chosen but nothing ever sends you to them. By id:

`genre.hyperpop_glitch_maximalism` · `genre.drill_sub_glide_syncopation` · `genre.amapiano_piano_percussion` · `genre.shoegaze_wall_of_fuzz` · `genre.neo_soul_jazzy_chords` · `genre.r_and_b_slow_burn_sensual` · `genre.country_modern_twang_crossover` · `genre.drum_and_bass_liquid_breakbeat` · `genre.midwest_emo_math_twinkle` · `genre.vaporwave_slowed_reverb_mall` · `genre.reggaeton_dembow_perreo` · `genre.future_bass_supersaw_wobble` · `genre.kids_teaching_tempo`

**And the shape of that list is the finding, not its length — this is a shelf on which every single card has a pair, and half of it is still unreachable by pointer.** The thirteen cards that *are* reached are the shelf's extremes: the loudest, the fastest, the calmest, the biggest, the most danceable, the brightest and the darkest. The thirteen unasked are its middles and its hybrids — a country *crossover*, a *neo*-soul, a melodic bass, a liquid breakbeat, a math-and-emo fusion, a trap variant, a slowed sample. **A reader who lands on the wrong blueprint is always sent to the opposite corner and never one step sideways**, because a pair field can only say *instead of this, that*, and the honest answer to most wrong blueprints is *this one, with the hats changed*. The card this shelf wants is the one that asks **how far do you actually have to move** — the swap of a single layer rather than a whole production architecture — and nothing here does that, because every card on this shelf is a complete architecture and the only verb the shelf owns is *replace*.

The second thing the list shows is at its foot. `genre.kids_teaching_tempo` is unasked and cannot come off the list: it is the one card here that is a number rather than a sound, both of its neighbours live in another family, and **its single pair field leaves the shelf and arrives at one half of a mutual pair that closes on itself outside**. The teaching-song question is therefore decided entirely elsewhere and lands in this shelf from outside, and nothing in this shelf ever reaches back. That is not a defect in the card. It is the shelf telling you that one of its members belongs to a different conversation, and the map records it here rather than hiding it under an axis value.

`genre.body_before_taste`

### Hyperpop Glitch Maximalism Blueprint

**Also called:** Bubblegum Bass, Cyber-Pop, Webcore Production  
**What it is:** High-energy, pitched vocal processing, heavily distorted 808s, supersaw synths, metallic foley percussion, and rapid micro-glitch stutter edits at fast tempos (140-175 BPM).  
**Effect on the audience:** Creates overwhelming sensory euphoria, playful chaos, and kinetic digital adrenaline.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Cyberpunk reels, Gen-Z fashion edits, high-speed gaming montages, and boundary-pushing pop anthems.  
**Best in:** Fast tempos (150-170 BPM), pitch-shifted chipmunk vocals, bitcrushed 808 subs, laser-sharp transients.  
**Avoid when:** Understated acoustic storytelling, somber documentaries, or corporate professional showcases.  
**Example:** `[Style: Hyperpop, Cyberpunk Bubblegum Bass, 160 BPM, bitcrushed autotune female vocal, metallic snare, stutter glitch fills, distorted 808 bass, bright supersaw synths, erratic ear candy, high energy maximalism]`  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; the tempo ranges are a genre habit and '808' names the Roland TR-808 sound, not a measure; set the tempo by ear.  
**Yields to:** `genre.indie_folk_acoustic_intimacy` — Understated acoustic storytelling instead of sensory maximalism.  
`genre.hyperpop_glitch_maximalism`

### Synthwave Outrun Retrofuturism Blueprint

**Also called:** Darksynth, Retrowave, 80s Cyber-Driving  
**What it is:** 1980s analog synth production driven by pulsing gated snares, LinnDrum samples, Juno-106 arpeggiated basslines, lush chorus pads, and soaring nostalgic electric lead guitars.  
**Effect on the audience:** Evokes nocturnal neon nostalgia, highway cruising thrill, and retro-futuristic romanticism.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Night driving scenes, retro arcade aesthetic videos, sci-fi cyberpunk shorts, and synth-heavy cinematic intros.  
**Best in:** Mid-tempo (100-128 BPM), gated reverb snares, 16th-note analog bassline arpeggios, analog pitch drift.  
**Avoid when:** Organic acoustic projects, modern trap rap, or rustic traditional folk.  
**Example:** `[Style: Synthwave, Outrun, 80s Retro-Electro, 118 BPM, gated reverb snare, pulsing analog bassline, Juno-106 synth pads, nostalgic neon melody, soaring electric guitar lead, cinematic nocturnal atmosphere]`  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; the 1980s is an era label for the sound being imitated, not a measured figure; set it by ear against reference tracks.  
**Yields to:** `genre.trap_triplet_hihat_808` — The piece is modern trap rap, not retro synth.  
`genre.synthwave_outrun_retrofuturism_blueprint`

### Drill Sub-Glide and Syncopation Blueprint

**Also called:** UK Drill, Brooklyn Drill, Slide 808 Production  
**What it is:** Dark, menacing trap variant defined by sliding portamento 808 bass notes hitting unexpected octaves, skipping syncopated hi-hat triplets, off-beat snares, and melancholic minor-key piano or string loops.  
**Effect on the audience:** Delivers heavy street tension, swaggering momentum, and visceral low-frequency impact.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Urban crime dramas, gritty training sequences, sports hype promos, and aggressive rap flows.  
**Best in:** 140-144 BPM (or 70-72 BPM half-time), complex 808 pitch glides, counter-rhythmic hi-hat rolls, detuned drill strings.  
**Avoid when:** Lighthearted comedy, uplifting brand spots, or peaceful travel vlogs.  
**Example:** `[Style: UK Drill, Dark Grime Rap, 142 BPM, deep sliding 808 sub bass glides, syncopated skipping hi-hat rolls, dark minor piano loop, aggressive vocal delivery, menacing atmospheric pad]`  
**Yields to:** `genre.afrobeats_log_drum_groove` — Uplifting brand spots and peaceful travel vlogs.  
`genre.drill_sub_glide_syncopation`

### Afrobeats Log-Drum and Swing Groove Blueprint

**Also called:** Afro-Pop, Naija Beat, African Dance Wave  
**What it is:** Infectious, syncopated west African rhythm built around the clave pattern, rolling shakers, warm electric guitar plucks, saxophone melodies, and melodic, smooth vocal cadences.  
**Effect on the audience:** Induces effortless hip-swaying danceability, sun-drenched joy, and irresistible rhythmic warmth.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Summer lifestyle commercials, vacation travel reels, feel-good dance sequences, and global pop radio tracks.  
**Best in:** 100-108 BPM, loose swing feel, prominent triplet percussion shakers, silky tenor vocals, bright horns.  
**Avoid when:** Tense cinematic action, dark horror, or aggressive heavy rock.  
**Example:** `[Style: Afrobeats, West African Pop, 104 BPM, syncopated shaker groove, warm muted guitar riffs, melodic tenor vocal, catchy brass stabs, bouncy kick drum, sunny uplifting vibe]`  
**Yields to:** `genre.djent_polyrhythmic_metal` — Aggressive heavy rock rather than sun-drenched swing.  
`genre.afrobeats_log_drum_groove`

### Amapiano Log-Drum Hybrid Blueprint

**Also called:** South African House, Piano Kwaito  
**What it is:** South African house genre characterized by deep, melodic Rhodes jazz chords, atmospheric vocal chants, and signature pitched log-drum sub basslines that bounce polyrhythmically.  
**Effect on the audience:** Generates a deep, hypnotic, meditative dance trance with sophisticated urban elegance.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Fashion show reels, late-night lounge scenes, summer terrace party videos, and stylish aesthetic content.  
**Best in:** 112-115 BPM, warm jazzy electric piano chords, hollow tuned log-drum bass hits, sparse percussive rimshots.  
**Avoid when:** Rushing high-octane battle scenes or fast-paced hard rock.  
**Example:** `[Style: Amapiano, South African Deep House, 113 BPM, signature punchy log drum bass, lush Rhodes piano chords, soulful vocal adlibs, hypnotic percussion shakers, deep groove]`  
**Yields to:** `genre.cinematic_orchestral_hybrid_epic` — Rushing high-octane battle scenes.  
`genre.amapiano_piano_percussion`

### Cinematic Orchestral Hybrid Blueprint

**Also called:** Hollywood Trailer Score, Epic Hybrid Orchestral  
**What it is:** Massive collision of full 80-piece symphony orchestra (violins, brass, taikos) with aggressive analog distortion, modern synth braams, risers, and industrial sub impacts.  
**Effect on the audience:** Inspires awe, cosmic gravity, breathtaking adrenaline, and monumental heroism.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Blockbuster movie trailers, climactic final battle scenes, launch reveal teasers, and high-stakes drama.  
**Best in:** 3-act escalation, massive Taiko drum hits, choir crescendos, low brass French horn fanfares, earth-shaking sub-drops.  
**Avoid when:** Casual conversational dialogue, intimate room scenes, or quiet lifestyle shorts.  
**Example:** `[Style: Epic Hybrid Orchestral, Hollywood Trailer Music, 130 BPM build to 150 BPM, thundering taiko percussion, aggressive brass braams, full choir crescendos, sweeping staccato strings, monumental heroic climax]`  
**Yields to:** `genre.lofi_hiphop_chill_tape` — Casual dialogue, intimate rooms, quiet lifestyle shorts.

### Lo-Fi Hip Hop Chill Tape Blueprint

**Also called:** Study Beats, Chillhop, Bedroom Beats  
**What it is:** Relaxed, nostalgic beat-making featuring vinyl crackle, detuned electric piano or jazz guitar samples, tape flutter, sidechained muffled kick-snare patterns, and warm sub bass.  
**Effect on the audience:** Lowers heart rate, promotes deep concentration, cozy comfort, and gentle melancholy.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Study live streams, cozy rainy room aesthetics, tutorial background beds, and chill narrative montages.  
**Best in:** 70-85 BPM, SP-404 vinyl simulator wobble, lazy unquantized swing, gentle rain/foley textures.  
**Avoid when:** High-energy dance sequences, competitive sports hype, or intense thriller climaxes.  
**Example:** `[Style: Lofi Hip Hop, Chillhop, 78 BPM, dusty vinyl crackle, detuned electric piano chords, lazy unquantized drum groove, warm tape saturation, mellow rainy night ambiance]`  
**Yields to:** `genre.trap_triplet_hihat_808` — Competitive sports hype and gym energy.

### Shoegaze Wall-of-Fuzz Blueprint

**Also called:** Dream Pop, Noise Pop, Wall of Sound  
**What it is:** Massive, wash of heavily reverbed and fuzz-distorted electric guitars, ethereal buried whispered vocals, driving steady bass, and floating dreamlike melodic textures.  
**Effect on the audience:** Wraps the listener in an immersive, bittersweet, cathartic sonic cocoon of warm noise.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Coming-of-age indie films, memory flashbacks, melancholic road trips, and emotional catharsis scenes.  
**Best in:** Huge stereo guitar spreads, reverse reverb pedals, whispery female or doubled male vocals, swirling flangers.  
**Avoid when:** Clean corporate presentations, rhythmic punchy dance tracks, or dialogue-heavy informational reels.  
**Example:** `[Style: Shoegaze, Dream Pop, 110 BPM, massive wall of fuzz electric guitars, shimmering reverse reverb, buried ethereal whisper vocals, driving melodic bass, nostalgic emotional wash]`  
**Yields to:** `genre.deep_house_four_on_floor_club` — Rhythmic punchy dance tracks, not a noise wash.  
`genre.shoegaze_wall_of_fuzz`

### Indie Folk Acoustic Intimacy Blueprint

### Darkwave Post-Punk Goth Blueprint

**Also called:** Coldwave, Goth Rock, Dark Synth  
**What it is:** Moody, driving minor-key cold wave sound featuring chorus-drenched jangly basslines, mechanical drum machine beats, analog polysynths, and deep, baritone detached vocal delivery.

### Reggaeton Dembow Perreo Blueprint

**Also called:** Latin Urban, Dembow Beat, Urbano  
**What it is:** High-octane Latin club groove driven by the classic syncopated "boom-ch-boom-chick" dembow rhythm, punchy round kick drums, tropical synth riffs, and rhythmic Spanish vocal flows with melodic hooks.  
**Effect on the audience:** Triggers instinctive body movement, infectious party euphoria, and tropical heat.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Latin dance parties, summer beach commercials, festival recaps, and club dancing scenes.  
**Best in:** 90-98 BPM, crisp syncopated snare pattern, tight sub bass, percussive vocal delivery, auto-tuned hook lines.

### Tempo for a Children's Song

**Also called:** kids BPM, pre-school tempo, the tempo that teaches
**What it is:** **On a song written to teach, tempo is comprehension, not feel.** Every other tempo figure in this package is a stylistic choice; this one decides whether a three-year-old can follow the words at all, and it is the one genre where the package had twenty-five BPM figures for weddings, martial dances and drill beats and nothing at all.
**Effect on the audience:** At the right tempo a small child joins in by the second refrain. Ten beats per minute too fast and they watch instead of singing, which looks like enjoyment and teaches nothing.
**Used for and where it works best:** **Pre-school, roughly ages two to four: 95 to 115 BPM**, and slower inside that band when each beat carries a new word. **Early primary, five to seven: 110 to 130.** A brief whose age band straddles the two (three to six, say) takes the younger band. The rule that matters more than the number: **one idea per bar, and the thing being taught lands on a downbeat**, so the child's own clapping falls on it. Leave a **whole bar of space after each new word** — the gap is where a young listener actually processes it, and filling it with music is the commonest way a teaching song stops teaching. On a generated track that bar is made by the arrangement or in the edit: Suno documents no silent bar inside a lyric (`suno.instrumental_solo_tags`), so write it as an instrumental line to audition, and thin it in the Song Editor or the edit where the take fills it. This bar is the gap after a new word; the answer gap after an ask is a different silence and is `expo.kids_repetition_ladder`'s. A refrain repeats **identically**, at the same tempo, every time; variation is for the verses, and a refrain that develops is a refrain a child cannot predict, which is the one thing they are there for. Where the song counts or lists, the tempo is set by the **slowest item** — if one number takes longer to say, the whole song runs at that speed.
**Best in:** formats: Kids Content, Animated Series, Educational, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video | genres: Children's, Educational
**Avoid when:** The song is for children but not *teaching* them — a mood piece, a lullaby or a title theme takes its tempo from the feeling. `pacing.wpm_children` owns the **spoken** rate and is a different number for a different job.
**Example:** `tempo: 104 BPM, 4/4, one taught word per bar landing on beat 1, a full bar of instrumental space after each, refrain identical at every return.`
**Yields to:** `lyric.lullaby` — A children's song that soothes rather than teaches.  
`genre.kids_teaching_tempo`
