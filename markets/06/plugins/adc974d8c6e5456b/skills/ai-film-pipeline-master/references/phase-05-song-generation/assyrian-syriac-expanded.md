# Assyrian & Syriac Expanded Folk, Epic & Classical Traditions

26 cards. Rare Assyrian mountain dances (Mamer, Tolama, Jani, Chobi), classical Syriac poetic meters (Mor Afrem, Mor Balai), authentic instruments (Zanboor, Tambura), and diaspora master songwriting techniques.

### Name the Subject Before the Sound

**Also called:** the expanded Assyrian selector, subject before sound, what is this piece about and what may it say  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `assyrian_exp.` shelf. It does not describe a sound. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **what is the piece about and what can the card stand behind, what is the room doing, and how many people are making the noise** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were not invented: they were read off the shelf's `Yields to:` conditions and cross-checked line by line against `Avoid when:`, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. The first axis is the heritage question and it comes first on purpose: on this shelf the pair fields that turn on the subject are all of the form *the subject is this, not that* — a place, a century, a faith, a legend — and none of them can be repaired by a better sound. Every card cited here is cited by id. **This card routes. It does not check a claim, and on a heritage shelf that distinction is the whole risk.**  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so that the card that does reach the screen was chosen against the subject the piece is actually about — the right region, the right church, the right kind of evidence — rather than against the sound somebody already had in their head.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf.** Answer one first and answer it about the subject, not the sound: *which place, which named past, or no name at all.* If your answer is a named past, **read the chosen card's whole body first, before a word of narration is written** — every card under that value states a history in its own words, and this map settles none of it. Two and three then leave a shortlist of one to four. Where two answers to axis one fight for one piece, that is two pieces.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** **The material is Iraqi-Arabic rather than Assyrian or Syriac** — `maqam.` governs there. **And when the material is the church's own liturgy or one of the core dance rhythms, open `assyrian.whose_before_what` first**: this shelf's own Example lines stand on that shelf's rhythms by name, nine of this shelf's outbound pair edges land there, and not one edge comes back — so this map will hand you a card here when the question belonged next door. **Distrust axis one whenever your honest answer is its fourth value**, the living tradition: more than half the shelf lives there, and on an ordinary folk brief this card has two questions left and is doing a fraction of its work. **Do not read a route as a ruling.** Several cards on this shelf state a date, a rank, an origin or an age in their own words — among them claims the package carries in attributed form — and this map hands every one of them over exactly as written. Where the package says what a narration line may claim, as `assyrian.beth_gazo_eight_modes` does when it requires *traditionally attributed to* rather than *composed by*, **that card outranks this map.** **Distrust the default on any short brief**: it is a long-form suite and it leaves the shelf in one step. And avoid this card entirely when you are matching an existing recording or a community's own supplied reference: there the sound is heard off the record and matched to it, not chosen from a shelf. **Three cards take a named songwriter's style as their reference** — `assyrian_exp.evin_agassi_storytelling`, `assyrian_exp.ashur_bet_sargis_elegance` and `assyrian_exp.george_chaharbakhshi_anthem`. The map routes to a style, never to the person: name the specific recording the style is taken from in the production note, and do not let the result be presented as that artist's work, voice or endorsement.  
**Example:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — What is the piece about, and what can the card stand behind?**
Asked first, because it is the only condition on the shelf that can make a technically perfect choice wrong. Five `Yields to:` lines turn on it and every one is written as *the subject is this, not that*: *"The subject is the Nineveh Plains, not Urmia"* (`assyrian_exp.urmia_lakeshore_lilt`), *"A Khabour agricultural subject, not the Plains"* (`assyrian_exp.nineveh_harvest_chant`), *"A contemporary rally, not a WWI commemoration"* (`assyrian_exp.agha_petros_epic_ballad`), *"A pre-Christian temple subject, not a church"* (`assyrian_exp.mart_mariam_bell_toll`) and — the sharpest, because it is not about taste at all — *"A documented royal subject rather than legend"* (`assyrian_exp.queen_shamiram_theme`). **That last condition is an evidence test wearing the clothes of a style note, and it is why this axis has a value for legend separate from a value for a named past.** The cross-check against `Avoid when:` is itself a finding: those fields carry the era half of this axis — *"Grimy modern crime scenes"*, *"Modern urban club scenes"*, *"High-tech modern industrial factories"* — and never the place half or the evidence half. The shelf only says *wrong subject* in its pair fields.

**Axis 2 — What is the room doing?**
Seven conditions turn on it and they are stated as facts about the scene, not feelings about the audience: *"Tragic battle defeat, not a village celebration"* (`assyrian_exp.tolama_village_rhythm`), *"An aggressive battle march or athletic stomp"* (`assyrian_exp.jani_stately_dance`), *"A solemn memorial or historical disaster"* (`assyrian_exp.aziyze_lyrical_dance`), *"A high-energy celebratory party"* (`assyrian_exp.liliye_ancestral_lullaby`), *"Hard-core martial war material"* (`assyrian_exp.ashur_bet_sargis_elegance`), *"Absolute despair, no resurrection hope"* (`assyrian_exp.qam_maran_resurrection_chant`) and *"A triumphant, monumental epic scale"* (`assyrian_exp.st_balai_5_syllable_meter`). `Avoid when:` carries the same line three more times — *"Harsh desert combat"*, *"Secular party songs"*, *"Triumphant victory parades"*.

**Axis 3 — How many people, and how loud may they be?**
Six conditions are about forces and the size of the room rather than mood: *"A solitary voice, no line of dancers"* (`assyrian_exp.chobi_line_dance`), *"A busy crowded scene with loud instruments"* (`assyrian_exp.rawe_mountain_recitative`), *"A low-key quiet dialogue scene"* (`assyrian_exp.circular_breathing_zurna`), *"A quiet bedtime lullaby"* (`assyrian_exp.george_chaharbakhshi_anthem`), *"Delicate modern chamber music"* (`assyrian_exp.zanboor_drone_pipe`) and *"A delicate modern urban pop ballad"* (`assyrian_exp.mamer_mountain_dance`). **Two cards on the shelf carry no pair field at all and are reached only through this axis's `Avoid when:` evidence**: `assyrian_exp.tambura_long_neck_lute` (*"Full Western symphonic brass movements where acoustic tambura is drowned out"*) and `assyrian_exp.davula_rim_click_breakdown` (*"Smooth orchestral strings with no drums"*). It is asked last because it never disqualifies — it only narrows.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `assyrian_exp.wedding_grand_finale_suite` — and it is argued, because the count refuses to produce one.** The most-yielded-to card on the shelf is `assyrian_exp.khabour_village_suite`, at two inbound edges, and the two fire on conditions from two different axes: `assyrian_exp.aziyze_lyrical_dance` sends you there on *"A solemn memorial or historical disaster"*, a room condition, and `assyrian_exp.nineveh_harvest_chant` on *"A Khabour agricultural subject, not the Plains"*, a subject condition. **One is a grief door and one is a place lock, and neither is a floor**: making it the default would put every piece on one river. Every other card here is reached once or not at all, and nothing outside the shelf yields into it anywhere in the package. So the default is argued from the shelf's text instead. **Four other cards name the wedding or the dance suite as their room**: `assyrian_exp.tolama_village_rhythm` (*"Wedding banquets"*), `assyrian_exp.jani_stately_dance` (*"Bridal preparation scenes, mother-of-the-bride dances"*), `assyrian_exp.circular_breathing_zurna` (*"The climax of extended Khigga and Gubareh wedding sets"*) and `assyrian_exp.davula_rim_click_breakdown` (*"Drum solo breaks in the middle of traditional dance suites"*) — **and the last two have no home outside it; they are written as parts of it.** It is the occasion the shelf is organised around. Its one door is the right door: on *"A 30-second cut: one rhythm, not a suite"* it yields to `assyrian.khigga_dance_rhythm`, the card the neighbouring shelf's selector argues as its own default. **The cost is stated rather than hidden: on a short brief this default lasts one step and leaves the shelf** — which is the one-decision-in-two-prefixes question showing through the default itself.

**The one against it is `assyrian_exp.rawe_mountain_recitative`, and the shelf drew the line itself, in both directions.** `assyrian_exp.chobi_line_dance` yields to it on *"A solitary voice, no line of dancers"*; it yields out on *"A busy crowded scene with loud instruments."* It inverts the default at every joint: a quarter of an hour against a prologue; a crowd against *"unmetered solo vocal"*; zurna and davula against *"without musical instruments"*; a tempo that climbs by the minute against no bar line at all; a hall against a gorge. And it inverts something more useful than texture: the default is the sound the community makes together, this is the sound of one person telling what happened. Its costs are real. An unmetered voice gives an editor nothing to cut on. And its own title and body carry age — *"Ancient Mountain Recitative"*, *"An ancient, unmetered solo vocal chant"* — which this map hands over as written and which the narration does not get to repeat on the map's authority. Its near cousin against the grain is `assyrian_exp.liliye_ancestral_lullaby`, which the loudest card on the shelf, `assyrian_exp.george_chaharbakhshi_anthem`, points to as its own opposite.

### Mamer Mountain Circle Dance Rhythm

**Also called:** Mamer, ܡܐܡܪ, northern mountain stomp, Hakkari circle dance  
**What it is:** An ancient 2/4 rhythm originating in the high Hakkari mountains, featuring heavy stomping downbeats and rhythmic shoulder sways.  
**Effect on the audience:** Instills raw, unyielding mountain survival energy, collective pride, and tribal solidarity.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Mountain festival celebrations, historical war mobilization, and heritage dance pageants.  
**Best in:** formats: Folk Music Video, Heritage Film | genres: Folk, Heritage (civilisation-focused)  
**Avoid when:** Delicate modern urban pop ballads.  
**Example:** `style: "Traditional Assyrian Mamer dance, 110 BPM 2/4 meter, heavy Davula drum accents, piercing high-register Zurna, raw male chorus shouts".`  
**Yields to:** `assyrian_exp.ashur_bet_sargis_elegance` — A delicate modern urban pop ballad.

### Tolama Traditional Village Rhythm

**Also called:** Tolama, ܬܘܠܡܐ, Nineveh plains dance, wedding round  
**What it is:** A lively 6/8 folk rhythm popular across the villages of the Nineveh Plains (Alqosh, Tel Keppe, Baghdeda), marked by syncopated foot-crossings.  
**Effect on the audience:** Fills the heart with warm village nostalgia, family celebration, and infectious communal joy.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Wedding banquets, harvest feasts, and village homecoming scenes.  
**Best in:** formats: Folk Single, Wedding Sequence | genres: Folk, Cultural  
**Avoid when:** Tragic battle defeat sequences.  
**Example:** `style: "Assyrian Tolama rhythm, 118 BPM 6/8 meter, lively accordion and clarinet leads, traditional dumbek rolls, joyous family singing".`  
**Yields to:** `assyrian.ninevite_homeland_lament` — Tragic battle defeat, not a village celebration.

### Jani Stately Women's Folk Dance

**Also called:** Jani, ܓܐܢܝ, elegant bridal step, ceremonial folk dance  
**What it is:** A slow, deeply graceful 4/4 dance (80 to 90 BPM) where dancers hold decorated silk handkerchiefs, swaying with royal dignity.  
**Effect on the audience:** Radiates ancient Mesopotamian bridal elegance, modesty, and queenly poise.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Bridal preparation scenes, mother-of-the-bride dances, and historical queen processions.  
**Best in:** formats: Wedding Film, Cultural Romance | genres: Folk, Romance, Heritage  
**Avoid when:** Aggressive battle marches or fast athletic stomps.  
**Example:** `style: "Assyrian Jani folk dance, 84 BPM, delicate acoustic Oud and acoustic violin harmonies, gentle riq tambourine, serene feminine vocals".`  
**Yields to:** `assyrian.sheikhani_martial_rhythm` — An aggressive battle march or athletic stomp.

### The Rawe (الراوي): Ancient Mountain Recitative

**Also called:** Rawe, ܪܐܘܐ, unmetered mountain chant, warrior oral recitative

### Liliye (The Ancestral Lullaby)

**Also called:** Liliye, ܠܝܠܝܐ, Sureth cradle song, mother's lullaby  
**What it is:** A tender, hypnotic lullaby sung in Sureth by mothers, rocking children to sleep with promises of protection and ancient blessings.  
**Effect on the audience:** Brings tears of profound childhood tenderness, maternal warmth, and deep ancestral comfort.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Mother and child scenes, wartime departures, and tender memorial reflections.
