# How the two reference works are used

The grimoire (`grimoire-01` … `grimoire-20`) and the clothing library (`clothing-01` … `clothing-11`)
are house canon: two works researched once so that no project researches them again, and no project
invents something that contradicts them. Both were single files until 2026-09-19 —
one world grimoire and one clothing library — and were split into routed parts, one file per
civilisation and one per wardrobe Part, with not a word changed. [`INDEX.md`](INDEX.md)
routes a period or a date to its file, and [`CANON-LOOKUP.md`](CANON-LOOKUP.md) is the id table, searched
rather than read; use them rather than reading either work whole.

## 1. The date bound comes first, and it shuts the canon

**Scope: roughly 4000 BC to 330 BC.** The bound has two ends and both of them shut the canon.

- **The ceiling, 330 BC.** Both works cover the visual world of twenty civilisations up to the
  Achaemenids. Nothing in them reaches the Christian era.
- **The floor, roughly 4000 BC.** The earliest chapter is [`grimoire-01-uruk.md`](grimoire-01-uruk.md),
  and there is nothing before it. **The canon holds no Neolithic material at all** — no Pre-Pottery
  Neolithic, no Göbekli Tepe or Karahan Tepe, no Çatalhöyük, no Hassuna, Samarra, Halaf or Ubaid. A
  subject dated before about 4000 BC answers the routing question "is this Mesopotamia before 330 BC?"
  with a truthful *yes* and then finds the earliest file on the shelf is five thousand years too
  late.

**This matters more than a missing chapter, because the failure is silent.** Every file in here is
about a later millennium and none of them says so, so a Neolithic brief opens the canon, reads Uruk
or Neo-Assyrian material and takes it as evidence. That is exactly the error §1 already names at the
other end of the range — *Neo-Assyrian reliefs are not evidence of how anyone at Nisibis looked; they
are the wrong millennium* — and it is the same error in the same size, pointing the other way. A
mud-brick temple at Uruk is not evidence for a T-pillar enclosure at Göbekli Tepe, and the gap
between them is larger than the gap between Uruk and the Achaemenids.

**On a subject below the floor, the canon stays shut and the research is done from the site
literature**, exactly as it would be for a period the package never claimed. Part A of
[`heritage-rules.md`](heritage-rules.md) — the standing editorial rules — still applies, because
those are the owner's rules and not a claim about any century. What does not apply is Part B.

**A subject after 330 BC is outside the canon, no matter how Assyrian the people in it are.** A
Syriac Christian subject of AD 350 to 600 - the School of Nisibis, for instance - does not open it.
Neo-Assyrian reliefs are not evidence of how anyone at Nisibis looked; they are the wrong
millennium, and using them is the exact failure this rule exists to prevent.

For a later subject: the canon stays shut, the real period is researched, and the canon may still be
cited for something genuinely continuous - a river, a place, a building that survived - **named as
continuity rather than as period truth.**

> This file is the authority on when the canon opens. Nothing else in this skill may widen the
> date bound, and a project that needs ancient material for a later subject records that as a
> stated decision rather than by treating the evidence as period truth.

## 2. Read it before you search

If the subject is Mesopotamian and inside the date bound, these two files are consulted **first**.
Only what they do not cover goes to a web search.

A web finding that contradicts the canon is **not applied quietly and not a question for anyone**: on a
realistic history piece take the better-sourced answer, record both and the choice under `decisions`,
note the canon line for correction, and carry on. **Where the canon is silent** on something the frame
shows (a lamassu's cap, whether a colossus was painted), search the web and take the most suitable
answer; §3 quotes only what the canon actually says.

## 3. Quote it, do not paraphrase it

A costume note, a material, a building detail: take the wording. The whole value of a canon is that
two projects six months apart describe the same thing the same way. A paraphrase is a new
description, and two new descriptions are a continuity break.

## 3b. Fiction set in the period, and a supplied art direction

**A fantasy, a game or a stylised piece set in Mesopotamia takes the canon as reference, not as a
lock.** The canon exists to stop a piece being built out of what everyone half-remembers. It is not
a claim that everything set in the period must look documentary.

So on invented material: **the canon is the floor under the invention** — the shapes, the materials,
the light, what a thing was actually *for* — and the piece departs from it deliberately and
knowingly, which is a different act from departing from it by accident. What still binds even here
is the **commonest wrong element**, because that is not a style choice, it is somebody else's
mistake arriving by default. A ziggurat is the standing example: the temple platform was a broad
stepped mass, wider than it was tall, and the sky-piercing tower everyone pictures is a Renaissance
painter's Tower of Babel. Invent a taller one on purpose if the piece wants one; just do not receive
it for free from the model.

**Where a client supplies a fixed art direction** — models, a palette, a style guide you may not
change — the supplied identity governs the *look* and the canon governs the *research underneath*
it. They answer different questions and the collision is usually imaginary. Where it is real the
brief decides the look and the choice is recorded under `decisions`; [`../../PRECEDENCE.md`](../../PRECEDENCE.md)
says how. §4's claim that the canon outranks invention is a claim about **accuracy**, not about art
direction.

## 3c. A claim's heading says how well it is known

**Where a claim sits decides how a reader treats it, so a contested cause must not sit under a
physical heading.** A bullet under *Architecture* reads as an observation of a building; the same
sentence under *Interpretation* reads as an argument somebody is making. Filed wrongly, a disputed
explanation is quoted into narration as description, and the picture then argues something the script
was careful not to say.

Two rules follow, and they cost nothing. **Put causal and contested claims under a heading that says
so**, never under a material, a building or an object. **And date the bullet, not the chapter** — a
chapter spanning six centuries with no per-bullet dates will put an object into a frame hundreds of
years before it existed, which is a failure no reference image can
catch, because the bullet reads as settled.

This is the same fault as a figure written as a correction, in [`../../ENGINE-CHECK.md`](../../ENGINE-CHECK.md):
**presentation implies a verification status the content does not have.** The canon's job is to be
the most confident document in the package, which is exactly why its confidence has to be graded.

## 4. It is house canon, not scripture

Binding for consistency, and it always outranks invention. On a history piece the user wants
realistic, a historical **claim** taken from it reaches the script with the canon's own date and citation,
and that is enough to use it; nothing waits on reopening the citation. It is a very good secondary source,
not a primary one. On any other piece, facts and sources are not part of the job.

## 5. Say when you used it

A design note that came from the canon says so, naming the civilisation and the heading. That is how
anyone later can tell a researched decision from a guess.

## 6. What the canon does not decide

It decides nothing about a production. It names no model, prices no batch, chooses no shot, and
overrides no project decision. It is a description of the ancient world, not an instruction to the
pipeline.

## Where this material came from

The grimoire was converted from a text-only PDF export in September 2026; the PDF carried
nothing the markdown does not, and it was retired because regenerating from it silently overwrote
hand corrections. Its own front matter, with that provenance, is at
[`grimoire-00-about.md`](grimoire-00-about.md). The clothing library is generated from the owner's
HTML catalogue of 1,250 garments, and its scope statement is at
[`clothing-00-about.md`](clothing-00-about.md). The sheet layouts live with the image stage, beside
the prompt guide that quotes them.

In the package source, these files are the copy of record. Nothing regenerates them here and nothing syncs them, so an
edit made here is the edit. Where a correction belongs upstream in the owner's own catalogue as
well, make it there too, or the next export will undo it.

**The one rule that survives from the room this came from:** never let two versions of the same
rule be live at once. That was the single failure that project kept having, and it is the reason
this canon is one copy in one place.
