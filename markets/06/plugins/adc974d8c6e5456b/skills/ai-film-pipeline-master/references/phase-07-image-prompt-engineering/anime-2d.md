# Anime and 2D Animation Techniques

59 cards. These decide a frame: what is in it, how it is lit, and what it is made of.

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, what to avoid, and the prompt written out in full.

**On a drawn piece that is not anime** — a Western cartoon, a kids' series, a picture-book look — take the drawing vocabulary from these cards and replace the words "anime style" that open many Prompt fields with the piece's own declared art style.

---

### Register Before the Device

**Also called:** the anime selector, register first, how much may the drawing admit  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `anime.` shelf. It describes no look. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **how far may this picture admit that it is drawn, what has to stay readable while it does, and does the device fire once or come back on a schedule** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were not invented: they were read off the fifty-one `Yields to:` conditions the cards carry and cross-checked line by line against every `Avoid when:` in the file, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id.  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so that the card that does reach the screen was chosen against the register, the thing that has to read and the schedule, rather than picked because it was remembered from a series.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf**. The first answer eliminates the large majority of the file; the second and third leave a shortlist of three to six. If you cannot answer question one — *how much may this picture admit it is drawn* — you are not ready to pick a device, you are ready to settle the style of the piece, and that decision is upstream of this shelf. Where two answers fight for one frame, that is two registers meeting, and the join is a cut, not a card.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** **The piece has already settled into ordinary anime grammar**, which is where most of this shelf lives — thirty of the fifty-six reachable cards sit under axis one's middle value, so on a standard series episode that question is already answered and this map has only two questions left and is doing two-thirds of the work it does on a piece whose register is still open. Also avoid it as a way of deciding between two traditions: the Japanese and Western halves of this shelf are not alternatives to each other, they are two vocabularies, and a card chosen out of the wrong one reads as an import in any register. **And it does not answer a safety question and must not be read as one.** Axis three asks *does this device come back*, which is not the same question as *how fast may it come back*. Two cards on this shelf make a bright thing and a dark thing take turns, and both of them now hand the rate and the stopping point to `safety.photosensitive_flash`, which outranks every answer on this map. This card sets no rate, and reading a rate out of its third axis is reading something that is not there.  
**Prompt:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

---

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — How far may this picture admit that it is drawn?**
Asked first, because it is the condition the shelf repeats most: seventeen of the fifty-one `Yields to:` lines turn on register, and they are stated as facts about the work rather than as feelings about the viewer — *"Realistic register: show the change in the eyes"* (`anime.power_up_aura`), *"Realistic work, or where the meme reading intrudes"* (`anime.naruto_run`), *"Realistic or historical work: simulate the dust"* (`anime.yutapon_cubes`), *"Serious drama: the symbol plays as childish"* (`anime.blush_lines`), *"Serious or realistic work: use backlight, not sparkles"* (`anime.shojo_sparkle_bg`), *"Real horror or heavy drama: vector softens it"* (`anime.cn_flat_vector`), *"Work needing polish: a product ad, a glossy series"* (`anime.line_boil`), *"Metal does not compress; shake the camera instead"* (`anime.squash_on_landing`). The `Avoid when:` fields say the same thing from the other side, over and over: *in realistic drama it breaks belief*, *in realistic work it looks silly*, *in a serious or formal scene the bounce undercuts the gravity*.

**Axis 2 — What has to stay readable, and what may be given up to get it?**
Twelve conditions turn on this and the recurring verb is literally *read*: *"Busy background: flatten it so the copies read"* (`anime.multiples`), *"Similar body shapes stop reading as silhouettes"* (`anime.silhouette_flat_action`), *"Wide shot or fast action: the small beat is invisible"* (`anime.kyoani_micro_expression`), *"Faces must stay readable; unsettle the line instead"* (`anime.fisheye_2d`), *"The reflection cannot be drawn large enough to read"* (`anime.eye_reflection`), *"The story needs a face the viewer can feel"* (`anime.upa_flat`), *"Instructional work: grain and scratches bury the detail"* (`anime.cuphead_look`), *"The audience cannot read the lettering's language"* (`anime.drawn_onomatopoeia`), *"The line that matters needs the face"* (`anime.talking_heads_bg_pan`). Every card on this shelf protects one thing and spends the rest of the frame on it. Naming the protected thing before choosing is what stops the answer being a preference.

**Axis 3 — Does it fire once, hold as a state, or come back on a schedule?**
Seven conditions are about duration and return rather than look — *"The travel must sustain: loop in place, move the background"* (`anime.run_cycle`), *"The handmade feel must run long without tiring the eye"* (`anime.rough_animation`), *"Realistic drama, or a second use in one episode"* (`anime.punch_through_frame`), *"Every memory graded alike: mark it by texture"* (`anime.flashback_desaturation`), *"Too many cards in a row: introduce by behaviour"* (`anime.character_intro_card`), *"Outside weekly anime: choose the credit design deliberately"* (`anime.ending_still_walk`), *"A whole season drops at once"* (`anime.next_episode_preview`) — and it is the dominant shape of the `Avoid when:` fields across the whole file, where *used every time, it dies* is said in more cards than any other warning. It is asked last because it is the only axis that reaches the serial-slot cards at all, and it is the axis a reader is most likely to skip, because a shot list has no column for *again next week*.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `anime.flat_cel_shading` on the surface with `anime.mouth_flap_three` on the talking — and it is measured by conditions, not by edges.** `anime.flat_cel_shading` is reached from three different regions of the shelf for three genuinely different reasons: `anime.line_boil` sends a reader there when the work needs polish, `anime.silhouette_flat_action` when similar body shapes stop reading, and `anime.cn_flat_vector` when real horror or heavy drama needs weight the vector look softens away. Three sources, three unrelated conditions, and the card's own `Best in:` says *genres: all*. Its partner is chosen the same way: only two reachable cards on this shelf carry *genres: all*, and the other is `anime.mouth_flap_three`, which is what makes a drawn character speak at a price the schedule can pay.

**`anime.kyoani_micro_expression` takes more inbound edges than either of them and is not the default.** Four cards send a reader to it — `anime.speed_lines_radial`, `anime.emotion_icons`, `anime.blush_lines` and `anime.upa_flat` — but the first three fire on one condition wearing three coats: *the scene has turned serious, so stop drawing the feeling and act it*. Counting edges makes it the shelf's fallback. Counting conditions makes it what it is: **the door out of the stylised half of the shelf into the naturalistic half**, and a door is not a floor. In-degree here measures how often the shelf runs out of register, not what it rests on.

**The one against it is `anime.ghibli_ma`.** Nothing on this shelf routes to it; it is named by no `Yields to:` field here; and it is the only card whose content is the absence of the shelf's own product — no event, no dialogue, no drawing being spent. The default pair exists to keep a character talking continuously and cheaply; `anime.ghibli_ma` stops the character talking and spends the shot on nothing, and it is the only card here that improves the cards on either side of it rather than the frame it occupies. Its one stated cost is placement — put down with no build in front of it, it reads as boredom rather than breath — which a preceding scene simply pays. Its cousin against the grain is `anime.storybook_look`, likewise never yielded to, and the only card on the shelf that answers a question about **evidence** rather than about style: it declares itself illustration instead of reconstruction, which is a claim about truth that no other card here is making.

### Animating on Ones

**Also called:** ones, full animation  
**What it is:** A new drawing on every video frame, twenty four drawings per second. The most expensive cadence there is.  
**Effect on the audience:** The smoothest, fastest feel available. Motion turns liquid and physical, as if the body has real weight.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The punch or the leap the viewer has to feel in their own body, or a fast camera move inside a drawn shot. In battle and sports anime confine it to the decisive seconds because of the cost; in a commercial, spend it on the one shot that sells the idea.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Action, Shonen Battle (anime), Sports Anime, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Mecha (anime), Superhero  
**Avoid when:** On slow motion it adds nothing the eye can see; you pay the cost and get no result on screen.  
**Prompt:** `animated on ones, a new drawing every single frame, ultra fluid weighty motion`  
**Yields to:** `anime.walking_on_twos` — Ordinary motion: the eight-frame economy carries it.  
`anime.on_ones`

### The Hold / Still Frame

**Also called:** held cel, freeze on a key drawing  
**What it is:** A single drawing held on screen for a second or more with no change at all, sometimes without even a blink. The shot becomes a hanging image. It differs from the Hold or Ma in the time chapter, where the shot keeps running with small live movement inside it: here there is no motion whatsoever, because it is one drawing held.  
**Effect on the audience:** Stops time. The viewer studies the drawing itself and gets room to feel the emotion instead of chasing the action.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Straight after the finishing blow, on a face that has just heard the bad news, or on the hero pose before the fight starts. In dramatic anime hold it until it becomes slightly uncomfortable; in a trailer use it as the stop before the title card.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Music Video, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Shonen Battle (anime), Drama, Seinen (mature anime), Mecha (anime), Tragedy, Action, Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** Held over information the viewer already got, it reads as dead air. And a weak drawing is the wrong one to hold, because the freeze exposes every flaw in it; the exception is a deliberately crude style where the roughness is the voice.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, a single held key drawing frozen on screen, no motion at all, ambient sound only`  
**Yields to:** `time.ma_hold` — The pause should keep running with live movement.  
`anime.hold`

### Run Cycle as a Shot

**Also called:** run loop  
**What it is:** A shorter, harder loop than the walk: the body pitched forward, a full airborne beat between steps, the arms cutting the air. Usually on twos or ones.  
**Effect on the audience:** Urgency and drive. Running turns a scene from a state into an objective: there is something to reach before it is too late.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The run to the battlefield, chasing a departing train, fleeing danger, and the final run in a romance toward the person before they leave. In sports anime it is the shot that builds tension before the strike.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Shonen Battle (anime), Sports Anime, Action, Romance, Coming of Age, Adventure, Thriller / Suspense  
**Avoid when:** A long run with no change in distance or angle loses its urgency and turns into running on the spot.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, run cycle with the body pitched forward and a clear airborne frame, background streaking past`  
**Yields to:** `kids.walk_loop` — The travel must sustain: loop in place, move the background.  
`anime.run_cycle`

### Walking on Twos

**Also called:** eight frame step, standard TV walk  
**What it is:** The standard economy formula: four drawings per step held on twos, eight frames per step, with the background sliding behind.  
**Effect on the audience:** A natural looking walk for less than half the cost. The regular beat gives the scene a calm, ordered pulse.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every ordinary walk in a TV episode: coming home from school, entering a room, crossing a street. Change the frames per step to change the mood of the walk, faster for anger and slower for exhaustion.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Drama Series Episode, Kids Content, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Slice of Life, Coming of Age, Drama, Comedy, Family, Shojo (anime romance), Romance  
**Avoid when:** The same frames per step in every scene makes all the walking mechanical and emotionless.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, standard four drawing walk held on twos, eight frames per step, calm steady pace`  
**Yields to:** `anime.on_ones` — The step must be felt in the body.  
`anime.walking_on_twos`

### Talking Heads with a Background Pan

**Also called:** walk and talk on a scrolling background  
**What it is:** Two characters walk side by side in profile on a short cycle, the background slides past, and an entire dialogue scene plays out over this one shot.  
**Effect on the audience:** Makes a long dialogue watchable without a single cut. The continuous movement keeps it from feeling static.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Friendship, confession and exposition scenes in school and slice of life anime. Keep it in profile so both faces read, and let the background change mood as the conversation turns, from street to riverbank when it gets personal.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Drama Series Episode, Kids Content, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Slice of Life, Coming of Age, Shojo (anime romance), Romance, Drama, Comedy, Shonen Battle (anime)  
**Avoid when:** A very long dialogue on one shot goes flat; cut to a close up on the line that matters.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, two students walking side by side in profile, scrolling riverside background, both faces visible, conversational pace`  
**Yields to:** `size.cu` — The line that matters needs the face.  
`anime.talking_heads_bg_pan`

### Three Position Mouth Flap

**Also called:** three mouth shapes, A I U flap  
**What it is:** Speech is drawn with very few mouth shapes: closed, half open, open. They alternate on the rhythm of the voice rather than on individual phonemes.  
**Effect on the audience:** Produces convincing speech at almost no cost and keeps attention on the eyes and the voice rather than the lips.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every dialogue scene in TV anime, and any dub, because a small shape set fits any language. Add one or two extra shapes only on a very tight close up where the line is pivotal.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Drama Series Episode, Kids Content, Explainer, AI-Generated Clip | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** On an extreme close up of the mouth the mismatch with the track becomes obvious, and it does not work for singing, which needs real sync.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, simple three shape mouth flap on the dialogue, eyes doing the acting, minimal facial motion`  
**Yields to:** `dubbing.bilabial_lipsync_alignment` — Singing, or a close-up on the mouth.  
`anime.mouth_flap_three`

### Eye Blink Cycle

**Also called:** blink, two frame blink  
**What it is:** Three or four drawings that close and open the eye, dropped in at irregular intervals over any otherwise held shot.  
**Effect on the audience:** Brings a dead drawing back to life. A blink is the smallest possible signal that this is a living, thinking being.  
**Used for and where it works best:** On every long held shot that keeps running, and specifically before the important line - a hold held for effect (`anime.hold`) drops the blink on purpose, because no blink is the shock - where one slow blink announces a decision. Two quick blinks read as confusion, and no blink at all reads as shock or as a non human presence.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Drama Series Episode, Kids Content, AI-Generated Clip | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** Perfectly regular blinks feel mechanical, and in horror you withhold the blink when you want the face to frighten.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, held close up with occasional natural blinks at irregular intervals, everything else motionless`  
`anime.blink_cycle`

### Line Boil

**Also called:** boiling line, wobbling outline  
**What it is:** The character outline never settles: it is redrawn slightly differently on every drawing, so the shape ripples and boils even when nothing moves.  
**Effect on the audience:** Reminds the viewer that a human hand made this. It reads as warmth and as unease at the same time.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The deliberately handmade look in short films, experimental work and music videos, and in scenes where a character is psychologically unstable. In kids content it reads as sketchbook drawing, which pulls a child closer to the image.  
**Best in:** formats: 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Short Film, Music Video, Experimental / Art Film, Kids Content, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Surreal / Absurdist, Psychological / Arthouse, Comedy, Family, Preschool / Early Learning, Drama, Dark Comedy / Satire  
**Avoid when:** In work that needs precision and polish, a product ad or a glossy series, the boil reads as a production fault.  
**Prompt:** `anime style with heavy line boil, the outlines wobbling and redrawn every frame, handmade sketchbook feel`  
**Yields to:** `anime.flat_cel_shading` — Work needing polish: a product ad, a glossy series.  
`anime.line_boil`

### Rough Animation / Sketch Animation

**Also called:** genga look, paint on glass look, rough key look  
**What it is:** The rough drawing is left uncleaned and unpainted: construction lines remain, brush strokes stay visible, and colour sometimes runs outside the line.  
**Effect on the audience:** Raw energy. The motion feels stronger because the eye sees the effort itself, and the emotion arrives unmediated.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The peak of a psychological fight or an emotional collapse, and passages where a character shifts into another state. The show Mob Psycho 100 made this look famous in its psychic outbursts; use it when you want to break the visual contract of your own series for a few seconds.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Short Film, Music Video, Experimental / Art Film, Trailer / Teaser, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Psychological / Arthouse, Action, Shonen Battle (anime), Surreal / Absurdist, Seinen (mature anime), Drama, Tragedy  
**Avoid when:** Used with no narrative reason it reads as an unfinished shot, and do not stretch it: raw drawing tires the eye fast.

**What it is:** The shadow is drawn as a single shape in a single colour with a hard edge and no gradient. A base colour and a shadow colour, sometimes a third for a hot highlight.  
**Effect on the audience:** Keeps the image clean, readable and instantly legible. Shape matters more than material, and a face reads at any distance.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The standard anime and cartoon look. Move the shadow edge itself to change mood: half the face in shadow for threat, a low soft shadow shape for warmth.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), 3D CG Animation, Kids Content, Drama Series Episode, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** If the work needs rich material feel, leather, rusted metal, heavy cloth, flat shading strips it out.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, flat two tone cel shading, hard edged shadow shapes, no gradients, clean readable colour`  
**Yields to:** `cg.arcane_painterly` — The work needs material: leather, rust, heavy cloth.  
`anime.flat_cel_shading`

### Flashback Desaturation

**Also called:** memory grade, faded flashback  
**What it is:** The memory is played with drained colour pushed toward brown or grey, with darkened edges and sometimes grain or blown out light.  
**Effect on the audience:** Tells the viewer this is the past with no caption needed, and the fading itself carries a sense of loss.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A childhood memory, the death of a parent, the origin of the hero's motivation. In shonen it lands mid fight as the reason to keep standing, and in heritage work it separates ancient time from the present.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Drama Series Episode, Feature Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Docudrama / Reenactment, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Shonen Battle (anime), Drama, Tragedy, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Melodrama, Seinen (mature anime)  
**Avoid when:** The same treatment on every memory becomes mechanical, so sometimes mark the flashback with sound or framing instead of colour.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, flashback look, drained sepia colour, darkened vignette edges, soft blown out highlights`  
**Yields to:** `anime.screen_tone` — Every memory graded alike: mark it by texture.  
`anime.flashback_desaturation`

### The Light Stripe Across the Eyes

**Also called:** eye band, light bar on the eyes  
**What it is:** A narrow horizontal band of light cutting the face at eye level with the rest in shadow, or the reverse, the face lit and a band of shadow across the eyes.  
**Effect on the audience:** Isolates the eyes from the face so they carry the whole message. It reads as threat, resolve or madness depending on the look itself.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The villain announcing intent, the hero before the charge, the character who has changed after a trauma. In noir and seinen, motivate the light with a real source, a gap in a blind, a door crack, so the scene stays grounded.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Noir / Neo-Noir, Seinen (mature anime), Thriller / Suspense, Shonen Battle (anime), Crime / Procedural, Horror, Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** Repeated on every close up it loses weight and becomes decoration, and in an open daylight scene nothing motivates it.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, a narrow horizontal band of light across the eyes with the rest of the face in deep shadow`  
**Yields to:** `size.italian` — Open daylight: isolate the eyes by framing, not light.  
`anime.light_stripe_eyes`

### The Superflat Pastel Dream

**Also called:** flat pastel dreamscape, candy dream look  
**What it is:** A scene in light pastel colour with almost no shadow, no depth and simple clean edged shapes. The space reads as a poster rather than a world.  
**Effect on the audience:** Separates the shot from reality without frightening. It reads as a gentle dream, a sanitised memory, or a world inside a head.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A dream sequence, an insert song passage inside an episode, a music video. In kids content it is easy on the eye and easy to read, and in psychological anime contrast that sweetness with harsh content to deepen the unease.

### Drawn Onomatopoeia

**Also called:** sound effect text, on screen sfx lettering  
**What it is:** The sound word is drawn inside the frame as part of the picture: large lettering that moves with the action, swelling on the hit, trembling with the shake.  
**Effect on the audience:** Makes sound visible. The feel turns graphic and direct, and the printed page enters the screen.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Comedy and youth anime, and any moment where you want sound to become part of the design. In short form watched with the sound off it is an excellent way to deliver the audio to the viewer.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Meme / Comedy Edit, Kids Content, Drama Series Episode, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Comedy, Shonen Battle (anime), Slice of Life, Dark Comedy / Satire, Sports Anime, Family, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** In a language your audience cannot read it is decoration nobody parses, and in serious drama it breaks belief.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, large hand drawn sound effect lettering integrated into the frame, swelling and shaking with the impact` Text treatment: seen, not read (`textimg.read_or_seen`); where the sound word must read letter for letter, it is set as a layer over the generated frame (`textimg.repair_not_regenerate`).  
**Yields to:** `vertical.captions` — The audience cannot read the lettering's language.  
`anime.drawn_onomatopoeia`

### Fisheye on 2D

**Also called:** drawn wide angle distortion, bulge perspective  
**What it is:** Straight lines are drawn curved, the centre swells and the edges are pulled away, imitating a fisheye lens, all of it drawn by hand.  
**Effect on the audience:** Warps the world, so the viewer feels instability or a non human point of view. The distortion itself says something is wrong.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The view of a collapsing character, the point of view of a monster, a security camera or a door peephole, the moment of vertigo. In psychological horror increase the distortion gradually across the scene rather than switching it on at once.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Psychological Horror, Horror, Surreal / Absurdist, Psychological / Arthouse, Seinen (mature anime), Thriller / Suspense, Comedy  
**Avoid when:** Too much distortion makes faces accidentally funny, and in a calm naturalistic scene there is no reason for it.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, hand drawn fisheye distortion, straight lines bowing outward, the centre bulging, edges stretched`  
**Yields to:** `anime.line_boil` — Faces must stay readable; unsettle the line instead.  
`anime.fisheye_2d`

### 2D Depth of Field Simulation

**Also called:** drawn bokeh, layer blur depth  
**What it is:** Foreground and background are drawn or treated with graded softness according to distance, sometimes with soft light circles added, so the shot reads as if shot on a fast lens.  
**Effect on the audience:** Gives drawings cinematic depth and isolates the character from the world. The image reads as more expensive and closer to live action.  
**Used for and where it works best:** An emotional close up at night or in rain, a city seen through a window, a romantic scene. In contemporary feature anime it is the standard, and in short form it lifts a face out of a busy background.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Romance, Drama, Seinen (mature anime), Coming of Age, Slice of Life, Noir / Neo-Noir, Cyberpunk  
**Avoid when:** Blurring everything but the face throws away the world you paid to paint, and in a deliberately flat style it contradicts itself.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, shallow depth of field, soft bokeh circles in the neon background, only the face in sharp focus`  
**Yields to:** `anime.bloom_hair` — A flat style, or a painted world worth keeping.  
`anime.dof_2d`

### Lens Flare on 2D

**Also called:** drawn flare, anamorphic streak on animation  
**What it is:** A drawn or composited flare: a horizontal blue streak, a chain of coloured circles, or a star of light, travelling over the drawn image with the camera or the light source.  
**Effect on the audience:** Convinces the eye that a real camera is present. It adds realism, beauty and nostalgia at once.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Sunrise and sunset, sun between buildings, a reflection on water, an explosion. In romance and youth drama put it on the emotional peak shot, and in science fiction on artificial light sources.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Feature Film, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Romance, Coming of Age, Sci-Fi, Drama, Slice of Life, Fantasy / Epic, Cyberpunk  
**Avoid when:** On every shot it tires the eye and hides the drawing you paid for, and in a dark interior nothing motivates it.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, warm anamorphic lens flare streaking across the frame as the sun breaks between the buildings`  
**Yields to:** `anime.light_stripe_eyes` — Dark interior: motivate the light from the room.  
`anime.lens_flare_2d`

### Bloom on the Hair

**Also called:** rim glow, hair light bleed  
**What it is:** The edge of the hair and shoulder is given a soft glow that bleeds into the background, the way strong backlight behaves on a real camera.  
**Effect on the audience:** Separates the character from the background and gives them a halo. The face feels warm and the person feels important.  
**Used for and where it works best:** An entrance, standing at a window or against a sunset, a confession beat. In shojo and romance it is the fastest way to make a character feel loved, and in mecha put it on metal so the machine reads as hot.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Shojo (anime romance), Romance, Drama, Slice of Life, Fantasy / Epic, Mecha (anime), Coming of Age  
**Avoid when:** Too strong and the glow eats the hair edge and destroys the drawing, and in a dark realistic scene it reads as fake.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, strong backlight blooming softly along the edge of the hair and shoulders, warm rim glow bleeding into the background`  
**Yields to:** `anime.dof_2d` — Dark realistic scene: separate by focus, not glow.  
`anime.bloom_hair`

### The Reflection in the Eye

**Also called:** eye mirror shot, reflected reveal  
**What it is:** An extreme close up of the eye with what the character is seeing reflected inside it: fire, a face, an explosion, a knife.  
**Effect on the audience:** Shows cause and reaction in a single frame. The viewer sees the event and the response to it at the same instant.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The instant of terror, discovery or silent grief. In horror and shonen it replaces two cuts, the event then the face, saving time and doubling the intensity, and in heritage work the burning city reflected in a child's eye delivers the tragedy with no blood.

**Avoid when:** If the reflection is drawn too small to read, the whole idea is lost, and do not repeat it: once an episode is enough.  
**Prompt:** `anime style, extreme close up of an eye with the burning city reflected clearly in the iris, pupil contracting`  
**Yields to:** `cut.reaction` — The reflection cannot be drawn large enough to read.

### Toon Shading

**Also called:** toon shader, NPR shading  
**What it is:** A rendering method that collapses the gradient into two or three hard edged bands and adds an outline, turning any 3D model into a drawing.  
**Effect on the audience:** Simplifies form and makes it readable. The image loses realism and gains identity and clarity.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Games, game cinematics, kids content and drawn style commercials. Keep a consistent apparent screen-space outline: taper the model-space line with distance only enough to prevent it thickening in wides. The style guide owns the near, mid and far line classes. Make the shadow a cool colour rather than black when that matches the approved palette.  
**Best in:** formats: 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Anime (Japanese 2D), Kids Content, Commercial / TVC, Explainer, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Family, Preschool / Early Learning, Shonen Battle (anime), Fantasy / Epic, Adventure, Sci-Fi, Comedy  
**Avoid when:** On a heavily detailed model, banded shading turns into a mess of lines. Simplify the model before you simplify the shading.  
**Prompt:** `anime style toon shading, two band hard edged shadows with a clean dark outline, flat readable colour`  
**Yields to:** `cg.arcane_painterly` — A detailed model you cannot simplify; paint it instead.  
`anime.toon_shading`

### The Next Episode Preview

**Also called:** next time on, preview cut

**Effect on the audience:** Pulls the viewer into next week. A familiar voice makes the promise feel personal rather than promotional.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every weekly series. Choose shots that raise a question instead of answering one, and in comedies let the preview itself be a joke unrelated to the episode, which has become a beloved convention.

### The Insert Song Montage

**Also called:** insert song sequence, song over montage  
**What it is:** A full song plays inside the episode, not in the opening or ending, over a montage of dialogue free shots: memories, a battle, a journey, a parting.  
**Effect on the audience:** Lifts the scene to a higher emotional register. The music takes over the narration, so time compresses and feeling concentrates.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The episode climax or the end of an arc: the win after a loss, the goodbye, the return home. Cut on the musical phrases rather than on the action and keep all dialogue out. **The technique is not anime-only, and this file is a bad place to look for it.** Anime named it and uses it most consistently, but *"the song takes the film over for a stretch"* is the same construction in a documentary that gives three minutes to a lament, a drama that stops for a performance, and a heritage piece built around an anthem — and a live-action director following the package would never open a file called `anime-2d.md`. The rule that matters in every one of them is the same: **cut to the musical phrase, not to the action, and put no dialogue under it.** For the two joins at either end of such a passage — where narration stops and where it returns — see `soundscape.narration_song_handover` in Phase 4, and record which clock each shot is on in the **Timing src** cell of the Phase 3 shot table.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Feature Film, Lyric Video, AI-Generated Clip, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Short Film, Video Essay | genres: Sports Anime, Shonen Battle (anime), Drama, Romance, Coming of Age, Tragedy, Melodrama, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Musical  
**Avoid when:** Used every episode it loses its value and becomes filler, and do not bury important dialogue under it: the song will swallow it.  
**Prompt:** `anime style emotional montage with no dialogue, cuts landing on the musical phrases, memories intercut with the present`  
**Yields to:** `sync.instrumental_break_dialogue_window` — Dialogue must survive inside the song passage.  
`anime.insert_song_montage`

### Ben-Day Dots and Misregistration

**Also called:** halftone print dots, colour offset, print artefacts  
**What it is:** Coloured printing dots are laid over the image with a slight offset between the colour channels, red and cyan pulled apart, like the misregistration of an old comic book press.  
**Effect on the audience:** Turns the image into a printed comic page. The feel is papery, nostalgic and slightly unstable, as if the world itself were printed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Superhero worlds, memory or dream shots, transitions between dimensions. Increase the offset in moments of tension or danger so the image seems to vibrate, and return it to zero when the scene settles.  
**Best in:** formats: 3D CG Animation, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Music Video, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Superhero, Action, Sci-Fi, Comedy, Adventure, Fantasy / Epic, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** Large dots over text or a small face destroy legibility, and in realistic work there is nothing to justify them. And the rising offset described above is a high-contrast pattern deliberately made to vibrate: at the point where it reads as an alternation rather than as a texture, how fast it may go and when it has to stop belong to `safety.photosensitive_flash`, not to this card.  
**Prompt:** `comic book print look, halftone Ben-Day dots over the image with slight red and cyan channel misregistration`  
**Yields to:** `anime.screen_tone` — Monochrome or manga register: grey halftone, no offset.

### The Klasky Csupo Distortion

**Also called:** asymmetric character design, ugly cute style  
**What it is:** Deliberately asymmetric character design: two eyes at different sizes and heights, large noses, spindly limbs and nervous wobbling lines. It is the house style of Klasky Csupo.  
**Effect on the audience:** Makes characters feel real and strange at once. Lovable ugliness gives instant personality and keeps the image away from packaged prettiness.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Kids and family comedy that wants to stand apart from the polished look, and any work told from a child's point of view where the world should feel larger and odder. ### The Flat Vector Cartoon Look

**Also called:** modern flat cartoon, vector shape style  
**What it is:** Characters built from simple vector shapes with an even weight outline or none at all, flat bright colour, and backgrounds in soft gradients. It became the dominant television cartoon look of the last decade.  
**Effect on the audience:** Keeps the image friendly, clear and easy to read on any screen, phones included. Simple shapes let emotion arrive faster.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Kids and family series, platform content and advertising. Give every character one signature colour and a unique silhouette so they read instantly, because this style loses distinction fast when shapes converge.  
**Best in:** formats: 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Kids Content, Drama Series Episode, Commercial / TVC, Explainer, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Comedy, Family, Preschool / Early Learning, Adventure, Fantasy / Epic, Slice of Life, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** If the work needs real horror or heavy drama, the vector smoothness softens everything.  
**Prompt:** `modern flat vector cartoon style, simple shapes, even weight outlines, bright flat colour, soft gradient background`  
**Yields to:** `anime.flat_cel_shading` — Real horror or heavy drama: vector softens it.  
`anime.cn_flat_vector`

### The Storybook Illustration Look

**Also called:** picture book style, illustrated film look  
**What it is:** The film is built like the pages of a picture book: flat shapes in a limited palette, paper or cloth texture, brushed shadows, and centred painterly compositions.  
**Effect on the audience:** Delivers warmth and the feeling of a told tale. The viewer feels someone is reading to them and accepts fantasy and abstraction without question.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Fairy tales, kids content and heritage work retelling an old legend. In heritage documentary it is the cleanest way to show a scene with no archaeological evidence behind it, because it declares itself as illustration rather than reconstruction.  
**Best in:** formats: 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Kids Content, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Feature Film, Short Film, Explainer, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Fairy Tale, Family, Preschool / Early Learning, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Fantasy / Epic, Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** If the work needs tension or real danger, the papery softness cancels it.  
**Prompt:** `storybook illustration animation, flat shapes in a limited palette, paper texture, brushed shadows, centred painterly composition`  
**Yields to:** `doc.dramatised_reconstruction` — The evidence exists and the scene may be reconstructed.  
`anime.storybook_look`

### Squash on Landing

**Also called:** landing squash, impact settle  
**What it is:** A fixed formula for any landing: stretch on the way down, a hard squash at the moment of contact, an expanding dust ring, then a small rebound and settle.  
**Effect on the audience:** Gives the character weight and retroactively establishes the height of the jump. The deeper the squash, the higher the audience believes the fall was.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every landing in cartoon, anime and 3D: a jump, a flier touching down, a heavy character arriving. In kids content exaggerate the squash and the dust, and in mecha reduce the squash and raise the camera shake instead, because metal does not compress.  
**Best in:** formats: 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Kids Content, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Commercial / TVC, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Comedy, Family, Action, Shonen Battle (anime), Superhero, Preschool / Early Learning, Adventure  
**Avoid when:** Squashing a rigid or armoured body destroys credibility, and with no rebound after the squash the motion feels unfinished.  
**Prompt:** `cartoon landing, stretch on the descent, hard squash on contact with an expanding dust ring, small rebound and settle`  
**Yields to:** `move.impact_shake` — Metal does not compress; shake the camera instead.  
`anime.squash_on_landing`
