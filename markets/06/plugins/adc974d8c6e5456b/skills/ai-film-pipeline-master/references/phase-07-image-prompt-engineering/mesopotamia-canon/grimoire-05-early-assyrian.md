## 5. The Early Assyrian Period (Ninevite V, approx. 2900 BC - 2025 BC)

### 1. Environment, Flora & Fauna

- Weather & Sky Mood: The sky is a vast, unforgiving, pale canopy, washed out by the glare of the northern Mesopotamian sun. The wind is relentless, carrying the sharp scent of dry earth and crushed limestone.

- Terrain: The city of Ashur is just a small, precarious settlement clinging to a sheer, rocky bluff. Below, the violent, churning, muddy-brown waters of the Tigris River cut through the dry, undulating scrubland.

- Flora & The Micro-Detail: The earth is stubbornly farmed. A single grain of einkorn wheat in a farmer's hand is rough to the touch, its pale, golden-brown husk covered in microscopic, scratching hairs, catching the dust-filtered sunlight. Thorny caper bushes cling to the cliff faces, and wild licorice roots grow in the damp river mud.

- Fauna: Flocks of small, wiry, black-and-white sheep graze on the sparse yellow grass. Sleek, semi-feral pariah dogs with curled tails and pointed ears scavenge near the city walls. High above, solitary osprey dive into the dangerous river currents for fish.

### 2. Architecture & Infrastructure

- City Layout: There are no colossal megaprojects yet. Ashur is a dense, claustrophobic cluster of modest mudbrick homes built tightly together for defense against highland raiders.

- Materials & Textures: The foundational visual is the Plano-Convex Brick. Unlike the flat bricks of later eras, these early mudbricks are flat on the bottom and distinctly curved on the top, resembling a loaf of bread. They are laid in a striking "herringbone" pattern, creating a textured, almost woven look to the pale, sun-baked walls.

- The Early Temple: The earliest shrines to the goddess Ishtar are humble. Rough stone foundations support uneven mud walls coated in a thin, chalky white plaster that flakes away at the base, exposing the dark clay beneath.

### 3. Demographics & Physical Traits

- Physical Features: The early Assyrians (mixing with indigenous Subartians) are hardened survivors. Skin tones are a deeply weathered, ruddy bronze. Faces are angular and gaunt from frequent crop failures, with dark, intense eyes.

- Hairstyles & Beards (Men): The aesthetic is highly influenced by the Sumerians to the south. Priests often shave their heads and faces completely with rough copper blades, their scalps nicked and shining with sesame oil. Common men wear rough, unkempt dark hair and short, practical beards, entirely lacking the oiled, geometric perfection of later empires. The shaved-scalp, shaved-face look here follows the Sumerian/Ur III convention as a plausible carryover, not a confirmed Early Assyrian source; where a named contemporary seal, figurine or excavation reference is available for a specific priest or common man, follow that reference instead.

- Hairstyles (Women): Hair is tied back fiercely with simple strips of woven linen or leather, keeping it out of the dust during grueling agricultural labor.

### 4. Fashion & Textiles

- Fabrics & Textures: The luxurious, dyed linens of the future do not exist here. The visual is dominated by the early kaunakes - heavy, scratchy skirts made of heavily tufted sheep or goat skin, the fleece left intact to create a layered, leaf-like texture.

- Clothing Cuts: Men wear the fleece skirts wrapped tightly around the waist, secured by a woven woolen belt, leaving the chest bare to the sun. Women wear simple, tubular garments of coarse, unbleached wool that scratch the skin, draped over one shoulder.

- Colors & Dyes: The palette is the color of the earth: dull, unbleached off-whites, dirty sheep-fleece browns, and pale, dusty ochres.
- Jewelry: No gold. Ornamentation is brutalist and natural. Heavy necklaces of roughly polished animal bone, pierced river shells, and early, dull-green, oxidized copper pins used to hold garments closed.

### 5. Economy & Daily Life

- Tools & The Harvest: The absolute reliance on stone. Farmers cut the golden wheat using sickle blades made of jagged, chipped flint or dark obsidian, tightly wedged into curved pieces of wood and secured with sticky, black bitumen.

- Pottery: The visual hallmark is Ninevite 5 Pottery, and **it has two successive looks, not one — this entry used to give only the second.** The **earlier** phase, roughly 3000 to 2750 BC, is *defined* by **painted** ware: dark monochrome designs on a pale ground, drawn freehand, and it is what the period is identified by in the excavation reports. The **later** phase is the incised and excised ware described below, which gradually replaces it. Pick by where in the period you are; showing painted pots and incised pots side by side in one frame is a five-hundred-year error. In the later phase the pale, grayish-yellow clay is deeply incised with sharp bone tools before firing, creating complex, deeply grooved geometric patterns - zigzags, hatched triangles, and stylized birds - that catch the shadows of the hearth fire.

- Diet: Extremely basic. Coarse, gritty barley bread baked directly on hot stones, strongly flavored with pungent wild garlic and raw onions.

### 6. Religion & Art

- Early Statuary: The art is not about imperial intimidation; it is about desperate piety. Small votive statues are carved from soft, pale local gypsum or limestone.

- Artistic Details: The carving is stiff and slightly crude. The figures stand with hands clasped in prayer, but their most arresting feature is the oversized, staring eyes. The eye sockets are violently hollowed out and filled with stark white shell, the pupils drilled and filled with black pitch, giving the statues a look of perpetual, terrified awe in the dim temple light.

- The God Ashur: He is not yet the winged warrior. At this stage, the god Ashur is literally the physical mountain spur that the city sits upon. The land itself is the deity.

### 7. Transport & Mobility

- Overland: The wheel is in its infancy here. Solid, heavy wooden discs made of three planks pinned together are attached to crude, incredibly heavy wooden sledges or rough carts, pulled excruciatingly slowly by massive, plodding oxen.

- Beasts of Burden: The donkey is the primary beast of burden. They are small, stubborn, and wear crude harnesses of braided esparto grass, carrying tightly woven reed baskets filled with river clay or grain.

- River Transport: The Tigris is too violent for elegant boats. Early Assyrians use the kelek - a simple raft of rough-hewn logs lashed tightly over a bed of inflated, pale-yellow, stitched goat skins, bobbing violently in the white-water rapids.

### 8. The Military Machine

- Force Structure: There is no professional army. There is only the town militia - desperate farmers picking up their tools to defend their grain silos from highland raiders. Battles are brief, chaotic, and intensely intimate skirmishes in the dust.

- Weapons: Iron and bronze are completely absent. Weapons are pure, heavy, dull-orange Copper. Men wield flat copper axes cast in simple open molds, tied to wooden shafts with leather thongs. Many simply fight with heavy wooden clubs inset with sharp flint, or use leather slings to hurl smooth, dense river stones with skull-crushing force.

- Armor & Shields: There is zero metal armor. A warrior might wrap his torso in extra layers of hardened leather or thick felt. Shields are simple affairs: rough wooden frames stretched with the untanned, hairy hide of a cow, smelling of dried blood and dust.
