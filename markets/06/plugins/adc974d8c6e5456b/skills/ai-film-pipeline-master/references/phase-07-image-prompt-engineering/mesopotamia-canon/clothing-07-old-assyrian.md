## Part IV. Assyria. The Old Assyrian Period

_The earliest dress of Ashur, through the Early and Old Assyrian ages, approximately 2000 to 1360 BC, the time when the city of Ashur lived above all by trade. Its merchants drove donkey caravans of tin and fine Ashur woolen cloth north to the colony at Kanesh and across Anatolia, while the City Assembly governed at home. The dress is simpler and earlier than the later empire: wrapped woolen robes, fringed shawls, plain belts and bronze pins, the cloth itself the city's chief wealth. One hundred and twenty-five garments follow._


### Old Assyrian: Children, Birth to Eighteen (Nos. 1 to 15)

**1. Newborn's Wool Swaddle.** _(Common, Ashur.)_ An undyed sheep-wool band wound in a spiral so only the face is free, tucked and tied with a wool cord. The common newborn wrap of early Ashur.

**2. Merchant-House Linen Swaddle.** _(Trading family.)_ A soft bleached linen band with a thin fringed edge, a carnelian bead at the throat, for the child of one of the trading houses that made Ashur rich.

**3. Poor Quarter Reed Swaddle.** _(Poor.)_ Coarse undyed wool and reused cloth bound with twisted reed cord, bare-headed, the wrap of the poor families of the old city.

**4. Lambswool Winter Bundle.** _(Cold season.)_ Carded lambswool over an inner linen band, bound with a felted wool sash in cream and brown, against the cold of the northern hills.

**5. Bead-Guarded Newborn Wrap.** _(Protective.)_ A plain wool swaddle with a string of faience and cowrie beads knotted into the chest cord to guard the newborn against the night demons.

**6. Infant's Linen Shift.** _(Everyday, 6 to 12 months.)_ A tiny sleeveless linen shift to the knees, undyed with a faint edge-stripe, gathered at the neck with a flax drawstring.

**7. Toddler's Short Wool Tunic.** _(Toddler, everyday.)_ A plain knee-length sleeveless wool tunic belted with a woven cord, the child barefoot. The plain dress of the small Ashur child.

**8. Toddler's Fringed Wrap.** _(Toddler, modest means.)_ A small wool wrap with a fringed edge and a thin cord belt, the head shaved with a single side-lock.

**9. Little Girl's Beaded Wrap.** _(Girl, 4 to 7.)_ A soft wool wrap pinned over one shoulder with a short string of faience and carnelian beads.

**10. Little Boy's Plain Kilt.** _(Boy, 4 to 7.)_ A short wrapped wool kilt with a cord belt, the chest bare, the head shaved with a side-lock.

**11. Merchant's-Son Boy's Tunic.** _(Boy, 7 to 12.)_ A clean wool tunic with a fringed edge and a small seal on a cord, learning the weights, the silver and the letters of his father's trade.

**12. Weaver-Girl's Gown.** _(Girl, 10 to 15.)_ A plain belted gown with the sleeves bound, spindle and thread at hand, weaving the fine Ashur cloth that the caravans carried to Anatolia.

**13. Apprentice-Trader Youth's Robe.** _(Young man, 15 to 18.)_ A wrapped wool robe and a dust-cloak, a satchel and a first seal, setting out with the donkey caravan to learn the long road north.

**14. Young Woman's Wrapped Gown.** _(Young woman, 15 to 18.)_ An ankle gown drawn over one shoulder and belted, a beaded necklace, the hair dressed, near the age of marriage.

**15. Ashur-Temple Festival Child's Robe.** _(Wealthy child, feast.)_ A soft pale linen tunic with a woven border and a beaded carnelian collar, a fillet, for a merchant's child at the festival of the god Ashur.

### Old Assyrian: Men by Age, Eighteen to Eighty (Nos. 16 to 37)

**16. Young Man's Wrapped Wool Robe.** _(18 to 30, everyday.)_ A simple wool robe wound about the body with a fringed edge and a plain belt, a trimmed beard. The everyday dress of a young man of early Ashur.

**17. Young Trader's Caravan Robe.** _(18 to 30, trade.)_ A sturdy belted robe under a dust-cloak, a head-cloth, a seal and a tablet-pouch, a money-belt of silver, riding with the tin-and-textile caravan toward Kanesh.

**18. Bridegroom's Linen Robe.** _(18 to 30, wedding.)_ A fresh wrapped linen robe with a fringed shawl, a fillet on oiled hair, new sandals and a ring, for the marriage feast.

**19. Young Worker's Short Kilt.** _(18 to 30, labor.)_ A short coarse wool kilt and a cord belt, the chest bare, working the fields and the wharves of the river city.

**20. Summer Linen Wrap.** _(18 to 30, hot season.)_ A light undyed linen wrap knotted at the waist, the shoulders bare and oiled, cool for the summer heat.

**21. Festival Robe at Ashur.** _(18 to 30, festival.)_ A wrapped robe dyed and bordered with a tasseled edge and a bronze armlet, the beard combed, for the festival of the city god.

**22. Young Man's Fringed Shawl.** _(18 to 30, formal.)_ A plain robe under a fringed wool shawl drawn over one shoulder, worn to call upon a master merchant or an elder.

**23. Young Caravan-Guard's Tunic.** _(18 to 30, escort.)_ A short belted tunic, a leather jerkin, a dagger and a sling, a dust-cloak, guarding the laden donkeys on the long northern road.

**24. Merchant's Long Fringed Robe.** _(30 to 50, everyday.)_ A full wool robe to the calf with a fringed edge, soberly belted, the beard full. The settled dress of an established Ashur trader.

**25. Master Merchant's Robe with Seal.** _(30 to 50, formal.)_ A fine fringed robe and a folded shawl, a heavy cylinder seal and a tablet-case, the head of a trading house that sends caravans abroad and keeps agents at Kanesh.

**26. Father's Work Tunic.** _(30 to 50, labor.)_ A sturdy knee tunic and a wide belt with tools, the sleeves bound, practical for the day's work and the raising of a family.

**27. Winter Wool Mantle.** _(30 to 50, cold season.)_ A heavy fulled-wool mantle over a long tunic, a rounded felt cap, the folds deep against the northern cold.

**28. Banquet Robe.** _(30 to 50, feast.)_ A bordered robe and a fringed shawl, an armlet and a cup, reclining among the merchants of the city at the feast.

**29. Temple Offering Robe.** _(30 to 50, devotional.)_ A clean fringed robe and a folded shawl, the beard combed, bringing an offering and a tithe of trade to the god Ashur.

**30. Caravan-Master's Travel Robe.** _(30 to 50, road.)_ A long sturdy robe under a hooded dust-cloak, a staff and a heavy purse, leading the season's great caravan over the mountains to the colonies.

**31. City-Assembly Member's Robe.** _(30 to 50, governance.)_ A dignified fringed robe and a seal, taking his seat in the City Assembly of Ashur, the gathering of merchants and elders that ruled with the city-ruler.

**32. Senior Merchant's Sober Robe.** _(50 to 65, everyday.)_ A long fringed robe in muted tones, a plain belt, the beard mostly grey, the bearing of a man long prosperous in trade.

**33. Elder of the Assembly's Robe.** _(50 to 65, standing.)_ A full fringed robe and a smooth staff, an old respected voice in the City Assembly and at the gate.

**34. Old Master-Trader's Robe.** _(50 to 65, retired.)_ A fine softened robe and a folded shawl, the seal and the routes passed to a son, reckoning past ventures by the hearth.

**35. Winter Fleece Robe.** _(50 to 65, cold season.)_ A long tunic under a fleece-lined mantle, a deep felt cap, the hands tucked by the hearth in the cold months.

**36. Devotional Robe of the Aged.** _(65 to 80, temple.)_ A clean soft robe and shawl, the white beard combed, seated long before the god Ashur, a lifetime of trade and tithe behind him.

**37. Mourning Wrap.** _(Any age, grief.)_ A coarse undyed sack-wool wrap, the chest bare and dusted with ash, the fine robe set aside, mourning a partner of the house or a child.

### Old Assyrian: Women by Age, Eighteen to Eighty (Nos. 38 to 59)

**38. Young Woman's Wrapped Wool Gown.** _(18 to 30, everyday.)_ An ankle gown of wool drawn over one shoulder and belted, the hair bound back. The daily dress of a young woman of Ashur.

**39. Bride's Adorned Gown.** _(18 to 30, wedding.)_ A dyed gown with a beaded collar of carnelian and lapis, a fillet and earrings, the hair oiled and scented, for the marriage feast.

**40. Young Wife's Nursing Gown.** _(18 to 30, motherhood.)_ A loose wrapped gown of soft wool, easily opened at the breast, belted high, a protective bead-string for the infant at the shoulder.

**41. Young Weaver-Wife's Working Gown.** _(18 to 30, trade cloth.)_ A plain belted gown with the sleeves bound, loom and spindle close, weaving the fine cloth her husband's caravan will sell in the north.

**42. Festival Gown at Ashur.** _(18 to 30, festival.)_ A brightly dyed gown, a tasseled shawl and jewelry of faience, carnelian and gold, the eyes kohl-lined, for the festival of the city god.

**43. Summer Linen Sheath.** _(18 to 30, hot season.)_ A light sleeveless linen sheath to the ankle, undyed and cool, a thin beaded cord at the neck, the hair tied up against the heat.

**44. Merchant-Wife's Managing Gown.** _(30 to 50, everyday.)_ A good belted gown with a seal at the waist, keeping the house, the stores and the firm's accounts while her husband is away for months on the road.

**45. Weaver-Mistress's Gown with Loom.** _(30 to 50, workshop.)_ A practical gown with the sleeves bound, directing the household weaving, where the women produced the textiles that were Ashur's chief export.

**46. Matron's Fringed Shawl Gown.** _(30 to 50, formal.)_ A long gown under a fringed wool shawl over the shoulders, hoop earrings and a strung necklace, for the temple and the feast.

**47. Household Manager's Gown with Seal.** _(30 to 50, authority.)_ A belted gown from whose waist hang a pouch and a personal seal, the woman who held property and signed her own tablets, as the Ashur wives did.

**48. Mother's Gown with Carrying Sling.** _(30 to 50, motherhood.)_ A practical wool gown belted high, a wide woven sling for a small child, an amulet pinned at the shoulder.

**49. Festival Matron's Adornment.** _(30 to 50, festival.)_ A richly dyed gown layered with carnelian, faience and gold, heavy earrings and a thin diadem, kohl-lined eyes, a tasseled shawl.

**50. Winter Wool-and-Felt Layers.** _(30 to 50, cold season.)_ A long wool gown under a thick felted mantle drawn over head and shoulders, a felt-lined wrap at the throat, in deep browns.

**51. Mourning Gown of Coarse Wool.** _(30 to 50, grief.)_ A coarse undyed gown, the jewelry set aside, the hair loosed and dusted with ash, the cloth torn at the breast.

**52. Senior Merchant-Wife's Full Gown.** _(50 to 65, everyday.)_ A full floor-length gown of good wool, soberly belted, the greying hair bound and a shawl over it, long mistress of a trading household.

**53. Matriarch's Fringed Mantle.** _(50 to 65, formal.)_ A long gown beneath a great fringed mantle over head and shoulders, heavy old necklaces from her youth, broad earrings.

**54. Mistress of the Household Gown.** _(50 to 65, authority.)_ A good-wool gown with a heavy belt of keys, seal and pouch, commanding the stores, the weaving and the younger women of the house.

**55. Grandmother's Practical Gown.** _(50 to 65, daily care.)_ A soft, easy gown belted high, a shawl always at the shoulder for a grandchild, an old amulet at the breast.

**56. Banquet Gown of an Elder Lady.** _(50 to 65, feast.)_ A deep-dyed gown with layered gold and carnelian, a diadem over a fine shawl, scented and kohl-lined, honored at the feast.

**57. Winter Fleece-Wrapped Elder.** _(65 to 80, cold season.)_ A heavy gown under a felted mantle, a fleece wrap over the knees, slippers and a pin at the throat, by the fire.

**58. Mourning Gown of an Elder Woman.** _(65 to 80, grief.)_ A coarse undyed gown, jewelry removed, the white hair loosed and ashed, the cloth rent. Grave and dignified grief.

**59. Aged Devotee's Robe.** _(65 to 80, devotional.)_ A fine soft pale gown and a gentle shawl, seated long in the temple's calm, a lifetime of household and prayer behind her.

### Old Assyrian: The Merchant Houses and the Anatolian Trade (Nos. 60 to 84)

**60. Head of a Trading Firm's Robe.** _(Master merchant.)_ A fine long fringed robe and a folded shawl, a great cylinder seal and a tablet-case, the master of a house that sends caravans north and keeps agents abroad.

**61. Traveling Agent's Caravan Robe.** _(The road north.)_ A sturdy belted robe under a dust-cloak, a head-cloth, a seal and a money-belt, riding the long route with the tin and the cloth toward Anatolia.

**62. Resident Agent's Robe at Kanesh.** _(The colony.)_ A neat fringed robe and a seal, the firm's man settled in the karum of Kanesh, selling the goods and gathering the silver among the Anatolians.

**63. Caravan Leader's Travel Dress.** _(Caravan head.)_ A long belted robe, a hooded cloak, a staff and a horn, leading the season's strings of laden donkeys over the mountain passes.

**64. Donkey-Driver's Wrap.** _(Transport.)_ A short coarse wrap and a head-cloth, a goad and a water-skin, driving the black donkeys that carried the bales of tin and textiles.

**65. Packer-Loader's Tunic.** _(Loading.)_ A short sturdy tunic with the sleeves bound, roping and balancing the heavy sealed bales onto the donkeys before the dawn start.

**66. Investor-Financier's Fine Robe.** _(The backer.)_ A rich fringed robe and a heavy seal, a tablet of shares, the man who funds a venture from the city and waits at home for the silver to return.

**67. Contract-Scribe's Robe.** _(Trade tablets.)_ A clean robe and a satchel of styluses and damp tablets, drawing up the loans, partnerships and shipping notes that ran the whole trade.

**68. Silver-Weigher's Robe.** _(The returns.)_ A sober good robe, a fine balance and a set of stone weights, weighing out the silver and gold brought back from the north.

**69. Toll-Collector's Robe.** _(Road tax.)_ A clean robe with a seal and a tablet, posted at a pass or a palace gate to take the rulers' toll on the passing caravans.

**70. Smuggler-Trader's Plain Dress.** _(The narrow track.)_ A plain inconspicuous robe and a light pack, slipping the costly tin along the back paths to avoid the tolls, as the letters sometimes confess.

**71. Anatolian Partner's Mixed Dress.** _(Local associate.)_ The dress of the highland country, a belted tunic and a heavy cloak, the local Kanesh partner who shared in the Ashur merchant's ventures.

**72. Merchant's Representative's Robe.** _(Proxy.)_ A respectable fringed robe and a seal of trust, acting and signing in his master's name while the master stays in the city.

**73. Textile-Buyer's Robe.** _(Sourcing cloth.)_ A practical robe and a measuring rod, moving among the weaving households to buy the fine Ashur cloth for the export caravans.

**74. Tin-Merchant's Robe.** _(Tin trade.)_ A sturdy robe and a seal, dealing in the precious tin brought from the east and sent on to the bronze-smiths of Anatolia.

**75. Warehouse-Keeper's Tunic.** _(Storage.)_ A clean tunic and a tally-tablet, the sleeves bound, guarding and recording the bales of wool, cloth and metal in the firm's store.

**76. Caravan Provisioner's Tunic.** _(Supply.)_ A short belted tunic, a list-tablet and a measure, laying in the grain, fodder and water-skins for the long road ahead.

**77. Trade Money-Lender's Robe.** _(Credit.)_ A quality robe, a locked tablet-box of loans at interest, a seal and a heavy purse, financing the ventures of younger traders.

**78. Junior Clerk's Tunic.** _(Apprentice record-keeper.)_ A plain clean tunic, a stylus behind the ear, copying letters and accounts in the bustling office of the trading house.

**79. Merchant-Widow's Managing Gown.** _(Woman, head of firm.)_ A dignified gown and a seal, a widow carrying on her late husband's trade, holding the contracts and directing the agents herself.

**80. Returning Merchant's Travel-Worn Robe.** _(Homecoming.)_ A dust-worn fringed robe and a heavy purse of silver, the road behind him, coming home to the city after a long season in the north.

**81. Rich Merchant's Festival Robe.** _(Wealth shown.)_ A fine bordered robe of imported dyed cloth, gold armlets and a tasseled belt, the prosperity of the trade displayed at the city feast.

**82. Caravan Guard-Captain's Dress.** _(Escort command.)_ A belted tunic and a leather jerkin, a sword and a bow, a dust-cloak, leading the armed guard that kept the valuable loads safe on the road.

**83. Quay-Trader's Tunic.** _(River transport.)_ A short belted tunic, a tally-tablet and a coiled rope, handling the goods that came and went by boat along the Tigris.

**84. Master of the Karum's Robe.** _(Colony head.)_ A fine fringed robe and a great seal, the respected head of the merchant colony's own assembly, settling the traders' disputes far from home.

### Old Assyrian: Social Classes and Common Trades (Nos. 85 to 102)

**85. Field Laborer's Loincloth.** _(Poor, man.)_ Bare to the waist, a short rough wool cloth knotted at the hip, the feet bare, a folded cloth on the head against the sun.

**86. Water-Carrier's Shoulder Cloth.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A plain undyed shift with a thick folded pad on one shoulder for the jar, a head-cloth, bare feet, carrying water from the river.

**87. Washerwoman's Tucked Gown.** _(Poor, woman.)_ A coarse gown hitched and tucked for wading, the sleeves bound, the hair wrapped, beating cloth clean at the river.

**88. Poor Widow's Patched Gown.** _(Poor, older woman.)_ A much-mended undyed gown, a thin worn shawl, no jewelry but a single clay bead, grey with washing and years.

**89. Bondservant's Plain Shift.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A knee-length coarse wool shift, undyed and corded, the hair cropped close. The household bondservant.

**90. Potter's Clay-Dusted Tunic.** _(Trade, man.)_ A worn tunic powdered with clay, the sleeves rolled, the hands wet and grey at the turning wheel.

**91. Smith's Apron Dress.** _(Trade, man.)_ A short tunic under a scorched-leather apron, the forearms wrapped, working the bronze of tools and weapons from the traded tin.

**92. Carpenter's Work Tunic.** _(Trade, man.)_ A sturdy tunic and a pocketed apron, an adze and chisels, building house, cart and boat.

**93. Leatherworker's Apron Dress.** _(Trade, man.)_ A tunic under a thick leather apron, awls and a knife, making the harness, bags and saddle-packs the caravans needed.

**94. Baker's Flour-Dusted Tunic.** _(Trade, man or woman.)_ A tunic powdered with flour, the sleeves bound and a cloth over the hair, baking the loaves of the city.

**95. Brewer-Woman's Working Gown.** _(Trade, woman.)_ A belted gown with the sleeves tied and a cloth over the hair, tending the vats and the bread of the brewing craft.

**96. Fisherman's River Wrap.** _(Trade, man.)_ A minimal wrap for the boat, a casting-net over one shoulder, the legs bare, a reed hat and a fish-spear.

**97. Shepherd's Fleece Cloak.** _(Trade, man.)_ A coarse tunic under a sheepskin cloak, a crook and a sling, watching the flocks whose wool was the city's living.

**98. Wool-Comber's Tunic.** _(Textile, man or woman.)_ A plain tunic dusted with fiber, combing and carding the fleece into the soft wool the weavers would turn into export cloth.

**99. Fuller's Wet Tunic.** _(Textile, man.)_ A tunic hitched and damp, the arms bare, treading, washing and finishing the woven cloth to ready it for the caravan bales.

**100. Townsman's Clean Robe.** _(Middle, man.)_ A decent wrapped wool robe with a fringed edge and a woven belt, a trimmed beard, leather sandals. The respectable everyday townsman.

**101. Shopkeeper's Wife's Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A good-wool gown with a fringed shawl, a few faience and silver beads, a small household seal at the belt.

**102. Middle Daughter's Festival Tunic.** _(Middle, young woman.)_ A linen gown with a dyed border, a beaded collar, small earrings and a fillet. Modest finery for a feast day.

### Old Assyrian: Soldiers and the City Watch (Nos. 103 to 110)

**103. City-Watch Spearman.** _(Wall guard.)_ A plain wool tunic and a wide belt, a leather cap, a spear and a round shield, standing the watch on the walls of Ashur. The trading city kept a guard, not yet a great army.

**104. Gate-Guard of Ashur.** _(Gate watch.)_ A belted tunic and a leather cap, a spear and a seal-checked tablet, watching the goods and the strangers that passed the city gate.

**105. Caravan-Escort Soldier.** _(Road escort.)_ A short tunic and a leather jerkin, a bow and a dagger, a dust-cloak, marching with the caravan to guard the tin and silver on the road.

**106. Ruler's Guard.** _(The ruler's men.)_ A clean fringed tunic and a bronze cap, a spear and a fine shield, standing about the ruler-steward of Ashur in hall and procession.

**107. Levy Militiaman.** _(Called up.)_ His own field kilt and a leather cap, a spear from the temple store, a citizen of Ashur called out for a season's defense.

**108. Light Bowman.** _(Skirmisher.)_ A short plain tunic, a simple bow and a quiver, light on his feet, guarding the flocks and the approaches of the city.

**109. Watch-Officer's Dress.** _(Command.)_ A finer tunic with a marked belt, a bronze cap and a sword, ordering the watch of a wall or a gate.

**110. Veteran Watchman.** _(Old hand.)_ A worn tunic and a dented cap, an old trusted spear, drilling the young men of the watch in the muster yard.

### Old Assyrian: Rulers, Court and Temple at Ashur (Nos. 111 to 120)

**111. The Ruler-Steward's Robe.** _(Ishiakkum.)_ A long fringed robe and a folded shawl, a seal and a staff, worn by the ruler who governed Ashur as steward and vice-regent of the god Ashur, the true king of the city.

**112. The Ruler's Consort's Gown.** _(Lady of Ashur.)_ A fine fringed gown and a shawl, gold and lapis at the throat and ears, a thin diadem, the lady of the ruler's house.

**113. The Eponym Official's Robe.** _(Limmu.)_ A dignified fringed robe and a seal, the man chosen to give his name to the year, holding the honored annual office by which Ashur counted its time.

**114. City Assembly Presider's Robe.** _(Governance.)_ A grave long robe and a great seal, presiding over the City Assembly of merchants and elders that ruled the affairs of Ashur and its trade.

**115. Palace Steward's Robe.** _(Household.)_ A clean long robe, a staff and a seal, ordering the household, the stores and the workers of the ruler's house.

**116. Ruler's Scribe's Robe.** _(Chancery.)_ A fine robe marking service, a seal and a leather document-case, keeping the letters and the treaties of the ruler and the City.

**117. High Priest of Ashur's Ritual Robe.** _(Chief priest.)_ A long fringed robe with a sacred border, a tall fillet, a long oiled beard, anointing oil and the emblem of Ashur in hand, before the god of the city.

**118. Temple Administrator's Robe.** _(Estate.)_ A clean long robe, a seal and a tally-tablet, ordering the lands, herds and offerings of the temple of Ashur.

**119. The God Ashur's Image Vestments.** _(Cult statue.)_ The rich garments in which the statue of Ashur was clothed and ceremonially changed, the holiest cloth of the city, made of the finest dyed and fringed weave.

**120. Ruler's Heir's Robe.** _(Successor.)_ A rich fringed robe just below the ruler's, a seal and a fillet, the son marked to succeed as steward of the god and the City.

### Old Assyrian: Garment Types and Materials (Nos. 121 to 125)

**121. The Wrapped Wool Robe.** _(Signature garment.)_ The plain early Assyrian robe, a length of wool wound about the body with a fringed edge, simpler than the embroidered robes of the later empire.

**122. The Fringed Shawl.** _(Drape.)_ A rectangular wool shawl with a fringed edge, drawn over one shoulder or the head, the common added wrap of Old Assyrian dress.

**123. Ashur Export Cloth.** _(Signature material.)_ The fine woven woolen textile of Ashur, woven mostly by the women of the city, the chief export carried by caravan to Anatolia and the foundation of the city's wealth.

**124. The Caravan Dust-Cloak.** _(Travel garment.)_ A heavy hooded wool cloak that turned dust, sun and cold on the long road north, the constant companion of the traveling merchant.

**125. Old Assyrian Wool and Linen.** _(Materials.)_ The wool of the city's flocks and the flax of its fields, woven plain or fringed, the staple cloth of early Ashur in natural and lightly dyed tones.
