# BOOK 2: Trade, Law & Chariots (Part 2)
## 10. The Middle Babylonian / Kassite Period (approx. 1595-1155 BC)

Here is the encyclopedic visual breakdown for the Middle Babylonian / Kassite Period. This era is defined by a massive cultural synthesis: foreign mountain conquerors (the Kassites) who completely adopted Babylonian high culture, ushered in centuries of stable, centralized peace, and brought the true "Age of the Horse" to the southern plains.

### 1. Environment, Flora & Fauna

- Weather & Sky Mood: A hazy, baking environment. The sky is often a bleached, dusty white at the horizon, blending into a pale blue zenith. The heat creates shimmering mirages over the vast, newly restored irrigation networks.
- Terrain: The lush, managed alluvial plains of Babylonia contrast with carefully maintained, fenced paddocks of rich green alfalfa and barley, grown specifically to feed a new, prized animal.
- Flora: Dense groves of date palms provide deep, dappled shade. Fields of flowering sesame plants (with pale, bell-shaped white and pink flowers) ripple in the hot breeze, grown extensively for oil.
- Fauna: The Horse (sissu) is the undisputed star of this era. They are slightly smaller than modern breeds, wiry, with thick necks, heavy manes, and coats of bay, chestnut, and dun. Marsh boars root in the southern reeds, and trained hunting dogs (early mastiff types) pant in the courtyards of the elite.

### 2. Architecture & Infrastructure

- Megaprojects: The brand-new capital city, Dur-Kurigalzu (modern Aqar Quf). The defining visual is its massive, sprawling ziggurat.
- Materials & Textures: The ziggurat's core is exposed in a highly distinct way: layers of rough, reddish-brown, sun-dried mudbrick alternate with thick, horizontal bands of heavily braided, yellow-gray reed matting. These reed ropes are saturated in weeping, black bitumen to prevent structural settling, creating a striking "striped" texture across the massive ruined core.
- Façades: The Kassites introduce spectacular, custom-molded baked brick facades (like the Temple of Karaindash at Uruk). Bricks are individually sculpted so that, when stacked, they form brilliant 3D high-reliefs of mountain gods and water deities holding flowing vases, their faces protruding directly out of the flat temple walls.

### 3. Demographics & Physical Traits

- Physical Features: A melting pot. The native Babylonians (dark-haired, olive-to-brown skin) mix with the Kassite elite, who may possess slightly different cranial structures but have completely assimilated visually. Faces are robust, well-fed, and slightly softer than the rugged Assyrian merchants.
- Hairstyles & Beards (Men): The elite Babylonian aesthetic is heavily maintained. Hair is grown long to the shoulders, heavily oiled, and set into precise, tight, horizontal rows of ringlets. Beards are square-cut, rigidly curled, and dyed black with henna and indigo.
- Hairstyles (Women): Elite women wear thick, heavy hair parted in the middle and falling in massive, crimped waves over their shoulders, often secured by wide, cloth headbands embroidered with gold thread.

### 4. Fashion & Textiles

- Fabrics & Textures: A shift toward more tailored, fitted garments rather than purely draped shawls. Fine, tightly woven wool and imported linen are the standards. The fabric is dense but softer and more heavily decorated than in older eras.
- Clothing Cuts: Both men and women of status wear long, short-sleeved tubular tunics that reach the ankles. Over this, they wear highly structured, deeply fringed garments wrapped multiple times around the body, creating heavy, cascading diagonal lines of fabric.
- Colors & Embroidery: A vibrant explosion of color. Deep lapis blues, rich ochres, and vivid Tyrian purples (imported from the Levant) begin to appear. Hems are heavily embroidered with geometric rosettes and star patterns in contrasting white and yellow threads.
- Jewelry & Materials: The Kassites are master glassmakers. While they still use gold and lapis, the visually striking trend is bright, opaque faience and core-formed glass. Necklaces feature glossy, smooth, vibrant aqua-blue and bright yellow glass beads, designed to flawlessly imitate rare turquoise and lapis.

### 5. Economy & Daily Life

- The Kudurru: The ultimate symbol of Kassite economic and legal power. These are massive, polished boundary stones made of dense, black limestone or dark diorite. They are planted in fields to record royal land grants. The stones are highly polished, catching the sun, and are entirely covered in deeply incised cuneiform text and registers of terrifying, heavily detailed divine symbols (horned serpents, scorpion-men, and dog-headed maces).
- Glass Industry: Workshops smell of intense heat and wood ash. Artisans trail threads of molten white and yellow glass over small, dark blue glass vessels built around a core of sand, using bronze hooks to drag the threads into striking zigzag (festoon) patterns before cooling and polishing them.
- Trade: Kassite kings engage in the "Amarna system" of royal gift exchange, sending massive caravans loaded with lapis lazuli, fine horses, and chariots to Egypt in exchange for massive quantities of Egyptian gold.

### 6. Religion & Art

- Statuary: Marduk becomes the undisputed king of the gods. Cult statues in the dimly lit, cedar-scented inner sanctums of Babylon are life-sized, carved from rare woods, and completely overlaid with hammered sheet-gold and encrusted with precious stones.
- Artistic Details (Cylinder Seals): A distinct shift in seal art. The deeply carved, highly active combat scenes of the past are replaced by elongated, highly polished agate and chalcedony seals. Visually, they feature a single, tall, praying figure standing before a line of text, maximizing the space for long, poetic prayers.
- Wall Painting: Temple interiors are whitewashed and painted with striking, rigid geometric patterns. Thick bands of iron-red, charcoal-black, and chalk-white frame the doorways and altars.

### 7. Transport & Mobility

- The Chariot: The Kassite chariot is a marvel of lightweight, high-speed engineering. Built from heat-bent ash or elm wood, the cab is small, light, and D-shaped, designed to hold only two men (a driver and an archer).
- Wheels: The solid disc wheels of Sumer are gone. These are fast, delicate-looking, but incredibly strong 6-spoked wheels. The rims are bound in wet rawhide that, as it dries in the Babylonian sun, shrinks and grips the wooden wheel like a vise.
- Tack & Gear: Horses are guided by bronze snaffle bits and wear heavy leather headstalls. High-status chariot horses wear large, polished bronze cheekpieces, sometimes shaped like galloping horses themselves, which flash brilliantly in the sun as they run.

### 8. The Military Machine

- Force Structure: The "Maryannu" (chariot-borne elite) dominate the battlefield. They are a swift, mobile strike force, kicking up massive, blinding clouds of dust as squadrons of chariots maneuver across the flat plains.
- Weapons: The recurved composite bow is the superweapon of the era. Made of layers of flexible horn, wood core, and tension-bearing animal sinew glued together and wrapped in waterproof birch bark, it has a deadly, snapping recoil and armor-piercing power.
- Armor & Shields: The introduction of bronze scale armor. Elite charioteers wear long leather tunics completely covered in hundreds of overlapping, leaf-shaped bronze scales. The scales are sewn tightly together with leather thongs, creating a heavy, metallic, fish-scale texture that clatters with movement and deflects incoming arrows.
