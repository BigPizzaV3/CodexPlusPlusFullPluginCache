### Akkad: The Social Classes (Nos. 126 to 150)

**126. Field Laborer's Loincloth.** _(Poor, man.)_ Bare to the waist, a short rough wool cloth knotted at the hip, the feet bare, a folded cloth on the head against the sun. The fieldhand of the plain.

**127. Water-Carrier's Shoulder Cloth.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A plain undyed shift with a thick folded pad on one shoulder for the jar, a head-cloth, bare feet, carrying water from the river.

**128. Pasture-Herder's Coarse Wrap.** _(Poor, man.)_ A coarse wool wrap and a fleece over the shoulders, a crook and a sling, watching the flocks across the open Akkadian country.

**129. Washerwoman's Tucked Gown.** _(Poor, woman.)_ A coarse gown hitched and tucked for wading, the sleeves bound, the hair wrapped, beating cloth clean at the canal.

**130. Brick-Carrier's Padded Kilt.** _(Poor, man.)_ A coarse kilt with a padded shoulder for the load of mud bricks, a wide belt, the body grey with clay.

**131. Poor Widow's Patched Gown.** _(Poor, older woman.)_ A much-mended undyed gown, a thin worn shawl, no jewelry but a single clay bead, grey with washing and years.

**132. Bondservant's Plain Shift.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A knee-length coarse wool shift, undyed and corded, the hair cropped close. The household bondservant.

**133. Beggar's Knotted Rags.** _(Poor, any.)_ Layered tattered scraps of wool knotted against the cold, a clay bowl in hand, the feet blistered and bare.

**134. Townsman's Fringed Mantle and Kilt.** _(Middle, man.)_ A decent kilt under a modest fringed mantle over one shoulder, a woven belt, a trimmed beard, leather sandals. The respectable townsman.

**135. Artisan's Work Tunic and Apron.** _(Middle, man.)_ A sturdy tunic under a thick apron, the sleeves bound, a tool-belt and a felt cap. The skilled craftsman.

**136. Shopkeeper's Wife's Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A good-wool gown with a fringed shawl, a few faience and silver beads, a small household seal at the belt.

**137. Weaver's Practical Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A plain belted gown with the sleeves bound, shuttle and thread at hand, the fingers stained with dye.

**138. Bronze-Smith's Apron Dress.** _(Middle, man.)_ A short tunic under a scorched-leather apron, the forearms wrapped, casting and hammering the bronze of the empire's tools and arms.

**139. Brewer-Woman's Working Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A belted gown with the sleeves tied and a cloth over the hair, tending the vats and the bread of the brewing craft.

**140. Middle Man's Temple-Day Robe.** _(Middle, man.)_ A clean longer robe and a folded shawl kept for temple and feast days, a modest fillet and good sandals.

**141. Middle Daughter's Festival Tunic.** _(Middle, young woman.)_ A linen gown with a dyed border, a beaded collar, small earrings and a fillet. Modest finery for a feast day.

**142. Nobleman's Long Fringed Mantle.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A full fringed mantle of fine soft wool drawn over one shoulder, a wide ornamented belt, gold armlets, an oiled and elaborately curled beard.

**143. Noblewoman's Fringed Gown and Collar.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A fine fringed gown, a broad collar of gold, carnelian and lapis, heavy earrings and a diadem, the eyes kohl-lined and the skin scented.

**144. Rich Estate-Holder's Draped Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A fine draped fringed robe and a seal of office, a staff and fine sandals, overseeing fields, herds and a steward of the imperial land.

**145. Noble Daughter's Betrothal Gown.** _(Wealthy, young woman.)_ A sumptuous dyed gown layered with the gold of her dowry, a fine shawl drawn back from the face, perfumed oils and kohl.

**146. Wealthy Matron's Banquet Gown.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A deeply dyed fine gown with the fullest jewelry, a wreath or diadem, reclining at the banquet while a servant holds the fan.

**147. Nobleman's Winter Fur-Trimmed Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A heavy fine robe trimmed with fur at collar and hem, a lined mantle, a decorated cap and a gold pin. Warmth that shows wealth.

**148. Lady's Fine Linen Summer Gown.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A gown of finely bleached linen, light gold jewelry, delicate sandals, a maid holding a shading cloth. Cool summer luxury.

**149. Heir's Coming-of-Age Mantle.** _(Wealthy, young man.)_ A first full fringed adult mantle of fine cloth, a new personal seal and a fillet, taking his place among the men of the house.

**150. Dowager Noblewoman's Robe.** _(Wealthy, older woman.)_ A rich but sober gown, heirloom gold worn lightly, a fine shawl over silvering hair, the bearing of one long obeyed.

### Akkad: Trades and Professions (Nos. 151 to 185)

**151. Master Scribe's Fringed Robe.** _(Akkadian cuneiform.)_ A fine long fringed robe, a cylinder seal and a reed stylus, a clay tablet under the arm, writing the Akkadian tongue that the empire spread across the land.

**152. Schoolmaster's Robe.** _(Tablet house.)_ A sober long robe and a discipline-rod, a wall of stacked tablets behind, training the scribes of the imperial administration.

**153. Temple Scribe's Linen Robe.** _(Sanctuary record.)_ A clean pale linen robe and a fringed shawl, a seal of the god's house, recording the offerings and the herds of the temple.

**154. Imperial Surveyor-Scribe.** _(Land measure.)_ A practical tunic, a measuring rod and a knotted cord, a tablet of plots, recording the king's fields across the conquered provinces.

**155. Merchant's Travel Robe.** _(Imperial trade.)_ A long belted robe under a dust-cloak, a seal and a tablet-pouch, a money-belt, trading along the roads the empire had made safe.

**156. Market Seller's Kilt and Apron.** _(Stall.)_ A knee kilt with a wide apron-sash of weights and a pouch, a folded awning-cloth, calling his wares in the market.

**157. Cloth Merchant's Fine Robe.** _(Textiles.)_ A robe showing his best wares, dyed cloth over the arm, a measuring rod and rings, dealing in the great trade of cloth.

**158. Carpenter's Work Tunic.** _(Wood.)_ A sturdy tunic and a pocketed apron, an adze and chisels at the belt, wood shavings on the cloth at the bench.

**159. Leatherworker's Apron Dress.** _(Hide.)_ A tunic under a thick leather apron darkened by the pits, awls and a curved knife, the hands stained brown.

**160. Fisherman's River Wrap.** _(River.)_ A minimal wrap for the boat, a casting-net over one shoulder, the legs bare, a reed hat and a fish-spear.

**161. Plowman-Farmer's Kilt.** _(Field.)_ A coarse belted kilt for the seeder-plow behind the oxen, a goad, a seed-bag at the hip, mud to the knee.

**162. Baker's Flour-Dusted Tunic.** _(Bread.)_ A tunic powdered with flour, the sleeves bound and a cloth over the hair, baking the loaves of the city.

**163. Barber's Neat Tunic.** _(Grooming.)_ A clean trim tunic, a roll of razor and shears, a basin and a strop, dressing the elaborate beards the Akkadians prized.

**164. Physician's Robe.** _(Healer.)_ A clean robe and a satchel of herbs, salves and tools, a protective amulet, a learned and reassuring calm.

**165. Temple Musician's Festival Dress.** _(Sacred song.)_ A decorated tunic or gown, a lyre or harp, a fillet and beads, the singer and player before the god at the feast.

**166. Dyer's Color-Stained Dress.** _(Vats.)_ A tunic blotched red, blue and yellow from the vats, the arms dyed to the elbow, by the dye pots.

**167. Goldsmith's Fine Tunic.** _(Precious work.)_ A clean careful tunic and a small apron, tiny tools and a balance, gold wire and beads on the bench, the eyes sharp and close.

**168. Boatman's River Wrap.** _(Canals.)_ A short wrap knotted at the waist, a pole and a coiled rope, moving grain and stone along the rivers and canals.

**169. Shepherd's Fleece Cloak.** _(Pasture.)_ A coarse tunic under a sheepskin cloak, a crook and a sling, a leather bag, watching the flocks of the open country.

**170. Date-Gardener's Tunic.** _(Orchard.)_ A short belted tunic for climbing and tending the date palms, a hooked knife and a fiber rope, sun-darkened arms.

**171. Master Bronze-Caster's Robe.** _(Metal art.)_ A clean robe over a work tunic, fine tools and wax models at hand, the master of the lost-wax casting that made the great Akkadian bronze heads.

**172. Reed-Worker's Wrap.** _(Mats and baskets.)_ A plain wrap and a cord belt, bundles of cut reed at hand, weaving the mats, baskets and boats of the waterways.

**173. Oil-Presser's Tunic.** _(Sesame oil.)_ A tunic stained and slick with oil, the sleeves bound, working the press for the oil of lamp and skin.

**174. Brick-Mason's Kilt.** _(Building.)_ A coarse kilt and a cord belt, a trowel and a plumb-cord, raising the walls and platforms of the imperial cities.

**175. Imperial Herald's Tunic.** _(Proclamation.)_ A clean clear-colored tunic and a staff, a strong voice, crying the king's decree through the streets of the empire's cities.

**176. Ale-House Keeper's Gown.** _(Woman, tavern.)_ A belted gown with the sleeves tied, a cloth over the hair, drawing beer for the men of the ale-house.

**177. Midwife's Practical Gown.** _(Birth.)_ A clean simple gown with the sleeves bound, a satchel of cloths, herbs and a birth-amulet, attending the women of the quarter.

**178. Caravan-Master's Robe.** _(Long-distance trade.)_ A long sturdy robe and a dust-cloak, a seal, a heavy purse and a staff, leading the donkey caravans toward Elam and the eastern hills.

**179. Seal-Cutter's Fine Tunic.** _(Glyptic art.)_ A clean tunic, a tiny drill and gravers, a magnifying eye, cutting the masterly combat and presentation scenes for which Akkadian seals are famed.

**180. Estate Steward's Robe.** _(Manager.)_ A clean long robe, a seal and a tablet of accounts, ordering the workers, herds and stores of a royal or temple estate.

**181. Road-Warden's Dress.** _(Imperial roads.)_ A belted tunic, a leather cap and a spear, a seal of the king, keeping the safety of the highways the empire held.

**182. Granary-Keeper's Robe.** _(Stores.)_ A clean robe with a seal and a tally-tablet, the sleeves bound, measuring the barley in and out of the great granary.

**183. Donkey-Driver's Wrap.** _(Caravan.)_ A short coarse wrap and a head-cloth, a goad and a water-skin, leading the laden donkeys along the trade track.

**184. Stonecutter's Dusty Tunic.** _(Hard stone.)_ A tunic grey with stone dust, the sleeves bound, a mallet and chisel, shaping the imported diorite of royal steles and statues.

**185. Master Potter's Robe.** _(Senior craft.)_ A clean robe softened by years, a felt cap, the wheel turned now by a son while he judges the form and the firing.

### Akkad: Soldiers and Military (Nos. 186 to 200)

**186. Royal Composite-Bow Archer.** _(Bowman.)_ A belted war tunic, a copper helmet, and the powerful composite bow that gave the Akkadian army its reach, a full quiver at the hip.

**187. Spearman of the Imperial Line.** _(Infantry.)_ A knee tunic and a wide belt, a copper helmet, a thrusting spear and a round shield, the footman of the conquering host.

**188. Mountain-Climbing Soldier.** _(Naram-Sin stele.)_ A short belted tunic and a helmet, a bow and a spear, climbing the wooded mountain behind the king as on the victory stele of Naram-Sin.

**189. Copper-Helmeted Heavy Infantryman.** _(Shock troop.)_ A thick wool tunic, a close copper helmet, a great shield and an axe, breaking the enemy line in the press.

**190. Axe-and-Shield Soldier.** _(Close combat.)_ A belted tunic and a leather cap, a socketed war-axe and a round shield, for the hand-to-hand of the assault.

**191. Slinger Skirmisher.** _(Light troop.)_ A short plain tunic, a pouch of sling-stones and a sling, light and quick, harrying ahead of the line.

**192. Officer's Distinguished Mantle.** _(Rank.)_ A finer fringed mantle with a marked belt, a crested helmet and a sword of office, directing the troops in the field.

**193. Royal Guard of the King.** _(Bodyguard.)_ A fringed tunic and a polished helmet, a tall spear and a fine shield, standing at the king's side in war and in hall.

**194. Standard-Bearer of the Empire.** _(Emblem.)_ A clean fringed tunic and a helmet, both hands on the pole of the god's emblem-standard, leading the marching army.

**195. Siege-Assault Soldier.** _(Siege.)_ A practical tunic and a leather cap, a shield held high and a short axe, climbing against the walls of a rebel city.

**196. Veteran Campaigner's Kit.** _(Old hand.)_ A battered tunic and a dented helmet, old scars and a trusted bow, having marched the length of the empire and back.

**197. Garrison Soldier of a Conquered City.** _(Occupation.)_ A belted tunic, a copper helmet and a spear, holding a far city for the king among a watchful conquered people.

**198. Light Scout's Tunic.** _(Reconnaissance.)_ A short light tunic and soft sandals, a bow and a water-skin, ranging ahead of the army to spy out the road and the foe.

**199. Tribute-Escort Soldier.** _(Guard duty.)_ A clean belted tunic and a spear, guarding the long lines of tribute and captives brought back to Agade from the conquered lands.

**200. Mustering Recruit's Kilt.** _(New levy.)_ A plain wool kilt and a cord belt, a wooden practice-spear, learning the line in the muster of the imperial army.

### Akkad: Rulers, Court and Royalty (Nos. 201 to 225)

**201. King Sargon's Robe.** _(Founder.)_ A long fringed mantle drawn over one shoulder, a wide belt, an elaborately curled beard and dressed hair, in the manner of the great bronze head. The founder of the empire.

**202. Naram-Sin's Divine War Dress.** _(Deified king.)_ A fringed kilt and a horned helmet of divinity, a great bow and a spear, the muscled body bare above, the god-king of the victory stele.

**203. Akkadian King's War Robe.** _(Campaign.)_ A belted war tunic under a fringed mantle, a crested helmet and a bow, leading the imperial army from the front.

**204. Akkadian Queen's Fringed Gown.** _(Consort.)_ A fine fringed gown and a broad collar of gold and lapis, a thin diadem over dressed hair, the lady of the imperial house.

**205. En-Priestess Princess's Robe.** _(Enheduanna.)_ A flounced robe and a rounded crown, a ring and rod, the dress of the king's daughter set as high priestess at Ur, as Sargon set Enheduanna, first named poet of the world.

**206. Crown Prince's Mantle.** _(Heir.)_ A rich fringed mantle just below the king's, a diadem-band, a personal seal and attendants, the son who will hold the empire.

**207. Princess's Fine Fringed Gown.** _(Royal daughter.)_ A sumptuous fringed gown, a fine diadem, the rarest gold, carnelian and lapis, scented and adorned from girlhood.

**208. King at the Banquet.** _(Feast.)_ A fine fringed robe and a folded shawl, a gold armlet and a cup, seated above his nobles and generals at the victory feast.

**209. King in Worship.** _(Devotion.)_ A pure robe and a fillet, a libation cup raised before the god, the king giving thanks for the conquests granted him.

**210. King's Coronation Robe.** _(Investiture.)_ A special robe assumed at the taking of kingship, charged with the symbols of rule and the favor of the gods, worn at the crowning.

**211. Royal Cup-Bearer's Robe.** _(Court.)_ A fine tunic and a folded shawl, a cloth over the arm and the royal cup in hand, a trusted officer close to the throne.

**212. Palace Steward's Robe.** _(Household.)_ A dignified long robe, a staff and a seal, ordering the household, the stores and the workers of the royal palace.

**213. Provincial Governor's Robe.** _(Ensi of the king.)_ A fine fringed robe with the seal of his province, a sword and attendants, ruling a conquered city in the king's name.

**214. Royal Herald's Dress.** _(Court.)_ A fine clear-colored tunic and a staff, proclaiming the king's word and bearing his decrees through the empire.

**215. Governor's Lady's Gown.** _(Residence.)_ A rich gown and a fringed shawl, court jewelry of gold and stone, presiding over the household and women of the governor.

**216. Grand Vizier's Robe.** _(Sukkal.)_ A magnificent long fringed robe, a staff of office and a great seal, standing just below the king as his right hand.

**217. Royal Scribe's Robe.** _(Chancery.)_ A fine robe marking court service, a seal and a leather document-case, an attendant bearing tablets, the scribe of the king's chancery.

**218. Royal Judge's Robe.** _(Law.)_ A grave long robe, a seal and the case-tablets, seated to hear and to judge in the king's name.

**219. Treasurer's Robe.** _(Tribute.)_ A sober rich robe, keys and seals at the belt, the ledgers of the silver and tribute that flowed to Agade from the conquered lands.

**220. Royal Guard Captain's Dress.** _(Command.)_ A fine fringed tunic and a crested helmet, a marked belt and a sword of office, commanding the guard of the king.

**221. Imperial Envoy's Robe.** _(Embassy.)_ A fine travel-worthy robe upholding the king's dignity abroad, sealed tablets and gifts borne behind, received in Elam and the eastern courts.

**222. Royal Child's Festival Robe.** _(Palace child.)_ A soft fine linen tunic with a saffron and blue border, a beaded carnelian collar and a gold fillet, a child of the royal house at the feast.

**223. King's Hunting Dress.** _(The hunt.)_ A fringed garment belted high for action, a composite bow and a quiver, an armlet and a fillet, riding against the lion and the wild bull.

**224. Conqueror's Triumph Robe.** _(Victory.)_ The king's richest fringed robe and crown, a mace in hand, receiving the bound captives and the heaped tribute of a beaten enemy.

**225. Dowager Queen's Robe.** _(Royal elder.)_ A rich but sober fringed gown, heirloom gold worn lightly, a fine shawl over silvering hair, the mother of the king long honored.

### Akkad: Priests, Scholars and Temple (Nos. 226 to 240)

**226. High Priest's Ritual Robe.** _(Chief priest.)_ A long flounced robe leaving one shoulder bare, a tall fillet, a long oiled beard, anointing oil and the god's emblem in hand, before the deity of the city.

**227. High Priestess's Robe.** _(En, royal daughter.)_ A fine flounced robe and a rounded crown, layered gold and lapis, a ring and rod, the office the Akkadian kings gave to their daughters at Ur and Nippur.

**228. Purification Priest's Linen Robe.** _(Shaven.)_ A head and body shaven smooth for purity, a plain bleached linen robe, a holy-water vessel in hand, performing the washings.

**229. Diviner's Robe.** _(Baru.)_ A clean robe and a fringed shawl, a clay model liver at his side, reading the gods' answer in the entrails, an art the Akkadians raised high.

**230. Lamentation Priest's Dress.** _(Kalu.)_ A robe for the chanting of laments, a kettledrum or lyre at hand, the voice raised to soothe the angry heart of the god.

**231. Temple Administrator's Robe.** _(Sanga.)_ A clean long robe, a seal and a tally-tablet, ordering the lands, herds and offerings of the great temple estate.

**232. Dream-Interpreter's Robe.** _(Visions.)_ A sober robe and a shawl, omen-tablets at hand, reading the meaning of dreams for those who come troubled to the temple.

**233. Exorcist's Night-Rite Dress.** _(Against demons.)_ A robe for the rites against the demons of the dark, protective amulets about him, a censer and a torch, incantations on the tablet in his hand.

**234. Novice Priest's Plain Robe.** _(Temple novice.)_ A shaven head and a plain undyed linen robe with a simple sash, learning the washings, the rites and the songs.

**235. Astronomer-Priest's Robe.** _(Sky-watcher.)_ A robe and a fringed shawl against the night air, a tablet of stars and omens, watching the sky from the temple platform.

**236. Sage-Scholar's Robe.** _(Ummanu, wisdom.)_ A fine sober robe and a fringed shawl, tablets of lore and omen close by, the trusted keeper of knowledge who counsels the king.

**237. Cloistered Priestess's Gown.** _(Devoted woman.)_ A modest fine gown and a drawn shawl, a personal seal at the wrist, vowed to the temple yet keeping her own lands and accounts.

**238. Hymn-Singer Priestess's Dress.** _(Sacred song.)_ A decorated gown and a fillet, beads of faience and carnelian, lifting the temple hymn in the tradition of Enheduanna's praises.

**239. Offering-Bearer Priest's Dress.** _(Procession.)_ A clean fringed robe and a folded shawl, both hands bearing a dish of dates, a kid or a bowl of grain to the god.

**240. Royal-Cult Priest's Robe.** _(Deified king.)_ A rich robe with sacred motifs, serving at the shrine of the deified king after Naram-Sin took the horns of godhood, a rare cult of the living ruler.

### Akkad: Garment Types and Materials (Nos. 241 to 250)

**241. The Fringed Draped Mantle.** _(Signature garment.)_ The defining Akkadian dress: a large fringed cloth wound about the body and drawn over the left shoulder, baring the right arm and chest, as on the steles and seals.

**242. The Long Fringed Robe.** _(Formal.)_ A full ankle robe edged with deep fringe, worn for dignity and office, the fringe falling in an ordered band along the trailing edge.

**243. The Wrapped Wool Kilt.** _(Everyday.)_ A length of wool wound at the hip and tucked, the chest bare, the common working lower garment of the Akkadian man.

**244. The Fringed Shoulder-Wrap.** _(Drape.)_ A rectangular fringed wrap drawn across the body and over one shoulder, the added garment of standing above the plain kilt.

**245. The One-Shoulder Bare Drape.** _(Style.)_ The distinctive Akkadian arrangement of leaving one shoulder and arm fully bare, the cloth gathered diagonally across the trunk, marking the heroic male figure.

**246. The Bordered Festival Robe.** _(Festival.)_ A robe woven with colored borders at hem and edge and finished with fringe, the bright dress of feast days and victory celebration.

**247. Akkadian Wool.** _(Material.)_ The staple fiber of the flocks, spun and woven into most Akkadian dress, in natural tones and the dyed shades of the well-off.

**248. Akkadian Linen.** _(Material.)_ Cloth of flax, finer and cooler than wool, bleached pale for priests, the wealthy and the festival best.

**249. The Fleece Cloak.** _(Warmth.)_ A cloak of sheepskin or heavy fleece worn over the shoulders against the cold, plain and practical for herder and traveler.

**250. Akkadian Trimmed and Dyed Cloth.** _(Material.)_ The finer wool finished with woven color and the deep fringe the Akkadians loved, the trim itself the measure of a garment's worth.
