### Sumer: The Social Classes (Nos. 126 to 150)

**126. Field Laborer's Loincloth.** _(Poor, man.)_ Bare to the waist, a short rough wool cloth knotted at the hip, the feet hard and bare, a folded cloth on the head against the sun. The fieldhand of the canals.

**127. Water-Carrier's Shoulder Cloth.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A plain undyed shift with a thick folded pad on one shoulder to bear the jar, a head-cloth, bare feet, carrying water all day from the river.

**128. Reed-Cutter's Marsh Wrap.** _(Poor, man.)_ A minimal wrap of coarse wool and reed-fiber cord, the legs bare for wading, damp and mud-flecked among the southern reeds.

**129. Washerwoman's Tucked Gown.** _(Poor, woman.)_ A coarse gown hitched and tucked at the waist for wading, the sleeves bound, the hair wrapped, beating cloth clean at the canal.

**130. Brick-Carrier's Padded Kilt.** _(Poor, man.)_ A coarse kilt with a padded shoulder for the load of mud bricks, a wide belt, the body grey with dried clay from the brick pits.

**131. Poor Widow's Patched Gown.** _(Poor, older woman.)_ A much-mended undyed gown, a thin worn shawl, no jewelry but a single clay bead, grey with washing and years.

**132. Bondservant's Plain Shift.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ The simplest issued garment, a knee-length coarse wool shift, undyed and corded, the hair cropped close. The household bondservant.

**133. Beggar's Knotted Rags.** _(Poor, any.)_ Layered tattered scraps of wool knotted together against the cold, a clay bowl in hand, the feet blistered and bare, at the temple steps.

**134. Townsman's Clean Kilt and Shawl.** _(Middle, man.)_ A decent wool kilt with a woven belt and a folded shawl over one shoulder, a trimmed beard, leather sandals. The respectable everyday townsman.

**135. Artisan's Work Tunic and Apron.** _(Middle, man.)_ A sturdy tunic under a thick cloth apron, the sleeves bound, a tool-belt and a felt cap. The skilled craftsman at his bench.

**136. Shopkeeper's Wife's Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A good-wool gown with a fringed shawl, a few faience and silver beads, a small household seal at the belt. Comfortable and capable.

**137. Weaver's Practical Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A plain belted gown with the sleeves bound, shuttle and thread at hand, the fingers stained with dye, at the loom of the city.

**138. Potter's Clay-Dusted Tunic.** _(Middle, man.)_ A worn tunic powdered with clay, the sleeves rolled, a cord belt, the hands wet and grey at the turning wheel.

**139. Brewer-Woman's Working Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A belted gown with the sleeves tied and a cloth over the hair, tending the fermenting vats and the bread, the craft that stood under Ninkasi.

**140. Middle Man's Temple-Day Robe.** _(Middle, man.)_ A clean longer robe and a folded shawl kept for temple and feast days, a modest fillet and good sandals. The respectable best of an ordinary man.

**141. Middle Daughter's Festival Tunic.** _(Middle, young woman.)_ A linen gown with a dyed border, a beaded collar, small earrings and a fillet. Modest finery for a feast day.

**142. Landowner's Long Fringed Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A full robe of fine soft wool with a deep fringed shawl elaborately wound, a wide ornamented belt, gold armlets, an oiled curled beard.

**143. Noblewoman's Flounced Gown and Collar.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A fine flounced gown, a broad collar of gold, carnelian and lapis, heavy earrings and a diadem, the eyes kohl-lined and the skin scented.

**144. Rich Estate-Holder's Kaunakes Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A dense tufted kaunakes robe and a wide belt, a cylinder seal of office, a staff, fine sandals, overseeing fields, herds and a steward.

**145. Noble Daughter's Betrothal Gown.** _(Wealthy, young woman.)_ A sumptuous dyed gown layered with the gold of her dowry, a fine shawl drawn back from the face, perfumed oils and kohl.

**146. Wealthy Matron's Banquet Gown.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A deeply dyed fine gown with the fullest jewelry, a wreath or diadem, reclining at the banquet to the lyre while a servant holds the fan.

**147. Nobleman's Winter Fur-Trimmed Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A heavy fine robe trimmed with fur at collar and hem, a lined mantle, a decorated cap and a gold pin. Warmth that displays wealth.

**148. Lady's Fine Linen Summer Gown.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A gown of finely bleached linen, light gold jewelry, delicate sandals, a maid holding a shading cloth. Cool summer luxury.

**149. Heir's Coming-of-Age Robe.** _(Wealthy, young man.)_ A first full adult robe of fine cloth with a fringed shawl, a new personal seal and a fillet, taking his place among the men of the house.

**150. Dowager Noblewoman's Robe.** _(Wealthy, older woman.)_ A rich but sober gown, heirloom gold worn lightly, a fine shawl over silvering hair, the bearing of one long obeyed.

### Sumer: Trades and Professions (Nos. 151 to 185)

**151. Master Scribe's Fringed Robe.** _(Dubsar.)_ A fine long fringed robe, a cylinder seal at the wrist, a reed stylus and a damp clay tablet, the calm bearing of the keeper of the written word.

**152. Edubba Schoolmaster's Robe.** _(Ummia.)_ A sober long robe, a thin discipline-rod, a wall of stacked tablets behind, the master of the tablet house where scribes are made.

**153. Temple Scribe's Linen Robe.** _(Sanctuary record.)_ A clean pale linen robe and a fringed shawl, a seal of the god's house, recording the offerings and the herds of the temple.

**154. Surveyor-Scribe's Field Dress.** _(Land measure.)_ A practical kilt and tunic, a measuring rod and a knotted cord, a tablet for the plots, dusty sandals in the open field.

**155. Merchant's Travel Robe.** _(Damgar.)_ A long sturdy belted robe under a dust-cloak, a seal and a tablet-pouch, a money-belt of silver rings, trading between the cities.

**156. Market Seller's Kilt and Apron.** _(Stall.)_ A knee kilt with a wide apron-sash holding weights and a pouch, a folded awning-cloth, calling his wares in the market lane.

**157. Cloth Merchant's Fine Robe.** _(Textiles.)_ A robe showing his own best wares, lengths of dyed cloth over the arm, a measuring rod and rings, dealing in the great trade of the land.

**158. Carpenter's Work Tunic.** _(Wood.)_ A sturdy tunic and a pocketed apron, an adze and chisels at the belt, wood shavings clinging to the cloth at the bench.

**159. Leatherworker's Apron Dress.** _(Hide.)_ A tunic under a thick leather apron darkened by the tanning pits, awls and a curved knife, the hands stained brown.

**160. Fisherman's Marsh Wrap.** _(River.)_ A minimal wrap for the boat and the water, a casting-net over one shoulder, the legs bare, a reed hat and a fish-spear.

**161. Plowman-Farmer's Kilt.** _(Field.)_ A coarse belted kilt for the seeder-plow behind the oxen, a goad in hand, a seed-bag at the hip, mud to the knee.

**162. Baker's Flour-Dusted Tunic.** _(Bread.)_ A tunic powdered with flour, the sleeves bound and a cloth over the hair, slapping loaves onto the wall of the clay oven.

**163. Barber's Neat Tunic.** _(Su-i.)_ A clean trim tunic, a roll of razor and shears, a basin and a strop, a careful hand for the city's shaving and grooming.

**164. Physician's Robe.** _(A-zu.)_ A clean robe and a satchel of dried herbs and salves, a small set of tools, a protective amulet, a learned and reassuring calm.

**165. Temple Musician's Festival Dress.** _(Nar.)_ A decorated tunic or gown, a great lyre of Ur in the arms, a fillet and beads, the singer and player before the goddess at the feast.

**166. Dyer's Color-Stained Dress.** _(Vats.)_ A tunic blotched red, blue and yellow from the vats, the sleeves bound and the arms dyed to the elbow, by the dye pots.

**167. Goldsmith's Fine Tunic.** _(Precious work.)_ A clean careful tunic and a small apron, tiny tools and a balance, beads and gold wire on the bench, the eyes sharp and close.

**168. Boatman's River Wrap.** _(Canals.)_ A short wrap knotted at the waist, a pole and a coiled rope, bare-legged on the reed boat that moves grain and stone along the canals.

**169. Shepherd's Fleece Cloak.** _(Sipa.)_ A coarse wool tunic under a sheepskin cloak, a crook and a sling, a leather bag, watching the flocks on the steppe edge.

**170. Date-Gardener's Tunic.** _(Orchard.)_ A short belted tunic for climbing and tending the date palms, a hooked knife and a fiber rope, sun-darkened arms.

**171. Smith's Apron Dress.** _(Metal.)_ A short tunic under a thick scorched-leather apron, the forearms wrapped, a sweat-rag at the neck, by the copper forge.

**172. Reed-Worker's Wrap.** _(Mats and baskets.)_ A plain wrap and a cord belt, bundles of cut reed at hand, weaving the mats, baskets and boats of the southern marsh.

**173. Oil-Presser's Tunic.** _(Sesame oil.)_ A tunic stained and slick with oil, the sleeves bound, working the press that yields the sesame oil for lamp and skin.

**174. Brick-Mason's Kilt.** _(Building.)_ A coarse kilt and a cord belt, a trowel and a plumb-cord, laying the mud bricks of wall and temple, grey to the elbow.

**175. City Herald's Tunic.** _(Proclamation.)_ A clean clear-colored tunic and a staff, a strong carrying voice, crying the ruler's word in the squares and at the gate.

**176. Ale-House Keeper's Gown.** _(Woman, tavern.)_ A belted gown with the sleeves tied, a cloth over the hair, drawing beer through a reed straw for the men of the ale-house.

**177. Midwife's Practical Gown.** _(Birth.)_ A clean simple gown with the sleeves bound, a satchel of cloths, herbs and a birth-amulet, attending the women of the quarter in their labor.

**178. Lamentation-Singer's Robe.** _(Gala.)_ A robe for the chanting of laments to the lyre and drum, the voice raised in the temple to soothe the heart of the god.

**179. Weigh-Master's Robe.** _(Market weights.)_ A sober good robe with a balance and a set of stone weights at the belt, a seal and a tablet, judging the fairness of the market.

**180. Estate Steward's Robe.** _(Sanga, manager.)_ A clean long robe, a seal of office and a tablet of accounts, ordering the workers, herds and stores of a temple or palace estate.

**181. Canal-Overseer's Kilt.** _(Water.)_ A practical belted kilt and a folded cloth, a measuring rod and a seal, directing the digging and the gates of the life-giving canals.

**182. Granary-Keeper's Robe.** _(Stores.)_ A clean robe with a seal and a tally-tablet, the sleeves bound, measuring the barley in and out of the great mud-brick granary.

**183. Donkey-Driver's Wrap.** _(Caravan.)_ A short coarse wrap and a head-cloth, a goad and a water-skin, leading the laden donkeys of the trade along the dusty track.

**184. Stonecutter's Dusty Tunic.** _(Stone.)_ A tunic grey with stone dust, the sleeves bound, a mallet and chisel, shaping the imported stone for statue, door-socket and bowl.

**185. Master Potter's Robe.** _(Senior craft.)_ A clean robe softened by years, a felt cap, the wheel turned now by a son while he judges the form and the firing.

### Sumer: Soldiers and Military (Nos. 186 to 200)

**186. Spearman of the Phalanx.** _(Line infantry.)_ A fringed cloak or plain kilt under a copper helmet, a great rectangular shield and a long spear, locked shoulder to shoulder as on the Stele of the Vultures.

**187. Copper-Helmeted Soldier.** _(War helm.)_ A close copper helmet shaped to the head with formed ears, a wool tunic and a wide belt, a spear and a dagger. The protected footman.

**188. Standard-of-Ur Infantryman.** _(Battle dress.)_ A studded fringed cape over a plain tunic, a copper helmet, an axe or spear, marching as in the war panel of the Standard of Ur.

**189. Battle-Car Driver.** _(Onager cart.)_ A belted tunic and a helmet, the reins of four onagers gathered, driving the heavy four-wheeled war-cart into the enemy line.

**190. Battle-Car Javelineer.** _(Cart crew.)_ A tunic and helmet, a quiver of javelins at the cart's front rail, hurling them over the driver's shoulder in the charge.

**191. Light Skirmisher's Kilt.** _(Sling and javelin.)_ A short plain kilt, a pouch of sling-stones and a sling, light and quick on bare feet, harrying ahead of the line.

**192. Officer's Distinguished Cloak.** _(Rank.)_ A finer fringed cloak and a marked belt, a helmet and an axe of office, directing the spearmen by voice and gesture.

**193. City-Guard Spearman.** _(Gate watch.)_ A plain tunic, a leather cap, a spear and a round shield, standing the watch at the city gate and on the mud-brick wall.

**194. War-Axe Bearer.** _(Shock.)_ A belted tunic and a helmet, a heavy socketed war-axe in hand, breaking the shield-line in the press of close battle.

**195. Royal Guard of the Ensi.** _(Palace.)_ A fringed tunic and a polished copper helmet, a tall spear and a fine shield, standing at the ruler's side in hall and procession.

**196. Wall-Assault Soldier.** _(Siege.)_ A practical tunic and a leather cap, a shield held high and a short axe, climbing against the brick walls of a rival city.

**197. Veteran Soldier's Worn Kit.** _(Old hand.)_ A battered tunic and a dented copper helmet, old scars and a trusted spear, drilling the young recruits in the muster yard.

**198. Levy Farmer-Soldier.** _(Called up.)_ His own field kilt and a borrowed leather cap, a spear from the temple store, a farmer called from the barley to the war-host.

**199. Divine Standard-Bearer.** _(Emblem.)_ A clean fringed tunic and a helmet, both hands on the pole of the god's emblem-standard, carried at the head of the marching host.

**200. Mustering Recruit's Plain Kilt.** _(New levy.)_ A plain wool kilt and a cord belt, no armor yet, a wooden practice-spear, learning the line in the temple muster.

### Sumer: Rulers, Court and Royalty (Nos. 201 to 225)

**201. Lugal's Tufted Kaunakes Robe.** _(King.)_ A dense tufted kaunakes robe drawn over one shoulder and bound with a wide belt, a rounded brimmed cap and a long beard, a mace in hand. The king of the city-state.

**202. Ensi's Fringed Robe.** _(City ruler.)_ A long fringed robe and a folded shawl, a seal of the city god, a staff of rule, governing the city as the god's appointed steward.

**203. En-Priest-King's Ritual Robe.** _(Priest-king.)_ A long flounced robe leaving one shoulder bare, a fillet or net cap, an offering in hand, joining the rule of the city to the service of its god.

**204. Queen's Flounced Gown and Diadem.** _(Nin.)_ A fine flounced tiered gown, a broad collar of gold and lapis, a thin diadem over dressed hair, the consort and lady of the royal house.

**205. Queen Puabi's Gold Headdress and Cloak.** _(Royal Ur.)_ A great headdress of gold leaves, ribbons and lapis-and-carnelian beads, a beaded cloak over a fine gown, in the manner of Queen Puabi of the royal tombs of Ur.

**206. Crown Prince's Robe.** _(Heir.)_ A rich fringed robe just below the king's, a diadem-band marking the heir, a personal seal and attendants, the son who will hold the throne.

**207. Princess's Fine Flounced Gown.** _(Royal daughter.)_ A sumptuous flounced gown, a fine diadem, the rarest gold, carnelian and lapis, scented and adorned from girlhood.

**208. King at the Banquet.** _(Standard of Ur, peace.)_ A long fringed flounced skirt, the chest bare, a cup raised, seated above his nobles to the sound of the lyre, as in the peace panel of the Standard of Ur.

**209. King in War.** _(Standard of Ur, war.)_ A long studded cloak over a fringed garment, descending from the war-cart over the fallen, as in the war panel of the Standard of Ur.

**210. King in Worship.** _(Devotion.)_ A pure robe and a fillet, the upper body bare or one shoulder draped, a libation cup poured before the god, the king as the god's first servant.

**211. Royal Cup-Bearer's Robe.** _(Court.)_ A fine tunic and a folded shawl, a cloth over the arm and the royal cup in hand, a trusted officer close to the throne.

**212. Palace Steward's Robe.** _(Nubanda.)_ A dignified long robe, a staff and a seal, ordering the household, the stores and the workers of the palace.

**213. Court Musician's Robe.** _(Lyre of Ur.)_ A fine decorated robe, the great bull-headed lyre before him, playing at the royal banquet and the funeral rite.

**214. Royal Herald's Dress.** _(Court.)_ A fine clear-colored tunic and a staff, proclaiming the lugal's decree in the city and bearing his word to the gathered men.

**215. Governor's Lady's Gown.** _(Residence.)_ A rich gown and a fringed shawl, court jewelry of gold and stone, presiding over the household and women of the city ruler.

**216. Chief Minister's Robe.** _(Sukkal.)_ A magnificent long fringed robe, a staff of office and a great seal, standing just below the ruler as his right hand.

**217. Royal Scribe's Robe.** _(Court record.)_ A fine robe marking court service, a seal of office and a leather document-case, an attendant bearing tablets, the scribe of the palace.

**218. Judge's Robe.** _(Di-kud.)_ A grave long robe, a seal and the case-tablets, seated to hear and to judge the disputes of the city, weighty and exact.

**219. Treasurer's Robe.** _(Stores of silver.)_ A sober rich robe, keys and seals at the belt, ledgers of silver and tribute under guard, watchful and careful.

**220. Royal Guard Captain's Dress.** _(Command.)_ A fine fringed tunic and a polished helmet, a marked belt and an axe of office, commanding the guard of the ruler's gate.

**221. Ambassador-Envoy's Robe.** _(Embassy.)_ A fine travel-worthy robe upholding the city's dignity abroad, sealed tablets and gifts borne behind, received in a rival court.

**222. Royal Child's Festival Robe.** _(Palace child.)_ A soft fine linen tunic with a saffron and blue border, a beaded carnelian collar and a thin gold fillet, a child of the royal house at the feast.

**223. King's Coronation Robe.** _(Investiture.)_ A special robe assumed at the taking of kingship, charged with the symbols of rule and the favor of the god, worn once at the crowning.

**224. King's Hunting Dress.** _(The hunt.)_ A fringed garment belted high for action, a bow and a quiver, an armlet and a fillet, riding out against the lion and the wild bull.

**225. Dowager Queen's Robe.** _(Royal elder.)_ A rich but sober flounced gown, heirloom gold worn lightly, a fine shawl over silvering hair, the mother of the king long honored.

### Sumer: Priests, Scholars and Temple (Nos. 226 to 240)

**226. High Priest's Ritual Robe.** _(En / sanga.)_ A long flounced robe leaving one shoulder bare, a tall fillet or net cap, a long oiled beard, anointing oil and the god's emblem in hand. The chief priest before the deity of the city.

**227. High Priestess's Robe.** _(En-priestess.)_ A fine flounced robe and a rounded crown, layered gold and lapis, a ring and rod, the grave office often held by a king's daughter, as Enheduanna held it at Ur.

**228. Purification Priest's Linen Robe.** _(Gudu, shaven.)_ A head and body shaven smooth for purity, a plain bleached linen robe, a holy-water vessel in hand, performing the washings of the temple.

**229. Lamentation Priest's Dress.** _(Gala.)_ A robe for the chanting of laments, a kettledrum or lyre at hand, the voice raised in the sacred dialect to soothe the angry god.

**230. Diviner's Robe.** _(Omens.)_ A clean robe and a fringed shawl, a clay model liver at his side, reading the will of the gods in the entrails of the sacrificed sheep.

**231. Temple Administrator's Robe.** _(Sanga.)_ A clean long robe, a seal and a tally-tablet, ordering the lands, herds, workers and offerings of the great temple estate.

**232. Dream-Interpreter's Robe.** _(Visions.)_ A sober robe and a shawl, omen-tablets at hand, reading the meaning of the night's dreams for those who come troubled to the temple.

**233. Exorcist's Night-Rite Dress.** _(Against demons.)_ A robe for the rites against the demons of the dark, protective beads and plaques about him, a censer and a torch, incantations on the tablet in his hand.

**234. Novice Priest's Plain Robe.** _(Temple novice.)_ A shaven head and a plain undyed linen robe with a simple sash, learning the washings, the rites and the songs of the temple.

**235. Astronomer-Priest's Robe.** _(Sky-watcher.)_ A robe and a fringed shawl against the night air, a tablet of stars and omens, eyes lifted from the top of the ziggurat to the wheeling sky.

**236. Sage-Scholar's Robe.** _(Ummia, wisdom.)_ A fine sober robe and a fringed shawl, tablets of lore and proverb close by, the trusted keeper of knowledge who counsels the ruler.

**237. Cloistered Priestess's Gown.** _(Devoted woman.)_ A modest fine gown and a drawn shawl, a personal seal at the wrist, an unmarried woman vowed to the temple yet keeping her own lands and accounts.

**238. Temple Singer-Priestess's Dress.** _(Sacred song.)_ A decorated gown and a fillet, beads of faience and carnelian, lifting the hymn to the goddess with lyre and voice at the rite.

**239. Offering-Bearer Priest's Dress.** _(Procession.)_ A clean fringed robe and a folded shawl, both hands bearing a dish of dates, a kid or a bowl of grain up the temple stair to the god.

**240. Festival Priest's Adorned Robe.** _(Akitu rite.)_ A rich robe with woven sacred motifs and a tall fillet, leading the New Year procession that carries the god from his house to the festival hall.

### Sumer: Garment Types and Materials (Nos. 241 to 250)

**241. The Kaunakes Tufted Skirt.** _(Signature garment.)_ The defining dress of Sumer: a knee skirt covered in rows of pointed wool tufts standing out like a fleece, worn with a bare chest, on king, worshipper and god alike.

**242. The Tiered Flounced Kaunakes Robe.** _(Formal.)_ The tufted fleece extended into a long robe of deep stepped tiers, drawn over one shoulder, the dress of rulers, priestesses and the divine.

**243. The Plain Wrapped Wool Kilt.** _(Everyday.)_ A length of natural wool wound at the hip and tucked at a rolled waistband, the chest bare, the common daily lower garment of the Sumerian man.

**244. The Fringed Shoulder-Wrap.** _(Drape.)_ A rectangular wool wrap with a fringed edge drawn across the body and over one shoulder, leaving the right arm free, the added garment of standing.

**245. The Votive Worshipper's Skirt.** _(Devotional.)_ The modest tufted skirt of the clasped-handed statues set in the temple to pray forever, the most familiar garment of Sumerian sculpture.

**246. The Sheepskin Fleece Cloak.** _(Warmth.)_ A cloak of true sheepskin worn fleece-out, the ancestor of the woven kaunakes, thrown over the shoulders of herders and the early figures.

**247. Sumerian Wool.** _(Material.)_ The staple fiber of the southern flocks, spun and woven into most Sumerian dress, in natural creams, browns and greys and the rarer dyed shades.

**248. Southern Flax Linen.** _(Material.)_ Cloth of flax grown along the canals, finer and cooler than wool, bleached pale for priests, the wealthy and the festival best.

**249. The Reed-Fiber Marsh Wrap.** _(Material.)_ Coarse wrappings and cords of twisted reed and palm fiber, the cheapest covering of the poorest marsh families of the far south.

**250. Sumerian Festival Dyed Cloth.** _(Material.)_ The rarer wool colored with madder red, weld yellow and the costly blues, brought out for feast and rite, color itself a sign of means.
