### Neo-Assyrian: The Social Classes (Nos. 126 to 150)

**126. Field Laborer's Loincloth.** _(Poor, man.)_ Bare to the waist, a short rough wool cloth knotted at the hip, the feet bare, a folded cloth on the head against the sun. The fieldhand of the northern plain.

**127. Water-Carrier's Shoulder Cloth.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A plain undyed shift with a thick folded pad on one shoulder for the jar, a head-cloth, bare feet, carrying water from the Tigris.

**128. Hill-Herder's Coarse Wrap.** _(Poor, man.)_ A coarse wool wrap and a fleece over the shoulders, a crook and a sling, watching the flocks across the northern hills.

**129. Washerwoman's Tucked Gown.** _(Poor, woman.)_ A coarse gown hitched and tucked for wading, the sleeves bound, the hair wrapped, beating cloth clean at the river.

**130. Brick-Carrier's Padded Kilt.** _(Poor, man.)_ A coarse kilt with a padded shoulder for the load of mud bricks, a wide belt, the body grey with clay from the king's building works.

**131. Poor Widow's Patched Gown.** _(Poor, older woman.)_ A much-mended undyed gown, a thin worn shawl, no jewelry but a single clay bead, grey with washing and years.

**132. Bondservant's Plain Shift.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A knee-length coarse wool shift, undyed and corded, the hair cropped close. The household bondservant.

**133. Deportee Laborer's Plain Tunic.** _(Poor, resettled.)_ A plain coarse tunic and a cord belt, the dress of a people resettled by the empire and set to labor on the canals and the palace mounds.

**134. Townsman's Fringed Tunic.** _(Middle, man.)_ A decent knee tunic with a fringed shawl and a woven belt, a trimmed squared beard, leather sandals. The respectable everyday townsman.

**135. Artisan's Work Tunic and Apron.** _(Middle, man.)_ A sturdy tunic under a thick apron, the sleeves bound, a tool-belt and a felt cap. The skilled craftsman.

**136. Shopkeeper's Wife's Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A good-wool gown with a fringed shawl, a few faience and silver beads, a small household seal at the belt.

**137. Weaver's Practical Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A plain belted gown with the sleeves bound, shuttle and thread at hand, the fingers stained with dye.

**138. Relief-Carver's Dusty Tunic.** _(Middle, man.)_ A tunic grey with stone dust, the sleeves bound, a chisel and mallet, carving the great stone reliefs that lined the palace walls.

**139. Brewer-Woman's Working Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A belted gown with the sleeves tied and a cloth over the hair, tending the vats and the bread of the brewing craft.

**140. Middle Man's Temple-Day Robe.** _(Middle, man.)_ A clean longer fringed robe and a folded shawl kept for temple and feast days, a modest fillet and good sandals.

**141. Middle Daughter's Festival Tunic.** _(Middle, young woman.)_ A linen gown with a dyed and rosette-bordered edge, a beaded collar, small earrings and a fillet. Modest finery for a feast day.

**142. Nobleman's Long Rosette Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A full robe embroidered with rosettes and a sacred-tree border under a deeply fringed shawl, a bossed belt, gold armlets, an oiled and elaborately curled beard.

**143. Noblewoman's Fringed Gown and Collar.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A fine fringed gown under a shawl over the hair, a broad collar of gold, carnelian and lapis, heavy earrings and a diadem, kohl-lined and scented.

**144. Rich Estate-Holder's Embroidered Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A fine embroidered robe and a seal of office, a staff and fine sandals, overseeing fields, orchards and a steward on his granted lands.

**145. Noble Daughter's Betrothal Gown.** _(Wealthy, young woman.)_ A sumptuous dyed gown layered with the gold of her dowry, a fine shawl drawn back from the face, perfumed oils and kohl.

**146. Wealthy Matron's Banquet Gown.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A deeply dyed fine gown with the fullest jewelry, a wreath or diadem, reclining at the banquet while a servant holds the fan.

**147. Nobleman's Winter Fur-Trimmed Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A heavy fine robe trimmed with fur at collar and hem, a lined mantle, a decorated cap and a gold pin. Warmth that shows wealth.

**148. Lady's Fine Linen Summer Gown.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A gown of finely bleached linen, light gold jewelry, delicate sandals, a maid holding a shading cloth. Cool summer luxury.

**149. Heir's Coming-of-Age Robe.** _(Wealthy, young man.)_ A first full fringed adult robe of fine embroidered cloth, a new personal seal and a fillet, taking his place among the men of the house.

**150. Dowager Noblewoman's Robe.** _(Wealthy, older woman.)_ A rich but sober gown, heirloom gold worn lightly, a fine shawl over silvering hair, the bearing of one long obeyed.

### Neo-Assyrian: Trades and Professions (Nos. 151 to 185)

**151. Master Scribe's Fringed Robe.** _(Records.)_ A fine fringed robe, a cylinder seal and a reed stylus, a clay tablet under the arm, keeping the vast records of the empire and the great library of Nineveh.

**152. Master Scholar's Robe.** _(Household and palace training.)_ A sober long robe, a wall of stacked tablets, a young apprentice at his elbow copying: the ummânu, the master scholar, training the next ṭupšarru of the imperial administration. (Source: "Nimrud: Materialities of Assyrian Knowledge Production, Scholarly Families," ORACC, https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/nimrud/ancientkalhu/thepeople/scholarlyfamilies/, checked 2026-09-23.)

> **Not the tablet house.** The *edubba*, the schoolroom with its rows of pupils and its discipline-rod, is the institution the **Old Babylonian** school texts describe, and the literary picture of it comes from them. By the seventh century BC scribal training in Assyria ran through the **family and the palace**: a boy learned from his father or an adoptive master in a scholarly household, in the working rooms of an administration, and the great scholarly families passed the craft down by name. Framing a Nineveh scribe in a classroom with a rod is a thousand-year jump, and it is the single most common error in reconstructions of this subject. The tablet-house entries in [`clothing-01-sumer-by-age.md`](clothing-01-sumer-by-age.md), [`clothing-02-sumer-by-station.md`](clothing-02-sumer-by-station.md) and [`clothing-05-babylon-by-age.md`](clothing-05-babylon-by-age.md) are correct **for their own periods** and must not travel here. (Source: "Old Babylonian Model Contracts: The Old Babylonian School," ORACC, oracc.museum.upenn.edu/obmc/oldbabylonianschool/index.html, checked 2026-09-23, dating the documented edubba to ca. 2000-1595 BC; and "Nimrud: Materialities of Assyrian Knowledge Production -- Scholarly Families," ORACC, oracc.museum.upenn.edu/nimrud/ancientkalhu/thepeople/scholarlyfamilies/, checked 2026-09-23, on family-based training of Neo-Assyrian scribes and scholars.)

**153. Temple Scribe's Linen Robe.** _(Sanctuary.)_ A clean pale linen robe and a fringed shawl, a seal of the god's house, recording the offerings and the omens of the temple of Ashur.

**154. Astronomer-Scribe's Robe.** _(Sky-watcher.)_ A fringed robe and a shawl against the night, a tablet of omens and eclipses, sending his reports of the heavens to the king.

**155. Merchant's Travel Robe.** _(Trade.)_ A long belted robe under a dust-cloak, a seal and a tablet-pouch, a money-belt, trading along the royal roads of the empire.

**156. Market Seller's Tunic and Apron.** _(Stall.)_ A knee tunic with a wide apron-sash of weights and a pouch, a folded awning-cloth, calling his wares in the market.

**157. Cloth Merchant's Fine Robe.** _(Textiles.)_ A robe showing his best wares, dyed and embroidered cloth over the arm, a measuring rod and rings, dealing in fine fabric.

**158. Carpenter's Work Tunic.** _(Wood.)_ A sturdy tunic and a pocketed apron, an adze and chisels, working the imported cedar and the local timber.

**159. Leatherworker's Apron Dress.** _(Hide.)_ A tunic under a thick leather apron darkened by the pits, awls and a curved knife, making the harness, boots and shields of the army.

**160. Fisherman's River Wrap.** _(River.)_ A minimal wrap for the boat, a casting-net over one shoulder, the legs bare, a reed hat and a fish-spear on the Tigris.

**161. Plowman-Farmer's Kilt.** _(Field.)_ A coarse belted kilt for the seeder-plow behind the oxen, a goad, a seed-bag, mud to the knee.

**162. Baker's Flour-Dusted Tunic.** _(Bread; man or woman.)_ A plain undyed tunic powdered with flour, the sleeves bound and a cloth over the hair, baking the loaves of the city.

**163. Barber's Neat Tunic.** _(Grooming.)_ A clean trim tunic, a roll of razor, shears and curling-irons, dressing the elaborate squared and curled beards the Assyrians prized above all.

**164. Physician's Robe.** _(Healer.)_ A clean robe and a satchel of herbs, salves and tools, a protective amulet, sending his diagnoses in writing to the court.

**165. Temple Musician's Festival Dress.** _(Sacred song.)_ A decorated tunic or gown, a lyre, harp or drum, a fillet and beads, the singer and player before Ashur and Ishtar.

**166. Dyer's Color-Stained Dress.** _(Vats.)_ A tunic blotched red, blue and yellow from the vats, the arms dyed to the elbow, by the dye pots.

**167. Goldsmith's Fine Tunic.** _(Precious work.)_ A clean careful tunic and a small apron, tiny tools and a balance, gold wire and beads on the bench, the eyes sharp and close.

**168. Palace Relief-Carver's Tunic.** _(Stone art.)_ A tunic grey with stone dust, the sleeves bound, a chisel and mallet, carving the great gypsum wall-reliefs of war, hunt and procession.

**169. Ivory-Carver's Fine Tunic.** _(Luxury art.)_ A clean careful tunic, the finest small tools, working the delicate carved and inlaid ivories that adorned the furniture of the palaces.

**170. Shepherd's Fleece Cloak.** _(Pasture.)_ A coarse tunic under a sheepskin cloak, a crook and a sling, a leather bag, watching the flocks across the hills.

**171. Master Builder's Robe.** _(Great works.)_ A fine robe and a folded shawl, a measuring reed and a plan-tablet, directing the raising of the king's vast palaces and city walls.

**172. Reed-Worker's Wrap.** _(Mats and baskets.)_ A plain wrap and a cord belt, bundles of cut reed at hand, weaving mats, baskets and the great reed bundles for the river crossings.

**173. Oil-Presser's Tunic.** _(Oil.)_ A tunic stained and slick with oil, the sleeves bound, working the press for the oil of lamp and skin.

**174. Brick-Mason's Kilt.** _(Building.)_ A coarse kilt and a cord belt, a trowel and a plumb-cord, laying the countless bricks of the king's mounded palaces and walls.

**175. Royal Road Courier's Dress.** _(King's post.)_ A short belted tunic and stout boots, a sealed letter-case and a staff, racing the king's word along the royal roads from province to capital.

**176. Ale-House Keeper's Gown.** _(Woman, tavern.)_ A belted gown with the sleeves tied, a cloth over the hair, drawing beer for the men of the ale-house.

**177. Midwife's Practical Gown.** _(Birth.)_ A clean simple gown with the sleeves bound, a satchel of cloths, herbs and a birth-amulet, attending the women of the quarter.

**178. Horse-Trainer's Dress.** _(Cavalry mounts.)_ A short fitted tunic and laced boots, a whip and a lead-rein, breaking and training the many horses of the chariots and the cavalry.

**179. Seal-Cutter's Fine Tunic.** _(Glyptic art.)_ A clean tunic, a tiny drill and gravers, a magnifying eye, cutting the cylinder and stamp seals of officials and merchants.

**180. Estate Steward's Robe.** _(Manager.)_ A clean long robe, a seal and a tablet of accounts, ordering the workers, herds and stores of a royal or temple estate.

**181. Royal Huntsman's Dress.** _(The hunt.)_ A short belted tunic and boots, a bow, a leash and a horn, tending the king's hounds and the caged lions for the royal hunt.

**182. Granary-Keeper's Robe.** _(Stores.)_ A clean robe with a seal and a tally-tablet, the sleeves bound, measuring the barley in and out of the great granary.

**183. Caravan Donkey-Driver's Wrap.** _(Transport.)_ A short coarse wrap and a head-cloth, a goad and a water-skin, leading the laden donkeys along the imperial roads.

**184. Quarry-Stonecutter's Tunic.** _(Stone.)_ A tunic grey with dust, the sleeves bound, a heavy mallet and wedges, cutting the colossal blocks for the palace gates and reliefs.

**185. Master Lamassu-Sculptor's Robe.** _(Gate-guardians.)_ A clean robe over a work tunic, fine and heavy tools at hand, the master who shapes the great winged human-headed bulls that guard the palace gates.

### Neo-Assyrian: Soldiers and Military (Nos. 186 to 200)

**186. Royal Composite-Bow Archer.** _(Bowman.)_ A long fringed tunic or scale corselet, a tall pointed helmet, a powerful composite bow and a full quiver, the deadly core of the Assyrian army.

**187. Spearman of the Line.** _(Infantry.)_ A belted tunic, a pointed helmet, a thrusting spear and a round shield, advancing in close ranks.

**188. Heavy Scale-Armor Infantryman.** _(Shock troop.)_ A long coat of overlapping bronze scales, a pointed helmet with cheek-flaps, a spear and a sword, the armored heavy foot.

**189. Tall-Shield Pavise-Bearer.** _(Cover.)_ A scale tunic and a helmet, a great curved wicker-and-leather pavise as tall as a man, screening the archers as they shoot.

**190. Slinger Skirmisher.** _(Light troop.)_ A short plain tunic and a helmet, a pouch of sling-stones and a sling, whirling his shot from beyond the line.

**191. Charioteer of the War-Chariot.** _(Chariot.)_ A scale tunic with crossed straps, a helmet, the reins of armored horses gathered, driving the heavy war-chariot with its crew of archer and shield-bearer.

**192. Cavalry Lancer.** _(Mounted.)_ A fitted scale tunic and wrapped leggings, a pointed helmet, a long lance and a sword, charging from horseback.

**193. Cavalry Horse-Archer.** _(Mounted bow.)_ A fitted tunic and leggings, a helmet, a composite bow drawn from the saddle, riding in pairs as the reliefs show.

**194. Siege-Ram Crew Soldier.** _(Siege.)_ A scale tunic and a helmet, driving the great wheeled battering-ram with its turret against the enemy wall under a hail of shot.

**195. Sapper and Siege-Tower Soldier.** _(Siege.)_ A scale tunic and a leather cap, a pick or a torch, undermining the wall or climbing the siege tower in the assault.

**196. Royal Bodyguard.** _(King's guard.)_ A fine fringed tunic under polished scale, a crested helmet, a spear and a decorated shield, standing close about the king in war and procession.

**197. Officer's Plumed Armor.** _(Rank.)_ A fine tunic under a scale corselet, a banded helmet crowned with a plume or crest, a sword of office and a decorated belt, commanding the troops.

**198. Standard-Bearer.** _(Emblem.)_ A scale tunic and a helmet, both hands on the pole of the divine standard of Ashur, carried at the head of the marching host.

**199. Veteran Campaigner's Kit.** _(Old hand.)_ A battered scale tunic and a dented helmet, old scars and a trusted bow, having marched the length of the empire and back.

**200. Subject-Levy Auxiliary.** _(Foreign troop.)_ The dress of his own homeland under an Assyrian helmet and belt, a spear or bow, a soldier of a conquered people serving in the imperial army.

### Neo-Assyrian: Rulers, Court and Royalty (Nos. 201 to 225)

**201. King Ashurnasirpal's State Robe.** _(Nimrud.)_ A long robe densely embroidered with rosettes and the sacred tree, a deeply fringed diagonal shawl, the banded polos crown (a truncated cone, not pointed, see No. 246), elaborate gold, a squared curled beard and a mace.

**202. King Ashurbanipal at the Lion Hunt.** _(The hunt.)_ A fringed tunic belted high for action over light armor, armlets and a diadem-band, a composite bow drawn, decorated boots, the great royal test against the lion.

**203. King Reviewing the Siege.** _(Sennacherib at Lachish.)_ A long fringed robe and the banded polos crown (a truncated cone, not pointed, see No. 246), seated on the high throne before a captured city, a bow at rest and attendants with fans behind.

**204. King in War, the Royal Chariot.** _(Campaign.)_ A fringed tunic under fine scale, the banded polos crown (a truncated cone, not pointed, see No. 246), a drawn bow, riding high in the royal war-chariot above the battle. (Source: J. E. Reade, "Fez, Diadem, Turban, Chaplet: Power-Dressing at the Assyrian Court," Studia Orientalia 106 (2009), 239-264, https://journal.fi/store/article/view/52465, checked 2026-09-23; the king's attested chariot headgear, back to the White Obelisk of Ashur-nasirpal I, is the cone-topped fez/polos, not a soldier's pointed helmet.)

**205. King in Worship before Ashur.** _(Devotion.)_ A pure fringed robe and a fillet, a libation cup or censer raised, the proud head bowed before the god Ashur and the sacred tree.

**206. King Beneath the Royal Parasol.** _(Court ceremony.)_ The king in his full rosette robe and crown shaded by a high fringed parasol and fanned by attendants, the very image of majesty in the reliefs.

**207. Queen's Banquet Gown with Mural Crown.** _(Ashurbanipal's queen.)_ A richly fringed and rosette-bordered gown, a turreted mural crown on dressed hair, heavy gold, carnelian and lapis, seated at the feast in the vine garden.

**208. Crown Prince's Robe.** _(Heir.)_ A rich fringed and embroidered robe just below the king's, a diadem-band marking the heir, a personal seal and attendants.

**209. Princess's Fine Gown.** _(Royal daughter.)_ A sumptuous fringed gown with a rosette border, a fine diadem, the rarest gold, carnelian and lapis, scented and adorned from girlhood.

**210. Court Eunuch Official's Robe.** _(Sha reshi, beardless.)_ A fine fringed robe and a smooth beardless face, the court mark of his office, a seal and tablet, standing close behind the throne in the reliefs.

**211. Royal Cup-Bearer's Robe.** _(Rab shaqe.)_ A fine fringed tunic and a shawl, a cloth folded over the arm and the king's cup in hand, a rank far greater than the name implies.

**212. Grand Vizier's Robe.** _(Sukkallu.)_ A magnificent long fringed and embroidered robe, a staff of office and a great seal, standing just below the king as his chief minister.

**213. Palace Chamberlain's Robe.** _(Household.)_ A dignified fringed robe, a staff and the keys of the palace, ordering the vast royal household and who may approach the king.

**214. Provincial Governor's Robe.** _(Shaknu.)_ A fine fringed robe with the seal of his province, a sword and attendants, ruling a great city in the king's name.

**215. Royal Herald's Dress.** _(Court.)_ A fine clear-colored tunic and a staff, proclaiming the king's decree in the squares and bearing his word through the empire.

**216. Royal Scribe's Robe.** _(Chancery.)_ A fine fringed robe marking court service, a seal and a leather document-case, two scribes recording each royal word in clay and on the leather scroll.

**217. Royal Judge's Robe.** _(Law.)_ A grave long fringed robe, a seal and the case-tablets, seated to hear and to judge in the king's name.

**218. Treasurer's Robe.** _(Tribute.)_ A sober rich robe, keys and seals at the belt, the ledgers of the gold, silver and tribute that flowed to Nineveh from the whole earth.

**219. Royal Guard Captain's Dress.** _(Command.)_ A fine fringed tunic under polished scale, a crested helmet, a marked belt and a sword of office, commanding the king's bodyguard.

**220. Tribute-Bearer's Dress.** _(Foreign vassal.)_ The distinctive dress of a far land, bearing gold, cloth, ivory or rare beasts up to the enthroned king, as the reliefs show the nations of the empire.

**221. Royal Envoy's Robe.** _(Embassy.)_ A fine travel-worthy fringed robe upholding the king's dignity abroad, sealed tablets and gifts borne behind, received in foreign courts.

**222. Royal Child's Festival Robe.** _(Palace child.)_ A soft fine linen tunic with a rosette border, a beaded carnelian collar and a gold fillet, a child of the royal house at the feast.

**223. King's Coronation Robe.** _(Investiture.)_ A special robe assumed at the crowning in the temple of Ashur, charged with the symbols of rule, worn at the taking of the throne.

**224. Commander-in-Chief's Robe.** _(Turtanu.)_ A magnificent fringed robe under fine armor, a sword of high office and a decorated belt, the great general second only to the king.

**225. Dowager Queen's Robe.** _(Royal elder.)_ A rich but sober fringed gown, heirloom gold worn lightly, a fine shawl over silvering hair, the queen mother long honored at court.

### Neo-Assyrian: Priests, Scholars and Temple (Nos. 226 to 240)

**226. High Priest of Ashur's Ritual Robe.** _(Chief priest.)_ A long fringed robe with sacred borders, a tall fillet, a long oiled and curled beard, anointing oil and the emblem of Ashur in hand, before the god of the empire.

**227. High Priestess's Robe.** _(En.)_ A fine fringed robe and a rounded crown, layered gold and lapis, a ring and rod, serving Ishtar in grave dignity.

**228. Purification Priest's Fish-Cloak.** _(Apkallu, ashipu.)_ The striking fish-skin cloak drawn over the back and head so the priest takes the shape of a great carp, after the sage Apkallu, a holy-water bucket and a purification cone in his hands.

**229. Diviner's Robe.** _(Baru.)_ A clean fringed robe and a shawl, a clay model liver at his side, reading the gods' answer in the entrails before the king goes to war.

**230. Lamentation Priest's Dress.** _(Kalu.)_ A robe for the chanting of laments, a kettledrum or lyre at hand, the voice raised to soothe the angry heart of the god.

**231. Exorcist's Night-Rite Dress.** _(Ashipu.)_ A robe for the rites against the demons of the dark, plaques of Pazuzu and bound Lamashtu about him, a censer and a torch, incantation tablets in hand.

**232. Astrologer-Scholar's Robe.** _(Court omens.)_ A fine fringed robe and a shawl, tablets of celestial omens, one of the learned men who watched the sky and wrote their warnings and counsel to the king.

**233. Dream-Interpreter's Robe.** _(Visions.)_ A sober robe and a shawl, omen-tablets at hand, reading the meaning of dreams for the king and the troubled who come to the temple.

**234. Temple Administrator's Robe.** _(Estate.)_ A clean long fringed robe, a seal and a tally-tablet, ordering the lands, herds, silver and offerings of the great temple of Ashur.

**235. Novice Priest's Plain Robe.** _(Temple novice.)_ A shaven head and a plain undyed linen robe with a simple sash, learning the washings, the rites and the songs.

**236. Royal Sage-Scholar's Robe.** _(Ummanu.)_ A fine sober fringed robe and a shawl, tablets of lore, omen and medicine close by, one of the learned circle that advised Ashurbanipal and stocked his library.

**237. Cloistered Priestess's Gown.** _(Devoted woman.)_ A modest fine gown and a drawn shawl, a personal seal at the wrist, a woman vowed to the temple yet keeping her own lands and accounts.

**238. Hymn-Singer Priestess's Dress.** _(Sacred song.)_ A decorated gown and a fillet, beads of faience and carnelian, lifting the temple hymn to Ishtar with lyre and voice.

**239. Offering-Bearer Priest's Dress.** _(Procession.)_ A clean fringed robe and a folded shawl, both hands bearing a dish of fruit, a kid or a bowl of grain to the god.

**240. Akitu Procession Priest's Robe.** _(New Year.)_ A rich robe with woven sacred motifs and a tall fillet, attending the image of the god borne in splendor at the New Year festival of Ashur.

### Neo-Assyrian: Garment Types and Materials (Nos. 241 to 250)

**241. The Rosette-Embroidered Robe.** _(Signature garment.)_ The defining Assyrian dress of rank: a long robe densely worked with embroidered rosettes, palmettes and the sacred tree, the richest cloth of the palace.

**242. The Diagonal Fringed Shawl.** _(Drape.)_ A long deeply fringed shawl wound diagonally across the body and over one shoulder above the tunic, the characteristic Assyrian over-garment.

**243. The Long Fringed Tunic.** _(Everyday formal.)_ A belted tunic, short-sleeved and reaching the calf or ankle, its hem and sleeves finished with fringe, the standard dress of the Assyrian man of means.

**244. The Sacred-Tree Bordered Robe.** _(Ceremonial.)_ A robe whose borders are woven or embroidered with the sacred tree and winged genii, worn by king and high official in rites and at court.

**245. The Bossed and Studded Belt.** _(Fastening.)_ A wide belt set with rows of metal bosses and studs, both ornament and light protection, worn by warriors and men of rank.

**246. The Banded Polos Crown.** _(Headwear.)_ The tall truncated-cone polos of the Assyrian king, bound with a decorated diadem band and rising to a smaller cone at its crest, the very mark of the throne; not a pointed crown. (Source: J. E. Reade, "Fez, Diadem, Turban, Chaplet: Power-Dressing at the Assyrian Court," Studia Orientalia 106 (2009), 239-264, https://journal.fi/store/article/view/52465, checked 2026-09-23.)

**247. The Bronze Scale Armor.** _(War garment.)_ A coat or tunic sewn with hundreds of overlapping bronze scales over a cloth backing, the body-armor that made the Assyrian soldier so feared.

**248. Assyrian Wool and Embroidery.** _(Material.)_ The staple woven wool of the empire, raised to luxury by the dense embroidery of rosette and tree for which Assyrian cloth was renowned.

**249. Assyrian Fine Linen.** _(Material.)_ Cloth of flax, finer and cooler than wool, bleached pale for priests, the court and the festival best.

**250. Assyrian Dyed and Tasseled Cloth.** _(Material.)_ The finest wool dyed in madder red, blue and the rare purple, finished with deep fringe and corded tassels, the cloth of kings and the highest court.
