## Part V. Assyria. The Middle Assyrian Empire

_The dress of the first Assyrian empire, approximately 1392 to 934 BC, when kings such as Tukulti-Ninurta the First and Tiglath-Pileser the First carried the arms of Ashur far and even held Babylon for a time. From this age come the Middle Assyrian Laws, which fixed who among women might draw a shawl over her head: the free wife and the betrothed, but not the slave or the harlot, the covering being a mark of standing and not of faith, never drawn across the face. The long fringed royal robe now rises toward its later glory. One hundred and twenty-five garments follow._


### Middle Assyrian: Children, Birth to Eighteen (Nos. 1 to 15)

**1. Newborn's Wool Swaddle.** _(Common.)_ An undyed sheep-wool band wound in a spiral so only the face is free, tucked and tied with a wool cord. The common newborn wrap of Middle Assyrian Ashur.

**2. Officer-Family Linen Swaddle.** _(Wealthy, military house.)_ A soft bleached linen band with a thin fringed edge, a carnelian bead at the throat, for the child of an officer of the rising imperial army.

**3. Poor Quarter Reed Swaddle.** _(Poor.)_ Coarse undyed wool and reused cloth bound with twisted reed cord, bare-headed, the wrap of the poor families of the city.

**4. Lambswool Winter Bundle.** _(Cold season.)_ Carded lambswool over an inner linen band, bound with a felted wool sash in cream and brown, against the northern cold.

**5. Pazuzu-Amulet Swaddle.** _(Protective.)_ A plain wool swaddle with a bronze Pazuzu head knotted into the chest cord to turn away Lamashtu, who threatens mother and newborn.

**6. Infant's Linen Shift.** _(6 to 12 months.)_ A tiny sleeveless linen shift to the knees, undyed with a faint edge-stripe, gathered at the neck with a flax drawstring.

**7. Toddler's Short Wool Tunic.** _(Toddler, everyday.)_ A plain knee-length sleeveless wool tunic belted with a woven cord, the child barefoot.

**8. Toddler's Fringed Tunic.** _(Toddler, modest means.)_ A small wool tunic with a fringed hem and a thin cord belt, the head shaved with a single side-lock.

**9. Little Girl's Beaded Wrap.** _(Girl, 4 to 7.)_ A soft wool wrap pinned over one shoulder with a short string of faience and carnelian beads.

**10. Little Boy's Fringed Kilt.** _(Boy, 4 to 7.)_ A short wrapped wool kilt with a fringed edge and a cord belt, the chest bare, the head shaved with a side-lock.

**11. Officer's-Son Boy's Tunic.** _(Boy, 7 to 12.)_ A clean fringed tunic and a beaded belt, learning the bow and the horse early, the son of one of the empire's officer families.

**12. Weaver-Girl's Gown.** _(Girl, 10 to 15.)_ A plain belted gown with the sleeves bound, spindle and thread at hand, learning the craft of cloth.

**13. Young Soldier-Cadet's Tunic.** _(Young man, 15 to 18.)_ A belted wool tunic and a leather cap, a practice-spear and a bow, a youth training for the campaigns of the king.

**14. Young Woman's Gown with Shawl.** _(Young woman, 15 to 18.)_ A long belted gown with a shawl over one shoulder, a beaded necklace, the hair dressed, near the age of marriage.

**15. Ashur-Festival Child's Robe.** _(Wealthy child, feast.)_ A soft pale linen tunic with a woven border and a beaded carnelian collar, a fillet, for a wealthy child at the festival of the god Ashur.

### Middle Assyrian: Men by Age, Eighteen to Eighty (Nos. 16 to 37)

**16. Young Man's Fringed Tunic and Shawl.** _(18 to 30, everyday.)_ A knee tunic under a fringed shawl drawn over one shoulder, a wide belt, a trimmed beard. The everyday dress of a young Middle Assyrian townsman.

**17. Young Man's Wrapped Robe.** _(18 to 30, formal.)_ A wool robe wound about the body with a fringed edge and a folded shawl, worn to call upon an officer or an elder.

**18. Bridegroom's Robe.** _(18 to 30, wedding.)_ A fresh fringed robe with an embroidered shawl, a fillet on oiled hair, new sandals and a ring, for the marriage feast.

**19. Young Worker's Short Kilt.** _(18 to 30, labor.)_ A short coarse wool kilt and a cord belt, the chest bare, working the fields and the building works of the city.

**20. Summer Linen Tunic.** _(18 to 30, hot season.)_ A light undyed linen knee tunic, short-sleeved and loose, plain sandals, cool for the summer heat.

**21. Festival Robe.** _(18 to 30, festival.)_ A robe dyed and bordered with a tasseled shawl and a bronze armlet, the beard combed, for the festival of Ashur.

**22. Young Soldier's Belted Tunic.** _(18 to 30, military.)_ A belted wool tunic and a bronze helmet, a spear and a bow, a young man of the army that was making Ashur an empire.

**23. Young Royal Attendant's Tunic.** _(18 to 30, court.)_ A clean fringed tunic and a beaded belt, the hair and beard dressed, serving in the growing court of the king.

**24. Prime Man's Long Fringed Robe.** _(30 to 50, everyday.)_ A full fringed robe to the calf with a shawl over one shoulder, soberly belted, the beard full. The settled dress of a man in his prime.

**25. Official's Fringed Robe and Shawl.** _(30 to 50, formal.)_ A long fringed robe under a deeper fringed shawl wound across the body, a seal of office, the dress of a man who serves the king and the state.

**26. Father's Work Tunic.** _(30 to 50, labor.)_ A sturdy knee tunic and a wide belt with tools, the sleeves bound, practical for the day's work and the raising of a family.

**27. Winter Wool Mantle.** _(30 to 50, cold season.)_ A heavy fulled-wool mantle over a long tunic, a rounded felt cap, the folds deep against the cold.

**28. Banquet Robe.** _(30 to 50, feast.)_ A bordered robe and a fringed shawl, an armlet and a cup, reclining among the men at the feast.

**29. Temple Offering Robe.** _(30 to 50, devotional.)_ A clean fringed robe and a folded shawl, the beard combed, bringing an offering before the god Ashur.

**30. Army Officer's Dress.** _(30 to 50, military.)_ A fringed tunic under a bronze scale corselet, a crested helmet and a sword of office, commanding men in the king's campaigns.

**31. Provincial Official's Robe.** _(30 to 50, administration.)_ A fine fringed robe and a seal, governing a town or a district for the king as the empire spread its rule outward.

**32. Senior Man's Sober Robe.** _(50 to 65, everyday.)_ A long fringed robe in muted tones, a plain belt, the beard mostly grey, the bearing settled.

**33. Elder Counselor's Fringed Robe.** _(50 to 65, standing.)_ A full fringed robe and a smooth staff, an old respected voice at the gate and in the counsels of the city.

**34. Old Veteran's Honored Robe.** _(50 to 65, old soldier.)_ A fringed robe over a scarred frame, an old campaign-belt kept with pride, telling the young of the wars that built the empire.

**35. Winter Fleece Robe.** _(50 to 65, cold season.)_ A long tunic under a fleece-lined mantle, a deep felt cap, the hands tucked by the hearth.

**36. Devotional Robe of the Aged.** _(65 to 80, temple.)_ A clean soft robe and shawl, the white beard combed, seated long before the god Ashur.

**37. Mourning Wrap.** _(Any age, grief.)_ A coarse undyed sack-wool wrap, the chest bare and dusted with ash, the fine robe set aside, mourning a wife or a child.

### Middle Assyrian: Women, Age and the Covering Law (Nos. 38 to 59)

**38. Free Wife's Head-Shawl Gown.** _(18 to 30, lawful covering.)_ A long gown with a fringed wool shawl drawn over the back of the hair and one shoulder, never across the face, the lawful mark of a free married woman under the Middle Assyrian Laws, a sign of rank and not of faith. The statute is Middle Assyrian only: no surviving Neo-Assyrian law text restates or repeals it, which is why Part VI No. 82 carries the same shawl shape without a legal claim. (Source: Middle Assyrian Laws, Tablet A, paragraph 40, reign of Tiglath-Pileser I, 12th century BC; trans. G. R. Driver and J. C. Miles, and T. J. Meek in Ancient Near Eastern Texts, 3rd ed., Princeton University Press, 1969; checked 2026-09-23.)

**39. Betrothed Maiden's Shawl Gown.** _(18 to 30, lawful covering.)_ A gown with the head-shawl drawn back over the hair, worn by a betrothed girl once promised, whom the law also permitted and required to cover in public.

**40. Unmarried Daughter's Uncovered Gown.** _(18 to 30, uncovered.)_ A belted gown with the hair dressed and bound but no head-shawl, the open-headed dress of the unwed daughter, who did not yet take the covering of a wife.

**41. Bride's Adorned Gown.** _(18 to 30, wedding.)_ A dyed gown with a beaded collar of carnelian and lapis, a fillet and heavy earrings, a fine shawl drawn back, the hair oiled and scented, for the marriage feast.

**42. Young Wife's Nursing Gown.** _(18 to 30, motherhood.)_ A loose gown of soft wool, easily opened at the breast, belted high, a shawl over the hair and a Pazuzu amulet to guard the infant.

**43. Festival Gown.** _(18 to 30, festival.)_ A brightly dyed gown with a tasseled shawl over the hair and the full jewelry of faience, carnelian and gold, the eyes kohl-lined.

**44. Free Matron's Head-Shawl Gown.** _(30 to 50, everyday.)_ A full ankle gown with a fringed shawl over the hair and one shoulder, belted, the everyday lawful dress of a free married woman of standing.

**45. Concubine's Conditional-Shawl Gown.** _(30 to 50, by law.)_ A good gown with a shawl drawn over the hair only when she walked abroad with her master's wife, as the law allowed the concubine, but not otherwise.

**46. Household Manager's Gown with Seal.** _(30 to 50, authority.)_ A belted gown with a shawl over the hair and a seal and pouch at the waist, the free wife who keeps the household's stores.

**47. Mother's Gown with Carrying Sling.** _(30 to 50, motherhood.)_ A practical wool gown belted high, a shawl over the hair, a wide woven sling for a small child, an amulet at the shoulder.

**48. Festival Matron's Adornment.** _(30 to 50, festival.)_ A richly dyed gown layered with carnelian, faience and gold, heavy earrings and a thin diadem over a fine head-shawl, kohl-lined eyes.

**49. Summer Linen Gown.** _(30 to 50, hot season.)_ A loose undyed linen gown with a light shawl over the hair, modest at the neck, a single strand of beads, cool for the heat.

**50. Winter Wool-and-Felt Layers.** _(30 to 50, cold season.)_ A long wool gown under a thick felted mantle drawn over head and shoulders, a felt-lined wrap at the throat, in deep browns.

**51. Mourning Gown of Coarse Wool.** _(30 to 50, grief.)_ A coarse undyed gown, the jewelry and the fine shawl set aside, the hair loosed and dusted with ash, the cloth torn at the breast.

**52. Senior Free Matron's Full Gown.** _(50 to 65, everyday.)_ A full floor-length gown with a fringed shawl over the greying hair, soberly belted, the settled dress of an older free wife.

**53. Matriarch's Heavy Mantle.** _(50 to 65, formal.)_ A long gown under a great fringed mantle over head and shoulders, heavy old necklaces from her youth, broad earrings.

**54. Mistress of the Household Gown.** _(50 to 65, authority.)_ A good-wool gown with a head-shawl and a heavy belt of keys, seal and pouch, commanding the stores and the younger women of the house.

**55. Grandmother's Practical Gown.** _(50 to 65, daily care.)_ A soft, easy gown belted high, a shawl over the white hair and one always at the shoulder for a grandchild, an old amulet at the breast.

**56. Banquet Gown of an Elder Lady.** _(50 to 65, feast.)_ A deep-dyed gown with layered gold and carnelian, a diadem over a fine head-shawl, scented and kohl-lined, honored at the feast.

**57. Slave-Woman's Plain Uncovered Shift.** _(Any age, forbidden the shawl.)_ A plain coarse shift and a cord belt, the hair bound but the head bare, for by the law a slave woman was forbidden the head-shawl of the free, on pain of punishment.

**58. Winter Fleece-Wrapped Elder.** _(65 to 80, cold season.)_ A heavy gown under a felted mantle and hood, a fleece wrap over the knees, slippers and a pin at the throat, by the fire.

**59. Mourning Gown of an Elder Woman.** _(65 to 80, grief.)_ A coarse undyed gown, all gold and the fine shawl set aside, the white hair loosed and ashed, the cloth rent.

**Outranks:** a frame that strips the lawful Middle Assyrian head-shawl from a free wife or a betrothed woman — Nos. 38, 39 and the covered entries that follow them. That covering is a mark of legal standing, the law made it compulsory for exactly those women and forbade it to the slave and the harlot, and a frame without it has removed the one garment that says who the woman is (rule 11 in [`heritage-rules.md`](./heritage-rules.md)).
**Yields to:** the brief, wherever it asks for a covering drawn across the face or styled as devotion. **Mesopotamia had no face veil**, so that is a modern choice made in the brief, not a period detail (rule 10 in [`heritage-rules.md`](./heritage-rules.md): religious imagery follows the brief).

### Middle Assyrian: Social Classes and Trades (Nos. 60 to 79)

**60. Field Laborer's Loincloth.** _(Poor, man.)_ Bare to the waist, a short rough wool cloth knotted at the hip, the feet bare, a folded cloth on the head against the sun.

**61. Water-Carrier's Shoulder Cloth.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A plain undyed shift with a thick folded pad on one shoulder for the jar, a head-cloth, bare feet, carrying water from the river.

**62. Washerwoman's Tucked Gown.** _(Poor, woman.)_ A coarse gown hitched and tucked for wading, the sleeves bound, the hair wrapped, beating cloth clean at the river.

**63. Poor Widow's Patched Gown.** _(Poor, older woman.)_ A much-mended undyed gown, a thin worn shawl, no jewelry but a single clay bead, grey with washing and years.

**64. Bondservant's Plain Shift.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A knee-length coarse wool shift, undyed and corded, the hair cropped close. The household bondservant.

**65. Deportee Laborer's Tunic.** _(Poor, resettled.)_ A plain coarse tunic and a cord belt, the dress of a people resettled by the expanding empire and set to labor on its fields and building works.

**66. Potter's Clay-Dusted Tunic.** _(Trade, man.)_ A worn tunic powdered with clay, the sleeves rolled, the hands wet and grey at the turning wheel.

**67. Smith's Apron Dress.** _(Trade, man.)_ A short tunic under a scorched-leather apron, the forearms wrapped, forging the bronze and the growing iron of the army's gear.

**68. Carpenter's Work Tunic.** _(Trade, man.)_ A sturdy tunic and a pocketed apron, an adze and chisels, building house, chariot and gate.

**69. Weaver's Practical Gown.** _(Trade, woman.)_ A plain belted gown with the sleeves bound, shuttle and thread at hand, the fingers stained with dye.

**70. Leatherworker's Apron Dress.** _(Trade, man.)_ A tunic under a thick leather apron, awls and a knife, making the harness, boots, shields and scale-backing of the army.

**71. Baker's Flour-Dusted Tunic.** _(Trade, man or woman.)_ A tunic powdered with flour, the sleeves bound and a cloth over the hair, baking the loaves of the city and the army's bread.

**72. Shepherd's Fleece Cloak.** _(Trade, man.)_ A coarse tunic under a sheepskin cloak, a crook and a sling, watching the flocks across the hills.

**73. Dyer's Color-Stained Dress.** _(Trade, man.)_ A tunic blotched red, blue and yellow from the vats, the arms dyed to the elbow, by the dye pots.

**74. Townsman's Fringed Robe.** _(Middle, man.)_ A decent wrapped wool robe with a fringed edge and a woven belt, a trimmed beard, leather sandals. The respectable townsman.

**75. Merchant's Robe.** _(Middle, man.)_ A good belted robe and a dust-cloak, a seal and a purse, carrying on the trade that the empire's roads now made safer.

**76. Shopkeeper's Wife's Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A good-wool gown with a head-shawl, a few faience and silver beads, a small household seal at the belt.

**77. Nobleman's Long Fringed Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A full fringed robe of fine wool with a deep fringed shawl, a bossed belt, gold armlets, an oiled and curled beard.

**78. Noblewoman's Gown with Head-Shawl.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A fine fringed gown under a shawl over the hair, a broad collar of gold, carnelian and lapis, heavy earrings and a diadem.

**79. Estate-Holder's Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A fine fringed robe and a seal of office, overseeing the lands granted to him for his service to the king, a steward at his side.

### Middle Assyrian: The Imperial Army (Nos. 80 to 97)

**80. Royal Bowman.** _(Archer.)_ A belted tunic and a bronze helmet, a composite bow and a full quiver, the archer of the army that made Ashur an empire.

**81. Spearman of the Line.** _(Infantry.)_ A knee tunic and a wide belt, a helmet, a thrusting spear and a round shield, advancing in close ranks.

**82. Scale-Armor Infantryman.** _(Shock troop.)_ A tunic sewn with early bronze scales, a pointed helmet, a spear and a sword, the armored foot of the expanding army.

**83. Shield-Bearer.** _(Cover.)_ A belted tunic and a leather cap, a tall shield carried to screen the archers as they shot.

**84. Charioteer.** _(War-chariot.)_ A scale tunic with crossed straps, a helmet, the reins gathered, driving the war-chariot that was the striking arm of this age.

**85. Chariot-Archer.** _(Chariot crew.)_ A scale tunic and a helmet, a composite bow drawn from the moving car, shooting over the driver's shoulder.

**86. Axe-Soldier.** _(Close combat.)_ A belted tunic and a helmet, a socketed war-axe and a round shield, for the hand-to-hand of the assault.

**87. Slinger.** _(Light troop.)_ A short plain tunic, a pouch of sling-stones and a sling, harrying from beyond the line.

**88. Officer's Dress.** _(Rank.)_ A fringed tunic under a scale corselet, a crested helmet and a sword of office, leading the troops in the king's campaigns.

**89. Royal Guard.** _(Bodyguard.)_ A fine fringed tunic and a polished helmet, a tall spear and a fine shield, standing about the king in war and hall.

**90. Standard-Bearer.** _(Emblem.)_ A scale tunic and a helmet, both hands on the pole of the divine standard of Ashur, carried at the head of the host.

**91. Siege Soldier.** _(Siege.)_ A scale tunic and a leather cap, a shield and a pick or axe, breaking into the walls of a besieged city.

**92. Veteran Campaigner.** _(Old hand.)_ A battered scale tunic and a dented helmet, old scars and a trusted bow, having marched the long campaigns to the mountains and the sea.

**93. Garrison Soldier.** _(Occupation.)_ A belted tunic, a helmet and a spear, holding a frontier fort or a conquered town for the crown of Ashur.

**94. Frontier Scout.** _(Reconnaissance.)_ A short light tunic and soft boots, a bow and a water-skin, ranging ahead of the army to watch the passes and the foe.

**95. Levy Conscript.** _(New levy.)_ A plain tunic and a leather cap, a spear from the store, a farmer called from the land to the season's campaign.

**96. Booty-Escort Soldier.** _(Guard duty.)_ A belted tunic and a spear, guarding the long lines of plunder and captives brought home from the conquered lands.

**97. King's Elite Guardsman.** _(Tukulti-Ninurta's guard.)_ A fine scale tunic and a crested helmet, a decorated shield and spear, of the chosen guard of the king who carried Ashur's arms into Babylon itself.

### Middle Assyrian: Rulers and Court (Nos. 98 to 115)

**98. The King's State Robe.** _(Royal dress.)_ A long fringed robe with a wound fringed shawl, a banded conical cap and a long curled beard, the royal dress now rising toward the splendor of the later empire.

**99. King Tukulti-Ninurta's War Robe.** _(Conqueror of Babylon.)_ A fringed war tunic under fine scale, a pointed helmet and a mace, the dress of the king who broke and held Babylon, shown kneeling before the sacred symbol on his cult pedestal.

**100. King Tiglath-Pileser's Hunt Dress.** _(The great hunter.)_ A fringed tunic belted high for action, a bow and a quiver, an armlet and a diadem-band, the dress of the king who boasted of slaying lions, wild bulls and even elephants.

**101. King in Worship before Ashur.** _(Devotion.)_ A pure fringed robe and a fillet, a libation cup or censer raised before the god Ashur, the king as the god's chief servant.

**102. King's Coronation Robe.** _(Investiture.)_ A special robe assumed at the crowning in the temple of Ashur, charged with the symbols of rule, worn at the taking of the throne.

**103. The Queen's Gown and Diadem.** _(Consort.)_ A fine fringed gown under a shawl over the hair, a broad collar of gold and lapis, a thin diadem, the lady of the royal house.

**104. Crown Prince's Robe.** _(Heir.)_ A rich fringed robe just below the king's, a diadem-band, a seal and attendants, the son who will hold the empire.

**105. Princess's Gown.** _(Royal daughter.)_ A sumptuous fringed gown with a fine head-shawl, a diadem, the rarest gold, carnelian and lapis, scented and adorned.

**106. Grand Vizier's Robe.** _(Sukkallu rabiu.)_ A magnificent long fringed robe, a staff of office and a great seal, the chief minister standing just below the king.

**107. Provincial Governor's Robe.** _(Governor.)_ A fine fringed robe with the seal of his province, a sword and attendants, ruling a conquered land in the king's name.

**108. Royal Cup-Bearer's Robe.** _(Court.)_ A fine tunic and a folded shawl, a cloth over the arm and the king's cup in hand, a trusted officer of the court.

**109. Palace Herald's Robe.** _(Court.)_ A fine clear-colored tunic and a staff, proclaiming the king's word in the palace and the city.

**110. Royal Scribe's Robe.** _(Chancery.)_ A fine robe marking court service, a seal and a leather document-case, keeping the annals and the letters of the king.

**111. Royal Judge's Robe.** _(Law.)_ A grave long robe, a seal and the law-tablets, seated to judge by the Middle Assyrian Laws in the king's name.

**112. Treasurer's Robe.** _(Treasury.)_ A sober rich robe, keys and seals at the belt, the ledgers of the plunder and tribute that the campaigns brought to Ashur.

**113. Royal Guard Captain's Dress.** _(Command.)_ A fine fringed tunic under polished scale, a crested helmet and a sword of office, commanding the king's guard.

**114. Royal Envoy's Robe.** _(Embassy.)_ A fine travel-worthy fringed robe upholding the king's dignity abroad, sealed tablets and gifts borne behind, sent to Babylon, Hatti or Egypt.

**115. Eunuch Court Official's Robe.** _(Sha reshi, beardless.)_ A fine fringed robe and a smooth beardless face, a seal and a tablet, a trusted official close to the king, as the court of Ashur kept even in this age.

### Middle Assyrian: Priests, Scholars and Garment Types (Nos. 116 to 125)

**116. High Priest of Ashur's Robe.** _(Chief priest.)_ A long fringed robe with a sacred border, a tall fillet, a long oiled beard, anointing oil and the emblem of Ashur in hand.

**117. Diviner's Robe.** _(Baru.)_ A clean fringed robe and a shawl, a clay model liver at his side, reading the gods' answer in the entrails before the king set out to war.

**118. Exorcist's Robe.** _(Ashipu.)_ A robe for the rites against the demons of the dark, plaques of Pazuzu and bound Lamashtu about him, a censer and incantation tablets in hand.

**119. Lamentation Priest's Dress.** _(Kalu.)_ A robe for the chanting of laments, a kettledrum or lyre at hand, the voice raised to soothe the angry heart of the god.

**120. Temple Scribe-Scholar's Robe.** _(Learning.)_ A fine fringed robe and a shawl, tablets of ritual, omen and the royal annals close by, keeper of the temple's learning.

**121. Novice Priest's Plain Robe.** _(Temple novice.)_ A shaven head and a plain undyed linen robe with a simple sash, learning the washings, the rites and the songs.

**122. The Rising Fringed Royal Robe.** _(Signature garment.)_ The long fringed robe of the Middle Assyrian king and elite, growing richer in fringe and border, the direct ancestor of the rosette-laden robes of the later empire.

**123. The Free Woman's Head-Shawl.** _(Status garment.)_ The fringed shawl drawn over the hair, by the Middle Assyrian Laws the lawful mark of the free wife and the betrothed, forbidden to slave and harlot, a sign of rank worn never across the face.

**124. Early Scale Armor.** _(War garment.)_ A tunic or corselet sewn with rows of small bronze scales, the developing body-armor of the Middle Assyrian soldier, forerunner of the famed coats of the Neo-Assyrian army.

**125. Middle Assyrian Wool and Fringe.** _(Materials.)_ The staple woven wool of the empire, finished increasingly with deep fringe and woven border, the cloth in which rank and standing were read.
