# Still-Life, Product and Set Staging

16 cards. These decide a frame: what is in it, how it is lit, and what it is made of.

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, what to avoid, and the prompt written out in full.

---

### Name the Duty Before the Arrangement

**Also called:** the staging selector, duty before arrangement, who is this frame answerable to  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `staging.` shelf. It does not describe a table. It asks the three questions this shelf asks of itself over and over — **who is this frame answerable to, how many things is it counting, and is this decision taken before the table is dressed or after the frame came back wrong** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were read off the shelf's own escape conditions and off the boundary clause every card carries; they were not invented. Every card named here is named by id.  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so the arrangement that does reach the screen was chosen against a duty, a count and a moment, rather than assembled out of habit.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **out loud, in order, in one sentence each, before the first object is placed**. The first answer eliminates two thirds of the shelf and tells you whether the answer is even on it — this shelf leaves itself more often than it stays. The second and third leave a shortlist of two to four. If your honest answer to question one is *nobody*, stop and read the map twice: most of what you want is on the composition and promotion shelves, and this shelf will send you there one card at a time.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** **The moisture, wear or state of a surface is the thing in question, because this shelf and the materials shelf disagree about who owns it and neither has conceded.** One card here says the state of a surface is almost entirely a staging decision and not a lighting one; a card on the neighbouring shelf says the same difference is almost entirely moisture and edge light. Route to both and settle it yourself; do not let this map pretend the boundary is closed. Also **distrust the third axis' last value**: only two cards here are fixes applied to a frame that already exists, so when your honest answer is *the frame came back wrong* this map is nearly empty and the repair you want lives in the failure family, not on this shelf. And **avoid this card on a frame whose subject is a person** — every card below stages objects, and the one card that admits a human being does it by leaving the chair pushed back and the person gone.  
**Prompt:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

---

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — Who is this frame answerable to: the past, the buyer, or nobody?**
Asked first, because it is the condition the shelf escapes on. Almost every outbound pointer here leaves the family, and they leave in three clean groups. The ones that fire when the frame owes a duty to commerce — *"Commerce needs the item shown whole"* (`staging.overlap_for_depth`), *"Clinical frame where any trace reads as contamination"* (`staging.human_trace`), *"Value is wholeness; show freshness on the surface"* (`staging.cut_and_reveal`). The ones that fire when it owes a duty to evidence — *"Cannot research everything; hold the rest unfocused"* (`staging.period_set_dressing`), *"Subject is abundance: market, feast, workshop"* (`staging.props_earn_place`). And the ones that fire when it owes nothing and the image itself is the product — *"The object is meant to read as suspended"* (`staging.height_and_lift`), *"The cut-out graphic read is the intent"* (`staging.background_separation`), *"Extreme minimalism, the disproportion is the statement"* (`staging.empty_space_as_object`). **The shelf's exits are sorted by duty**, which is what an axis is.

**Axis 2 — How many things is the frame counting: one, a few in relation, a population, or none?**
The second most repeated condition, and the only one two cards leave the same card for: *"Subject is genuinely a collection or product family"* (`staging.hero_and_support`) and *"The order is the point: row, grid"* (`staging.triangle_arrangement`) both give way to the same card for the same underlying reason — the frame is counting a set and you had been treating it as a hero. Counting also sorts the boundary clauses that never became pointers: a subject *genuinely a collection*, a subject whose *order is the point*, a subject that is *abundance*, a break *lost in density*, a ground carrying more contrast than the thing standing on it.

**Axis 3 — Is this decided before the table is dressed, or after the frame came back?**
Four cards state the moment inside their own definition — the surface chosen *before anything is placed on it*, the colour relationship built *rather than staging first and correcting afterwards*, the props gated on whether they carry information, the period dressing *checked rather than trusted*. Two others describe themselves as what you reach for once something has already failed: the separation is *the usual fix when a well-lit object still reads as flat*, and the scale companion answers an image that has already been misread. Asked last because it is the only axis that tells you whether you are still allowed to make the choice.

**The default, and the one against it**

**In-degree cannot choose a default on this shelf, and saying so is the first honest thing this card does.** Nothing here is pointed at more than twice from anywhere in the package. The card pointed at twice — `staging.stack_and_repeat` — takes both of its inbound edges **on the same condition**: the frame is counting a set and the reader arrived believing it was counting a hero. One condition, two edges. That makes it the shelf's escape hatch out of its own opening assumption, not its fallback. Two of the remaining inbound edges start at named failures on the repair family, which are instructions pointing *into* the map from outside it rather than routes across it, and they are discounted here for that reason.

**So the default is argued rather than counted: `staging.surface_choice` governing, with `staging.hero_and_support` placed on top of it.** The surface is the only decision on this shelf taken while the frame is still empty, and it is the only one whose answer changes every other card's answer — the same vessel on scrubbed pine, on polished marble and on packed earth is three different frames before a second object exists. `staging.hero_and_support` is what the shelf spends its own internal pointers arguing with, which is the tell that it is what everyone reaches for first. Together they are the ordinary answer, and they are ordinary: a clean ground, one lit thing, everything else deferring to it. That is the frame this shelf produces when nobody asks it a question.

**The one against it is `staging.human_trace`, and the argument is that it breaks the shelf's one shared assumption.** Every other card here arranges objects for a viewer who is understood to be alone with them; this one puts a person into the frame by having them leave the room. It is the only card whose boundary clause names half of its own shelf as the enemy — a clinical frame where any trace reads as contamination is precisely the frame the default builds — and the only one that converts an arrangement into a moment, which no amount of triangle, overlap or lift can do. It is also the only card here that a different shelf recognises as the alternative to working on the object itself: a reader deciding how to age a hero object is sent away from distressing it and told to carry the use in the staging instead. Its cost is stated and real — it cannot serve a frame that has to look untouched — and a spoon set down across a rim pays it in one gesture.

---

**The unasked**

**Nine of the fifteen cards are the target of no pointer anywhere in the package.** They can be chosen, but nothing ever sends you to them. By id:

`staging.hero_and_support` · `staging.triangle_arrangement` · `staging.overlap_for_depth` · `staging.surface_choice` · `staging.cut_and_reveal` · `staging.height_and_lift` · `staging.colour_relationship` · `staging.wet_and_dry` · `staging.empty_space_as_object`

**And the shape of that list is the finding, not its length.** Line it against the third axis and the pattern is exact: **every card that is reached is a card that answers a problem with a frame that already exists** — the set turned out bigger than a hero, the ground ate the subject, the hero object may not be distressed, the prop has to carry story, the size was misread, the background was invented. **Not one pointer in the package sends a reader to a decision taken before the table is dressed.** Both cards that gate the frame before it exists are unasked, and so is every card about arranging a few things in space. The pair field on this shelf is a repair channel, not a choice channel — which is also why almost every pointer here leaves the family. **This shelf is built as a departure lounge**, and a reader who trusted its pointers as a map of it would conclude that staging is a set of corrections rather than a set of decisions.

The second thing the list shows: **nothing on this shelf has a clock.** The state cards name moisture, dust, dryness and steam as conditions, and none of them says how long the arrangement has to hold — ice that melts, herbs that wilt, condensation that runs dry between the wide and the close, a cut face that browns while the light is being set. The nearest card records that failure in its own boundary clause, where everything wet at once reads as artificial, and nothing routes a reader there to read it. The card this shelf wants is the one that says *the arrangement has a life, and the order you shoot it in is part of the staging*. The second gap is smaller and just as clean: nothing here decides what the frame is **for** — whether it will be cropped square, fed to a video stage, or carry type across it. One card touches it, in a clause about frames where type will be added, and nothing sends anybody there either.

`staging.duty_before_arrangement`

### One Hero, Everything Else Supports

**Also called:** hierarchy of objects, hero product  
**What it is:** Deciding which single object the frame is about and arranging every other element to lead toward it, in size, placement, focus and light.  
**Effect on the audience:** The eye knows where to go. A still life with three equal objects gives the viewer no reason to look anywhere, and the image reads as a catalogue page.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Product and packaging work, food, still life, and any arranged frame with more than one object. Best in product stills and food work.  
**Best in:** formats: Product Still / Packshot, Still Life Photography, Food Photography, Commercial / TVC, Editorial / Magazine Image, Poster / Static Design, Props Sheet, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually flattens a frame whose subject is genuinely a collection, such as a family of products or a market table. The exception is giving one item a small advantage in light or focus, which orders the group without breaking it.  
**Prompt:** `the vessel is the hero, centred and sharp with the brightest surface in frame; the cloth and the scattered grain sit lower, softer and darker, leading the eye to it`  
**Yields to:** `staging.stack_and_repeat` — Subject is genuinely a collection or product family.  
`staging.hero_and_support`

### Arrange in Triangles, Not Rows

**Also called:** odd grouping, triangular composition  
**What it is:** Placing objects so their centres form triangles rather than lines, usually in odd numbers, with varied heights and overlapping edges.  
**Effect on the audience:** The group reads as arranged by a person rather than by a shelf. Rows and even numbers read as inventory.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Still life, food, product families, and props sheets where several objects share a frame. Best in still life and food work.  
**Best in:** formats: Still Life Photography, Food Photography, Product Still / Packshot, Props Sheet, Editorial / Magazine Image, Commercial / TVC, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually fights a subject whose order is the point, a row of identical vessels, a grid of tiles. The exception is a deliberate grid broken by one displaced object.  
**Prompt:** `three objects at different heights, their centres forming a triangle, the nearest overlapping the second, none aligned on a shared baseline`  
**Yields to:** `staging.stack_and_repeat` — The order is the point: row, grid.  
`staging.triangle_arrangement`

### Overlap to Build Depth

**Also called:** object occlusion, layered arrangement  
**What it is:** Letting nearer objects partly cover the ones behind them, so the eye reads a front-to-back order rather than a flat plane.  
**Effect on the audience:** Depth without a wide lens. Overlap is the strongest depth cue available in a still arrangement, stronger than focus or perspective.  
**Used for and where it works best:** All still life and food work, product families, and any arrangement on a table. Best in still life and food.  
**Best in:** formats: Still Life Photography, Food Photography, Product Still / Packshot, Props Sheet, Editorial / Magazine Image, Commercial / TVC, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually hides information when a product must be shown whole for commercial reasons. The exception is a hero shot that shows the item complete, with the overlapped arrangement kept for the supporting frames.  
**Prompt:** `the bowl in front partly covering the base of the jug, the jug partly covering the folded cloth behind, three clear layers front to back`  
**Yields to:** `frame.hero_shot` — Commerce needs the item shown whole.  
`staging.overlap_for_depth`

### Choose the Surface First

**Also called:** ground, tabletop, backdrop selection  
**What it is:** Selecting the surface the objects sit on as a deliberate decision, for its colour, texture, reflectivity and period, before anything is placed on it.  
**Effect on the audience:** The surface sets the whole register. The same vessel on scrubbed pine, on polished marble and on packed earth belongs to three different worlds.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Still life, food, product and props work, and heritage reconstruction where the surface is evidence. Best in food, still life and heritage plates.  
**Best in:** formats: Still Life Photography, Food Photography, Product Still / Packshot, Props Sheet, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Commercial / TVC, Editorial / Magazine Image, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually competes when the surface has more texture and contrast than the subject. The exception is a deliberately dominant surface, where the object is small and the ground is the image.  
**Prompt:** `set on scrubbed pine boards running left to right, dry and pale, fine grain visible, no polish and no reflection`  
**Yields to:** `staging.background_separation` — Ground out-textures and out-contrasts the subject.  
`staging.surface_choice`

### Every Prop Earns Its Place

**Also called:** prop discipline, remove one thing  
**What it is:** Including only objects that carry information about the subject, the period or the use, and removing anything present only to fill space.  
**Effect on the audience:** The image says one thing clearly. Decorative clutter dilutes the subject and, in heritage work, multiplies the chance of an anachronism.  
**Used for and where it works best:** All still life, food, product and heritage staging. Best in heritage plates and product work.  
**Best in:** formats: Still Life Photography, Food Photography, Product Still / Packshot, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Props Sheet, Commercial / TVC, Editorial / Magazine Image, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually sterilises a frame whose subject is abundance, a market, a feast, a workshop. The exception is abundance built from objects that all belong, which is fullness rather than clutter.  
**Prompt:** `only what belongs: the vessel, the grain it held, and the cloth it was carried in. No decorative filler, no objects from another century`  
**Yields to:** `comp.maximalism` — Subject is abundance: market, feast, workshop.  
`staging.props_earn_place`

### Cut It Open

**Also called:** the cut face, internal reveal  
**What it is:** Opening, cutting or breaking one item in the arrangement so its interior is visible beside the intact version.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer learns what the thing is made of. A cut face shows freshness, structure and quality in a way an intact exterior cannot.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Food and produce work, product demonstration, and material and craft documentation. Best in food and product stills.  
**Best in:** formats: Food Photography, Product Still / Packshot, Still Life Photography, Commercial / TVC, Explainer, Editorial / Magazine Image, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually damages a subject whose value is wholeness, such as a ceremonial object or a sealed package. The exception is a separate frame for the cut, keeping the hero intact in the main image.  
**Prompt:** `one fruit cut and laid open beside the whole one, the cut face fresh and wet, seeds and structure visible, the whole fruit behind and slightly higher`  
**Yields to:** `material.food_surface` — Value is wholeness; show freshness on the surface.  
`staging.cut_and_reveal`

### Lift Things Off the Table

**Also called:** elevation, risers, height variation  
**What it is:** Raising some elements on hidden supports so the arrangement has vertical variation rather than sitting flat on one plane.  
**Effect on the audience:** Rhythm and depth. Everything at one height reads as a shelf; varied heights read as composition.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Product families, food, still life and props boards. Best in product stills and food work.  
**Best in:** formats: Product Still / Packshot, Food Photography, Still Life Photography, Props Sheet, Commercial / TVC, Editorial / Magazine Image, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually looks unstable when the lift is visible or the object appears to float. The exception is a deliberately suspended object in a graphic composition, where the impossibility is the idea.  
**Prompt:** `the small jar raised on a hidden block so its rim sits above the bowl behind it, the supports invisible, the objects appearing to rest naturally at different heights`  
**Yields to:** `promo.levitation` — The object is meant to read as suspended.  
`staging.height_and_lift`

### Leave a Human Trace

**Also called:** evidence of use, the interrupted moment  
**What it is:** Placing something that implies a person just left: a spoon set down, a chair pushed back, a half-poured cup, crumbs, a folded cloth.  
**Effect on the audience:** The frame stops being an arrangement and becomes a moment. The absent person is felt more strongly than a present one would be.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Food, still life, heritage reconstruction and editorial work. Best in food, heritage and editorial.  
**Best in:** formats: Food Photography, Still Life Photography, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Editorial / Magazine Image, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Travel Photography, Props Sheet, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography | genres: Slice of Life, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Drama, Family, Verite / Observational, Mystery / Whodunit  
**Avoid when:** It usually contradicts a clinical product frame where any trace reads as contamination. The exception is a lifestyle frame in the same campaign, where the trace carries the warmth the packshot cannot.  
**Prompt:** `the spoon lying across the rim where it was set down, a single drop on the wood beside it, the cloth pushed aside rather than folded`  
**Yields to:** `photo.packshot` — Clinical frame where any trace reads as contamination.  
`staging.human_trace`

### Stage the Colour Relationship

**Also called:** palette staging, colour pairing on set  
**What it is:** Choosing the objects, surface and background together for the colour relationship they will produce, rather than staging first and correcting afterwards.  
**Effect on the audience:** Colour that works in camera. A relationship built on set survives grading; one imposed afterwards fights the materials.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Food, product, still life and campaign work where the palette is part of the brand. Best in food, product and campaign sets.  
**Best in:** formats: Food Photography, Product Still / Packshot, Still Life Photography, Commercial / TVC, Editorial / Magazine Image, Poster / Static Design, Thumbnail / Social Card, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually over-designs when every element is chosen for palette and nothing for truth, which in food reads as artificial. The exception is a graphic campaign frame where the artificiality is the style.  
**Prompt:** `the deep red of the fruit against the dull green of the glaze, both on pale pine, with a single cool grey cloth to separate them`  
`staging.colour_relationship`

### Put Something Known Beside It

**Also called:** scale object, size reference in frame  
**What it is:** Including an object of known size in the arrangement so the subject's scale cannot be misread.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer knows how big the thing is. An isolated object on a plain ground has no size at all, and product returns are full of that misunderstanding.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Product and craft work, archaeological and museum documentation, and any object photographed alone. Best in product stills and heritage plates.  
**Best in:** formats: Product Still / Packshot, Props Sheet, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Still Life Photography, Explainer, Editorial / Magazine Image, Commercial / TVC, Food Photography, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually distracts when the companion object is interesting in itself. The exception is a neutral rule, coin or hand, which reads as measurement rather than as content.  
**Prompt:** `a hand entering from the right holding the vessel, giving the scale without becoming the subject`  
**Yields to:** `material.scale_cue` — Companion too interesting; use the material's own grain.  
`staging.scale_companion`

### Separate the Subject From the Ground

**Also called:** tonal separation, subject isolation on set  
**What it is:** Making sure the tone and colour behind each edge of the subject differs enough for the edge to read, adjusting the ground or the light until it does.  
**Effect on the audience:** The subject has a shape. A dark object against a dark ground loses its silhouette regardless of how well it is lit.  
**Used for and where it works best:** All product, still life and food work, and the usual fix when a well-lit object still reads as flat. Best in product and still life.  
**Best in:** formats: Product Still / Packshot, Still Life Photography, Food Photography, Props Sheet, Commercial / TVC, Editorial / Magazine Image, Thumbnail / Social Card, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually over-separates when a bright rim is added all the way round, which reads as a cut-out. The exception is a deliberate graphic treatment where the cut-out is the intent.  
**Prompt:** `the dark bronze read against a mid-grey ground, with a soft gradient behind so the top edge sits against the lighter area and the base against the darker`  
**Yields to:** `comp.silhouette_shape` — The cut-out graphic read is the intent.  
`staging.background_separation`

### Stack, Repeat and Break

**Also called:** repetition staging, pattern with an exception  
**What it is:** Building an arrangement from repeated identical items and then breaking the repetition once, deliberately, at the point the eye should land.  
**Effect on the audience:** Rhythm that ends in a destination. Pure repetition is decorative; repetition with one break is composition.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Product ranges, craft and market documentation, and graphic campaign work. Best in product, editorial and campaign frames.  
**Best in:** formats: Product Still / Packshot, Still Life Photography, Editorial / Magazine Image, Commercial / TVC, Poster / Static Design, Thumbnail / Social Card, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Food Photography, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually loses the break when the arrangement is too dense to read. The exception is a very tight crop on the break itself, generated as a second frame.  
**Prompt:** `nine identical jars in a grid, all upright and aligned, one at the centre right turned to show its label`  
**Yields to:** `size.insert` — Break lost in density; crop tight to it.  
`staging.stack_and_repeat`

### Wet, Dry and the State of Things

**Also called:** moisture staging, freshness state  
**What it is:** Deciding the moisture state of every surface in the frame and staging it deliberately: condensation, oil, dust, dryness, steam.  
**Effect on the audience:** Appetite and age. Moisture is what separates fresh from stale and new from old, and whether it is there is almost entirely a staging decision rather than a lighting one — whether the camera sees it is the edge light `material.food_surface` places to reveal it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Food and drink work, heritage reconstruction where age matters, and product work with cold or hot items. Best in food and heritage plates.  
**Best in:** formats: Food Photography, Still Life Photography, Product Still / Packshot, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Commercial / TVC, Editorial / Magazine Image, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually reads as artificial when everything is wet at once, which is the standard failure of over-styled food. The exception is a deliberately hyperreal commercial register where the excess is the convention.  
**Prompt:** `condensation beading on the cold glass and running once down the side, the wooden board beside it completely dry, a light dusting of flour still powdery`  
`staging.wet_and_dry`

### Period-Truthful Set Dressing

**Also called:** historical staging, era-correct objects  
**What it is:** Filling the frame only with objects, materials and arrangements that existed in the period and place, and checking each one rather than trusting the overall impression.  
**Effect on the audience:** The reconstruction survives an informed viewer. One anachronistic object undoes an otherwise careful frame, and unspecified background is where they always enter.  
**Used for and where it works best:** All heritage and historical reconstruction, museum and exhibition work. Best in heritage documentary and historical plates.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Still Life Photography, Props Sheet, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Editorial / Magazine Image, Documentary Photography, Food Photography, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Faith / Biblical Epic, War, Tragedy  
**Avoid when:** It usually stalls production when every object must be researched to the same depth. The exception is researching what is close to camera and keeping the rest in shadow or out of focus.  
**Prompt:** `only period-correct objects in frame, each one checked against the evidence; the background held in shadow rather than filled with unverified detail`  
**Yields to:** `lens.shallow_dof` — Cannot research everything; hold the rest unfocused.  
`staging.period_set_dressing`

### Treat the Empty Space as an Element

**Also called:** negative space staging, breathing room  
**What it is:** Composing the empty area of the arrangement as deliberately as the objects, deciding its shape, its tone and how much of the frame it takes.  
**Effect on the audience:** The arrangement gets somewhere to sit. Objects pushed to fill the frame read as crowded, while shaped emptiness reads as intention.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Still life, product, editorial and poster work, and any frame where type will be added. Best in still life and editorial.  
**Best in:** formats: Still Life Photography, Product Still / Packshot, Editorial / Magazine Image, Poster / Static Design, Food Photography, Thumbnail / Social Card, Commercial / TVC, Interior Photography, Real Estate Photography, Fine Art Photography, Abstract Photography, Travel Photography | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** It usually reads as under-filled when the emptiness has no shape of its own and just looks like a small subject. The exception is extreme minimalism, where the disproportion is the statement.  
**Prompt:** `the arrangement held in the lower left third, the upper right two thirds left as clean unbroken surface with a slow tonal gradient across it`  
**Yields to:** `comp.minimalism` — Extreme minimalism, the disproportion is the statement.  
`staging.empty_space_as_object`
