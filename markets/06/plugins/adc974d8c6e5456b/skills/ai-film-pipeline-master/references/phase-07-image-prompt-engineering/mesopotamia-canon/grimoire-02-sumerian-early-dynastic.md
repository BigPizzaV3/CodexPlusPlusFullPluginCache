## 2. The Sumerian Early Dynastic Period (approx. 2900 BC - 2350 BC)

Here is the visual lore breakdown for The Sumerian Early Dynastic Period (approx. 2900 BC - 2350 BC). Prepare your rendering engines for the era of Gilgamesh, the Royal Tombs of Ur, and fiercely independent, warring city-states. The visual language here shifts from the primal mud of Uruk to a world of glaring, high-contrast wealth: blinding gold, deep blue lapis lazuli, polished copper, and the organized, brutal geometry of the first true armies.

### 1. Environment, Flora & Fauna

- Weather & Sky: A harsh, baking environment. The climate has dried out significantly since the Uruk period. The sky is an intense, dusty cerulean blue, creating sharp, high-contrast, nearly black drop shadows on the ground.

- Terrain & Hydrology: The rivers are heavily managed, their banks raised by centuries of dredged mud into high natural levees — and it is those levees that make the whole system work, because water runs off them by gravity onto the fields below. **The canals are not straight, and this file used to draw them that way.** Third-millennium irrigation followed the land: short cuts through the levee at a crevasse splay — a natural or widened break where the river spills sideways — feeding branching, meandering channels that thread the fields and silt up and are redug. Read them as veins, not as a grid: irregular, bifurcating, following the fall of the ground, silver where they hold water and pale cracked clay where they do not.
  - **The long straight state-planned canal is a much later form** — it belongs to the Sasanian and early Islamic period, whose comprehensively planned networks (the Nahrawan is the famous one) are separated from Early Dynastic Sumer by roughly three thousand years. Drawing a ruled geometric canal grid at Ur or Lagash is the same size of error as putting a Neo-Assyrian relief in a fifth-century church, and it is easy to make because the later system is what aerial photographs of Iraq mostly show.

- Flora: Massive, organized plantations of date palms provide dappled shade. Below them, vast, flat fields of pale-golden barley wave in the dry wind. The horizon is dotted with towering stands of green marsh reeds along the waterways.

- Fauna: The steppe is populated by herds of sleek, grey-brown onagers (wild asses) kicking up dust clouds. Flocks of fat-tailed sheep with thick, matted, creamy-white wool are ubiquitous. Elite hunting parks hold captive tawny Asiatic lions and imported, heavily muscled bulls.

### 2. Architecture & Infrastructure

- City Layout: The cities are tightly packed, claustrophobic fortresses. They are entirely enclosed by massive, towering mudbrick walls (some up to 6 miles long, like at Uruk), punctuated by heavy, arched gateways. The streets are narrow, unpaved, and heavily shadowed.

- The Brickwork (Plano-Convex): A critical visual detail for your texture artists. Bricks of this era are not flat; they are "plano-convex" (flat on the bottom, rounded on the top like a bread roll). They are laid in a distinct, alternating herringbone pattern, giving uncovered mudbrick walls a highly textured, woven, zigzag appearance.

- Temples (The Oval Temple): Temples are elevated on immense terraces of solid mudbrick, surrounded by thick, oval-shaped perimeter walls. The walls are plastered in a thick, rough gypsum wash, baking to a blinding, matte white under the sun.

- Details: Drainpipes made of interlocking, baked terracotta cylinders jut from the roofs to direct rare, torrential rains away from the mud walls. Doorways are marked by thick cedar posts heavily coated in shiny, viscous black bitumen.

### 3. Demographics & Physical Traits

- Facial Features: Deep-set, large, almond-shaped eyes heavily rimmed with thick black soot and crushed antimony. Prominent, straight noses. In art, the "unibrow" is heavily emphasized as a sign of beauty.

- Skin Tones: Rich, leathery bronze and deep olive, heavily weathered by the unrelenting sun.
- Hair & Beards (The Elite Division):
  - Priests and Rulers: Often completely depilated. Their scalps and faces are shaved entirely smooth and heavily oiled, gleaming with high specularity.

  - General Men: Wear thick, coarse, jet-black hair parted down the middle, alongside massive, square-cut beards that ripple with natural waves.
  - Women: Hair is incredibly elaborate, tightly braided and wrapped into heavy, sweeping chignons at the nape of the neck or piled atop the head, held by thick nets of dark wool.

### 4. Fashion & Textiles

- The Kaunakes: The absolute defining garment of the era. This is a skirt or dress made of raw sheep's fleece or woven wool. It is constructed in overlapping, cascading tiers of thick, petal-like tufts. The tufts are heavy, swaying with momentum as the person walks. They are mostly natural, creamy off-white, though elite versions feature tiers dyed in deep ochre-yellow and madder red.

- Elite Adornment (Queen Puabi's Court): The wealth is staggering. Elite women wear massive, incredibly heavy headdresses consisting of hundreds of hammered gold willow and beech leaves, interspersed with bright blue lapis lazuli and deep red, translucent carnelian beads.

- Jewelry: Massive, heavy gold hoop earrings (some so heavy they required a band over the head to support the weight). Cylinder seals made of glowing lapis and polished white shell are worn pinned to garments with long, thick gold or copper pins.

### 5. Economy & Daily Life

- The Workshop: The air rings with the sound of hammering. Coppersmiths work over charcoal braziers, casting early bronze and pure copper into heavily socketed axe heads. The metal is polished to a warm, bright penny-copper gleam.

- The Standard of Ur: **the richest picture source for this period, and not a reference for daily life.** Two cautions before using it. **Nobody knows what the object is**: Woolley imagined it carried on a pole, which is where the name came from; the sound-box of an instrument is another proposal; the British Museum now catalogues it as a *box*. And its scenes are **ceremonial and narrative** — a banquet with the ruler enlarged, a procession of tribute, a battle — so they show what a court wanted depicted, not what an ordinary day looked like. Take the objects, the dress and the animals from it with confidence; do not write a narration line calling it a window onto everyday life. Feasts involve elites seated on small wooden stools with incredibly elaborate, carved bull hooves for legs. They drink thick, unfiltered, foamy brown barley beer from large vats using long, curved silver or lapis-tipped reeds.

- Pottery & Tools: Wheel-thrown pottery is ubiquitous. Vases are elegant, tall, and made of pale, buff-colored clay. Sickles for harvesting grain are no longer just clay; they are curved wooden handles set with razor-sharp, dark flint teeth glued in with bubbling black bitumen.

### 6. Religion & Art

- The Votive Statues (Tell Asmar): These define the spiritual aesthetic. Rigid, geometric statues carved from pale gypsum or translucent alabaster. Their hands are tightly clasped in prayer.

- The Eyes: The most terrifying and mesmerizing feature. The statues have massively enlarged, wide-open, staring eyes. The whites are inlaid with bright, polished shell, and the pupils are pitch-black limestone or brilliant blue lapis lazuli. In a dim, incense-filled temple, these eyes catch the flickering light of oil lamps.

- Inlay Art: Woodwork (lyres, game boards, boxes) is heavily decorated with mosaics. Pieces of bright white shell, red limestone, and blue lapis are pressed into thick layers of black bitumen to create highly detailed, miniature narrative scenes.

### 7. Transport & Mobility

- The Battle Wagon: There are no true chariots yet. The heavy armor of the era is the four-wheeled battle wagon.

  - The Wheels: They are tripartite - made of three solid planks of heavy wood, doweled together, bound in thick leather tires, and studded with massive, rounded copper nails.
  - The Build: The wagon has a high, woven wicker dashboard. It is pulled by four sleek, muscular, grey-brown onagers (wild asses). They are controlled not by bits, but by silver or copper rings pierced directly through their upper lips or noses, attached to leather reins that run through a heavy, double-ringed copper rein-guide mounted on the yoke.
  - - Watercraft: The rivers are crowded with highly buoyant, deeply curved basket-boats (quffas) woven from reeds and entirely slathered in shiny, waterproof black pitch. Larger timber boats feature high, sharply curving prows and sterns.

### 8. The Military Machine

- The Phalanx: The defining military visual of the Early Dynastic period. The infantry fights as a terrifying, unified machine. They march in a dense, heavily disciplined block, stepping directly over the naked, bleeding bodies of their enemies.

- The Shields: The front rank holds massive, rectangular, interlocking shields that cover them from chin to ankle. They are made of stretched, speckled leopard or cow hide over wooden frames, studded with six large, gleaming copper bosses in the center to deflect blows.

- Weapons: A forest of thick, heavy ash-wood spears with long, leaf-shaped copper heads protrudes between the shields. The king and shock troops wield heavy, socketed copper axes and curved, bronze sickle-swords.

- Helmets: The soldiers wear distinctive, tight-fitting, conical helmets of polished copper. They feature a leather inner lining and fit closely over the ears, securing under the chin. The King's helmet (like the Helmet of Meskalamdug) is solid, hammered gold, beautifully chased to resemble an intricate, braided hairstyle and bun.
