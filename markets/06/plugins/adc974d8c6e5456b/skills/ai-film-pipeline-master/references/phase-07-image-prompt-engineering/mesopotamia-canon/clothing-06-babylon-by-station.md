### Babylon: The Social Classes (Nos. 126 to 150)

**126. Field Laborer's Loincloth.** _(Poor, man.)_ Bare to the waist, a short rough wool cloth knotted at the hip, the feet bare, a folded cloth on the head against the sun. The fieldhand of the canals.

**127. Water-Carrier's Shoulder Cloth.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A plain undyed shift with a thick folded pad on one shoulder for the jar, a head-cloth, bare feet, carrying water from the Euphrates.

**128. Reed-Cutter's Marsh Wrap.** _(Poor, man.)_ A minimal wrap of coarse wool and reed cord, the legs bare for wading, damp and mud-flecked among the river reeds.

**129. Washerwoman's Tucked Gown.** _(Poor, woman.)_ A coarse gown hitched and tucked for wading, the sleeves bound, the hair wrapped, beating cloth clean at the canal.

**130. Brick-Carrier's Padded Kilt.** _(Poor, man.)_ A coarse kilt with a padded shoulder for the load of mud bricks, a wide belt, the body grey with clay from the pits.

**131. Poor Widow's Patched Gown.** _(Poor, older woman.)_ A much-mended undyed gown, a thin worn shawl, no jewelry but a single clay bead, grey with washing and years.

**132. Bondservant's Plain Shift.** _(Poor, man or woman.)_ A knee-length coarse wool shift, undyed and corded, the hair cropped close. The household bondservant.

**133. Beggar's Knotted Rags.** _(Poor, any.)_ Layered tattered scraps of wool knotted against the cold, a clay bowl in hand, the feet blistered and bare at the temple steps.

**134. Townsman's Draped Robe.** _(Middle, man.)_ A decent draped wool robe with a fringed edge and a woven belt, a trimmed beard, leather sandals. The respectable everyday townsman.

**135. Artisan's Work Tunic and Apron.** _(Middle, man.)_ A sturdy tunic under a thick apron, the sleeves bound, a tool-belt and a felt cap. The skilled craftsman.

**136. Shopkeeper's Wife's Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A good-wool gown with a fringed shawl, a few faience and silver beads, a small household seal at the belt.

**137. Weaver's Practical Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A plain belted gown with the sleeves bound, shuttle and thread at hand, the fingers stained with dye.

**138. Potter's Clay-Dusted Tunic.** _(Middle, man.)_ A worn tunic powdered with clay, the sleeves rolled, a cord belt, the hands wet and grey at the turning wheel.

**139. Brewer-Woman's Working Gown.** _(Middle, woman.)_ A belted gown with the sleeves tied and a cloth over the hair, tending the vats and the bread of the brewing craft.

**140. Money-Lender's Sober Robe.** _(Middle, man.)_ A plain but quality draped robe, a locked tablet-box of contracts, a seal and a heavy purse, in the great banking city of Babylon.

**141. Middle Daughter's Festival Tunic.** _(Middle, young woman.)_ A linen gown with a dyed border, a beaded collar, small earrings and a fillet. Modest finery for a feast day.

**142. Nobleman's Long Draped Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A full robe of fine soft wool with the fringe spiraling across the body, a wide ornamented belt, gold armlets, an oiled and combed beard.

**143. Noblewoman's Pleated Gown and Collar.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A fine pleated gown under a fringed shawl, a broad collar of gold, carnelian and lapis, heavy earrings and a diadem, kohl-lined and scented.

**144. Rich Estate-Holder's Draped Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A fine draped robe and a seal of office, a staff and fine sandals, overseeing fields, date orchards and a steward by the river.

**145. Noble Daughter's Betrothal Gown.** _(Wealthy, young woman.)_ A sumptuous dyed gown layered with the gold of her dowry, a fine shawl drawn back from the face, perfumed oils and kohl.

**146. Wealthy Matron's Banquet Gown.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A deeply dyed fine gown with the fullest jewelry, a wreath or diadem, reclining at the banquet while a servant holds the fan.

**147. Nobleman's Winter Fur-Trimmed Robe.** _(Wealthy, man.)_ A heavy fine robe trimmed with fur at collar and hem, a lined mantle, a decorated cap and a gold pin. Warmth that shows wealth.

**148. Lady's Fine Linen Summer Gown.** _(Wealthy, woman.)_ A gown of finely pleated and bleached linen, light gold jewelry, delicate sandals, a maid holding a shading cloth. Cool summer luxury.

**149. Heir's Coming-of-Age Robe.** _(Wealthy, young man.)_ A first full draped adult robe of fine cloth with a fringed shawl, a new personal seal and a fillet, taking his place among the men of the house.

**150. Dowager Noblewoman's Robe.** _(Wealthy, older woman.)_ A rich but sober gown, heirloom gold worn lightly, a fine shawl over silvering hair, the bearing of one long obeyed.

### Babylon: Trades and Professions (Nos. 151 to 185)

**151. Master Scribe's Draped Robe.** _(Records.)_ A fine draped fringed robe, a cylinder seal and a reed stylus, a clay tablet under the arm, keeping the letters, contracts and laws of the busy city.

**152. Schoolmaster's Robe.** _(Tablet house.)_ A sober long robe and a discipline-rod, a wall of stacked tablets, training the many scribes the city of clerks required.

**153. Temple Scribe's Linen Robe.** _(Esagila.)_ A clean pale linen robe and a fringed shawl, a seal of Marduk's house, recording the offerings and the rites of the great temple.

**154. Astronomer-Scribe's Robe.** _(Sky-watcher.)_ A draped robe and a fringed shawl against the night air, a tablet of planets and omens, charting the heavens from the temple tower, the famed science of Babylon.

**155. Merchant's Travel Robe.** _(Trade.)_ A long belted robe under a dust-cloak, a seal and a tablet-pouch, a money-belt of silver, trading along the great river.

**156. Market Seller's Kilt and Apron.** _(Stall.)_ A knee kilt with a wide apron-sash of weights and a pouch, a folded awning-cloth, calling his wares in the market.

**157. Cloth Merchant's Fine Robe.** _(Textiles.)_ A robe showing his best wares, dyed cloth over the arm, a measuring rod and rings, dealing in the great trade of cloth.

**158. Carpenter's Work Tunic.** _(Wood.)_ A sturdy tunic and a pocketed apron, an adze and chisels, wood shavings on the cloth at the bench.

**159. Leatherworker's Apron Dress.** _(Hide.)_ A tunic under a thick leather apron darkened by the pits, awls and a curved knife, the hands stained brown.

**160. Fisherman's River Wrap.** _(River.)_ A minimal wrap for the boat, a casting-net over one shoulder, the legs bare, a reed hat and a fish-spear.

**161. Plowman-Farmer's Kilt.** _(Field.)_ A coarse belted kilt for the seeder-plow behind the oxen, a goad, a seed-bag, mud to the knee.

**162. Baker's Flour-Dusted Tunic.** _(Bread.)_ A tunic powdered with flour, the sleeves bound and a cloth over the hair, baking the loaves of the city.

**163. Barber's Neat Tunic.** _(Grooming.)_ A clean trim tunic, a roll of razor and shears, a basin and a strop, a careful hand for the city's shaving and grooming.

**164. Physician's Robe.** _(Healer.)_ A clean robe and a satchel of herbs, salves and tools, a protective amulet, a learned and reassuring calm.

**165. Temple Musician's Festival Dress.** _(Sacred song.)_ A decorated tunic or gown, a lyre or harp, a fillet and beads, the singer and player before Marduk at the feast.

**166. Dyer's Color-Stained Dress.** _(Vats.)_ A tunic blotched red, blue and yellow from the vats, the arms dyed to the elbow, by the dye pots.

**167. Goldsmith's Fine Tunic.** _(Precious work.)_ A clean careful tunic and a small apron, tiny tools and a balance, gold wire and beads on the bench, the eyes sharp and close.

**168. Glazed-Brick Maker's Tunic.** _(Ishtar Gate.)_ A work tunic spotted with bright glaze, the hands stained blue and gold, molding and firing the glazed bricks of dragon and lion for the great gate and the processional way.

**169. Date-Orchard Keeper's Tunic.** _(Orchard.)_ A short belted tunic for climbing and tending the date palms along the river, a hooked knife and a fiber rope, sun-darkened arms.

**170. Shepherd's Fleece Cloak.** _(Pasture.)_ A coarse tunic under a sheepskin cloak, a crook and a sling, a leather bag, watching the flocks beyond the fields.

**171. Banker-Exchanger's Robe.** _(Finance.)_ A quality draped robe, a locked tablet-box of loans and deposits, a seal and a balance for silver, in the manner of the great Babylonian banking houses.

**172. Reed-Worker's Wrap.** _(Mats and baskets.)_ A plain wrap and a cord belt, bundles of cut reed at hand, weaving the mats, baskets and boats of the waterways.

**173. Oil-Presser's Tunic.** _(Sesame oil.)_ A tunic stained and slick with oil, the sleeves bound, working the press for the oil of lamp and skin.

**173b. Brick-Maker's Clay-Stiffened Kilt.** _(Brickyard.)_ A short coarse kilt gone stiff and pale with dried clay, the forearms grey to the elbow, a wooden mould and a stamp at his feet, treading and moulding the plain baked bricks the city is built from - the trade behind the fifteen million, as distinct from the mason who lays them and the glaze-worker who colours the gate.

**174. Brick-Mason's Kilt.** _(Building.)_ A coarse kilt and a cord belt, a trowel and a plumb-cord, laying the countless bricks of the king's walls and temples.

**175. City Herald's Tunic.** _(Proclamation.)_ A clean clear-colored tunic and a staff, a strong voice, crying the king's decree in the squares and the processional way.

**176. Ale-House Keeper's Gown.** _(Woman, tavern.)_ A belted gown with the sleeves tied, a cloth over the hair, drawing beer for the men of the ale-house.

**177. Midwife's Practical Gown.** _(Birth.)_ A clean simple gown with the sleeves bound, a satchel of cloths, herbs and a birth-amulet, attending the women of the quarter.

**178. Caravan-Master's Robe.** _(Long-distance trade.)_ A long sturdy robe and a dust-cloak, a seal, a heavy purse and a staff, leading the donkey caravans across the trade roads.

**179. Seal-Cutter's Fine Tunic.** _(Glyptic art.)_ A clean tunic, a tiny drill and gravers, a magnifying eye, cutting the cylinder seals that signed every Babylonian contract.

**180. Estate Steward's Robe.** _(Manager.)_ A clean long robe, a seal and a tablet of accounts, ordering the workers, herds and stores of a temple or private estate.

**181. Astrologer-Diviner's Robe.** _(Omens.)_ A draped robe and a fringed shawl, tablets of celestial omens and a model liver, reading the will of the gods, the renowned art of Babylon.

**182. Granary-Keeper's Robe.** _(Stores.)_ A clean robe with a seal and a tally-tablet, the sleeves bound, measuring the barley in and out of the great granary.

**183. Donkey-Driver's Wrap.** _(Caravan.)_ A short coarse wrap and a head-cloth, a goad and a water-skin, leading the laden donkeys along the trade track.

**184. Surveyor-Scribe's Field Dress.** _(Land measure.)_ A practical tunic, a measuring rod and a knotted cord, a tablet of plots, recording the fields and the boundary stones.

**185. Master Builder-Architect's Robe.** _(Great works.)_ A fine robe and a folded shawl, a measuring reed and a plan-tablet, directing the raising of the walls, gates and temples of the king.

### Babylon: Soldiers and Military (Nos. 186 to 200)

**186. Royal Archer.** _(Bowman.)_ A long fringed tunic, a quiver and a strong bow, a pointed helmet, the bowman of the Babylonian battle line.

**187. Spearman of the Line.** _(Infantry.)_ A knee tunic and a wide belt, a helmet, a thrusting spear and a round shield, the footman of the army.

**188. Heavy Infantryman with Scale.** _(Shock troop.)_ A tunic under a coat of bronze scales, a pointed helmet, a spear and a short sword, the armored core of the host.

**189. Helmeted Shield-Bearer.** _(Cover.)_ A belted tunic and a leather cap, a tall body-shield carried to screen the archers behind, advancing step by step.

**190. Axe-and-Shield Soldier.** _(Close combat.)_ A belted tunic and a helmet, a socketed war-axe and a round shield, for the hand-to-hand of the assault.

**191. Slinger Skirmisher.** _(Light troop.)_ A short plain tunic, a pouch of sling-stones and a sling, light and quick, harrying ahead of the line.

**192. Officer's Distinguished Dress.** _(Rank.)_ A finer fringed tunic with a marked belt under light armor, a crested helmet and a sword of office, directing the troops.

**193. Royal Guard of the King.** _(Bodyguard.)_ A fringed tunic and a polished helmet, a tall spear and a fine shield, standing at the king's side in war and in hall.

**194. Wall-Watch of Babylon.** _(City defense.)_ A plain tunic, a leather cap, a spear and a round shield, standing the watch on the famed mighty double walls of Babylon.

**195. Siege Soldier.** _(Siege.)_ A scale tunic and a leather cap, a shield and a short axe, manning the ram and the ramp in the king's sieges of distant cities.

**196. Veteran Campaigner's Kit.** _(Old hand.)_ A battered tunic and a dented helmet, old scars and a trusted bow, having marched the long campaigns of the king.

**197. Chariot Crewman.** _(War-chariot.)_ A fitted tunic with crossed straps, a helmet, the body guarded, driving or fighting from the war-chariot.

**198. Cavalry Rider.** _(Mounted.)_ A fitted tunic with wrapped leggings for the horse, a bow and a short spear, a helmet and riding boots, the mounted soldier of the later army.

**199. Garrison Soldier.** _(Occupation.)_ A belted tunic, a helmet and a spear, holding a frontier fort or a distant city for the Babylonian crown.

**200. Mustering Recruit's Kilt.** _(New levy.)_ A plain wool kilt and a cord belt, a wooden practice-spear, learning the line in the muster of the army.

### Babylon: Rulers, Court and Royalty (Nos. 201 to 225)

**201. King Hammurabi's Robe.** _(Law-giver.)_ A long fringed robe to the ankles, a rounded brimmed cap and a long combed beard, standing before Shamash to receive the rod and ring, the insignia of just rule, as on the great stele. He is not being handed a law code; that is a modern gloss on the scene, and the laws are inscribed in the text below the relief rather than passed in the picture. A caption saying "receiving the laws from the god" describes something the stone does not show. (Source: Musee du Louvre, Code of Hammurabi, Sb 8, https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl010174436, checked 2026-09-23: Shamash presents the king with the rod and ring, the insignia of power.)

**202. King Nebuchadnezzar's State Robe.** _(Neo-Babylonian.)_ A magnificent long fringed and bordered robe, a tall conical crown with a band, heavy gold, a scepter, the splendor of the king who rebuilt Babylon.

**203. Babylonian King's War Robe.** _(Campaign.)_ A belted war tunic under a fringed mantle, a crested helmet and a bow, leading the army against the cities of the west.

**204. Babylonian Queen's Gown and Diadem.** _(Consort.)_ A fine pleated and fringed gown, a broad collar of gold and lapis, a thin diadem over dressed hair, the lady of the royal house.

**205. King at the Akitu, Taking Marduk's Hand.** _(New Year rite.)_ A pure robe and a fillet, the royal insignia set briefly aside before the god, then taking the hand of Marduk to renew his kingship at the New Year.

**206. Crown Prince's Robe.** _(Heir.)_ A rich fringed robe just below the king's, a diadem-band, a personal seal and attendants, the son who will hold the throne.

**207. Princess's Fine Gown.** _(Royal daughter.)_ A sumptuous pleated and fringed gown, a fine diadem, the rarest gold, carnelian and lapis, scented and adorned from girlhood.

**208. King at the Banquet.** _(Feast.)_ A fine fringed robe and a folded shawl, a gold armlet and a cup, seated above his nobles in the painted and tiled hall.

**209. King in Worship.** _(Devotion.)_ A pure robe and a fillet, a libation cup or censer raised before Marduk in the Esagila, the king as the god's first servant.

**210. King's Coronation Robe.** _(Investiture.)_ A special robe assumed at the taking of kingship, charged with the symbols of rule and the favor of Marduk, worn at the crowning.

**211. Royal Cup-Bearer's Robe.** _(Court.)_ A fine tunic and a folded shawl, a cloth over the arm and the royal cup in hand, a trusted officer close to the throne.

**212. Palace Steward's Robe.** _(Household.)_ A dignified long robe, a staff and a seal, ordering the household, the stores and who may approach the king.

**213. Provincial Governor's Robe.** _(Governor.)_ A fine fringed robe with the seal of his province, a sword and attendants, ruling a city in the king's name.

**214. Royal Herald's Dress.** _(Court.)_ A fine clear-colored tunic and a staff, proclaiming the king's decree along the processional way and at the gate.

**215. Governor's Lady's Gown.** _(Residence.)_ A rich gown and a fringed shawl, court jewelry of gold and stone, presiding over the household and women of the governor.

**216. Grand Vizier's Robe.** _(Chief minister.)_ A magnificent long fringed robe, a staff of office and a great seal, standing just below the king as his right hand.

**217. Royal Astronomer's Robe.** _(Court science.)_ A fine draped robe and a fringed shawl, tablets of star and planet, the court scholar who read the heavens for the king of Babylon.

**218. Royal Judge's Robe.** _(Law.)_ A grave long robe, a seal and the case-tablets, seated to hear and to judge by the law that Hammurabi set in stone.

**219. Treasurer's Robe.** _(Treasury.)_ A sober rich robe, keys and seals at the belt, the ledgers of silver and tribute under guard, watchful and careful.

**220. Royal Guard Captain's Dress.** _(Command.)_ A fine fringed tunic and a crested helmet, a marked belt and a sword of office, commanding the guard of the king.

**221. Royal Envoy's Robe.** _(Embassy.)_ A fine travel-worthy robe upholding the king's dignity abroad, sealed tablets and gifts borne behind, received in foreign courts.

**222. Royal Child's Festival Robe.** _(Palace child.)_ A soft fine linen tunic with a saffron and blue border, a beaded carnelian collar and a gold fillet, a child of the royal house at the feast.

**223. King's Hunting Dress.** _(The hunt.)_ A fringed garment belted high for action, a bow and a quiver, an armlet and a fillet, riding against the lion and the wild bull.

**224. King's Building-Dedication Robe.** _(Temple works.)_ A pure fine robe and a fillet, a foundation-deposit in hand, dedicating a new temple, gate or wall, as the great kings recorded in their bricks.

**225. Dowager Queen's Robe.** _(Royal elder.)_ A rich but sober gown, heirloom gold worn lightly, a fine shawl over silvering hair, the mother of the king long honored.

### Babylon: Priests, Scholars and Temple (Nos. 226 to 240)

**226. High Priest's Ritual Robe.** _(Chief of the Esagila.)_ A long flounced robe leaving one shoulder bare, a tall fillet, a long oiled beard, anointing oil and Marduk's emblem in hand, the chief priest of the great temple.

**227. High Priestess's Robe.** _(En.)_ A fine flounced robe and a rounded crown, layered gold and lapis, a ring and rod, serving the goddess in grave dignity.

**228. Purification Priest's Linen Robe.** _(Shaven.)_ A head and body shaven smooth for purity, a plain bleached linen robe, a holy-water vessel in hand, performing the washings.

**229. Diviner's Robe.** _(Baru.)_ A clean robe and a fringed shawl, a clay model liver at his side, reading the gods' answer in the entrails of the sacrificed sheep.

**230. Lamentation Priest's Dress.** _(Kalu.)_ A robe for the chanting of laments, a kettledrum or lyre at hand, the voice raised to soothe the angry heart of Marduk.

**231. Exorcist's Night-Rite Dress.** _(Ashipu.)_ A robe for the rites against the demons of the dark, plaques of Pazuzu and bound Lamashtu about him, a censer and a torch, incantation tablets in hand.

**232. Astronomer-Priest's Robe.** _(Chaldean sky-watcher.)_ A draped robe and a fringed shawl against the night, a tablet of planets and eclipses, charting the heavens from the ziggurat, the famed Chaldean learning.

**233. Dream-Interpreter's Robe.** _(Visions.)_ A sober robe and a shawl, omen-tablets at hand, reading the meaning of dreams for those who come troubled to the temple.

**234. Temple Administrator's Robe.** _(Shatammu.)_ A clean long robe, a seal and a tally-tablet, ordering the lands, herds, silver and offerings of the vast estate of Marduk.

**235. Novice Priest's Plain Robe.** _(Temple novice.)_ A shaven head and a plain undyed linen robe with a simple sash, learning the washings, the rites and the songs.

**236. Sage-Scholar's Robe.** _(Ummanu, wisdom.)_ A fine sober robe and a fringed shawl, tablets of lore, omen and number close by, the trusted keeper of knowledge who counsels the king.

**237. Cloistered Priestess's Gown.** _(Naditu of Sippar.)_ A modest fine gown and a drawn shawl, a personal seal at the wrist, a naditu of Shamash at Sippar, vowed yet keeping her own lands and accounts.

**238. Hymn-Singer's Dress.** _(Sacred song.)_ A decorated gown and a fillet, beads of faience and carnelian, lifting the temple hymn to the goddess with lyre and voice.

**239. Akitu Procession Priest's Robe.** _(New Year.)_ A rich robe with woven sacred motifs and a tall fillet, attending the image of Marduk borne in splendor down the processional way at the New Year.

**240. Offering-Bearer Priest's Dress.** _(Procession.)_ A clean fringed robe and a folded shawl, both hands bearing a dish of dates, a kid or a bowl of grain to the god.

### Babylon: Garment Types and Materials (Nos. 241 to 250)

**241. The Draped Fringed Robe.** _(Signature garment.)_ The defining Babylonian dress: a long robe of wool or linen wound smoothly about the body, the fringed edge falling in an ordered line, dignified and flowing.

**242. The Spiral-Fringed Wrapped Style.** _(Drape.)_ The distinctive arrangement in which the fringed border spirals from the hem up and across the body to the shoulder as the long cloth is wound.

**243. Pleated Linen Garment.** _(Fine dress.)_ A garment of finely pleated linen holding crisp parallel folds, cool and elegant, favored by the well-off of Babylon in the heat.

**244. The Rounded Brimmed Cap.** _(Headwear.)_ The smooth cap with a rolled brim worn by the Babylonian king and notables, close and dignified, as in the image of Hammurabi.

**245. The Fringed Shawl.** _(Wrap.)_ A rectangular shawl with fringed ends folded over one shoulder or drawn over the head, the common added wrap of Babylonian dress.

**246. The Tasseled Cord Belt.** _(Fastening.)_ A long twisted cord ending in heavy tassels, wound at the waist over robe or gown, a distinctive Babylonian finish.

**247. Babylonian Wool.** _(Material.)_ The staple fiber of the flocks, spun and woven into most Babylonian dress, in natural tones and the dyed shades of the well-off.

**248. Babylonian Fine Linen.** _(Material.)_ Cloth of flax, finer and cooler than wool, pleated and bleached pale for priests, the wealthy and the festival best.

**249. The Conical Royal Crown.** _(Headwear.)_ The tall conical crown with a banded base worn by the Neo-Babylonian king, rising above the royal robe in ceremony and relief.

**250. Babylonian Dyed and Bordered Cloth.** _(Material.)_ The finer wool and linen finished with woven colored borders and fringe, the color and the border together marking a garment's worth.
