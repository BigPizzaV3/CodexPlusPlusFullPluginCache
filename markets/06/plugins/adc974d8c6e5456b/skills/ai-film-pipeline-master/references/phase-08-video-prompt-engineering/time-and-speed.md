# Time and Speed

49 cards. These decide a shot: how it moves, how it cuts, and how it sits in time.

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, what to avoid, and the prompt written out in full.

---

### Which Time Are You Deciding — The Telling, or the Clip

**Also called:** the time selector, telling or clip, the two-phase fork
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `time.` shelf — three files across Phase 1 and Phase 8. It does not describe a time device. It asks **one fork and then three questions on whichever side of the fork you landed**, and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The fork is *are you deciding when this reaches the audience, or how fast this clip runs*, because `SHELVES.md` §2b says these are two decisions made months apart by two phases — and the shelf's own pair graph confirms it by measurement rather than by filing. The axes were not invented: they were read off the 96 `Yields to:` conditions the shelf carries and the 16 `PAIR-AUDIT.md` rows that explain the cards carrying none, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id.
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen or the page. It exists so that the time device that does reach them was chosen against the work's shape, the stretch in hand and the shoot, rather than picked because it was the one the writer or the editor already knew.
**Used for and where it works best:** **Settle the fork out loud before opening any file.** The test is mechanical: *do you need the whole work on the desk for this decision to exist?* If it survives with one clip in hand it is a RUN decision and lives in Phase 8; if it needs the shape of the piece around it, it is an ORDER decision and lives in Phase 1. Then answer that half's three questions in one sentence each. **On the ORDER half each card sits under exactly one of the three questions, so the answer is a shortlist of one to six.** On the RUN half every card has an answer to all three, so the answer is the **intersection** of the three shortlists and is normally one to four cards wide. Where a moment is genuinely both — a flashback delivered as a reverse-motion run, a year crossed by a time-lapse — **it is two decisions and you make them in order**, ORDER first, RUN second, never at once.
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all
**Avoid when:** **You cannot answer the fork honestly.** The commonest error on this shelf is being wrong about which decision you are making, and a wrong fork cannot be repaired by re-answering questions two and three, because the whole map under it is laid out for a different question. The tell is that the card you land on keeps needing the other half's cards to work. **Distrust this map on the ORDER half whenever your honest answer to O1 is *the calendar governs, forward*** — that is `time.linear_spine` and it eliminates nothing, so on an ordinary linear piece the map has two questions left and is doing two-thirds of the work it does on a shaped one. **Distrust the RUN half's R2 whenever your answer is *in the edit*:** seventeen of the forty-one RUN cards live there, and that value is where the shelf stops discriminating. And **do not bring this card to a piece that already exists and does not add up** — there the shelf has its own front door, `time.timeline_audit`, and diagnosis precedes choosing. It also has nothing to say where the rate is a fixed engine setting or a plate you are matching: there the number is read off a record, not chosen from a shelf.
**Prompt:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

**The axes**

**The first fork — do you need the whole work on the desk for this decision to exist?**
Asked before anything, and it is the shelf's own answer rather than a filing convention. `SHELVES.md` §2b states it — *"Two decisions, made in two phases, months apart"* — and the pair graph confirms it by measurement: **of the 91 within-shelf `Yields to:` edges, 86 stay inside their own half and 5 cross, 5.5%.** Routed by decision rather than by file the figure is **4 of 91, 4.4%**. A shelf answering one question shows its halves citing each other freely, the way `light.` does across its portrait, location and hour regions. This one does not. The two halves are **the sixty-eight cards that decide when material reaches the audience** and **the forty-five that decide what one clip's seconds do**.

**And the two halves are not the same kind of shelf, which is why their maps are not the same shape.** Every RUN card has an answer to all three RUN questions — a clip always has a rate, a place where its rate was made, and a duration for the effect. The ORDER half does not work that way: `time.timeline_audit` has no answer to *how much telling does this stretch get*, and `time.scene_duration` has no answer to *what shape does the whole telling have*. So ORDER is partitioned the way `scene.` is, one card under one value of one question, and RUN is intersected the way `light.` is. **That difference is measurable, and it is a second, independent argument for the fork.**

**ORDER — you are deciding when this reaches the audience**

**Axis O1 — What governs the order: the calendar, or something else?**
The condition the ORDER half repeats most at the whole-work scale; **nineteen of its twenty-four cards state it in their own `Yields to:` line**, and they are stated as facts about the material, not about the reader's feelings: *"Engine is the gap between event and belief"* (`time.linear_spine`) against its exact reverse *"Plot is a chain of physical consequences"* (`time.nonlinear_assembly`); *"You need the reader to keep hoping"* (`time.reverse_chronology`); *"Frame exists only to license a voice"* (`time.frame_narrative`); *"Second strand only exists to explain the first"* (`time.dual_timeline`); *"One strand is materially weaker"* (`time.parallel_now`); *"The change needs longer than one day"* (`time.single_day`); *"Characters must travel, sleep or change slowly"* (`time.real_time`); *"Record written later by someone who already knows"* (`time.log_time`); *"Undo must cost something; author the loop"* (`time.interactive_rewind`).

**Axis O2 — For the stretch of story you are placing, how much telling does it get?**
**Every one of its cards states its condition — nineteen of nineteen, the only block on the shelf with no silent card in it**, which is why it is an axis and not a leaf: *"Journeys, routines and transitions"* (`time.scene_duration`) against *"Passage holds the decision the book turns on"* (`time.summary_duration`); *"The skipped interval contains the decision"* (`time.ellipsis`); *"Moment not consequential enough to bear expansion"* (`time.dilation`); *"No question outstanding; skip rather than stop"* (`time.descriptive_pause`); *"Reader is mid-question; cross the gap, do not leap"* (`time.time_jump`); *"Carrying facts the images cannot dramatise"* (`time.montage`). **And the same question governs material that is not in the present at all**, in the same words: *"Present scene is at maximum tension"* (`time.dramatised_flashback`) against *"The event is the load-bearing wall of the book"* (`time.cold_memory`) is `time.scene_duration` ↔ `time.summary_duration` restated for the past, and `time.nested_flashback`, `time.borrowed_memory`, `time.dream_sequence` and `time.historical_present` all yield into that same pair.

**Axis O3 — The audience has just been moved in time: how do they know, and who is keeping count?**
**12 conditions, and they are about the marker rather than the move:** *"Audience needs precise dates, as in procedural or mystery"* (`time.passage_marker`); *"Long strands where past-perfect anchoring would punish"* (`time.double_anchor`); *"Three or more strands; the tense code fails"* (`time.tense_shift`); *"Chapters are point-of-view units"* (`time.chapter_length_clock`) against *"The deadline can be extended"* (`time.countdown_chapter`); *"Written or serialised work; label the units"* (`time.clock_device`); *"Three or fewer, co-located cast"* (`time.ensemble_chronology`); *"Let the changed frame announce the jump"* (`time.years_later`) against *"Framing cannot be matched, or the first shot was weak"* (`time.same_shot_aged`). Asked last because it is the only axis that reaches the bookkeeping cards at all, and because **eight of the ten named failures on this shelf are failures of this axis, not of the other two** — the shift was made and nothing marked it.

**RUN — you are deciding what one clip's seconds do**

**Axis R1 — Against life, what does this clip's own time do?**
The shelf's own subject, and the only question every RUN card answers without strain. Six conditions turn on it directly — *"Detail the eye cannot catch in life"* (`time.slow_motion`) against *"Subject has no internal detail to open up"* (`time.slowmo_120_240`), *"Ordinary human movement rather than physics"* (`time.extreme_slowmo`), *"Compressing continuous change, not a daily record"* (`time.one_second_per_day`), *"Clear human motion gives the ping-pong away"* (`time.reverse_loop`), *"Vertical short-form, where full duration kills the hook"* (`time.real_time_playback`) — and it is asked first because it eliminates five of its seven values at a stroke.

**Axis R2 — Where does the manipulation get made, and what must the shoot supply?**
**The loudest condition on this half: 16 of its 37 conditions state it**, and they name a *place* rather than a preference — *"Speed not decided on set, or sync sound needed"* (`time.overcrank`) against *"Fast movement, busy background, or below half speed"* (`time.post_retime`), *"Sync dialogue, or a scene that must stay serious"* (`time.undercrank`), *"No array rig, space or alignment time"* (`time.timeslice`), *"Performers cannot truly hold still"* (`time.freeze_flyaround`), *"No motion-control hardware or schedule"* (`time.go_motion`), *"Performer cannot hold the poses"* (`time.pixilation`), *"No dark background or controlled light"* (`time.chronophotography`), *"Tight schedule, or the move judders"* (`time.motion_timelapse`), *"No anchor point over that travel distance"* (`time.hyperlapse`), *"No hours of locked-off time for the ramp"* (`time.day_to_night`), *"First and last frame cannot be matched"* (`time.loop`).

**Axis R3 — Is the whole clip in it, or is it a moment the clip drops into and comes out of?**
**12 conditions, and they are about duration and placement rather than look:** *"Marking the moment rather than launching out of it"* (`time.speed_ramp_up`) against *"The energy starts here rather than lands here"* (`time.speed_ramp_down`); *"No musical hit to place the change on"* (`time.ramp_to_beat`); *"The scene must continue after the hold"* (`time.freeze_frame`); *"The pause must run past two seconds"* (`time.freeze_release`) against *"Vertical short-form, where a live hold loses the viewer"* (`time.ma_hold`); *"The hit is illegible until it is replayed"* (`time.impact_slowmo_recovery`) against *"The event was already clear the first time"* (`time.slowmo_reveal_hit`); *"The slot is longer than a second"* (`time.boomerang`); *"Brokenness must read as the character, not the file"* (`time.frame_drop_stutter`). Asked last because it is the only axis that separates the applied cards from the technique cards they are built out of.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default on the ORDER half is `time.linear_spine`, and for once the shelf and the card agree.** The card argues for itself — *"Reach for it by default, and depart from it only when you can name the dramatic profit"* — and the pair graph votes for it independently: **six cards yield to `time.linear_spine`, the most inbound edges of any card on the shelf**, and they come from every corner of the half — `time.in_medias_res`, `time.nonlinear_assembly`, `time.reverse_chronology`, `time.frame_narrative`, `time.parallel_now` and the named failure `time.chapter_two_dump`. Nothing else on the ORDER half is named more than four times. **The shelf, asked sixty-eight times where to fall back to, falls back here.** Under it, `time.scene_duration` and `time.summary_duration` answer O2 (four inbound each) and `time.passage_marker` answers O3 (four inbound).

**The one against it on the ORDER half is `time.iterative_habit`.** Nothing routes to it. It is the only card on the half that narrates *many occurrences at once* — and in doing so it dissolves the single event, which is the unit every other card here assumes it is placing. `time.scene_duration`, `time.summary_duration`, `time.ellipsis`, `time.dilation` and the whole flashback block all decide how much telling **one** occurrence gets; `time.iterative_habit` says the occurrence is plural and the singular is the exception. Its only stated cost is *"you never break the pattern"* — which one rupture, placed once, pays in a sentence. **Its near cousin against the grain is `time.reverse_chronology`**, which inverts the half's whole geometry and is likewise never yielded to, but its cost is not payable: it *"kills hope structurally and cannot be talked out of it"*, and that is a price, not a discipline.

**The default on the RUN half is `time.real_time_playback` — and the shelf does not vote for it, which is a finding rather than a contradiction.** The card calls itself *"the baseline every other time manipulation is measured against"*, and **nothing on the shelf yields to it: zero inbound edges.** The measured fallback on this half is `time.timelapse`, three inbound, reached when `time.motion_timelapse`, `time.day_to_night` and `time.one_second_per_day` each become unaffordable. So when a compression card fails, the shelf hands you back a plainer compression — but **when any card fails, nothing at all hands you back plain speed.** Forty-one cards, and not one of them says *do less*. Use the default; know that the shelf is structurally incapable of recommending it.

**The one against it on the RUN half is `time.reverse`.** It is a true orphan — nothing routes to it and it routes to nothing — and it is the only card here where **causality** is inverted rather than the rate being stretched, compressed or stopped. Every other card on this half keeps the arrow of time and argues about its speed. Its stated cost is precise and it contains its own escape: *"anything natural in the frame, a flame, water or cloth in the wind, gives the reversal away instantly unless that is the point"* — and *unless that is the point* is the entire card, because the technique it names, performing the action backwards so that reversing it comes back uncannily natural, exists nowhere else on the shelf and is a shooting idea rather than an edit. **Its near cousin is `time.long_exposure`**, also an orphan, and the only card where movement stops being movement and becomes a static shape.

### Real Time

**Also called:** 1:1 time, normal speed  
**What it is:** The shot plays back at the speed it was captured: one second on screen equals one second of life. This is the baseline every other time manipulation is measured against.  
**Effect on the audience:** Truth and presence. The viewer lives the duration exactly as the character does, and when the duration runs long they start to feel its weight.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Dialogue and waiting scenes, tension that comes from duration itself such as defusing a bomb or a call that will not end, and all observational documentary. In drama keep the scene before the emotional explosion in real time so the explosion itself earns the slow motion that follows.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Feature, Interview / Video Podcast, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Limited Series, News Report / Reportage | genres: Drama, Verite / Observational, Thriller / Suspense, Legal Drama, Medical Drama, Slice of Life, Comedy  
**Avoid when:** When the only job of the scene is to deliver information, real time just makes it long. In vertical short-form full duration kills the hook.  
**Prompt:** `real time playback, natural motion at 24fps, no speed manipulation`  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; set the frame rate in the prompt to the project's delivery rate.  
**Yields to:** `time.shortform_compression` — Vertical short-form, where full duration kills the hook.  
`time.real_time_playback`

### Slow Motion at 48 or 60 fps

**Also called:** half speed, light slow-mo  
**What it is:** Capture at a higher rate played back at 24: 48 frames per second gives half speed, 60 gives 0.4 speed. Motion slows and still reads as real.  
**Effect on the audience:** A gentle emotional magnification. The movement becomes grand without losing its truth, and the viewer notices detail without feeling shouted at.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The hero walk, a head turning, the release of the ball in sport, the burst of joy in a wedding film. This is the right slow motion for drama and commercials because it praises the moment without turning it into a stunt.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Wedding / Event Film, Trailer / Teaser | genres: Drama, Action, Romance, Sports Drama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused)  
**Avoid when:** If the whole piece is slow, slow motion stops meaning anything. On spoken dialogue it destroys lip sync.  
**Prompt:** `slow motion at 48fps played at 24, half speed, smooth and cinematic`  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; set the capture rate against the playback rate of the project and check the speed on the rendered clip.  
**Yields to:** `time.slowmo_120_240` — Detail the eye cannot catch in life.  
`time.slow_motion`

### High-Speed Slow Motion at 120 or 240 fps

**Also called:** fifth speed, tenth speed  
**What it is:** Capture between 120 and 240 frames per second, dropping motion to a fifth or a tenth of real speed. It needs far more light because each frame gets a much shorter exposure.  
**Effect on the audience:** The moment opens up. The viewer sees what the eye cannot catch in life: the dust, the droplet, the muscle shaking under the skin.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The impact in action, splash and pour in commercials, hair and fabric in a music video, the blast in a trailer. It hits hardest as a single short shot inside a fast scene so the slowdown reads as a mark, not a habit.  
**Best in:** formats: Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Music Video, Trailer / Teaser, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Feature Film, Fashion Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | genres: Action, Sports Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Musical, Romance  
**Avoid when:** On a subject with no internal detail, this speed only exposes the emptiness. Without enough light it comes back dark and noisy.  
**Prompt:** `high-speed capture at 240fps, every particle of spray suspended, crisp shutter, hard even light`  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; the fifth and tenth speed are arithmetic against 24 fps playback; set the capture rate by the shot and the light available.  
**Yields to:** `time.slow_motion` — Subject has no internal detail to open up.  
`time.slowmo_120_240`

### Extreme Slow Motion at 1000 fps and Above

**Also called:** ultra slow-mo, phantom speed  
**What it is:** Capture at a thousand frames per second or more on a high-speed camera. One second of the event becomes forty seconds or more on screen.  
**Effect on the audience:** Physics becomes visible. Familiar matter such as water, glass or flame turns into a landscape nobody has seen before.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Glass shattering, a balloon bursting, an object entering water, powder falling in a coffee commercial. Its strongest use is one shot inside a commercial or a science documentary that explains something the naked eye can never see.  
**Best in:** formats: Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Documentary Feature, Documentary Series, Music Video, Experimental / Art Film, Video Essay | genres: Science / Educational, Nature / Wildlife, Action, Surreal / Absurdist, Thriller / Suspense  
**Avoid when:** It demands very heavy light and an event worth the cost. On ordinary human movement it comes back dead and pointless.  
**Prompt:** `ultra high-speed capture at 1000fps, shattering glass frozen in mid-flight, powerful even lighting`  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; the forty-times figure is arithmetic (1000 fps played back at 25 fps, about 42 at 24 fps); set the capture rate against the camera actually booked.  
**Yields to:** `time.slow_motion` — Ordinary human movement rather than physics.  
`time.extreme_slowmo`

### Overcrank

**Also called:** shooting high frame rate, in-camera slow motion  
**What it is:** The production term for running the camera faster than the playback rate so the action comes back slow. The word survives from the days of hand-cranked cameras.  
**Effect on the audience:** Slow motion that was actually captured stays sharp in every frame, so the eye trusts it far more than anything retimed afterwards.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Any moment you already know will be slowed: the jump, the blast, the hero entrance, the goal. The decision is made on set and not in the edit, and it is the difference between cinematic slow motion and rubbery slow motion.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Commercial / TVC, Music Video, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Trailer / Teaser, Short Film | genres: Action, Sports Drama, Drama, Musical, War  
**Avoid when:** If you overcrank and then do not need the slow motion, you have lost usable sync sound and locked yourself into one speed. A dialogue scene shot this way usually loses its voice track; the exception is dialogue you intend to replace, or a scene you will play silent.  
**Prompt:** `overcranked capture, filmed at a high frame rate for clean in-camera slow motion`  
**Yields to:** `time.post_retime` — Speed not decided on set, or sync sound needed.  
`time.overcrank`

### Undercrank / Fast Motion

**Also called:** speeding up in camera, fast motion  
**What it is:** Capture below the playback rate, for example at 12 or 16 frames per second, so the action comes back faster than life with a slight stutter built into it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Either wild energy or comedy. Bodies move in a way bodies cannot, so the scene turns either more savage or more funny.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Chases and fights when you want them to sting, silent-comedy business, and scenes of busy labour such as a market or a kitchen. In action it creates danger because the movement outruns the viewer's eye.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Commercial / TVC, Music Video, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Meme / Comedy Edit, Short Film, Drama Series Episode | genres: Action, Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Adventure, Survival, Road Movie  
**Avoid when:** On an emotional scene it breaks the seriousness and turns into farce. With dialogue it is impossible because sync sound falls apart.  
**Prompt:** `undercranked action, slightly sped-up frantic movement with a hard staccato feel`  
**Example:** Mad Max: Fury Road, the road battles.  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; set the capture rate by testing the retime on the delivery screen.  
**Yields to:** `time.post_retime` — Sync dialogue, or a scene that must stay serious.  
`time.undercrank`

### Post Retime / Optical Flow Slow Motion

**Also called:** time remap, frame interpolation  
**What it is:** Slowing or speeding a shot that was captured at normal rate inside the edit, with the software generating the missing frames by tracking pixel motion.  
**Effect on the audience:** It buys slow motion from a shot that was never planned for it. The result is smooth when the motion is simple and starts to melt when it is complex.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Rescuing a shot in the edit, easing a vertical clip, and the gentle 70 or 80 percent slowdown nobody notices but which lets a shot breathe. It belongs to social, documentary and wedding work where there is no chance to reshoot.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Documentary Feature, Corporate / Training, Wedding / Event Film, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Drama, Romance, Slice of Life, Verite / Observational, Sports Drama  
**Avoid when:** On fast movement against a busy background the edges turn rubbery and tear. Pushing below half speed from 24fps material usually shows.  
**Prompt:** `smooth retimed slow motion at 70 percent speed, natural motion blur, no rubbery edges`  
**Yields to:** `time.overcrank` — Fast movement, busy background, or below half speed.  
`time.post_retime`

### Time-Lapse

**Also called:** interval shooting  
**What it is:** One frame captured at a set interval of a second, a minute or an hour, then played in sequence so hours compress into seconds. The camera is usually locked off.  
**Effect on the audience:** It reveals patterns slower than human patience: cloud movement, city traffic, a flower opening, a shadow stretching.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The bridge between chapters of a documentary, an opening that says the city is alive, or a build, a restoration or a dish that passes in minutes. In heritage documentary put it on the site itself and let the light travel across the stone from dawn to dusk.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Documentary Series, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Corporate / Training, Video Essay, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form | genres: Nature / Wildlife, Science / Educational, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Drama  
**Avoid when:** It has become a cliche as the opening of any city film. If nothing inside the frame genuinely changes, a time-lapse is just filler.  
**Prompt:** `time-lapse of clouds racing over the ruins, locked-off camera, long shadows sweeping across the stone`  
**Example:** Koyaanisqatsi, the city and cloud sequences.  
**Yields to:** `time.motion_timelapse` — Locked frame with too little internal change.  
`time.timelapse`

### Motion Time-Lapse

**Also called:** motion-control time-lapse, slider lapse  
**What it is:** A time-lapse in which the camera advances a small calculated distance between frames on a slider or a motion-control head.  
**Effect on the audience:** It adds a third dimension to compressed time: the clouds race while the camera creeps, so the image feels alive rather than a still photograph on fast forward.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The premium opening of a documentary or travel piece, stars turning over an archaeological site, sunset across a facade in a property film. It works best with something close in the foreground so the shift in perspective is readable.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Commercial / TVC, Corporate / Training, Music Video | genres: Nature / Wildlife, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Science / Educational, Drama, Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** Too long a move brings judder and tearing in the foreground. It also eats hours on location, so it does not fit a tight schedule.  
**Prompt:** `motion time-lapse, slow controlled slider move while clouds streak overhead and shadows swing`  
**Yields to:** `time.timelapse` — Tight schedule, or the move judders.  
`time.motion_timelapse`

### Hyperlapse

**Also called:** walking lapse, stabilised long-distance lapse, moving time-lapse, tracking lapse  
**What it is:** A time-lapse where the camera travels a large distance between frames, a step, a metre or a street, then the frames are stabilised onto one fixed point in post.  
**Effect on the audience:** Human flight. The viewer crosses a street or a city in seconds while the centre stays nailed in place, which reads as rush and rotation at once.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The opening of a travel reel, a location change inside a vlog, a walkthrough of a mall, a fair or a heritage site, and the run from dawn to dusk across a single monument. The strongest version keeps one element, a doorway, a statue or a tower, dead centre from the first frame to the last. In heritage documentary it shows an archaeological site under changing light.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Corporate / Training, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Video Essay | genres: Adventure, Road Movie, Slice of Life, Science / Educational, Historical / Biopic, Nature / Wildlife, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Drama, Cyberpunk  
**Avoid when:** With no clear anchor point it is just nausea, and in places with people close to camera the motion breaks into ugly stutter. Do not use it in a scene that needs human presence either; the speed erases people.  
**Prompt:** `hyperlapse walking toward the gate, one frame per step, the gate locked dead centre the whole way`  
**Yields to:** `time.motion_timelapse` — No anchor point over that travel distance.  
`time.hyperlapse`

### Day to Night in One Shot

**Also called:** holy grail time-lapse, sunset to stars  
**What it is:** A time-lapse that crosses sunset and keeps running into night with exposure ramped gradually so the image never breaks. Photographers call it the holy grail because of how hard the ramp is to hold.  
**Effect on the audience:** It hands the viewer a whole day in a single stroke, and with it a sense of transience: the light leaves and the place stays.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of a documentary chapter, the shot that says the day is done above an ancient site, or the opening of a travel film. In heritage documentary it is the strongest way to say this place watched thousands of sunsets before we arrived.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Series, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Video Essay | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Nature / Wildlife, Historical / Biopic, Drama, Science / Educational  
**Avoid when:** It needs hours of locked-off time and preparation, so do not promise it on a tight schedule. If the exposure ramp is not smooth, the flicker gives the whole thing away.  
**Prompt:** `day to night time-lapse over the ruins, sun setting then stars appearing, seamless exposure ramp`  
**Yields to:** `time.timelapse` — No hours of locked-off time for the ramp.  
`time.day_to_night`

### The Seasons Pass

**Also called:** four seasons cut, same frame across the year  
**What it is:** The same frame shot at four points in the year or in four weather states, cut together with the composition matched exactly. Sometimes dissolved, sometimes hard cut.  
**Effect on the audience:** Time passes without anyone announcing how much. The tree, the snow or the colour of the ground does all the work.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A year passing in a family drama, a stretch of mourning, healing or waiting, or a full cycle in a nature documentary. Anime uses it on a school gate or a single tree between chapters.

**Avoid when:** If the composition does not match, the trick collapses into a scenery montage. Do not run it twice in the same piece.  
**Prompt:** `the same tree in the same frame across four seasons, identical composition, only light and foliage change`  
**Yields to:** `time.passage_marker` — The composition cannot be matched.  
`time.seasons_pass`

### One Second a Day

**Also called:** daily diary cut, long-term daily record  
**What it is:** A very short clip recorded every single day for months or years, then cut in sequence. Technically it is not a time-lapse because every clip runs at real speed.  
**Effect on the audience:** The accumulation gives a sense of a lifetime. Each clip is trivial alone, and together they become a life.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A vlog of a journey, a pregnancy or a recovery, a channel year in review, a long build, or a passage inside a personal documentary. It lands hardest when one thing changes slowly through it: a child, a wound, a house.  
**Best in:** formats: Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Documentary Feature, Corporate / Training, Wedding / Event Film, Video Essay, Kids Content | genres: Slice of Life, Family, Drama, Science / Educational, Coming of Age  
**Avoid when:** With no thread tying the clips together it is just a boring archive. If every clip shares one angle and one mood, the sense of change disappears.  
**Prompt:** `a one second a day diary cut, the same child growing across dozens of short clips`  
**Yields to:** `time.timelapse` — Compressing continuous change, not a daily record.  
`time.one_second_per_day`

### Speed Ramp Up

**Also called:** ramp into fast, accelerating retime  
**What it is:** The shot starts slow or at normal speed and accelerates progressively inside the same shot with no cut.  
**Effect on the audience:** An energy burst. The viewer feels the event slip out of time's grip and take off.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The sprinter leaving the blocks, the sword drawn before the charge, the top of the chorus in a music video, the this-is-where-it-starts beat in a reel. Excellent on the first hit of a sports commercial.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Feature Film, Dance Film | genres: Action, Sports Drama, Musical, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Shonen Battle (anime), Thriller / Suspense

**Prompt:** `speed ramp accelerating from slow motion into real time as he breaks into a run`  
**Yields to:** `time.speed_ramp_down` — Marking the moment rather than launching out of it.  
`time.speed_ramp_up`

### Speed Ramp Down

**Also called:** ramp into slow, decelerating retime  
**What it is:** The reverse: the shot starts fast and decelerates until it reaches the peak moment and stays slow there.  
**Effect on the audience:** It marks the moment. The viewer understands this is the important thing before they have even seen all of it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Just before the crash, the instant eyes meet, the catch, or the line that ends the scene. In a trailer ramp down right before the title so the title arrives into stillness.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Feature Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Dance Film | genres: Action, Romance, Sports Drama, Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Musical  
**Avoid when:** Ramping down onto a moment that does not deserve it only proves the scene has nothing in it.  
**Prompt:** `speed ramp decelerating from real time into slow motion at the exact moment of contact`  
**Yields to:** `time.speed_ramp_up` — The energy starts here rather than lands here.  
`time.speed_ramp_down`

### Speed Ramp to the Beat

**Also called:** beat-synced ramp, rhythm ramp, ramp in ramp out, time warp  
**What it is:** The speed change is placed exactly on the drum hit or on a marked point in the track, so the picture breathes with the sound.  
**Effect on the audience:** Physical sync. The viewer feels the hit in the body because the image moved with it on the same frame, and a simple movement such as a turn or a walk starts to feel like an event.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Dance videos, reel edits, sports promos, title sequences, travel and fashion content. The rule: put the slowest point on the silence and the fastest point on the hit, slowing the preparation and speeding up the action. Shoot at a high frame rate so the slow section stays smooth, and use it once or twice in a video, no more.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Dance Film, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, Meme / Comedy Edit, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Fashion Film | genres: Musical, Action, Sports Drama, Comedy, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Slice of Life, Romance, Adventure  
**Avoid when:** Two frames out of sync and the whole feeling is gone, and repeating it every two seconds makes the edit feel cheap and dizzying. If the take was shot at a normal frame rate the slow section comes out stuttering.  
**Prompt:** `speed ramp timed to the drum hit, slow through the silence then snapping to full speed on the beat`  
**Yields to:** `time.speed_ramp_down` — No musical hit to place the change on.  
`time.ramp_to_beat`

### Ramp Into a Freeze on the Title

**Also called:** title freeze, end card freeze  
**What it is:** The shot decelerates until it stops dead on a single frame, and the title or logo burns in over that frozen frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** A full stop. The movement becomes a photograph, and the photograph becomes a poster that stays in the memory.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of a trailer, the last beat of a sports commercial, the final shot of a main title, the close of a reel before the call to follow. It is strongest when the frozen frame holds the peak of a movement: a jump, a strike, a laugh.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video | genres: Action, Sports Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Superhero, Musical  
**Avoid when:** If the last frame is not strong, the title lands on a dead picture and the ending collapses.  
**Prompt:** `the action ramps down and freezes on the peak of the jump as the title burns in`  
`time.ramp_to_title_freeze`

### Freeze Frame

**Also called:** frozen frame, still hold  
**What it is:** One frame repeated so time stops inside the shot, with sound either continuing under it or cutting out, depending on the intent.  
**Effect on the audience:** Suspension. It cuts the flow of life and tells the viewer to hold on and store this image.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of a film on a face whose fate is left open, a character introduction, or a comedy beat where someone freezes in a ridiculous pose. In documentary, hold on a face inside archive footage just before the narration lands on it.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Feature, Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Meme / Comedy Edit | genres: Drama, Comedy, Tragedy, Sports Drama, Crime / Procedural, Coming of Age  
**Avoid when:** Dropped mid-scene for no reason it reads as a technical fault. As an ending it is well worn, so the face has to genuinely deserve the freeze.

**Used for and where it works best:** Introducing a crew or a team in crime and comedy, naming players in a sports opener, labelling elements in an instructional reel. It earns its keep when you have five people and ten seconds to make them distinguishable.

`time.freeze_frame_text`

### Freeze and Release

**Also called:** hold and go, stutter stop  
**What it is:** The image freezes for a second or two, then resumes from the same point with no cut and no jump.  
**Effect on the audience:** A held breath. The viewer stops with it and starts again with it, so the restart hits harder than the shot would have on its own.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Just before the first blow of a fight, on a face at the instant it hears the news, or in comedy to hold a stunned expression before chaos. In a reel it works as a hook: freeze, let the caption land, then release.

`time.freeze_release`

### Bullet Time / Frozen Moment

**Also called:** frozen instant orbit, time slice, matrix shot, frozen orbit  
**What it is:** Time inside the scene stops or crawls while the camera keeps moving around the event. Built either with an array of cameras or as a 3D simulation.  
**Effect on the audience:** It lifts the viewer out of physics. The event becomes a solid object in space you can walk around and inspect from any side.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The moment a power lands, an orbit around a blast or a leap, the goal or the strike in sport, or a commercial presenting a product inside suspended spray. In anime and games it is the special-move beat where the movement itself is being praised.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Music Video, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, 3D CG Animation, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Action, Sci-Fi, Superhero, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Cyberpunk, Shonen Battle (anime), Fantasy / Epic, Sports Drama

### Time-Freeze Fly-Around / Frozen Tableau

**Effect on the audience:** The viewer becomes the only moving thing in the world. It reads as dream, as power, or as a moment suspended between life and death.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A time-stop scene in fantasy, a walk inside a memory, a gallery of characters in a music video, or a crime film where the detective walks through the frozen accident. Ideal in music video because it keeps the band still and makes the camera the performer.

### Timeslice / Camera Array Capture

**Also called:** array capture, still-camera rig  
**What it is:** Dozens of fixed cameras arranged around the subject fire at the same instant, or milliseconds apart, and their images are then played in sequence.  
**Effect on the audience:** An impossible shot: a viewpoint that revolves around a frozen or crawling instant without any real camera move at all.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Sports and product commercials, a showcase section in a music video, and event content such as the array booth at parties and weddings. It reads best when there is spray, dust or flying fabric to prove the freeze.

`time.timeslice`

### Chronophotography / Multi-Flash Motion Study

**Effect on the audience:** Time is displayed as distance. The viewer sees every stage of the movement at once instead of one after another.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Explaining an athletic move or a physical phenomenon in documentary, an art image in a music video, an analysis card in a sports commercial. In educational work it is the cleanest way to show the path of a strike or a jump in one picture.

### Reverse Motion

**Also called:** backwards playback, reversed action  
**What it is:** The shot plays from its last frame to its first. Sometimes the action itself is performed backwards so that reversing it produces something uncannily natural.  
**Effect on the audience:** It breaks cause and effect. The broken thing reassembles and the water returns to the glass, so the mind reads magic, dream or a rewind of time.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The magic beat in fantasy, a dream or memory running backwards, or the end of a commercial that restores the product to its original state. In music video and short-form it is the cheapest way to stage something impossible with no visual effects at all.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Feature Film, Short Film, Kids Content, Experimental / Art Film, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Fantasy / Epic, Surreal / Absurdist, Sci-Fi, Comedy, Psychological / Arthouse, Fairy Tale, Musical  
**Avoid when:** Anything natural in the frame, a flame, water or cloth in the wind, gives the reversal away instantly unless that is the point. Reversing a shot with speech in it usually destroys the line, because reversed speech cannot be read; the exception is when the strangeness of that sound is exactly what the scene wants.  
**Prompt:** `reverse motion, the shattered pieces flying back together into a whole cup`  
**Example:** Tenet, the inverted action sequences.  
`time.reverse`

### Rewind Reveal

**Also called:** rewind to the truth, tape rewind  
**What it is:** The scene races backwards to an earlier point, then plays forward again and reveals information we did not see the first time.  
**Effect on the audience:** The shock of rereading. The same images carry a different meaning, and the viewer feels cleverly deceived.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The reveal of the trick in a heist, the truth in a mystery, and the payoff in a reel that runs back to the start and explains what actually happened. In advertising it makes a smart ending: rewind and show the detail that was missed.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Commercial / TVC, Trailer / Teaser, Short Film, Meme / Comedy Edit | genres: Heist, Mystery / Whodunit, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Noir / Neo-Noir  
**Avoid when:** If the revealed information is weak the rewind feels like a cheap trick. Do not use it twice in one piece or the viewer starts waiting for it.  
**Prompt:** `the scene rushes backwards in a blur then plays forward again revealing the hand under the table`  
**Yields to:** `time.overlapping_time` — Show the missed truth from another angle.  
`time.rewind_reveal`

### Reverse-to-Start Loop / Ping-Pong Loop

**Also called:** palindrome loop, back and forth loop  
**What it is:** The clip plays forward to its end, then runs backwards to its beginning, so it repeats forever with no visible cut.  
**Effect on the audience:** A pulse. The movement becomes a breath that never stops, and the eye stays locked in because it never finds an end point.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A moving background for a cover or a title card, a product shot rocking back and forth, or a reel that has to loop invisibly. It is a lifesaver when the material is short and the slot is long.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Product Video / Motion Design, Commercial / TVC, Lyric Video, Kids Content, AI-Generated Clip, Corporate / Training | genres: Comedy, Family, Preschool / Early Learning, Slice of Life, Surreal / Absurdist, Science / Educational  
**Avoid when:** On clear human motion it looks artificial and funny, and walking gives it away instantly — unless funny is the point: a snap of a second or less on a clap or a jump is `time.boomerang`, which spends that artifice on purpose. Keep it for abstract or natural material.  
**Prompt:** `seamless ping-pong loop, the smoke rises then reverses back down, endlessly repeating`  
**Yields to:** `time.loop` — Clear human motion gives the ping-pong away.  
`time.reverse_loop`

### The Loop

**Also called:** seamless loop, cinemagraph loop  
**What it is:** A clip whose last frame matches its first exactly, so it repeats without limit and without a jump.  
**Effect on the audience:** Time gets trapped. The repetition is mildly hypnotic and gives a sense of something recurring with no way out.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Live backgrounds on screens and titles, the reel you want watched three times before anyone notices, and a loop that stands for a suffocating routine in drama. In digital advertising it is the default form of the in-store screen.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Product Video / Motion Design, Commercial / TVC, Corporate / Training, Lyric Video, AI-Generated Clip, Experimental / Art Film | genres: Slice of Life, Surreal / Absurdist, Psychological / Arthouse, Science / Educational, Comedy, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic  
**Avoid when:** If the join is not exact, the jump is all anyone sees. Do not loop content that carries sequential information.  
**Prompt:** `perfectly seamless loop, the first and last frame identical, continuous ambient motion`  
**Yields to:** `time.reverse_loop` — First and last frame cannot be matched.  
`time.loop`

> **The long loop is a different job from the short one, and the method is assembled rather than
> given.** Everything above is written for a clip of a few seconds, where the join is the whole
> object. A gallery or installation piece — ten, twenty, forty minutes, running all day — loops for a
> different reason: not to be watched repeatedly, but so that **a visitor arriving at any moment is
> not arriving late**. Four things make it work, and three of them live elsewhere in this package:
>
> 1. **One generated plate, used twice.** Do not make two frames that resemble each other and hope;
>    make the final shot and the opening shot from the **same image**, so the join is identical by
>    construction rather than by matching. This is the part nothing in the package said out loud.
> 2. **A hard cut at the join, never a dissolve.** A dissolve announces itself as an edit at exactly
>    the moment nothing should be announced — see `cut.invisible` in Phase 3 for what makes a cut
>    disappear, and `vertical.loop_point` for the same problem at short-form scale.
> 3. **Nothing sequential across the join.** No count, no accumulating light, no narration mid-
>    sentence, and nothing that makes a returning visitor feel they have missed a beginning.
> 4. **The shape is a cycle, not an arc.** A piece that must not end cannot use the ending craft: the
>    Avoid-when on `open.loop_opening` and `end.loop_close` both say so, correctly, because those two
>    are about a *story* that loops. A cycle with no beginning is built from movements that return —
>    marsh to flood to drought to marsh — and the return **is** the structure rather than a device
>    concealing a missing one.
>
> **Every visitor sees a fragment.** Ninety seconds of forty minutes, entered at random. So each
> movement carries its own complete sensation and needs nothing before it, which is the same
> discipline as a walk-up exhibit — `inter.walk_up_entry` in Phase 1 is written for exactly this
> audience, and is worth reading even though the interactive route will not send you there.

### Boomerang

**Also called:** short back-and-forth clip  
**What it is:** A very short clip, a second or less, that snaps forward and back at speed. Social platforms turned it into a fixed form.  
**Effect on the audience:** Energy and lightness. A tiny moment is inflated into a joke or a celebration, and nobody expects meaning from it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Clapping and jumping moments on social, an interaction with a product such as opening, shaking or pouring, plus event, wedding and behind-the-scenes content. It rescues a shot that is weak on its own but holds one nice small movement.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Meme / Comedy Edit, Wedding / Event Film, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Product Video / Motion Design, Commercial / TVC, Concert / Live Event | genres: Comedy, Family, Slice of Life, Romance, Musical  
**Avoid when:** It has no place in serious or sad content. Repeated inside one post it reads as adolescent and silly.  
**Prompt:** `one second boomerang, the toss snapping forward and back, bright and playful`  
**Yields to:** `time.reverse_loop` — The slot is longer than a second.  
`time.boomerang`

### Step Printing

**Also called:** stutter print, smeared slow motion, step print, stutter smear, frame-repeat blur  
**What it is:** Each frame is printed several times with a longer exposure, producing slow motion that stutters and smears at once: the background melts while the action advances in jerks. Wong Kar-wai and Doyle made it a visual signature.  
**Effect on the audience:** Detachment from reality. The character feels out of phase with everyone around them, and time reads as broken rather than merely slowed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A lone character inside a crowd, moving through a throng, waiting in a busy place, longing, a state of drunkenness or shock, a memory that will not hold together. Excellent in urban romance, music video and night-time advertising, where a single night-city shot carries a strong signature.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Short Film, Drama Series Episode, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Fashion Film, Experimental / Art Film, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Psychological / Arthouse, Noir / Neo-Noir, Romance, Crime / Procedural, Surreal / Absurdist, Drama, Cyberpunk, Melodrama, Coming of Age  
**Avoid when:** On a scene that must deliver clear information it destroys legibility. It is also strongly associated with one house style, so leaning on it reads as imitation.  
**Prompt:** `step-printed motion, smeared stuttering slow motion, neon background dissolving into trails`  
**Example:** Chungking Express, the crowd passages shot by Christopher Doyle.  
**Yields to:** `time.shutter_drag` — The house-style stamp is unwanted.  
`time.step_printing`

### Frame Drops / Stutter

**Also called:** dropped frames, judder, glitch stutter  
**What it is:** Frames are removed from inside the movement so it jerks like a failing broadcast, or frames are doubled and dropped to create an irregular pulse.  
**Effect on the audience:** Malfunction. The viewer senses something wrong with the image itself rather than the scene, so unease enters with no visible cause.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The entity appearing in horror, a psychological break, a hack or system failure in sci-fi, and aggressive short-form edits. In a trailer it works as a pulse right before the title.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video, Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Meme / Comedy Edit, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Horror, Psychological Horror, Cyberpunk, Thriller / Suspense, Supernatural Horror, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** Used in ordinary content the viewer assumes the file is broken and leaves. Over a face that is speaking it usually swallows the line, because the mouth and the words come apart; the exception is when the breakdown of the speech is the subject.  
**Prompt:** `harsh frame drops and stutter, the figure jumping between positions like corrupted footage`  
**Yields to:** `time.step_printing` — Brokenness must read as the character, not the file.  
`time.frame_drop_stutter`

### Ghosting / Echo Trails

**Also called:** frame blending, echo effect, trails  
**What it is:** Previous frames stay visible as translucent layers over the current one, so every movement leaves a fading ghost behind it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Hallucination. The viewer loses track of where the real body is, and time turns sticky and heavy.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A drugged, feverish or fainting state, a dream, or a dance in a music video you want to feel liquid. In science documentary it is useful for showing the path of a movement across time inside a single shot.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Feature Film, Experimental / Art Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Video Essay | genres: Psychological / Arthouse, Surreal / Absurdist, Horror, Sci-Fi, Musical, Psychological Horror, Fantasy / Epic  
**Avoid when:** It hides detail and makes faces unreadable, so keep it out of any scene where we must know who is doing what.  
**Prompt:** `echo trails following the dancer, previous frames fading behind each movement`  
**Yields to:** `time.chronophotography` — We must still know who is doing what.  
`time.ghost_echo`

### Shutter Drag / Strobe Motion

**Also called:** shutter angle, slow shutter, 45 degree shutter  
**What it is:** Control of the shutter angle: a wide angle gives each frame a longer exposure and heavy motion blur, a narrow angle gives a short exposure and makes every movement crisp and staccato.  
**Effect on the audience:** Blur reads as dream, flow and speed you cannot parse. Staccato reads as violence and dryness and makes bodies feel like they are breaking as they move.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Drag for drunkenness, dream states and night movement in music video, staccato for battle, blasts and any violence meant to hurt. In a war film a narrow shutter on an explosion makes every fragment visible and terrifying.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Music Video, Trailer / Teaser, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Commercial / TVC, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: War, Action, Horror, Psychological / Arthouse, Thriller / Suspense, Surreal / Absurdist, Martial Arts / Wuxia  
**Avoid when:** Too much drag loses faces and detail. Staccato over a calm scene feels jarring and manufactured.  
**Prompt:** `narrow 45 degree shutter, crisp staccato motion with almost no blur in the explosion`  
**Example:** Saving Private Ryan, the narrow shutter of the landing sequence.  
`time.shutter_drag`

### Long Exposure and Light Trails

**Also called:** light streaks, star trails, slow shutter stills  
**What it is:** Each frame takes a long exposure, so moving light prints as a continuous streak while static elements stay sharp. It lives inside time-lapse work and inside stills used in video.  
**Effect on the audience:** Time becomes a line you can see. The viewer reads how much movement passed from how long the streak runs.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Car trails in a night city, stars wheeling above an ancient site, fire or sparks drawing in the air. Its best job is a transition shot in documentary, or an opening that says the world keeps running while the protagonist stands still.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Corporate / Training, Video Essay | genres: Nature / Wildlife, Science / Educational, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Noir / Neo-Noir, Cyberpunk, Drama  
**Avoid when:** It needs absolute stability and long takes. Any camera movement turns the whole frame into smear.  
**Prompt:** `long exposure light trails, headlights drawn as continuous ribbons, the building razor sharp`  
`time.long_exposure`

### Stop Motion

**Also called:** frame by frame animation, claymation, puppet animation  
**What it is:** One frame is shot, the object is moved a small amount, the next frame is shot, and so on until the movement is complete. The motion is built by hand in real time on the table.  
**Effect on the audience:** Weight and craft. The viewer feels the material and the touch, and senses a human hand behind every second.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Puppet and clay films, handmade commercials, a teaching segment for children, and inventive product transitions where ingredients move on their own. It is a gift for brand identity because it feels honest and warm against polished imagery.  
**Best in:** formats: Stop Motion, Kids Content, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Short Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Explainer, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | genres: Family, Fairy Tale, Fantasy / Epic, Comedy, Preschool / Early Learning, Adventure, Horror  
**Avoid when:** It is slow and expensive in time, so do not promise long sequences. Any lighting shift between frames shows up as ugly flicker.  
**Prompt:** `stop motion animation, handcrafted puppet moved frame by frame, visible fingerprints in the clay`  
**Example:** Coraline, the whole film.  
**Yields to:** `time.go_motion` — Fast creatures, where the staccato breaks the weight.  
`time.stop_motion`

### Go Motion

**Also called:** motion-blurred stop motion  
**What it is:** Stop motion in which the puppet actually moves during the exposure, driven by motors or by hand, so real motion blur is recorded in each frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** It removes the stutter and makes the creature feel heavy, alive and fast. The eye accepts it as a body in the world rather than a puppet.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Fast-moving creatures: a dragon, a monster, a flying object. Use it when you want the handmade value of stop motion without its staccato rhythm, especially in classic fantasy and science fiction.  
**Best in:** formats: Stop Motion, Feature Film, Short Film, Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, 3D CG Animation | genres: Fantasy / Epic, Sci-Fi, Monster / Kaiju, Adventure, Horror, Mythology / Ancient Epic  
**Avoid when:** It is harder and slower than plain stop motion and needs dedicated hardware. Too much blur and the creature loses its edge.  
**Prompt:** `go motion creature animation, real motion blur trailing the wings, weighty and fast`  
**Example:** Dragonslayer, the dragon sequences.  
**Yields to:** `time.stop_motion` — No motion-control hardware or schedule.  
`time.go_motion`

### Pixilation

**Also called:** human stop motion, live-action frame animation  
**What it is:** Stop motion applied to real people: the performer shifts position slightly between frames, so they move in ways a body cannot.  
**Effect on the audience:** Playful strangeness. A person glides, flies or vanishes, and the viewer knows it is a trick and enjoys it precisely because it is visible.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Playful music videos, children's content, handmade-feeling commercials, and social clips built on instant transformation such as changing clothes on a jump. It is ideal when you want magic with no 3D work at all.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Kids Content, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Short Film, Commercial / TVC, Experimental / Art Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Stop Motion | genres: Comedy, Family, Fairy Tale, Surreal / Absurdist, Preschool / Early Learning, Musical  
**Avoid when:** It is exhausting for the performer and needs a locked camera and constant light. In a serious scene it destroys credibility.  
**Prompt:** `pixilation, the man glides across the floor without walking, one frame per pose`  
**Example:** Neighbours by Norman McLaren.  
**Yields to:** `time.undercrank` — Performer cannot hold the poses.

### The Hold / Ma

**Also called:** the pause, the empty beat, dead time  
**What it is:** A shot that keeps running after the event has finished, or a pause that stretches time between two events. Nothing new happens; time simply passes. It differs from the anime Hold, which freezes a single drawing with no motion at all, and from the Pillow Shot, which is a separate shot of an empty place inserted between two scenes.  
**Effect on the audience:** Breath. The pause gives the viewer room to feel what just happened, and it makes everything on either side of it heavier.  
**Used for and where it works best:** After bad news, after someone leaves, and before a decision. In anime and Japanese drama it is a core tool, a shot of sky, a train, water, and in heritage documentary it is the shot that lets the stone speak.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Drama Series Episode, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Short Film, Limited Series | genres: Drama, Slice of Life, Tragedy, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Psychological / Arthouse, Coming of Age, Romance  
**Avoid when:** In vertical short-form a hold loses the viewer in one second. Placed with no emotional reason it is slackness, not silence.  
**Prompt:** `the shot holds on the empty doorway for several seconds after she leaves, nothing moves but the curtain`  
**Example:** Spirited Away, the quiet train ride.  
**Yields to:** `time.freeze_release` — Vertical short-form, where a live hold loses the viewer.  
`time.ma_hold`

### The Slow-Motion Walk

**Also called:** hero walk, group walk  
**What it is:** A group or a single figure walking toward or alongside the camera in slow motion, usually in a wide or medium shot over a steady musical pulse.  
**Effect on the audience:** Coronation. An ordinary walk becomes a statement of power, and the viewer understands that these are the protagonists.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Introducing the crew in a heist or sports film, the hero walking out of the blast, the bride entering in a wedding film, the band arriving in a music video. In short-form it is the fastest way to make ordinary people look like stars.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Wedding / Event Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Drama Series Episode | genres: Action, Heist, Crime / Procedural, Sports Drama, Gangster, Musical, Superhero  
**Avoid when:** It is one of the most worn-out shots in circulation, so with no dramatic reason it reads as imitation. In realist drama it breaks the tone outright.  
**Prompt:** `slow motion hero walk toward camera, wide lens, dust and smoke behind them, steady confident stride`  
**Example:** Reservoir Dogs, the opening walk.

### Slow-Motion Impact Then Real-Time Recovery

**Effect on the audience:** It separates the pain from its consequence. The viewer sees the blow clearly, then feels the real chaos that follows it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Boxing and martial arts, crashes, a fall in sport, and the instant of physical shock in drama. It beats full slow motion because the return to real speed brings the danger straight back.

### Matched-Speed Cut

**Effect on the audience:** Continuity. The viewer reads both shots as one event even when the place or the angle has changed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A run of slow-motion shots inside a fight, a sports montage cutting between different cameras, a music video crossing locations while holding one pulse. Without it a slow-motion sequence feels chopped and confused.

### The Time Jump / X Years Later

**Also called:** title card jump, after the jump  
**What it is:** A cut between scenes with the elapsed time announced outright: a title card, a line of narration, or an unmistakable change of appearance.  
**Effect on the audience:** It resets the viewer's expectations in one stroke. Everything before becomes past, and everything after has to prove itself.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The start of act two after the disaster, a character returning from prison or war, or an episode ending that opens on a new era. In historical documentary the card is the cleanest way to travel between periods.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Docudrama / Reenactment, Trailer / Teaser | genres: Drama, Historical / Biopic, Crime / Procedural, War, Tragedy, Coming of Age, Heritage (civilisation-focused)  
**Avoid when:** Using it to skip a hard scene that should have been written makes the story feel like it ran away. Repeating it fragments the rhythm. ### The Same Shot, Aged

**Also called:** return to the same frame, matched frame across years  
**What it is:** The same composition from the same angle shot at two distant points in time, with the only difference being what has changed in the people or the place.  
**Effect on the audience:** It hurts precisely because it says nothing. The viewer makes the comparison themselves, and no comparison lands harder than the one you make yourself.  
**Used for and where it works best:** An ending that returns to the first shot, a family scene years later, a documentary comparing a location between an old record and today. In heritage work: the archive photograph of the site, then the identical frame shot now.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Series, Limited Series, Drama Series Episode, Video Essay | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Family, Coming of Age, Nature / Wildlife  
**Avoid when:** If the framing is not truly matched the comparison dies and it is just two scenes. It also relies on the viewer remembering the first shot, so do not use it if that shot was weak.  
**Prompt:** `the identical framing revisited years later, same angle and same light, everyone older`  
**Example:** Boyhood, the same actor filmed across twelve years.  
**Yields to:** `time.years_later` — Framing cannot be matched, or the first shot was weak.  
`time.same_shot_aged`

### The Real-Time Episode

**Also called:** real-time structure, continuous time story  
**What it is:** A whole episode or film runs at the viewer's own time: an hour on screen is an hour in the story, with no jumps at all.  
**Effect on the audience:** Unbroken pressure. The viewer never gets relief because there is no time cut to rescue them, so tension simply accumulates.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A crisis episode, an operation, a siege, a negotiation, a single night. In series it is a special episode written once a season because it burns enormous energy.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Feature Film, Vertical Micro-Drama, Short Film, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Crime / Procedural, Medical Drama, War, Survival, Political Thriller, Drama  
**Avoid when:** It needs an event strong enough to carry the whole duration. If the writing is thin, real time exposes it inside ten minutes.  
**Prompt:** `continuous real-time coverage of the operation, no time skips, clock visible on the wall`  
**Example:** 24, the real-time season structure.  
**Yields to:** `time.single_day` — The event cannot carry unbroken duration.  
`time.real_time_episode`

### Overlapping Time

**Also called:** parallel timelines, repeated moment from another angle  
**What it is:** The same stretch of time is shown more than once from different angles or strands, or timelines of different lengths are intercut as if they shared one moment.  
**Effect on the audience:** It makes the viewer assemble the truth from pieces. Each new pass changes the meaning of what came before.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Multi-narrator stories, a crime film revisiting the moment through another witness, films where three strands of different length converge. In documentary it is the way to show one event through several sources. It shares a name with `time.parallel_now` but not an engine: there, strands running at the same moment are cut between and nothing is shown twice; here a stretch is shown again, or strands of different length are cut as if they shared one moment.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Limited Series, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Feature, Short Film, Docudrama / Reenactment, Video Essay | genres: Crime / Procedural, Mystery / Whodunit, Thriller / Suspense, Drama, War, Noir / Neo-Noir, True Crime  
**Avoid when:** If it is not clear that we have gone back, the result is confusion rather than intelligence. It needs a clear picture or sound marker.  
**Prompt:** `the same minute replayed from a second angle, matching sound cues marking the overlap`  
**Example:** Dunkirk, the three intercut timelines.  
**Yields to:** `time.rewind_reveal` — No clear marker that we have gone back.  
`time.overlapping_time`

### The Flash-Forward Glimpse

**Also called:** flash-forward, cold open from later  
**What it is:** One or two short shots from an event that has not happened yet, dropped at the start or inside a scene with no explanation.  
**Effect on the audience:** It plants a question. The viewer keeps watching not to learn what happens, but to learn how we arrive there.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A cold open showing the disaster before the story starts, a glimpse before the battle in a trailer, the short-form hook that shows the result in the first two seconds. It is strongest when the glimpse withholds who and why.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Limited Series, Documentary Feature, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Mystery / Whodunit, Drama, True Crime, War, Tragedy  
**Avoid when:** Reveal too much and you kill the suspense you were building. Fail to pay it off clearly and the viewer feels cheated instead of haunted.  
**Prompt:** `a two second flash-forward of the burning car, no context, then hard cut back to the quiet morning`  
**Example:** Breaking Bad, the flash-forward cold opens.  
**Yields to:** `time.in_medias_res` — The glimpse would reveal too much.  
`time.flash_forward_glimpse`
