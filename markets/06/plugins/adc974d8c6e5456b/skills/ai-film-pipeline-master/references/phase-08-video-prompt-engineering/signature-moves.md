# Signature and Compound Moves

97 cards. These decide a shot: how it moves, how it cuts, and how it sits in time.

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, what to avoid, and the prompt written out in full.

---

### Name the Gate Before the Name

**Also called:** the signature selector, the gate before the name, is this move affordable and is it earned  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `sig.` shelf. It does not describe a move. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **what has to exist before this move is possible at all, how much unbroken time does the move own, and what must the beat be doing for it to be earned** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were not invented: they were read off the ninety-two `Yields to:` conditions the cards carry and the boundaries their `Avoid when:` lines draw, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id.  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so that the move that does reach the screen was chosen against the location, the performer and the beat, instead of being reached for because it is famous.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf** — and answer the first one honestly, because it is the only one with a bill attached. The first answer eliminates roughly two-thirds of the file; the second and third leave a shortlist of three to six. Question three is the shelf's own second half made mechanical: a signature move is a loan taken out against the beat, and if the beat is not turning, climbing or withholding, nothing here is earned and the honest answer is a plain card from `move.`  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** **Two-thirds of this shelf's own conditions point off the shelf**, so a shortlist from this map is only right if the plain card underneath it was tried first and rejected — run `move.`, `cut.` and `frame.` before you come here, and arrive only when the plain version genuinely does not do the job. Avoid it too when you are continuing an established grammar rather than opening one: there the move is inherited from the piece you are matching, not chosen from a shelf, and `sig.motion_control_repeat` is the card that says so. **Distrust axis one whenever your honest answer is its fourth value** — thirty of the ninety-six live there, so for those this map has only two questions left and is doing two-thirds of the work it does on a real location. And it sorts **moves, not attributions**: it cannot tell you whether the name on a card is earned by the shot or inherited by copying, and at least two pairs inside the map below are one move filed twice under two names.  
**Prompt:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

---

**The axes**

**Axis one — What has to exist in front of the camera before this move is possible at all?**
Asked first because it is the only question with a bill attached, and because it is the condition this shelf repeats most. Thirty-three conditions say a **place** is missing something: *"No background performers to fill the sweep"* (`sig.atonement_beach_oner`), *"The space is not genuinely symmetrical"* (`sig.one_point_perspective`), *"No room to orbit"* (`sig.circling_standoff`), *"Open ground with nothing to tear past"* (`sig.evil_cam`), *"An open space with no corridor to define it"* (`sig.park_hallway_sidescroll`), *"No real weather, or a tight space"* (`sig.kurosawa_telephoto_weather`), *"Nothing to land on"* (`sig.drop_shot`), *"No staircase, or stairs carry no story meaning"* (`sig.bong_staircase`). Twelve say a **performance** cannot be held: *"Performer cannot hold absolutely still"* (`sig.jaws_beach_dolly_zoom`), *"Choreography not drilled enough for an uncut take"* (`sig.hard_boiled_hospital_oner`), *"The performer is unskilled"* (`sig.wide_body_action_hold`), *"The performer has no silent performance"* (`sig.push_in_on_listener`). Twenty-two name a **piece of kit, a lens or a post process** you must bring and do not have by default: *"No rig to repeat the move exactly"* (`sig.motion_control_repeat`), *"No miniature that matches the real location"* (`sig.aster_dollhouse_pan`), *"The radius cannot tighten smoothly"* (`sig.orbit_to_push`), *"No total location control"* (`sig.russian_ark_take`). The rest are gated by **nothing you do not already own**, and for those the whole decision falls through to axis three.

**Axis two — How much unbroken time does the move own?**
The axis that decides which neighbouring shelf you are about to fall off onto, and it is stated as fact rather than as feeling. Twenty-five conditions turn on a whole scene carried in one block, and they argue with the cut in both directions: *"Cutting serves the scene better"* (`sig.oner`), *"The join will be spotted; an honest cut is better"* (`sig.stitched_oner`), *"A film that cuts; hold one locked frame instead"* (`sig.tarr_long_take`), and pointing the other way, *"An emotional scene needing continuous time"* (`sig.jump_cut_rhythm`). Ten cards do not govern a shot at all but a run of joins, and their conditions name a `cut.` card outright — *"More than two strands, or unrelated events"* (`sig.split_screen_tracking`), *"The POV angle cannot be matched to the watcher"* (`sig.rear_window_pov_chain`). Twenty govern no unit in particular; they are standing treatments laid over everything you shoot, and their own lines say so: *"This is not a shot choice, it is a decision at the level of a whole film"* (`sig.tarr_long_take`), *"either lock it or do not do it"* (`sig.fincher_locked_creep`). Ask this second, because it is what tells you whether you are still choosing a shot.

**Axis three — What must the beat be doing for the move to be earned?**
This is the shelf's second half, and it is a question about the beat, never about whose name is on the card. Eighteen conditions demand a **turn**, something reversing at this exact instant: *"Nothing is actually overturning inside the character"* (`sig.dolly_zoom`), *"The mirror is already visible; no reversal to play"* (`sig.mirror_reveal`), *"The subject is in a position of power"* (`sig.gods_eye_overhead`). Twenty demand a **climb**, pressure rising for the whole length of the shot: *"No escalation at every step"* (`sig.true_detective_oner`), *"The standoff must escalate rather than stall"* (`sig.refn_tableau_push`), *"The music is not building"* (`sig.chazelle_360_spin`). Twenty-two demand a **debt**, something withheld now and paid before the shot ends: *"No payoff worth withholding"* (`sig.reveal_turn`), *"The far side does not earn the entry"* (`sig.lynch_dolly_into_dark`), *"The view beyond is ordinary and will not pay off"* (`sig.drone_ridge_reveal`), *"The detail cannot carry a move that size"* (`sig.sweep`). The rest owe the beat nothing, because the piece has **declared** the camera visible and the move is its voice — and that is the value a realistic piece may not use, which is what *"A straight realistic scene"* (`sig.trombone_push_pull`), *"Documentary realism forbids an impossible path"* (`sig.fincher_object_track`), *"A film that hides its camera"* (`sig.hong_zoom`) and *"Contemporary realistic drama"* (`sig.kurosawa_wipe`) are all saying in four different sentences.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `sig.wide_body_action_hold`, and it is measured rather than asserted.** It is the most-yielded-to card on the shelf, and what matters is that the three edges arriving at it carry three unrelated reasons: `sig.chaos_handheld` sends a reader there when *"The viewer must read the geography"*, `sig.park_hallway_sidescroll` when there is *"An open space with no corridor to define it"*, and `sig.chazelle_whip_montage` on *"A quiet passage the speed would argue with"*. Legibility, geometry and tempo — three different failures, one answer: put the whole body in a wide frame, hold it, and let the performance carry the shot instead of the camera. **Set against it `sig.stitched_oner`, which looks like a rival for the job.** Its inbound edges, from both shapes of `sig.russian_ark_take`, fire on one condition, *the unbroken take cannot be guaranteed*. That is a door out of the shelf, not a fallback, and counting edges instead of counting conditions would have installed it as the default and been wrong.

**The one against it is `sig.ozu_pillow_shot`.** Nothing routes to it, and it is the only card on this shelf whose subject is the absence of the event: no performer, no camera move, no advance. Every other withholding card here still shows you a person and then takes something away — `sig.shallow_follow_blinkered` blurs the room around them, `sig.dardenne_follow` turns the head, `sig.giallo_killer_pov` hides the face — which is a subtraction the audience can feel being performed on them, and resent. The pillow shot never makes the promise, so it never breaks it, and its only stated cost is that it is filler if you choose the wrong object, which is a cost paid in one take of an empty room rather than in a day of the schedule. **Its near cousin against the grain is `sig.hitchcock_object_size`**, which is not a shot at all but a rule applied to every shot in the piece, and which nothing yields to either. *Choosing the best move is not a creative goal*: the default here is the safest frame in the file, and the shelf will keep handing it to you until you ask for something the pair graph was never built to recommend.

**And the shape of that list is the finding, not its length.** Sixty-three of the ninety-two conditions do not point at another card on this shelf at all: seventeen point at `move.`, thirteen at `cut.`, eight at `frame.`, five at `block.`, four at `size.`, three each at `lens.` and at `angle.`, and the remainder at `comp.`, `trans.`, `time.`, `seq.`, `light.` and `ai.` **A signature move's alternative is almost never another signature move — it is the plain move underneath it.** This shelf is a layer, not a family, and that is exactly where it stops. When the honest answer is *just move the camera*, you have left `sig.` and you are on `move.`; when it is *just cut*, you are on `cut.`; when it is *where does the camera stand*, you are on `angle.`; and when it is *what is the camera bolted to*, you are on `rig.` — which receives no edge from this shelf whatsoever, because the only two cards that have a twin there, `sig.snorricam` and `sig.nolan_rotating_set`, carry that twin in a declared sentence inside themselves rather than in a pair field. A boundary stated in prose is a boundary no map can walk.

The second thing the list shows is about money. **Nine of the eleven cards gated by a performance are unasked**, and every edge leaving that region runs downhill in cost or stays level, never uphill: `sig.hard_boiled_hospital_oner` sends you to `sig.chaos_handheld`, both shapes of `sig.russian_ark_take` send you to `sig.stitched_oner`, `sig.cassavetes_handheld` sends you to `size.master`, `sig.touch_of_evil_crane` to `cut.cross`. **The pair graph was written by readers protecting a shoot**, so it routes away from an expensive performance and never toward one. Nothing on this shelf will ever tell you that the shot you have planned is too cheap for the beat, which is the failure axis three exists to catch by hand.

And the card the shelf wants is the one its own subject implies and never writes. Fifty-five of the ninety-six carry a name in their id and most of the rest name a film in an example, yet **exactly one card treats the audience recognising the quotation as content rather than as trivia** — `sig.trunk_shot`, which records the failure in its own `Avoid when:` (*a quotation with no reason inside the story*) with nothing routing a reader there to read it. The shelf names its moves after the people who made them and then never asks whether the viewer is meant to know. The missing card is the one that says *this move is meant to be recognised, and here is what the recognition costs the scene* — and its absence is the reason the second half of this shelf, *is it earned*, keeps being read as a question about the director instead of a question about the beat.

### Dolly Zoom / Vertigo Effect / Contra-Zoom

**Also called:** zolly, trombone shot, Vertigo shot, push-pull zoom, telescoping shot  
**What it is:** The camera pushes forward while the lens zooms wide at a precisely matched rate, or the reverse. The subject holds the same size in frame while the background stretches and pulls away behind them.  
**Effect on the audience:** The ground drops out from under the character while they stand perfectly still. It reads as vertigo and as a reversal of meaning: the same place stops being the same place.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The moment of the shattering news, the discovery of the body, or the line that overturns everything the character believed. Strongest in thriller and psychological horror, and in trailers where you want one image the audience remembers. In heritage documentary it can sit on a monument when you want the viewer to feel the place outgrow their own sense of scale.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Music Video, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Horror, Psychological Horror, Drama, Mystery / Whodunit, Noir / Neo-Noir, Sci-Fi  
**Avoid when:** Usually once per film, because its power depends on surprise; the exception is when the repetition itself is a declared style and it returns as a motif. If nothing is actually overturning inside the character at that beat, the move becomes an empty trick and the audience feels the mechanics.  
**Prompt:** `dolly zoom, camera pushes slowly forward while the lens zooms wide, subject held at constant size in frame, background stretching and warping away behind him, ending locked on his face`  
**Example:** Vertigo, the bell tower stairwell. Goodfellas, Henry and Jimmy at the diner window.  
**Yields to:** `move.dolly_in` — Nothing is actually overturning inside the character.  
`sig.dolly_zoom`

### Reverse Dolly Zoom

**Also called:** reverse contra-zoom, compression zolly  
**What it is:** The same principle run backwards: the camera retreats while the lens zooms in. The subject stays the same size and the background compresses and closes in around them.  
**Effect on the audience:** The world folds in on the character instead of falling away. It reads as entrapment, as a closing wall, as the exits quietly disappearing.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A panic attack, the moment a character realises they are surrounded, or the beat where the truth closes in on someone trying to run. Strong in psychological horror, in collapse-driven drama, and in a trailer on the last line before the cut to black.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Trailer / Teaser, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Psychological Horror, Horror, Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Tragedy, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Noir / Neo-Noir  
**Avoid when:** Do not pair it with a standard dolly zoom in the same scene; the two meanings cancel each other. If the background is empty there is nothing to compress and the move simply does not read.  
**Prompt:** `reverse dolly zoom, camera pulls back while the lens zooms in, subject size held constant, background compressing and crowding inward around her, settling on a tight frame`  
**Example:** Poltergeist, the hallway stretching away from the mother.  
**Yields to:** `cut.tighten` — Background too empty to compress.  
`sig.reverse_dolly_zoom`

### The Seated Dolly Zoom / Jaws Beach Shot

**Also called:** sitting vertigo shot, reaction zolly  
**What it is:** A very short dolly zoom on a seated or motionless figure watching something off frame. The move starts and finishes inside two seconds and the subject never moves at all.  
**Effect on the audience:** It puts us inside the head of someone at the instant their worst guess is confirmed. The body freezes and the world behind it is what distorts.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The reaction shot in horror and thriller: the father sees the attack, the witness sees the weapon, the mother sees her son where he should not be. Best in features and series, and it survives vertical micro-drama because the distortion reads even on a small screen.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Vertical Micro-Drama, Short Film, Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Horror, Thriller / Suspense, Survival, Drama, Disaster, Monster / Kaiju, Crime / Procedural  
**Avoid when:** If the subject moves or turns during the move the whole illusion collapses. Do not stretch it either; length turns a shock into contemplation. And do not repeat it through the coverage: it is a dolly zoom, and `sig.dolly_zoom` holds it to once a film unless the return is a declared motif.  
**Prompt:** `fast dolly zoom on a seated man facing camera, he stays frozen, the beach and crowd behind him stretching violently away, holding on his stunned face`  
**Example:** Jaws, Brody on the beach as the attack starts.  
**Yields to:** `frame.reaction_shot` — Performer cannot hold absolutely still.  
`sig.jaws_beach_dolly_zoom`

### Repeated Push and Pull / Trombone Move

**Also called:** in-and-out move, breathing push  
**What it is:** The camera pushes in and retreats more than once inside a single uncut shot. The trombone name is also used by some people for the dolly zoom, so be clear which one you mean.  
**Effect on the audience:** An unstable rhythm, like disturbed breathing. The viewer never gets a resting frame, which lands as unease or as comedy depending on the speed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Hallucination, dream logic and drunkenness in psychological drama, and the beat of a character who cannot commit in comedy. In music video and vertical short-form it works as a purely rhythmic move locked to the beat rather than a psychological one.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Music Video, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Experimental / Art Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Surreal / Absurdist, Psychological / Arthouse, Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Psychological Horror, Drama  
**Avoid when:** In a straight realistic scene it reads as an accidental joke. Usually one shot per scene, because repetition turns into noise; the exception is when that repeated rhythm is the declared style of the piece, as in music video.  
**Prompt:** `camera pushes in toward her face then retreats, repeating twice within the shot, unsteady rhythm, ending pushed in close`  
**Yields to:** `move.breathing` — A straight realistic scene.  
`sig.trombone_push_pull`

### Over-the-Shoulder Push

**Also called:** OTS push-in, shoulder push, tightening OTS  
**What it is:** An over-the-shoulder framing combined with a slow dolly in toward the far face. The foreground shoulder either stays in frame or drifts out of it as the push completes.  
**Effect on the audience:** The strongest confrontation framing there is. The viewer advances with the speaker toward the opponent, so the pressure is felt physically rather than understood intellectually.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Interrogation, a negotiation that is falling apart, a confession, the I know what you did scene. The killer variant is to let the foreground shoulder slide out of frame as the push lands, so you open on a relational frame and end on a clean single: the relationship broke during the sentence itself.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Vertical Micro-Drama, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Legal Drama, Gangster, Political Thriller, Mystery / Whodunit, Noir / Neo-Noir  
**Avoid when:** In a warm or gently romantic scene it feels aggressive for no reason. Do not do it on both characters in the same scene either; the meaning cancels and the scene turns into a pushing contest.  
**Prompt:** `over-the-shoulder push-in, slow dolly forward past the foreground shoulder, tightening on his face, the foreground shoulder drifting out of frame at the end`  
**Yields to:** `frame.ots` — A warm or romantic scene the push would bully.  
`sig.ots_push`

### The Oner / Long Take

**Also called:** single take, unbroken take, plan-sequence  
**What it is:** A complete scene carried in one uncut shot, with the camera taking on all the movement and reframing itself. Shot size changes through movement and blocking rather than through editing.  
**Effect on the audience:** Real time. There is no escape and no editorial rescue, so tension accumulates because the viewer knows no relief is coming.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The long tension scene, an entry into unknown territory, a battle, a party, a walk through a hospital or a hotel. In a series it is the shot the episode gets talked about for, and in heritage documentary it lets a site read as one connected real place rather than a set of pictures.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Anime (Japanese 2D), Music Video | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Drama, War, Crime / Procedural, Action, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Verite / Observational  
**Avoid when:** With no internal design the oner is just a long dead shot. Do not do one to prove you can either; if cutting serves the scene better, cut.  
**Prompt:** `continuous single take, no cuts, the camera negotiates the whole space with the character, reframing from wide to close by movement alone, settling on her face at the end`  
**Yields to:** `frame.coverage_triangle` — Cutting serves the scene better.  
`sig.oner`

### The Designed Oner

**Also called:** blocked long take, staged-in-depth oner  
**What it is:** A oner built out of three or four mini scenes inside it: characters enter and exit frame, information arrives in sequence, and shot size changes through staging in depth rather than through cuts. Spielberg made it a core working method.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer feels life unfolding in front of them with no interference, while in fact being led precisely through each beat in the exact order intended.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The rich establishing scene, the family around a table, an opening that introduces a place and its people at once. Excellent in family drama, period work and docudrama because it delivers a whole coherent world in a single shot.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Docudrama / Reenactment, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation | genres: Drama, Family, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Coming of Age, War, Slice of Life, Tragedy  
**Avoid when:** With no real depth in the location, a wall right behind the actors, there is nowhere to stage. Do not load it with more than three or four beats either, or it becomes a skills demonstration.  
**Prompt:** `continuous take, characters entering and exiting frame at different depths, the camera reframing from wide to medium to close by movement alone, ending on the boy in the foreground`  
**Yields to:** `block.lateral_staging` — The location has no depth to stage in.  
`sig.designed_oner`

### The Stitched Oner / Invisible-Cut Oner

**Also called:** faux oner, hidden-cut long take, fake single take  
**What it is:** A scene or a whole film that appears to be one shot but is assembled from many, the joins hidden behind a passing body, a patch of darkness, a hard whip around a corner or a digital stitch.  
**Effect on the audience:** It delivers the continuous-time feeling of a true oner with total design freedom: you can cross hours and distances without ever appearing to breathe.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Ambitious openings, the one-shot episode, and short-form pieces that cross locations with no visible cut. It is also the practical way to build a oner out of short AI clips: generate each segment and stitch on a body crossing the lens or on a plunge into darkness.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video, Trailer / Teaser, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, AI-Generated Clip, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Action, War, Crime / Procedural, Psychological / Arthouse, Comedy, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** A join that gets spotted is worse than an honest cut, because the audience starts hunting for the next one. Hiding the join in motion or in darkness is the safe choice, since a calm frame exposes it; the exception is when you want the joins noticed and the seams become part of the style.  
**Prompt:** `continuous flowing take, a figure crosses the lens filling the frame completely, and on the other side we are already inside the new room, the move never stopping`  
**Example:** Rope. Birdman. 1917.  
**Yields to:** `cut.hard` — The join will be spotted; an honest cut is better.  
`sig.stitched_oner`

### The Feature-Length Real Single Take

**Also called:** true single-shot feature, unbroken feature, run-and-gun oner, live single take, the real-time city single take (this card was two until September 2026; the second one's id was sig.victoria_real_take)
**What it is:** An entire feature carried in one genuinely unbroken shot, no cuts and no stitching. It takes two shapes. **Through one great space:** a Steadicam or gimbal moving through dozens of locations and performers - Russian Ark ran the full ninety-odd minutes inside the Hermitage. **Through a moving city:** a take that keeps moving through changing real locations, streets, cars and buildings, shot handheld or on a gimbal with disciplined improvisation - Victoria ran over two hours in one real take through Berlin.
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer becomes a ghost walking the space, unable to blink or leave. Time stops belonging to the film and starts being shared with it. On the moving-city shape, real jeopardy transmits to the viewer: the sense that anything could go wrong and the film would keep running is itself dramatic content.
**Used for and where it works best:** An experimental or heritage project inside one great space: a museum, a palace, a temple, a historic building. It works when the building itself is the subject, because every step of the camera becomes a tour rather than a transition. Through a city it serves any genre built on unbroken time: a single-night story, a crime that escalates, a chase that turns into a disaster, the long night, the heist coming apart, the encounter that will not end.
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Experimental / Art Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, 360 / VR, Short Film, Vertical Micro-Drama, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | genres: Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Surreal / Absurdist, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Heist, Coming of Age, Romance, Survival
**Avoid when:** Without punishing rehearsal and total control of the location it is not practical, and it needs performers who can carry hours with no safety net. Do not attempt it when the story needs time jumps either; this form refuses fractured time. If the story depends on word-perfect scripted precision, this form will fight you the whole way.
**Prompt:** `one unbroken continuous take, the camera drifts like a ghost from hall to hall through a vast palace, never stopping, passing among costumed crowds` - or, through a city: `unbroken handheld take following two people out of a nightclub, down the street, into a car and away, no cuts, the camera staying with them the entire time`
**Example:** Russian Ark, for one great space; Victoria, for a moving city.
**Yields to:** `sig.stitched_oner` — No total location control, the story needs time jumps, or word-perfect scripted precision is required.
`sig.russian_ark_take`

### The Showpiece Opening Oner

**Also called:** bravura opening take, prologue oner  
**What it is:** A long opening that appears to be one shot, threading through dozens of people while introducing a character, an event and a location at once. De Palma built one of these for the arena opening of Snake Eyes.  
**Effect on the audience:** It declares the film's method in the first minute: this is a crowded world and we are entering it alongside him without a break.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The opening of a film or an episode when you need to introduce a fast-talking character inside a crowd: a casino, an arena, a market, a wedding, a temple courtyard. Very strong in crime and thriller, and in premium advertising when one shot has to establish the size of the event.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Music Video, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Noir / Neo-Noir, Gangster, Action, Mystery / Whodunit, Sports Drama  
**Avoid when:** If the character has no energy to carry the move, the shot exposes the emptiness. Do not open with one if the rest of the film is quiet; it becomes a promise you never keep.  
**Prompt:** `long continuous take through a packed arena, the camera walking backward ahead of a talking man, weaving past crowds and vendors, ending as he steps into the ringside seat`  
**Example:** Snake Eyes, the arena prologue.  
**Yields to:** `sig.designed_oner` — The film that follows is quiet.  
`sig.snake_eyes_opening`

### The Epic Sweep Oner

**Also called:** tableau oner, panoramic long take  
**What it is:** A long Steadicam take that walks through a square or a field dense with detail and background action, moving from tableau to tableau without stopping. Atonement did it across the Dunkirk beach in about five minutes.  
**Effect on the audience:** A catastrophe seen as a landscape rather than as an event. The viewer is not hit by one image; they are slowly saturated until the scale registers.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A battlefield after the collapse, a refugee camp, an ancient city market, a temple courtyard on a feast day. Excellent in war and period work, and in heritage documentary when one move has to present a whole civilisation.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Docudrama / Reenactment, Music Video, Trailer / Teaser, 3D CG Animation | genres: War, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Drama, Tragedy, Disaster, Mythology / Ancient Epic  
**Avoid when:** It needs real background performers and designed action, without them it is a tour of an empty space. Do not use it for an intimate two-hander either; the scale swallows the intimacy.  
**Prompt:** `continuous Steadicam take walking through a vast beach camp, passing soldiers, horses and a wrecked ferris wheel, the camera never stopping, ending on a wide of the shoreline`  
**Example:** Atonement, the Dunkirk beach sequence.  
**Yields to:** `sig.villeneuve_drift` — No background performers to fill the sweep.  
`sig.atonement_beach_oner`

### The Corridor Combat Oner

**Also called:** action long take, sustained gunfight take  
**What it is:** An action oner that stays with the fighters along a corridor or a floor, the camera trailing, retreating and turning with them while the fight continues uncut. Hard Boiled ran one through a hospital with a hidden join inside the lift.  
**Effect on the audience:** The action becomes physical and exhausting instead of chopped and comfortable. The viewer tires alongside the hero because no cut ever gives them a rest.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The action climax of a feature, or an episode-ending set piece. Strong in action and martial arts and in game cinematics, and a ten second excerpt with no cut makes an excellent vertical hook.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Short Film, Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Anime (Japanese 2D) | genres: Action, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Crime / Procedural, War, Thriller / Suspense, Spy / Espionage, Survival  
**Avoid when:** Without fully rehearsed choreography it reads as meaningless chaos. Do not attempt it for a short fight either; a long take needs internal escalation.  
**Prompt:** `continuous take following two shooters down a hospital corridor, the camera retreating ahead of them then swinging behind, gunfire and falling debris, no cuts, ending on the lift doors`  
**Example:** Hard Boiled, the hospital sequence.  
**Yields to:** `sig.chaos_handheld` — Choreography not drilled enough for an uncut take.  
`sig.hard_boiled_hospital_oner`

### The Raid Oner

**Also called:** extraction long take, six-minute oner  
**What it is:** A oner that stays with one character through a raid coming apart: into a house, across yards and fences, out into the street, everything getting worse without a cut. True Detective built a six minute version of it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Sustained danger with no way out. Because there is no cut, the viewer counts time the way the character does and feels the exit getting further away.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The climax of a crime series episode, an operation collapsing, an escape from a sealed location. This is the form that turns one television episode into an event, so save it for a beat that deserves it.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Feature Film, Short Film, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Trailer / Teaser | genres: Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Action, True Crime, Drama, Political Thriller, Spy / Espionage  
**Avoid when:** Without escalating danger at every step it is just a long walk. It works best when the character leaves the shot in a different state from the one they entered in, because length without change buys nothing; the exception is when the inability to change anything is the point of the scene.  
**Prompt:** `continuous handheld take following a man through a house, over back fences and across yards under gunfire, ending as he pulls a hostage into a waiting car`  
**Example:** True Detective season one, the Who Goes There raid.  
**Yields to:** `seq.escape_sequence` — No escalation at every step; build it in cut scenes.  
`sig.true_detective_oner`

### The Unbroken Car Shot

**Also called:** in-car oner, 360 car rig take  
**What it is:** The camera stays inside a moving car in one shot, rotating a full 360 degrees among the passengers while the event unfolds both inside the vehicle and outside it. Cuarón built a dedicated rig to make it possible.  
**Effect on the audience:** Confinement. The viewer is riding with them and cannot get out, so anything happening outside the windows lands harder because they cannot reach it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** An ambush, an escape, or a conversation that turns into a catastrophe mid-journey. Excellent in road movies, dystopia and crime, and it suits vertical micro-drama because a car interior is a natural vertical frame.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Vertical Micro-Drama, Short Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Road Movie, Drama, Action, Survival  
**Avoid when:** Without a rig that allows the rotation it collapses into a series of locked-off angles. Do not use it for a calm conversation with no event either; the motion has no reason to exist.  
**Prompt:** `continuous shot inside a moving car, the camera rotating between the passengers and out through the windscreen, an attack developing outside, no cuts, ending on the driver's face`  
**Example:** Children of Men, the car ambush.  
**Yields to:** `sig.kiarostami_car_window` — A calm car conversation with no event.  
`sig.cuaron_car_oner`

### The Digitally Built Long Take

**Also called:** CG-assisted oner, virtual camera long take  
**What it is:** A long take whose path is built partly or entirely in the computer, so the camera crosses physically impossible distances and positions and can even travel inside a helmet or a body. Gravity opened with roughly thirteen minutes built this way.  
**Effect on the audience:** Total freedom of movement combined with continuous time. The viewer has no reference for the size of the space and loses their sense of scale completely.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Science fiction and space, openings that introduce a world with no edges, and any beat where you need to travel from a vast exterior into an eye or a helmet without cutting. Very useful in game cinematics and 3D animation.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Trailer / Teaser, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, 360 / VR, AI-Generated Clip, Anime (Japanese 2D) | genres: Sci-Fi, Space Opera, Survival, Disaster, Fantasy / Epic, Adventure, Thriller / Suspense  
**Avoid when:** If the path does not serve a story, the freedom becomes a cold technical demo. Do not break the physics of your own world without a reason inside a realistic story.  
**Prompt:** `continuous take drifting from a wide of the earth toward a tiny figure, rotating with her as she tumbles, pushing in through the visor into her point of view, no cuts`  
**Example:** Gravity, the opening sequence.  
**Yields to:** `sig.oner` — A realistic world whose physics must not break.

### The Single-Take Fight Round

**Also called:** one-shot round, unbroken bout  
**What it is:** A complete round of a bout or a match shot in one take, the camera inside the ring or the field, circling and retreating with the fighters. Creed shot a full round this way.  
**Effect on the audience:** A sporting truth that editing cannot deliver. The viewer feels the body tiring because not one second of time has been removed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The climax of a sports drama or a duel in a period or samurai piece. Use it once per film on the round the audience has to feel on their skin, and cut the rest conventionally so the difference registers.

**Avoid when:** Without performers drilled on the full choreography every pulled punch is exposed. Do not do it on a round with no turn in it; a long take needs a breaking point.  
**Prompt:** `continuous take inside a boxing ring, the camera circling between the two fighters at head height, ducking and retreating with them, no cuts, ending in the corner as the bell rings`

### The POV Prologue Oner

**Also called:** killer POV long take, subjective opening take  
**What it is:** A complete prologue in one take from the point of view of someone we never see: the camera is their eye, walking, entering, opening doors, glimpsing their own hands. Halloween opened with an unbroken subjective take that ends by revealing whose eyes they were.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer is locked inside a stranger and made complicit in what they do. The reveal at the end turns the guilt back on the audience.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The horror or slasher prologue, the stalking beat before the first victim, and any scene where you must hide the identity of the doer without hiding the deed. It travels well into vertical short-form and games because subjective point of view is native to that audience.

**Avoid when:** Held too long without escalation it becomes a boring walk. Do not use it after the identity is known either; it loses its entire function.  
**Prompt:** `continuous point of view take, the camera is the character's eyes, walking around the outside of a house, picking up a knife, climbing the stairs, no cuts`

### The Back-Entrance Steadicam Entrance

**Also called:** Copacabana shot, the long entrance take  
**What it is:** A Steadicam follows two people in from the street, through a service door, along corridors and a kitchen, into a packed room and settles at a table set for them. Scorsese built one of the most famous versions of this shot.  
**Effect on the audience:** Power is explained by distance rather than by dialogue. Every door that opens and every worker who steps aside proves the man's standing without a single line.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Introducing a powerful character through the eyes of someone dazzled by them, and the date or showing-off scene. Excellent in gangster films, in luxury advertising for a hotel, a restaurant or a store, and in music video.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Fashion Film | genres: Gangster, Crime / Procedural, Drama, Romance, Musical, Comedy, Noir / Neo-Noir  
**Avoid when:** If the spaces you pass through say nothing about the character, it is a long trip with no information. It should normally end somewhere stronger than where it began, because the destination is what justifies the journey; the exception is when the fall itself is the subject and you land deliberately somewhere poorer.  
**Prompt:** `continuous Steadicam take following a couple from a street door, down a service corridor and through a busy kitchen, out into a crowded nightclub, settling as a table is carried in and placed for them`  
**Example:** Goodfellas, the Copacabana entrance.  
**Yields to:** `block.crowd_parts` — The spaces passed through say nothing about him.

### The Party Steadicam Follow

**Also called:** hand-off long take, baton-pass oner  
**What it is:** A Steadicam moves through a party or a crowded space, following one person then handing off to another who crosses frame, then to a third, all in one shot. Paul Thomas Anderson made it a signature of his openings.  
**Effect on the audience:** The location becomes a network of relationships rather than a set of individuals. The viewer learns who knows who, who is the centre of the night and who is at its edge, with no exposition.  
**Used for and where it works best:** An opening that introduces a whole family, crew or organisation, a wedding, the backstage before a show. Excellent in ensemble drama, in documentary and in wedding films because it gives every person their moment.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Wedding / Event Film, Documentary Feature, Music Video, Concert / Live Event, Commercial / TVC, Short Film | genres: Drama, Comedy, Musical, Gangster, Family, Romance, Slice of Life, Coming of Age  
**Avoid when:** If the characters have no relationship the hand-offs feel random. It is usually better not to hand off to someone we will never see again, because the hand-off promises that this person matters; the exception is when you mean to show the place as a passing crowd rather than a network.  
**Prompt:** `continuous Steadicam take through a crowded party, following one guest until another crosses frame and the camera takes them instead, then a third, finally settling on the host by the pool`  
**Example:** Boogie Nights, the opening club sequence.  
**Yields to:** `sig.altman_probing_zoom` — The people have no relationship to hand off.  
`sig.pta_party_steadicam`

### One-Point Perspective Tracking

**Also called:** symmetrical corridor track, vanishing-point dolly, Kubrick corridor  
**What it is:** The camera travels down a corridor or a symmetrical space with the vanishing point pinned to the exact centre of frame for the whole shot. Kubrick made it his known signature.  
**Effect on the audience:** Cold geometry and an inhuman order. The space reads as designed by a mind rather than built by accident, and the viewer feels they are inside a system rather than a building.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Corridors, temples, palaces, institutions and hospitals. Best in psychological horror and science fiction, and in heritage documentary when you walk a colonnade or a temple passage and want the viewer to feel the mind behind the architecture.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Psychological Horror, Horror, Sci-Fi, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Mystery / Whodunit, Drama, Seinen (mature anime)  
**Avoid when:** If the space is not genuinely symmetrical the shot reads as a technical error rather than a style. Do not use it in a warm domestic scene; the symmetry chills everything it touches.  
**Prompt:** `one-point perspective tracking shot gliding down a long symmetrical corridor, vanishing point locked dead centre, steady constant speed, ending as a door fills the centre of frame`  
**Example:** The Shining, the hotel corridors.  
**Yields to:** `move.dolly_through` — The space is not genuinely symmetrical.

### The Reverse Tracking Lead

**Also called:** leading Steadicam, backward tracking shot, retreat-ahead move  
**What it is:** The camera retreats backwards ahead of the subject at their exact pace, so the face holds steady in frame while the world drains away behind them. Kubrick built entire inspection and marching scenes on it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Unstoppable intent. The viewer backs away in front of the character the way an opponent would, and feels that the advance is aimed at them personally.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A commander entering, the walk before the confrontation, an inspection or a dressing-down, the no going back beat. Excellent in war and drama, and in sports and premium advertising when the subject should feel like they are coming out of the screen at the viewer.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Commercial / TVC, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | genres: War, Drama, Action, Thriller / Suspense, Sports Drama, Political Thriller, Historical / Biopic, Shonen Battle (anime)  
**Avoid when:** If the character has nowhere they are going, the move has no engine. Do not use it when you want the viewer to fear what lies ahead of the character either; that belongs to the trailing shot.  
**Prompt:** `reverse tracking shot, the camera retreating backwards ahead of the officer as he walks the line, his face filling the centre of frame, the barracks sliding away behind him`  
**Example:** Full Metal Jacket, the drill instructor walking the line.  
**Yields to:** `move.trail_track` — You want dread of what lies ahead, not intent.  
`sig.reverse_track_lead`

### Symmetrical Snap Pan

**Also called:** Wes Anderson whip pan, 90-degree snap, centred whip  
**What it is:** A perfectly centred symmetrical composition, then a fast decisive ninety degree pan, landing on another perfectly centred symmetrical composition. Wes Anderson turned it into a complete language.  
**Effect on the audience:** Comedy that comes from precision itself. The world reads as obsessively constructed and every turn feels like a page turning in an illustrated book.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Comedy, kids content, playful advertising, and any run of successive introductions, one character then the next, one object then the next. Very strong in vertical short-form because the decisive move holds attention and builds rhythm against the music.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Commercial / TVC, Kids Content, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Short Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Music Video, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Product Video / Motion Design | genres: Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Family, Rom-Com, Fairy Tale, Adventure, Preschool / Early Learning, Drama  
**Avoid when:** If the composition is not precisely centred the move loses its whole meaning and becomes an ordinary fast pan. Do not use it in a tragic scene; the precision starts mocking the pain.  
**Prompt:** `perfectly centred symmetrical composition, fast snap pan ninety degrees right, coming to a hard stop on a second perfectly centred symmetrical composition`  
**Example:** The Grand Budapest Hotel.  
**Yields to:** `move.whip_pan` — Composition cannot be precisely centred.  
`sig.wes_snap_pan`

### Planimetric Lateral Dolly

**Also called:** planimetric staging, flat-on tracking, frontal lateral move  
**What it is:** Everything in frame sits parallel to the camera plane, walls, faces and furniture all square on at ninety degrees, and the camera tracks sideways in a straight line parallel to the wall. Anderson and Kubrick are its best known users.  
**Effect on the audience:** The world flattens into a puppet theatre or a page. The feeling is knowingly artificial: an ordered world, and sometimes a trapped one.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A successive presentation of rooms, people or products: a cutaway house, a run of shelves, a line of soldiers. Excellent in advertising, kids content and animation, and it works vertically if you let elements enter from the side of frame.  
**Best in:** formats: Commercial / TVC, Kids Content, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), 3D CG Animation, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Product Video / Motion Design, Feature Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Stop Motion | genres: Comedy, Family, Fairy Tale, Preschool / Early Learning, Dark Comedy / Satire, Drama, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** The form kills depth on purpose, so do not use it when you want distance or realism. And if the angle is not exactly square it looks like a mistake rather than a style.  
**Prompt:** `planimetric lateral dolly moving left, everything square to the camera at ninety degrees, wall and doorways sliding past in a straight flat line, stopping centred on the last door`  
**Yields to:** `move.truck` — You want depth and realism, not a flat plane.  
`sig.planimetric_dolly`

### The Flat-Lay Overhead Insert

**Also called:** top-down object insert, knolling shot, God's-eye insert  
**What it is:** A perfectly vertical camera looking straight down at a surface, with objects or hands arranged in precise parallel inside the frame. Anderson uses it as a parenthetical sentence inside his scenes.  
**Effect on the audience:** It stops time and delivers clean unemotional information. The ordered layout makes the object read as deliberate and important.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Showing the contents of a bag, a pocket or a box, a step in a recipe, or a product with its components. A staple of advertising, product video and instructional short-form, and in heritage documentary for laying out artefacts in order.

**Avoid when:** If the arrangement is careless the whole idea collapses, because order is the point of this shot. Do not hold it long either; it is a parenthesis, not a scene.  
**Prompt:** `perfectly vertical overhead insert looking straight down at a table, hands entering frame to place objects in a precise row, static camera, even soft light`  
**Yields to:** `size.insert` — The objects cannot be arranged with real order.  
`sig.flat_lay_overhead_insert`

### The Locked Creep

**Also called:** motion-control creep, machine push, clinical push-in  
**What it is:** A camera that looks completely locked but carries a very slow push or pan of mathematical precision with no handheld shake at all. Fincher is known for it as a standing method.  
**Effect on the audience:** Cold observation. The image feels like a machine eye recording, which makes the character feel examined rather than sympathised with.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Interrogation, the case room, an office at night, any scene where the viewer should observe rather than be drawn in. Best in crime, psychological thriller, serious documentary and premium corporate work.

**Avoid when:** If the scene needs warm empathy, the coldness pushes the viewer away. Any trace of handheld shake destroys the signature entirely, so either lock it or do not do it.  
**Prompt:** `motion-control precision, imperceptibly slow push-in on the seated man, absolutely no handheld shake, ending on a tight clean frame of his hands on the table`  
**Example:** Zodiac.  
**Yields to:** `sig.breathing_handheld` — The scene needs warmth and human presence.  
`sig.fincher_locked_creep`

### The Object-Tracked Invisible Camera

**Also called:** impossible camera move, threaded camera, object-path move  
**What it is:** The camera follows the path of a small object or threads a tight gap: through the handle of a coffee pot, through a keyhole, between a stair rail and a step, and carries on into the next room. It is built by blending live plates with digital sections.  
**Effect on the audience:** The camera loses its body. The viewer senses that this is not a human eye, which generates quiet anxiety inside an otherwise calm scene.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A sealed house with a threat inside it, explaining the geography of a location in one move, linking two floors or two rooms with no cut. Strong in thriller, in advertising where the camera travels through a product, and in games.

**Avoid when:** With no narrative reason it is a technical showpiece. Do not use it in a film claiming documentary realism either; the move announces itself as impossible.  
**Prompt:** `continuous camera move descending a staircase, threading through the gap in the banister, passing through the handle of a coffee pot on the table, continuing into the next room`

**Yields to:** `move.dolly_through` — Documentary realism forbids an impossible path.  
`sig.fincher_object_track`

### The Static Long Hold

**Also called:** fixed long take, locked wide hold, observational static  
**What it is:** A wide or medium frame locked off with no camera movement at all, held for a minute or more while the entire event plays out inside it. Haneke built his method on it.  
**Effect on the audience:** The camera becomes a witness that refuses to intervene or to guide. The viewer becomes responsible for where to look, and that responsibility is itself uncomfortable.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A violent scene you refuse to glamourise, a heavy stretch of waiting, a full family argument in one frame. Best in arthouse drama and psychological horror, and in observational documentary when the event should breathe without interference.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Experimental / Art Film, Drama Series Episode, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Limited Series | genres: Psychological / Arthouse, Drama, Tragedy, Psychological Horror, Verite / Observational, Thriller / Suspense, Slice of Life, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** If nothing develops inside the frame, the shot is emptiness rather than contemplation. Do not use it in fast short-form either; a vertical audience will scroll past a minute-long hold that waits for its event — a short stillness whose composed image carries on its own is `sig.refn_tableau_push`, a different shot.  
**Prompt:** `locked static wide shot, no camera movement at all, held for a long time while the argument plays out in the far corner of the room, natural sound only`  
`sig.static_long_take_hold`

### The Locked Candlelit Static

**Also called:** period natural-light static, firelight tableau  
**What it is:** A locked frame or an extremely slow move lit only by period sources, candle, lantern, fire or window, with no obvious modern lighting. Eggers works this way across his period films.  
**Effect on the audience:** The past becomes tangible rather than theatrical. The darkness is real, so the viewer feels the cold and the fear people actually lived in.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Period work, folk horror, and night scenes in heritage documentary and reenactment. Ideal for any evening scene in an ancient civilisation: an oil lamp, an altar, a scribe's room at the end of the night.

**Avoid when:** Add even a little modern light that reads as modern — a second direction, a colour no flame makes — and the whole truth of it goes; a hidden unit matched to the flame in colour and direction, as `light.candlelight` allows for a wider shot, is not that. Do not use it for a scene that needs full clarity of detail; this form conceals more than it reveals.  
**Prompt:** `locked static shot lit only by a single candle on the table, deep shadow filling the rest of the frame, the flame flickering, no camera movement`

`sig.eggers_candlelit_static`

### The Neon Tableau with a Slow Push

**Also called:** static neon frame, symmetrical neon push  
**What it is:** A static symmetrical composition in saturated neon, the figure standing motionless, the camera either locked or pushing very slowly forward. Refn built an entire style on it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Time stretches and the threat becomes stylistic rather than physical. The viewer waits for something to happen, and the waiting is the event.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The silence before violence, a wordless standoff, an entrance into a place with its own rules. Very strong in music video, fashion advertising and neo-noir, and it works in vertical short-form because the image alone carries without an event.

**Avoid when:** In a scene that must deliver information quickly, the stillness stalls the story. Repeat it too often in one piece and it turns into a moving poster.

### Centre-Punch Framing

**Also called:** centre framing, eye-line centre composition, cut-friendly centring  
**What it is:** Every shot is composed so the point of interest sits dead centre, even in a wide chaotic frame. Miller applied it rigorously so that he could cut at very high speed.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer's eye never has to search between shots, so you can cut every half second and the scene stays legible. Speed without confusion.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Fast action and chases, short-form advertising cutting, and vertical edits cut to the beat. It is a shooting rule before it is an editing rule: frame centre if you already know you will cut fast.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Commercial / TVC, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Trailer / Teaser, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), Sports Broadcast / Sports Film | genres: Action, Adventure, Thriller / Suspense, War, Western, Superhero, Shonen Battle (anime), Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic  
**Avoid when:** In calm scenes centre framing kills composition and goes flat — this card's centring, which serves the cut rather than the frame; a centred frame composed for its own symmetry and held is `sig.refn_tableau_push`, and that one is built for the calm. Do not apply it to a scene you intend to cut slowly either; you give up composition and get nothing back.  
**Prompt:** `wide action shot with the point of interest composed dead centre of frame, the chaos of vehicles and dust arranged around it, hard cut-ready framing`  
**Example:** Mad Max Fury Road.  
**Yields to:** `comp.rule_of_thirds` — A calm scene cut slowly.  
`sig.center_punch_framing`

### The Double Dolly / Floating Dolly Shot

**Also called:** Spike Lee shot, dual dolly, glide shot  
**What it is:** The performer stands on one dolly and the camera rides a second one, and both travel together. The result is a person gliding through space without their legs moving at all.  
**Effect on the audience:** The character detaches from the world and becomes a state of mind rather than a body in a place. The viewer feels the ground pulling her rather than seeing her walk.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat of shock, dream or dissociation: hearing of a death, walking away after a loss, entering an altered state. Excellent in drama and music video, and in advertising for introducing a character in a deliberately unreal way.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Fashion Film, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Psychological / Arthouse, Surreal / Absurdist, Romance, Crime / Procedural, Coming of Age, Melodrama  
**Avoid when:** Used in an ordinary realistic scene it reads as a technical error. Once or twice per piece at most, because it announces itself loudly.  
**Prompt:** `the man glides forward through the street without walking, his body still and centred, the background moving past him, dreamlike floating motion`  
**Example:** Malcolm X, the walk to the ballroom.  
**Yields to:** `sig.shallow_follow_blinkered` — A realistic scene where a glide reads as an error.  
`sig.double_dolly`

### The Low Hero Orbit

**Also called:** 360 hero circle, low-angle orbit, heroic turnaround  
**What it is:** A low camera circles a person or a group through a full or near-full revolution, usually with a sunset, smoke or rotor wash behind them. Bay made it the mark of his style.  
**Effect on the audience:** Pure glorification. The subject becomes the centre of the world because the world literally revolves around them and the sky opens behind them.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The hero beat, the victory, the team squaring up before the mission, and the product turn in sports or automotive advertising. Very strong in trailers and vertical short-form because the rotation holds the eye even with sound off.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, Feature Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Product Video / Motion Design | genres: Action, Superhero, War, Sports Drama, Adventure, Sci-Fi, Disaster, Shonen Battle (anime)  
**Avoid when:** It is now one of the most worn out cliches, so only use it when the character has genuinely earned the glorification. If the background is empty the rotation gives no sense of motion at all.  
**Prompt:** `low angle camera orbiting the standing hero a full 360 degrees, sunset behind him, dust and smoke drifting, he stays centred and still, ending as he looks into lens`  
**Yields to:** `angle.hero_low` — An empty background where the orbit cannot read.  
`sig.bay_hero_orbit`

### The Speed Ramp Hit

**Also called:** ramping, slow-fast-slow, time ramp  
**What it is:** The speed changes inside the shot itself: slow, then a sudden burst at the moment of impact, then slow again. Snyder popularised it as a complete fight language.  
**Effect on the audience:** The blow registers twice: once as slow anticipation and once as impact. The body feels the gap between the two speeds more than it feels the hit.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every decisive blow in an action scene, the goal in sports, any drop in a music video or a vertical edit. Excellent in trailers because it gives you a visual hit in a single second.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Commercial / TVC, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video, Feature Film, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Anime (Japanese 2D) | genres: Action, Superhero, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Sports Drama, Fantasy / Epic, War, Shonen Battle (anime), Mecha (anime)  
**Avoid when:** If every shot ramps there is no normal speed to measure against and the idea dies. Do not use it in a dialogue drama scene either; it feels like a commercial.  
**Prompt:** `slow motion as the fist starts to travel, snapping to real speed at the exact moment of impact, then dropping back to slow motion as the body falls`  
**Yields to:** `cut.on_impact` — Everything ramps, so no normal speed remains.  
`sig.speed_ramp_hit`

### The Mundane Action Crash-Zoom Montage

**Also called:** Edgar Wright montage, kinetic routine cut, comic zoom montage  
**What it is:** A run of very short shots of trivial daily actions, opening a door, pouring coffee, pulling on a coat, each one carrying a crash zoom or a whip pan and a sharp sound effect. Edgar Wright built it as a signature.  
**Effect on the audience:** Comedy out of disproportion: action-movie energy attached to things that do not deserve it. It also communicates mechanical routine.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Showing a character's whole day in ten seconds, passing time, preparation before a mission, or a step by step explanation. One of the best structures for vertical short-form and short advertising because it delivers rhythm, sound design and information at once.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Commercial / TVC, Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Meme / Comedy Edit, Product Video / Motion Design, Explainer, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Music Video | genres: Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Action, Rom-Com, Crime / Procedural, Heist, Slice of Life, Adventure  
**Avoid when:** Without a sharp sound design hit on every shot it is just fast cutting with no joke. Do not use it in an emotional scene either; the speed cancels feeling.  
**Prompt:** `rapid montage of tiny everyday actions, each shot a hard crash zoom onto the object, kettle, cup, coat, key, with a snap sound on every hit, cutting faster toward the end`  
**Example:** Shaun of the Dead.  
**Yields to:** `sig.hiphop_montage` — The routine is emotional, not a joke.  
`sig.wright_crash_zoom_montage`

### The Crash Zoom to the Face

**Also called:** snap zoom reaction, revenge zoom, recognition zoom  
**What it is:** An explosive zoom, one or two frames, from a medium to a tight close-up on an eye or a face, usually with a stab of music arriving at the same instant. Tarantino used it as the mark of recognition between two characters.  
**Effect on the audience:** A visual exclamation mark. It declares that this exact instant is what the whole scene has been about.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Recognising the enemy, spotting the name on the list, the line that flips the scene. Excellent in revenge stories, martial arts and anime, and in vertical short-form as the turn at the third second.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Meme / Comedy Edit, Drama Series Episode, Game Cinematic / Gameplay | genres: Action, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Samurai / Chanbara, Thriller / Suspense, Crime / Procedural, Dark Comedy / Satire, Shonen Battle (anime), Western  
**Avoid when:** More than once or twice in a scene and it becomes a tic. Do not use it in a straight realistic scene either; the stylisation breaks the tone.  
**Prompt:** `instant crash zoom from a medium shot to an extreme close-up on her eyes, one snap, holding tight and completely still afterwards`  
**Example:** Kill Bill, the recognition zooms.  
**Yields to:** `cut.axial` — A realistic scene the snap zoom would break.  
`sig.kill_bill_crash_zoom`

### The Circling Standoff Camera

**Also called:** duel orbit, standoff circle, showdown ring move  
**What it is:** The camera circles two or three people locked in a standoff, or moves between them in successive circling shots, and the rotation accelerates as the decision approaches.  
**Effect on the audience:** Time stretches and the location stops being neutral. The viewer feels the circle tightening even though nobody has moved.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The duel, the armed standoff, the second before the shot, and the unarmed family confrontation. Leone made it the western standard, and it works identically in samurai stories, battle anime and sports drama.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Music Video, Short Film | genres: Western, Samurai / Chanbara, Action, Thriller / Suspense, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Crime / Procedural, Drama, Shonen Battle (anime)  
**Avoid when:** A slow uniform circle with no acceleration drains the escalation out of the scene. Do not circle a single person in a standoff either; the rotation means a relationship between sides.  
**Prompt:** `the camera circles the three gunmen standing in a wide dusty circle, the orbit speeding up with each pass, cutting tighter each time, ending on the eyes`  
**Yields to:** `cut.srs_accel` — No room to orbit; escalate in accelerating cuts.  
`sig.circling_standoff`

### The Evil Cam / Force POV Rush

**Also called:** Raimi cam, unseen presence POV, shakycam rush  
**What it is:** A very low camera rushing at speed across the ground or through trees toward the victim, shaking and roaring, from the point of view of something we never see. Sam Raimi invented its known form.  
**Effect on the audience:** Evil acquires a body, a speed and a direction. The viewer becomes the pursuer, and that reversal doubles the panic.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The horror pursuit through woods or a house, the arrival of the unseen thing, the turn from calm into nightmare. Excellent in horror, in games and in vertical short-form because the rush grabs the thumb instantly.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Trailer / Teaser, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Horror, Supernatural Horror, Slasher, Folk Horror, Monster / Kaiju, Thriller / Suspense, Zombie  
**Avoid when:** If you reveal the source of the move in the same scene, the idea dies. Do not use it in open ground with no obstacles either; the power comes from tearing past things.  
**Prompt:** `low fast camera rush skimming just above the forest floor, smashing through undergrowth toward the cabin, violent shake, ending as it slams into the door`  
**Example:** The Evil Dead.  
**Yields to:** `sig.giallo_killer_pov` — Open ground with nothing to tear past.  
`sig.evil_cam`

### The Gloved Killer POV

**Also called:** giallo POV, black-glove shot, subjective stalker  
**What it is:** A subjective shot from the killer's point of view where only their black-gloved hands enter frame. The movement is handheld and hesitant and the face is never revealed. Italian giallo established the form.  
**Effect on the audience:** Deferred identity plus complicity. The viewer looks through the doer's eyes knowing a reveal is coming, so anticipation mixes with guilt.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The stalking scene before the crime in horror and procedural, and the introduction of an unknown perpetrator in crime drama. It works well in short-form and games, and in a light version in advertising when an anonymous hand reaches for a product.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Slasher, Horror, Crime / Procedural, Mystery / Whodunit, Thriller / Suspense, True Crime, Noir / Neo-Noir, Psychological Horror  
**Avoid when:** Used too often, the audience learns that every subjective shot means the killer and stops being surprised. Letting it see something the killer could not see usually breaks the contract the device depends on; the exception is when the film is deliberately lying to the audience and later admits it.  
**Prompt:** `handheld point of view moving slowly along a dark hallway, black-gloved hands entering frame to push the door open, never revealing the face`  
**Yields to:** `frame.stalker_pov` — The gloved-hand convention is spent or unreadable.  
`sig.giallo_killer_pov`

### Snorricam / Body Mount

**Also called:** body rig, chest-mounted camera, actor-mount  
**What it is:** The camera is rigged to the performer's body facing their face, so they stay perfectly fixed in the centre of frame while the entire world lurches and flies behind them. Aronofsky is known for it. This entry is the signature shot and its dramatic function, unlike the Snorricam in the rigs chapter, which covers the harness itself, its build and the performer's safety.  
**Effect on the audience:** Total dissociation. The viewer feels the character is the only fixed point and the universe has lost its footing.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Addiction, panic, flight, madness, drunkenness. Best in psychological drama and horror, and it is very strong in music video and vertical short-form because the face stays locked in the vertical frame while the background goes wild.

**Avoid when:** When the character is in a normal state the rig lies to the viewer. Do not sustain it too long either; it induces real nausea and kills the scene.  
**Prompt:** `snorricam body-mounted shot, the man locked dead centre and perfectly still, the street and crowd swaying and rushing violently behind him as he runs`  
**Example:** Requiem for a Dream.  
`sig.snorricam`

### The Rapid Insert Montage

**Also called:** hip-hop montage, macro-insert burst, ritual montage  
**What it is:** A run of extreme close-ups in fast succession covering parts of a single act: a pill, an eye, a lighter, a breath, each with its own hard sound effect. Aronofsky named and popularised it.  
**Effect on the audience:** A whole ritual compressed into two seconds. The repetition makes the act read as automatic, as something that owns the person rather than the reverse.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The repetition of a habit, an addiction or a routine, the passage of time, an internal escalation with no dialogue. Excellent in vertical short-form, in advertising for preparing a coffee or making a product, and in music video locked to the beat.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Commercial / TVC, Music Video, Product Video / Motion Design, Drama Series Episode, Meme / Comedy Edit, Anime (Japanese 2D) | genres: Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Dark Comedy / Satire, Slice of Life, Coming of Age, Action  
**Avoid when:** Without designed sound on every shot it is just fast cutting. Do not use it to explain anything complex either; it carries feeling, not information.  
**Prompt:** `rapid montage of extreme close-ups, pill, pupil dilating, lighter flame, mouth exhaling, each shot only a few frames long with a hard sound hit on every cut`

`sig.hiphop_montage`

### Chaos Handheld / Shaky Cam

**Also called:** shakycam, verite action, chaos cinema  
**What it is:** A handheld camera with deliberate violent shake, imperfect framing, and an operator who visibly fights to hold the event. Greengrass established it in action cinema.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer loses control of the information at the same moment the character does. The chaos transfers physically rather than intellectually.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Close-quarters fighting, an explosion, panic in a crowd, a raid. Best in grounded action, war, news and reenactment, because it carries the signature of something that really happened before anyone was ready.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, News Report / Reportage, Docudrama / Reenactment, Short Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Trailer / Teaser | genres: Action, War, Thriller / Suspense, Spy / Espionage, Survival, Disaster, Crime / Procedural, Zombie  
**Avoid when:** If the scene is not actually chaotic the shake is a lie and irritates for nothing. Do not use it in a scene where the viewer must understand the geography either.  
**Prompt:** `aggressive handheld, violent camera shake, the operator struggling to keep the two fighting men in frame, quick reframes and stumbles, ending as one of them hits the floor`  
**Example:** The Bourne Ultimatum.  
**Yields to:** `sig.wide_body_action_hold` — The viewer must read the geography.  
`sig.chaos_handheld`

### Breathing Handheld

**Also called:** soft handheld, human float, operator breath  
**What it is:** A handheld camera with a small continuous life to it: the operator's breathing and tiny corrections, with no shake and no framing drift. The frame is alive without calling attention to itself.  
**Effect on the audience:** Human presence. The viewer senses that someone is standing in the room with them, which produces quiet authenticity and a low grade unease.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Grounded drama, interviews, family scenes and documentary. It is the best default for any scene that should feel real without announcing the camera, and it is also the cleanest way to bring life into an otherwise static AI shot.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Feature, Interview / Video Podcast, Documentary Series, Short Film, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Drama, Verite / Observational, Family, Romance, Slice of Life, Coming of Age, Tragedy, Medical Drama  
**Avoid when:** In a scene that needs stillness and control, a cold interrogation, architecture, a premium commercial, the breathing weakens the image. Letting it slide into shake usually costs you the effect, because shake is a different move with a different meaning; the exception is when the scene escalates and you want the camera to escalate with it.  
**Prompt:** `subtle handheld, natural operator breathing and micro corrections, no shake, the frame gently alive around a static two-shot at the kitchen table`  
**Yields to:** `sig.fincher_locked_creep` — The scene needs cold stillness and control.  
`sig.breathing_handheld`

### The Intimate Handheld

**Also called:** face-hunting handheld, searching camera, actor-led operating  
**What it is:** A handheld camera very close to the faces, searching and reframing during the performance and sometimes lagging behind the actor. Cassavetes worked this way as a complete system.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer feels like an uninvited witness to a private moment. The imperfect framing is precisely what generates the truth.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The family argument, the emotional breakdown, the scene where the performance matters more than the picture. Best in grounded drama and short film, and in observational documentary when a subject opens up and the camera must not interrupt with designed reframing.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Series, Interview / Video Podcast, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Drama, Melodrama, Tragedy, Romance, Verite / Observational, Family, Coming of Age, Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** If the performance does not earn this proximity, the camera exposes the emptiness. Do not use it in a scene that has to deliver spatial information.  
**Prompt:** `close handheld camera moving between two faces during an argument, reframing on the fly, occasionally losing and recovering the speaker, no rehearsed move`  
**Example:** A Woman Under the Influence.  
**Yields to:** `size.master` — The scene must deliver spatial information.  
`sig.cassavetes_handheld`

### The Behind-the-Head Follow

**Also called:** nape shot, over-the-head trail, walking follow  
**What it is:** A handheld camera walks behind the character at shoulder height, holding their neck and the back of their head at a close constant distance for the whole scene. The Dardenne brothers built their method on it.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer is bound to the character without access to their face. Denied the emotional information, we concentrate on the action and the direction, and the result is moral tension.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The walk toward a hard decision, a small street chase, a character hiding something from us. Best in social drama and short film, and in horror because not seeing the face removes all reassurance.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Feature, Vertical Micro-Drama, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Docudrama / Reenactment | genres: Drama, Verite / Observational, Thriller / Suspense, Coming of Age, Crime / Procedural, Tragedy, Survival, Horror  
**Avoid when:** Held at an unchanging distance for too long it becomes monotonous. Do not use it when we need a reaction; this shot hides the face by definition.  
**Prompt:** `handheld camera following close behind the boy's head and shoulders as he walks fast through a housing estate, never seeing his face, staying at the same distance`  
**Example:** Rosetta.  
**Yields to:** `frame.reaction_shot` — We need the face and the reaction.  
`sig.dardenne_follow`

### The Blinkered Shallow Follow

**Also called:** tunnel-vision follow, out-of-focus world, single-subject lock  
**What it is:** A close follow on one person with a normal lens wide open, so everything around them is out of focus and unreadable. The image deliberately refuses to let the viewer see the world.  
**Effect on the audience:** The world becomes a blur. The viewer shares the character's denial or forced focus and senses the horror without being shown it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Walking through a place that cannot be looked at, trauma, psychological siege. Excellent in war and tragedy, and practically useful when you cannot build a full background, since an unreadable background becomes a stylistic choice rather than a shortfall.

**Avoid when:** Sustained across a whole film with no single wide, the viewer loses any sense of place. Do not use it when the background detail is what you want to show.  
**Prompt:** `close handheld follow on one man's face and shoulder, shallow depth of field, everything behind him unrecognisable and out of focus, walking through a crowded yard`

**Yields to:** `lens.deep_focus` — The background detail is what you must show.  
`sig.shallow_follow_blinkered`

### The Tumbling Camera

**Also called:** rolling camera, spiral descent, gravity-loss move  
**What it is:** The camera rotates on its own axis and tumbles while travelling through space, so the horizon spins completely and the viewer loses up from down. Gaspar Noé uses it as a core method.  
**Effect on the audience:** A real physical loss of balance. The viewer is pulled out of the observer position and into an altered state.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Psychological collapse, an overdose, a descent into hell, a party turning into a nightmare. Best in surrealist work, psychological horror and experimental music video.

`sig.noe_tumble`

### The Camera as Ghost

**Also called:** wandering camera, disembodied drift, spirit move  
**What it is:** The camera moves freely through a location without following anybody: it crosses rooms, rises over rooftops, descends through a ceiling and obeys no physical logic.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer becomes an invisible presence in the space. The sense of a watcher with no body creates loneliness even in a scene with no threat.  
**Used for and where it works best:** An opening that introduces a place before its people, a move from house to house across a neighbourhood, the moment of death or a soul leaving. `sig.camera_as_ghost`

### The Side-Scrolling Corridor Fight

**Also called:** 2D fight oner, lateral combat take, side-on hallway fight  
**What it is:** A long single-take fight in a narrow corridor with the camera tracking sideways parallel to the wall like a two-dimensional game, never entering the action. Park Chan-wook made the most famous version of it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Genuine exhaustion instead of heroics. Because the camera never closes in and never cuts, the blows look heavy, slow and human, and the hero reads as a tired man rather than a machine.  
**Used for and where it works best:** One man against many, a fight that wants sympathy rather than admiration, the end of a revenge arc. Strong in grounded action, martial arts and games, where the side-on language is already native to players.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Music Video, Trailer / Teaser | genres: Action, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Samurai / Chanbara, Seinen (mature anime), Shonen Battle (anime)  
**Avoid when:** Without fully drilled choreography the take exposes every mistake. Do not use it in an open space either; the idea depends on the narrowness of the corridor.  
**Prompt:** `continuous side-on tracking shot moving right along a narrow corridor, one man fighting a dozen others in profile, the camera never turning in, ending as the last man falls`  
**Example:** Oldboy, the corridor fight.  
**Yields to:** `sig.wide_body_action_hold` — An open space with no corridor to define it.  
`sig.park_hallway_sidescroll`

### The Full-Body Action Hold

**Also called:** Hong Kong action framing, full-figure fight shot, wide combat hold  
**What it is:** The fight or the stunt is covered in a wide frame holding the whole body from head to feet, on a relatively wide lens and in relatively long takes with little cutting. It is the classic Hong Kong action rule.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer believes it because they see the whole body working with no editorial help. The skill becomes measurable, which earns a respect that fast cutting never gets.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Any action built on real skill: martial arts, dance, parkour, sport. Best in martial arts, dance film and sports short-form, because the audience wants proof that nothing was faked.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Dance Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Anime (Japanese 2D), Music Video, Short Film | genres: Martial Arts / Wuxia, Action, Sports Drama, Comedy, Adventure, Musical, Sports Anime  
**Avoid when:** If the performer is not genuinely skilled, the wide frame exposes them immediately. Do not use it when you want confusion and chaos either; the clarity cancels it.  
**Prompt:** `wide static shot holding the fighter's full body head to toe, long unbroken take of the whole combination, no cuts and no close-ups, camera reframing only slightly`  
**Yields to:** `sig.chaos_handheld` — The performer is unskilled, or you want chaos.  
`sig.wide_body_action_hold`

### The Whip-Pan Performance Montage

**Also called:** instrument whip montage, performance insert burst  
**What it is:** Extreme close-ups of the parts of a performance, a hand, a drum head, a lip, a string, a bead of sweat, joined by whip pans or hard cuts locked to the music itself. Chazelle built whole scenes on it.  
**Effect on the audience:** The performance becomes violence. The viewer feels the physical labour more than the music, and art turns into combat.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A playing, training or making scene, the climax of an arts or sports drama, any labour montage. Excellent in music video, vertical short-form and sports advertising because the rhythm is built straight onto the track.

### The God's-Eye Overhead

**What it is:** A perfectly vertical camera directly above a person or an action, the frame holding the whole body and the floor around it. **Used for and where it works best:** Explaining how a place works, a casino, a factory, an airport, a market, and revealing that somebody is watching. Excellent in crime, documentary and corporate video, and in heritage documentary for showing the plan of a city or a temple from above.

### The Fast Push and Freeze

**Used for and where it works best:** Introducing characters in an opening montage, explaining how an operation works, marking the beat where everything changed. Excellent in documentary, video essays and explanatory short-form because the freeze buys you time to place text on screen.

### The Push In on the Listener

**Effect on the audience:** The emotion travels through the reaction rather than the news. The viewer takes the meaning of the line from another person's face, which is stronger because they see the effect rather than the cause.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Receiving news, a child seeing something impossible, the person who finally understands. Best in drama, family and science fiction, and in documentary on a listener's face during a hard testimony.

**Avoid when:** If the performer has no silent performance worth watching, the shot exposes the emptiness. Do not use it when the news itself is the most important visual event.  
**Prompt:** `slow push-in on the boy's face as he listens to the news off screen, his expression shifting from confusion to understanding, ending on a soft close-up`

### Motion Control Repeat / Match Move

**Also called:** repeatable camera move, motion control pass, match-move comparison  
**What it is:** The exact same camera move is repeated across two or more shots using a programmed rig or digital tracking, so framing, speed and path match perfectly and the only variable is the subject.  
**Effect on the audience:** A forced comparison. Because the camera did not change, the viewer's mind concentrates entirely on what did change and measures it precisely.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Before and after, years passing over the same place, a character transformation, an advertisement showing two versions of a product in the same frame. Excellent in heritage documentary: the same move over the site today and over a reconstruction of how it stood.  
**Best in:** formats: Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Feature Film, Explainer, Corporate / Training, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), 3D CG Animation | genres: Science / Educational, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Drama, Sci-Fi, Comedy, Family, Nature / Wildlife  
**Avoid when:** If the two moves are not exactly identical, the mismatch confuses the comparison and the viewer feels the error. Do not use it when there is nothing worth comparing.  
**Prompt:** `identical camera move repeated twice, the same slow push across the courtyard with the same speed and framing, only the season and the building have changed`  
**Yields to:** `time.same_shot_aged` — No rig to repeat the move exactly.  
`sig.motion_control_repeat`

**Effect on the audience:** Pure energy and a viewpoint no human could take. The viewer receives a sense of physical freedom and at the same time grasps the full geography of the place.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A one-shot tour of a venue, a restaurant, a gym, a factory, an archaeological site, a vertical short-form opening, an action beat. ### The Long-Lens Compression Push

**Used for and where it works best:** The chase that gets nowhere, an approach through heat haze or a crowd, the beat of hopelessness. Excellent in drama, war and western, and in vertical short-form on a person walking toward camera down a crowded street.

### Hero Orbit / Rise-and-Orbit

**Also called:** rising circle, ascending orbit, spiral up, rising orbit, climbing circle  
**What it is:** The camera circles the subject while rising at the same time, so the path becomes an ascending spiral ending on a high angle over them.  
**Effect on the audience:** Glorification plus withdrawal. We begin at his level and rise above him, so the feeling is compound: heroism followed by solitude.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of a battle, a victory beat with a price attached, the arrival of a sacred or royal figure in epic, and the closing of a premium commercial on a watch, a car or a perfume inside its space. **Used for and where it works best:** Historical and heritage documentary, archive passages, any section where no moving footage exists.
