# Relationship and ensemble complaints

Part of the symptom router. Open this file when the complaint is about relationship and ensemble. Each complaint below is followed by the cards that repair it, best first; `LOOKUP.md` says which file holds each card.

---

### "The relationship is just two people talking"

1. A relationship is a unit with a want of its own
   `rel.relationship_want` · `rel.relationship_as_protagonist` · `rel.relationship_with_no_want`
2. Give it a founding event and an unspoken agreement it runs on
   `rel.founding_event` · `rel.unspoken_agreement` · `rel.the_debt`
3. Track its arc separately from either character's
   `rel.relationship_arc` · `rel.asymmetric_investment`

### "The pair is static"

1. Change the register of the relationship rather than the plot
   `rel.register_change` · `rel.locked_pair`
2. Add a third
   `rel.triangle` · `scene.third_party_arrival`
3. Pick the pair shape deliberately
   `rel.marriage_on_the_page` · `rel.parent_and_adult_child` · `rel.sibling_order` · `rel.mentor_and_student` · `rel.professional_pair` · `rel.rivalry_as_friendship`

### "The group scene is unreadable"

1. Legibility is a design problem with known solutions
   `rel.ensemble_scene_legibility` · `rel.pair_off_the_room` · `rel.rotating_spotlight`
2. Above five people, screen time must be unequal or all contrast is lost
   `rel.more_than_five` · `rel.pair_matrix`
3. Show the hierarchy rather than stating it
   `rel.group_hierarchy_display` · `rel.who_defends_whom`
4. Not everyone needs to speak — giving each person a line in turn is the named failure
   `scene.everyone_speaks` · `dial.everyone_gets_a_line`

### "The group has no tension"

1. The named failure: everyone agrees
   `rel.everyone_agrees`
2. Give them a shared secret, or pressure from outside
   `rel.group_secret` · `rel.group_under_pressure` · `rel.shifting_coalition`
3. Put someone outside it
   `rel.outsider_in_the_group` · `rel.chosen_family`
4. Build the group as a system rather than a crowd
   `rel.ensemble_as_system`

### "The best friend has nothing to do"

1. The named failure, and one of the most common in drafts
   `rel.listener_friend` · `char.supporting_character_want`

### "The crowd scene is just noise"

1. Intention, not volume. Name in one line what the crowd wants at each beat — *to see her admit it*, *to be told it is not their fault*, *to keep her there* — and change the wording, the movement and the sound when that intention changes. Then give it one face and take it away: a single named member who hesitates, is looked at, and rejoins
   `anta.the_crowd`
2. Distinct from an institution, which has rules you can learn, and from ambient pressure, which has no intention at all
   `anta.institutional` · `anta.ambient_pressure`
3. And if the problem is legibility rather than opposition, it is the ensemble discipline you want instead
   `rel.ensemble_scene_legibility` · `dial.everyone_gets_a_line`
4. Where the scale itself is the subject — the queue, the protest, the camp, the factory floor — the move is the opposite of intention: lift one person out, name them, give them one want completely indifferent to the event, and return to them exactly twice. Choose them *before* the mass is established, so the scale lands as "and all of these are like her" rather than as an illustration picked afterwards
   `char.crowd_member`
5. Never make that person a spokesperson for the crowd. The failure is the representative individual, selected to embody the average and therefore wanting nothing in particular
   `char.specificity_not_representation`


### "They meet, it's charming, and that's all it is"

1. Build the meeting so it establishes the relationship's permanent problem rather than its charm — the thing they will still be doing wrong in act three
   `rel.first_meeting`
2. Then decide what they each think this is. Two people running the same relationship under different names is a whole engine, and neither of them has checked
   `rel.misnamed_relationship`
3. And give the pair an official story about itself that the audience can measure against the private evidence
   `rel.public_face`

### "The breakup is one speech"

1. Nobody ends a relationship in one move. Build it as a sequence of tactics, because the shape of the failing is the content
   `rel.breakup_architecture`
2. If they reconcile, do it on explicitly worse or different terms — reduced access, a subject permanently closed — rather than returning to the pre-rupture state
   `rel.reconciliation_on_new_terms`
3. The fast version is `rel.instant_reconciliation`: apology offered, accepted and the pair functional again inside one scene, because the plot needed them functional

### "They had a terrible fight and the next scene is normal"

1. That is `rel.rupture_without_aftermath` — no altered permissions, no changed logistics, no residue
2. The residue is the story. The reconciliation card above is what to write instead of a reset
   `rel.reconciliation_on_new_terms`

### "The apology lands flat and I need it to work"

Whether an apology is received depends on the order of its content rather than on sincerity, and audiences are unusually expert at this because they have given and received bad ones.

1. Responsibility first and unhedged — *I did it, it was my fault* — with no explanation attached, because an explanation arriving in the same breath is heard as a defence
   `scene.apology_that_lands`
2. An offer of repair second, and this is the element writers most often omit: a concrete thing the apologiser will do, which gives the wronged party something to accept or refuse and therefore gives the scene a second half
3. Cut the request for forgiveness. It is the weakest element, it hands the scene back to the apologiser, and its absence is what stops the apology feeling like a transaction
4. The value turn is not forgiveness. It is the moment the apologiser stops explaining — so write everything up to it as explanation and the turn as the silence after it stops
5. Then let the wronged party set the terms rather than returning the pair to the pre-rupture state. The failure twin is directly above
   `rel.reconciliation_on_new_terms` · `rel.instant_reconciliation`
6. Do not give it to a character who has not lost anything yet. An apology arriving before the cost has landed is a bid to skip the cost, and the audience scores it that way. And where the story's argument is that this person cannot change, you want the failing version instead

### "There are eight people in this pilot and the reader can't hold them"

1. Stop introducing them as individuals. Introduce them as **pairs and factions**, which is what an audience can actually retain on a first pass
   `rel.ensemble_pilot`
2. Then accept that above five, screen time must be unequal or all contrast is lost
   `rel.more_than_five` · `rel.pair_matrix`
3. A bounded occasion does the same job for an old group — a funeral, a wedding, a sale of the house — because every dormant relationship has to reactivate at once
   `rel.reunion_ensemble`

### "The supporting cast exists to be impressed by the hero"

1. That is `rel.admiration_chorus`: an ensemble whose members have no relationships with each other, only with the protagonist
2. It is the group-scale version of the single-character failure, and the repair is the same — a want of their own, pointed at each other
   `rel.listener_friend` · `char.supporting_character_want`

### "They meet again after years and it plays as an ordinary scene"

1. Write it as a collision between two obsolete models — each arrives holding a version of the other that stopped updating the day they parted
   `rel.reunion_after_separation`
2. If the gap is a deliberate jump in the telling, hand the audience the new state of the bond and make them reconstruct the missing middle from evidence
   `rel.bond_across_a_time_jump`

### "One of them is dead and the relationship stopped with them"

1. It does not stop. The living one still consults, argues with, performs for and hides from someone who cannot answer, and the bond keeps changing
   `rel.bond_with_the_dead`
2. The one-sided bond with someone living and unaware works on the same mechanism, and shapes decisions, language and identity just as hard
   `rel.parasocial_bond`

### "The two of them can barely talk to each other"

1. If it is language, the bond is made of what *can* be said rather than what is meant — a reduced vocabulary, a third language, an interpreter
   `rel.across_a_language`
2. If it is refusal, route every exchange through a third party who edits it on the way — a child, a lawyer, a mutual friend
   `rel.bond_through_a_go_between`
3. If it is distance, the channel itself becomes the relationship, and every gap in the exchange reads as a message
   `rel.correspondence_bond`

### "The difference between them is only ever mentioned"

1. Class shows up in small automatic behaviours neither of them chose, not in argument
   `rel.class_divided_pair`
2. Some bonds are fully alive in one setting and nowhere else — a ward, a shift, a boat, a summer — which makes relocation, not conflict, the real threat
   `rel.location_bound_bond`

### "The care storyline is just sadness"

1. Put love and resentment in the same tasks, which is where they actually live
   `rel.caretaker_and_cared_for`
2. If the pair is finished but cannot stop negotiating because a third person requires it, that is its own long structure
   `rel.co_parents`
3. And an institution can be a full participant in a relationship — it promises, demands, rewards and betrays
   `rel.institutional_bond`

### "The dog / the ship / the AI is set dressing"

1. Give it a real position on the relationship map, with its own rules and a relationship to more than one human
   `rel.non_human_member`
2. For an animal, the construction card is the one to reach for first: one want the story can obstruct, expressed as movement toward or away from something, and a world built out of the sense it actually leads with, which is almost never sight. Then let it be *wrong* about a human situation in a way that is right for the animal — that gap is the whole instrument
   `char.animal_character`
3. The failure it names is the emotional support animal: present in every scene, reacting correctly to human emotion, wanting nothing, and dying on schedule. Solve it with behaviour and sensory order, never with an invented interior monologue
   `pov.nonhuman_pov`
4. For a machine or an artificial mind, define it by its *constraint set* — what it is required to do, what it cannot do, what it cannot know, and what happens at the edge of those rules — rather than by how human it seems. Write the rules down and never break them for convenience, because a machine that becomes flexible at the climax is a deus ex machina with a face
   `char.machine_character` · `end.deus_ex_machina`
5. Locate the pathos in the mismatch, not in a yearning to be human. The strongest contemporary version is the machine that is working perfectly and is therefore the problem

### "Three friends, and one of them is spare"

1. Write from the excluded position — present, tolerated, quietly doing the arithmetic
   `rel.third_wheel`
2. If it is jealousy, build it as a triangle in which desire runs through a **model**: the character wants it because that particular rival wants it, so the rival is the real object of attention
   `rel.mimetic_jealousy`

### "The group has to lose someone and I don't know how to stage it"

1. An expulsion is a group decision with a mechanism — who raises it, who stays silent, who is made to deliver it
   `rel.expulsion`
2. If the one leaving is the person the group was organised around, the remaining relationships have to re-form rather than carry on
   `rel.leader_removed`
3. And if several people are competing to replace an aging holder of power, it is the incumbent's refusal to decide that keeps them all in place
   `rel.succession_fight`
