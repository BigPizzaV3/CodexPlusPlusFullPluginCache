# Antagonist and opposition complaints

Part of the symptom router. Open this file when the complaint is about antagonist and opposition. Each complaint below is followed by the cards that repair it, best first; `LOOKUP.md` says which file holds each card.

---

### "The villain is cartoonish"

1. Write their case as though you agreed with it
   `anta.write_the_case` · `anta.motiveless`
2. Give them the same want, so only one can have it
   `anta.shared_want`
3. Let them be right about something and give them one quality the protagonist lacks
   `anta.right_about_something` · `anta.better_version`
4. Stop them explaining themselves
   `anta.explains_themselves`
5. If the second draft answered the note by attaching a suffering, that is the named failure. Two tests: does the backstory change what he *does* in a scene, and would he be exactly as dangerous with the flashback cut
   `anta.backstory_as_excuse`
6. And if you have gone the other way, hold the limit — a sympathetic antagonist the narrative protects is not sympathetic, only unopposed
   `anta.sympathetic_limit`

### "The opposition is too weak"

1. They must win something irreversible before the end
   `anta.opposition_wins` · `anta.incompetent`
2. Give them a plan that would succeed if the protagonist did nothing
   `anta.opponents_plan`
3. Raise the price, not the size
   `anta.obstacle_ladder`
4. Write down what they actually hold — money, people, information, time, legal cover — and make the protagonist spend it. Once the audience knows the ledger they can tell a win from a delay, and the middle stops being a queue of obstacles
   `anta.finite_resources`

### "The conflict feels arbitrary"

1. The next obstacle should be caused by the last attempt
   `anta.self_generated`
2. Design the opponent to attack this protagonist's specific weakness
   `anta.tailored_opponent`
3. And check there are actual stakes, not just obstruction
   `anta.absent_stakes`

### "I don't want a villain"

Opposition is a function, not a character type. Eight forms with no villain in them:

1. An institution that is not corrupt, only immovable
   `anta.institutional`
2. A place or condition with rules
   `anta.nature`
3. Someone who loves them and is partly right
   `anta.beloved`
4. A condition inside them
   `anta.internal`
5. A deadline
   `anta.time` · `anta.ambient_pressure`
6. A future that has already been decided — never on its own, because it cannot be argued with. Give it a human agent who believes in it and play the scene against them
   `anta.fate`
7. A group with a shared intention and nobody in it authorised to change its mind
   `anta.the_crowd`
8. A refusal nobody will admit to owning, where the drama moves from the decision to the relay — the clerk who agrees with her and still cannot help
   `anta.no_author` · `anta.institutional`

And the habit to break:
   `anta.opposition_equals_villainy`

### "The world doesn't resist enough"

1. Build opposition at three scales that do not agree with each other
   `anta.three_levels` · `anta.multiple_one_question`
2. Put some of it inside the support system
   `anta.opposition_in_ally` · `anta.fake_ally`
3. Show what they will become if they do not change
   `anta.future_self` · `arc.mirror_character`

### "The real antagonist shows up too late"

`anta.late_arrival` · `anta.absent`

### "The villain's backstory made him less frightening"

1. The named failure, and the one every *make him three-dimensional* note gets answered with. A character asking for pity is smaller than one pursuing a want
   `anta.backstory_as_excuse`
2. The repair is a present want, written as if you agreed with it
   `anta.write_the_case` · `anta.shared_want`
3. In horror the same instinct produces the origin scene, which turns an unknown into a solved problem. **Rules are not origins** — the audience needs to know how the thing behaves, not where it came from
   `anta.overexplained_monster` · `anta.monster_rules`

### "He was frightening in season one"

1. The named failure, and the point of naming it is that it is one defect rather than a run of bad scenes: capability that rises and falls to suit whatever each scene needs
   `anta.power_drift`
2. The repair is bookkeeping, not restraint — spend from a written ledger of what they hold, never from the plot's convenience
   `anta.finite_resources`
3. If he has to return every week, decide which track he is on: the episodic opponent is beaten by competence, the recurring one is only ever survived
   `anta.recurring_vs_episodic`

### "The antagonist is strong and hasn't moved since episode one"

Not the same complaint as the one above. There the power drifted; here it is stable and the character is static.

1. Give the opposition a shape of its own, and make the protagonist cause it. An opponent who deteriorates on their own schedule is a subplot
   `anta.antagonist_arc`
2. The reliable version is not a change of heart but a ladder of subtractions — write their options at the start and remove one per act
   `tens.shrinking_options` · `anta.finite_resources`
3. Forces do not arc. Nature, time and institutions continue, and giving them an arc anthropomorphises them
   `anta.nature` · `anta.time`

### "The weekly opponent and the season's opponent are doing the same job"

1. Run two tracks and keep them apart with one rule: if the lead can beat the arc villain with the skills that closed this week's case, the arc villain is just a long case
   `anta.recurring_vs_episodic`
2. In a format that resets, the case itself is the opposition — and it must oppose the lead's *method*, not merely their goal
   `anta.procedural_opposition`
3. The layer that supplies most of the actual opposition scenes, and lets the principal stay rationed and large
   `anta.middle_tier`

### "Season two has nothing left to threaten anyone with"

1. Retiring the only antagonist is how a good second season becomes a bad third one. The failure is never felt in the finale — it is felt in the following premiere
   `anta.retirement`
2. Write the replacement pressure before you write the exit, and have the successor already on screen in a minor capacity
   `anta.middle_tier`
3. The exit itself must close the question the antagonist was asking, or the character reads as removed rather than concluded
   `end.retiring_a_character` · `end.season_ending`
4. If there was only ever one source of opposition, that is the diagnosis and it predates the finale
   `anta.three_levels`

### "The monster stops being frightening once we see it"

1. Withholding has to be productive — each partial appearance delivers one piece of hard information, and the full view lands where the protagonist *loses* a round, not wins one
   `anta.withheld_appearance`
2. Fear compounds on rules rather than on reveals: three to five, demonstrated rather than stated, one discovered late and at a price, and never broken at the climax
   `anta.monster_rules` · `tens.threat_with_rules`
3. If there is an origin scene, that is what killed it
   `anta.overexplained_monster`
4. The disclosure needs a plan made at outline stage, in four stages, none of them skipped: evidence, a part, the whole in bad conditions, the whole in good conditions. Full sight in good conditions belongs after the seventy per cent mark or nowhere, and after it the threat must do something new within one scene, or the reveal was your peak
   `tens.reveal_clock`
5. The named failure underneath the complaint is `tens.threat_shown_too_early`, and the diagnostic takes five minutes: find the frame where the threat is first fully visible and the frame where it first takes something irreversible. Sight before cost is the defect, and the repair is reordering rather than deletion — move the full look behind the first permanent loss and change nothing else
6. Note the honest tension with `tens.threat_never_acts`: that card says the danger must act early, this one says it must not be *seen* early. Both are true. Delay the sight; never delay the evidence

### "The comedy needs an obstacle and a villain would kill it"

1. Build a blocking character who is wrong in a fixed way and never learns — rigid where the protagonist is adaptable, so the obstruction and the jokes come out of one mechanism
   `anta.comic_antagonist`
2. Give them one genuine competence, or you have an incompetent blocker with a laugh track
   `anta.incompetent` · `comedy.straight_man`
3. Or take the enmity out entirely: the same indivisible prize, no ill will, asymmetric in method and symmetric in merit
   `anta.rival_not_enemy` · `rel.rivalry_as_friendship`

### "The villain is right and I don't know what to do about it"

1. Let them win the argument and lose the fight, in different currencies — and then do not compensate by revealing them to be a hypocrite. Refusing that compensation is the whole technique
   `anta.wins_argument`
2. Distinct from conceding one point late, which is a smaller and more common move
   `anta.right_about_something`
3. If the work is asking the audience to act, concede a point and never the case
   `theme.anti_theme`

### "The villain's change of heart doesn't convince"

1. Count the scenes between the first crack in his position and the turn. Fewer than three and this is the card
   `anta.unearned_redemption` · `char.unearned_change`
2. The repair is not a larger sacrifice — atonement has to fit the crime rather than outbid it, and a bigger death only makes the arithmetic more obvious. What is missing is earlier, smaller, unwitnessed change, a price proportionate to the harm, and somebody who does not forgive him
   `anta.redemption`

### "The audience likes the villain more than the hero"

1. Sympathy is built from want and cost, not from suffering — and the half that gets skipped is the limit: his actions must land on someone the audience also cares about, on screen, at full weight
   `anta.sympathetic_limit`
2. Check the opposition is actually taking something irreversible rather than being enjoyable
   `anta.opposition_wins`
3. And check you are not simply asking for a feeling the material has not paid for
   `emo.manipulated_beat`

### "The children's story needs a threat and I don't want to frighten them"

1. Bound it three ways before designing anything — the duration, the register, and the aftermath
   `anta.bounded_threat`
2. For preschool and song, the opponent is a situation with a name, a sound and one behaviour: a puddle, a stuck zip, a hill
   `anta.preschool_problem`
3. If the defeat is conversion, charge the protagonist for it and convert in the last act, never in the second scene
   `anta.befriendable`
4. That card specifies the opponent. The pressure curve and the discharge afterwards are the other half of the same design and are written with it — including the recovery beat, whose rule inverts the adult release rule in this database. See "The child has to be scared and then has to sleep" in Tension
   `tens.kids_bounded_threat`

### "The child watched it and couldn't sleep"

1. The named failure. Its invisible half is resolution without acknowledgement — an adult reads the plot resolution as complete and cuts the aftermath line as redundant
   `anta.unresolved_kids_threat`
2. Both repairs are required: close the threat inside the unit, **and** give one line in which a character names the feeling
   `anta.bounded_threat` · `emo.kids_emotion`
3. And leave the child something they can carry out of the room
   `end.child_carry_ending`

### "The documentary has no story yet"

1. It usually has no opposing force yet. You cannot manufacture conflict in non-fiction, but you can find it and order the film around it
   `anta.documentary_opposition`
2. The strongest documentary opponent is often not a person but a process with a date attached
   `anta.time`
3. For portrait and observational work, imposing an antagonist produces a false film — the honest structure is ambient
   `anta.ambient_pressure`

### "The brand film needs an enemy and the client has competitors"

1. The opposition is the customer's own frustration, specific enough to film — not "inefficiency" but the 4:40pm email that means missing the nursery run. And never a named rival: a good share of the audience already uses them and will defend their own choice rather than reconsider it
   `anta.problem_as_enemy`
2. In genuine advocacy the rule inverts — softening a real opponent with power into "a problem" is how a campaign becomes a mood
   `anta.institutional`
3. The feeling itself has to come from the category's real friction
   `emo.brand_emotion`
