# Short-form and serial complaints

Part of the symptom router. Open this file when the complaint is about short-form and serial. Each complaint below is followed by the cards that repair it, best first; `LOOKUP.md` says which file holds each card.

---

### "Nobody watches past three seconds"

1. Decide which of the four promises you are making — result, reveal, lesson or tension — before writing anything
   `open.first_second_hook`
2. Open on the answer to a question the audience never heard
   `scene.entering_on_the_answer` · `open.enter_on_the_answer`
3. There is no runtime for arrival — start after the scene started
   `scene.late_entry` · `open.open_mid_situation`
4. Deliver the backstory as an accusation, not an explanation
   `dial.exposition_accusation`
5. For non-fiction short-form, say the promise out loud — this is the one place on-the-nose is correct
   `open.stated_promise` · `dial.on_the_nose`

### "The piece gets no rewatches"

1. Write the opening so it follows from the ending, and make the second viewing mean something different
   `open.loop_opening`
2. A loop that only joins seamlessly is a trick; one that recontextualises is craft
3. In longer work the same effect is built backwards: write the reveal, then find lines that are *already* true under the new reading and change nothing. A line inserted for the purpose has no first-pass job and reads as a plant
   `sub.second_reading` · `info.recontextualising_reveal`

### "The episode doesn't hold to the end"

1. One value turn per episode. Two reads as two episodes badly joined
   `scene.value_turn`
2. Every scene must turn or be cut
   `scene.dead_scene_test`
3. Structure first, language second — build the timestamp skeleton before the dialogue

### "The cliffhanger doesn't work"

1. Cut on the question, not the answer — two seconds earlier than feels safe
   `scene.early_exit` · `dial.unanswered_line`
2. The last line should not be the protagonist's; give it to the antagonist or the world
   `dial.last_word`
3. Do not over-explain it. The audience will feel it

### "Sixty seconds is not long enough for anything to be left unsaid"

1. Preload the fact that makes the indirection legible from *outside* the piece — a caption, or a situation everyone already recognises — then play the remaining fifty seconds entirely on the surface, with no reference to it
   `sub.short_form` · `tens.first_five_seconds`
2. Do not attempt a value turn as well. There is not room for a turn and a subtext in the same minute, and the turn will win
   `scene.value_turn`
3. And if the loading fact is spoken, sound-off viewing loses it

### "The vertical episode is all antagonist and no story"

1. At sixty to ninety seconds a staged antagonist scene costs a third of the unit, so spend them on the button: the opponent's *effect* opens the episode and the opponent's *arrival* closes it
   `anta.offscreen_shortform` · `time.shortform_compression`
2. Give them one unexpectedly expert line every few episodes — a villain who says one competent thing is upgraded to a real threat for free
   `seq.shortform_serial`
3. In long form the same deferral stops being this card past the midpoint and becomes the late-arrival failure
   `anta.late_arrival`

### "Each episode is fine and the season doesn't build"

Two clocks running at different lengths, and the allocation is what most drafts get wrong.

1. The episode arc belongs to a guest or secondary character; the season arc belongs to the lead; and the lead's movement in any single episode is one visible increment — a thing said that was not said before, a thing no longer done, eleven words that go unremarked
   `arc.episode_vs_season`
2. Make the increments *unequal*. Three flat episodes then a large move reads as a series with a plan; equal movement every week reads as a mechanism. And the named failure here is the season arc delivered entirely in the last two episodes — eight hours of episodic story followed by a transformation nobody watched accumulate
3. Have the episode arc rhyme with whatever increment the season arc is currently making, so the guest story does thematic work instead of filling time
   `tens.season_long_pressure` · `emo.episode_emotional_shape`
4. If the format cannot let the lead change at all, give the arc away every week instead: to a guest whose Lie is a version of the lead's, changed by pressure the lead applies without intending to teach anything, and at a cost the guest pays rather than the lead
   `arc.borrowed_arc` · `arc.flat`
5. Leave at least one guest a season unconverted, or the fixed lead curdles into smugness. Build that lead deliberately
   `char.long_series_character` · `char.protected_static_role`

### "Book three and he has nothing left to learn"

1. Because each instalment was not given a complete movement of its own. The rule that prevents it: let the Truth of book one become the *new Lie* of book two by being over-applied — the boy who learns to trust people learns it too well and is betrayed; the woman who learns to stand alone can no longer let anyone in. That reads as a life rather than a checklist
   `arc.multi_book`
2. Plot the internal and external conflicts separately — one resolves per book, one does not — or book one ends at the series' first plot point with nothing complete
3. The failure that cancels a reader's investment in all of it is the reset: reinstating the book-one flaw in chapter two of book two, which teaches them that change here is temporary
4. A sequel commissioned after success has to begin with a fresh Lie rather than pretending the first arc was incomplete, which retroactively devalues the original ending
4b. The technique for that: find the *new* problem the completed change created, rather than taking the change away. It is off the table if the first arc ended in acceptance or in a death — there is no second arc in an accepted unchangeable situation without unaccepting it, and then the honest options are a different protagonist, a prequel, or handing the curve to the role
   `arc.sequel_reopening` · `arc.acceptance` · `arc.role_arc`
5. The viewpoint version of the same exhaustion is a separate complaint with a separate card
   `pov.series_pov`

### "Thirty seconds isn't long enough for anyone to change"

1. It is long enough for one turn, which is all the format is buying: he does, at the end, the single thing he refused at the start. Establish the refusal as an *action* within the first few seconds rather than as a line about a character trait, apply exactly one pressure to it, and make the turn a repetition of the opening shot's situation with one element changed
   `arc.sub_minute`
2. Do not try to fit a Lie in. At this length you get behaviour, and the belief behind it has to be inferred
   `char.ten_second_person` · `char.vertical_archetype`
3. The failure is the caption doing the arc — text on screen announcing that she finally stood up for herself, over footage in which she does not
4. And many good short pieces have no arc at all. Bolting a turn onto a demonstration, a joke or a hook costs the last four seconds, which is where the payoff lives
   `time.shortform_compression`

### "The series has nowhere left to go"

1. Raise the price rather than the obstacle
   `scene.escalating_cost`
2. Run a corruption arc, one defensible step per season
   `arc.corruption`
3. Grant the thing the series has been withholding, and let having it be worse
   `scene.granted_wish`
