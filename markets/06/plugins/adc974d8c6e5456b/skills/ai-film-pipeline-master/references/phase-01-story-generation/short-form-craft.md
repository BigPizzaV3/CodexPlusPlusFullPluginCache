# Short-Form Craft & Scripting Cards — Master Edition

87 cards. Built specifically for vertical short-form video (Reels, TikTok, Shorts).
Matches the exact 8-field card schema of the Writing Creativity Database, Image Creativity Database, and Video Creativity Database.

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, its machine-filterable tags, what to avoid, a worked micro-example, and its stable unique id.

**The examples show the shape of a line, not facts.** A date, a figure or a historical claim inside an example is illustrative and unsourced; a piece that uses one takes it from the brief or, on a history piece the user wants realistic, searches for it and uses the closest figure found (root [`SKILL.md`](../../SKILL.md) §0); nothing waits on it.

### Name the Material Before the Hook

**Also called:** the short-form selector, material before hook, what have you actually got to point at  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `sf.` shelf. It describes no technique. It asks the three questions this shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **what have you actually got to point at, what is the piece allowed to do to that subject, and does this fire once or govern every second** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were not invented: they were read off the `Yields to:` conditions the cards carry, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id.  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so that the hook on the finished reel was chosen against the material, the register and the clock, rather than taken from the top of the file because it was read first.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf**. The first answer eliminates roughly two-thirds of the file; the second and third leave a shortlist of three to seven. If you cannot answer question one — *what have you actually got to point at* — you are not ready to write a hook, you are ready to do research, and the piece does not exist yet. Where two answers fight for one piece, that is two pieces, and `sf.one_point` already says so in its own words.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** **You are building the sentence rather than deciding the piece.** This shelf stops at the decision. The `sfl.` family writes the line — word order, where the verb lands, what a muted reader meets first — and four rules live in both families on purpose. That pair is a precedence ranking, not a fork: the `sf.` card decides whether the piece obeys the rule and the `sfl.` card decides how the sentence is built, so asking this map which of the two to use returns nothing, because both apply and the `sf.` one is upstream. Also **distrust axis two whenever your honest answer is its fourth value** — more than half the shelf sits there, so on a register-neutral piece this map has only two questions left and does two-thirds of the work it does on a children's song or a memorial. And **four of this shelf's `Yields to:` fields name no card at all**: they name the record — a named publication, the project canon, a contemporary chronicle. Those are not alternatives and this map cannot offer them. Where the subject is the documented past, the map hands you a shortlist and the record still overrules it.  
**Example:** `[Selector — produces no example. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the example.]`

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — What have you actually got to point at?**
Asked first, because it is the condition the shelf repeats most: twenty-six of the fifty-four `Yields to:` lines turn on what the material is — *"Stakes are small and personal, not titanic"* (`sf.stakes_first`), *"Impersonal listicle; number the milestones instead"* (`sf.micro_scenario`), and its exact reverse in `sf.progress_bar_psychology` (*"Organic poetic or immersive fiction"*), *"Broad geo-political overview with no single object"* (`sf.anchor_artifact`), *"Abstract trend with no physical object"* (`sf.artifact_spotlight`), *"Small portable object, scale contrast says nothing"* (`sf.monumental_scale_contrast`), *"Long-range overview with no ticking clock"* (`sf.countdown_ultimatum`), *"The site identification is genuinely disputed"* (`sf.ancestral_geography_bridge`), *"The withheld reveal is the whole structure"* (`sf.front_load`).

**Axis 2 — What is the piece allowed to do to this subject: play with it, fight over it, or only honour it?**
Seventeen conditions turn on this and they are stated as facts about the deliverable, not as feelings in the viewer: *"Gentle children's content; open warm, not fearful"* (`sf.negative_framing`), *"Child-friendly or lighthearted piece"* (`sf.forbidden_warning`), *"Adult dramatic heritage reel"* (`sf.kids_rhyme_hook`), *"Tragic death narrative"* (`sf.specific_comment_cta`), *"The piece ends on memorial silence"* (`sf.grammatical_loop`), *"Solemn commemoration"* (`sf.infinite_list_wrap`), *"Calm third-person archival narration"* (`sf.exposition_as_accusation`), *"True tragedy with no silver lining"* (`sf.reversal_promise`), *"Sacred names, lineage, verified truth"* (`sf.deliberate_flaw`).

**Axis 3 — Does it fire once, at a placed second, or does it govern every second?**
Eleven conditions are about placement and frequency rather than content: *"Below twenty seconds it stops being a beat grid"* (`sf.four_beat_reel`), *"Under about fifteen seconds, no retention dip"* (`sf.midroll_rehook`), *"Standalone reel that owes resolution"* (`sf.cliffhanger_drop`), *"You actually want the rewatch loop"* (`sf.button_punchline`), *"Simple linear chronology, nothing to close"* (`sf.causal_ouroboros`), *"Static speech video, no kinetic gesture"* (`sf.in_medias_res_restart`), *"High-bpm dance edit needs a kinetic seam"* (`sf.ambient_drone_bridge`) — plus the once-only limits the cards carry against themselves: *"Overused; once per video maximum"* (`sf.anticipation_hook`), *"More than once per video; repetition feels like hesitation"* (`sf.deliberate_breath_pause`), *"Two value turns in one reel"* (`sf.value_turn_singular`). It is asked last because it is the only axis that separates the shelf's rules from its moves.

**The default, and the one against it**

**First, what the measurement refuses to give.** In-degree does not name a default on this shelf, and saying so is the finding. Three cards take the most inbound edges — `sf.progress_bar_psychology`, `sf.emotional_anchor_close` and `sf.button_punchline` — and **each of the three takes all of its edges on a single condition.** Everything that reaches `sf.progress_bar_psychology` reaches it because the material turned out to be an enumerated chain rather than a scene (*"impersonal listicle"*, *"points form one indivisible chain"*, *"simple linear tutorial"* — three wordings of one boundary). Everything that reaches `sf.emotional_anchor_close` reaches it because the subject turned out to be the dead (*"tragic death narrative"*, *"memorial silence"*, *"solemn commemoration"*). Everything that reaches `sf.button_punchline` reaches it because the piece owes a real ending instead of a seam. **All three are doors out of a region, not fallbacks inside one.**

**Counting distinct conditions instead of edges leaves exactly two cards standing, and one of them is the default.** `sf.micro_scenario` is reached from two unrelated boundaries — *"Stakes are small and personal, not titanic"* (`sf.stakes_first`, a failure of scale) and *"Organic poetic or immersive fiction"* (`sf.progress_bar_psychology`, a failure of mode). `sf.compressed_timeline` is the other, reached from *"Long-range overview with no ticking clock"* and *"Abstract trend with no physical object"*. Nothing else on this shelf is reached for more than one reason. **When the monumental framing is too big for the material and the numbered list is too cold for it, the shelf puts the viewer inside a moment — so the default is `sf.micro_scenario` for the way in, governed by `sf.four_beat_reel` for the shape.** `sf.four_beat_reel` earns the second half of that pair on its own text rather than on the graph: it is the only card here that carries a runtime band, states where its own grid stops in both directions, and names the card that replaces it below twenty seconds. A default that does not know its own runtime is not a default on a shelf about runtime.

**The one against it is `sf.deliberate_breath_pause`, and the shelf is built against it.** Almost every card here removes air: `sf.sub_second_cut` strips inhales to zero frames, `sf.every_word_earns` deletes any word that can go, `sf.visual_disruption_interval` refuses a static frame for more than three and a half seconds, `sf.cadence_staccato` cuts sentences to two or three words. This one card puts a single audible human breath back in, and it is the only card on the shelf whose whole value is that the piece stops. Nothing routes to it. More than that: `sf.sub_second_cut` carries the exact condition it answers — *the pause is honouring grief, keep it whole* — and sends the reader **off the shelf** rather than four cards down the file to the card that already does it. **A shelf-mate is the answer to another card's own stated failure and no edge connects them.** Its near cousin against the grain is `sf.bystander_perspective`, which refuses the throne for the gate sentry and is likewise the target of nothing.

**The unasked**

**Sixty-seven of the eighty-six cards are the target of no `Yields to:` field anywhere on this shelf.** Only nineteen are ever pointed at: `sf.front_load`, `sf.curiosity_gap_extreme`, `sf.micro_scenario`, `sf.progress_bar_psychology`, `sf.compressed_timeline`, `sf.kids_rhyme_hook`, `sf.assyria_heritage_reel`, `sf.deadpan_gravity`, `sf.destiny_inversion`, `sf.polarizing_dilemma`, `sf.button_punchline`, `sf.myth_vs_shovel`, `sf.unsettled_debate`, `sf.specific_comment_cta`, `sf.emotional_anchor_close`, `sf.grammatical_loop`, `sf.whip_pan_reset`, `sf.ambient_drone_bridge`, `sf.artifact_spotlight`. Everything else can be chosen and nothing ever sends you there.

**And the shape of that list is the finding, not its length.** Two regions are unasked as whole blocks.

**First, the hooks.** Sixteen of the twenty cards under axis three's first value are unasked, and the four that are reached are reached from the body or from a register boundary, never from another hook. **Not one opening card on this shelf says *not me — that one* to another opening card on the grounds of what the piece is made of.** When a hook card yields at all it yields outward, to `open.`, to `vertical.`, to `promo.`, to `time.` — off the shelf entirely. Seventeen hooks sit side by side in one file and the pair graph treats them as seventeen separate answers to seventeen separate questions rather than as rivals for one slot. That is why axis one is on this card and why it is asked first: the fork the hooks need was never written into their pair fields, because a reader comparing two hooks was comparing two pieces, not two options for one piece.

**Second, the delivery.** Eleven cards decide how the voice and the cut behave — `sf.cadence_staccato`, `sf.speech_rate_acceleration`, `sf.frequency_dip_transition`, `sf.deliberate_breath_pause`, `sf.whisper_to_boom`, `sf.kinetic_text_sync`, `sf.visual_disruption_interval`, `sf.sensory_reset_point`, `sf.dopamine_escalator`, `sf.pacing_rollercoaster`, `sf.sub_second_cut` — and not one of them is the target of anything here. When they yield they yield into the audio phase and the script phase: to `pacing.wpm_documentary`, to `mix.lufs_loudness_targets`, to `sensory_sonic.cadence_expansion`, to `doc.reflective_silence`. **Their alternatives are not on this shelf.** That is the same boundary the `sfl.` twins mark at the other end: this shelf owns the decision, the neighbouring families own the execution, and the map should be read as stopping there rather than as having a hole.

**The new card this shelf wants is the one for the second watch.** Three cards engineer a rewatch — `sf.grammatical_loop`, `sf.causal_ouroboros`, `sf.infinite_list_wrap` — and `sf.audio_anchor_loop`, `sf.visual_reset_vector`, `sf.whip_pan_reset` and `sf.in_medias_res_restart` hide the seam so the loop is not noticed. Then nothing says what the second pass is supposed to give that the first did not. Every card on this shelf is written for a stranger meeting the piece once. The nearest thing that exists is `sf.micro_callback`, which inverts a phrase's meaning at second twenty — inside one watch, not across two — and nothing routes a reader there to notice the difference.

### The First Line Works Twice

**Also called:** sound-off first line, the dual hook  
**What it is:** The opening idea is written to work in both spoken audio and the muted frame, because most of the audience meets it muted. The two channels carry the same promise without requiring identical words.  
**Effect on the audience:** A spoken hook with the sound off is no hook at all, and the viewer is gone before the sound question is ever settled.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every reel, short and vertical piece. Essential on a feed where autoplay is muted. Let accessibility captions transcribe the voice; any separate hook text should add the missing subject, stakes or context rather than duplicate the line word for word, and it takes its own seconds before or after the spoken line, never the same ones (`phase-04-audio-narration/HOW-PHASE-4-WORKS.md`, Step 2).  
**Best in:** formats: Silent Autoplay Feed Short, TikTok / Reels Cold Open | genres: Educational, Documentary, Mystery  
**Avoid when:** The piece is delivered where sound is on by default, such as a cinema, an exhibition or a subscribed podcast feed.  
**Example:** On-screen hook, 0:00-0:01.5: *"A sealed tablet, 2,600 years later."* Voiceover from 0:01.5: *"It was never meant to be read by human eyes."*  
**Yields to:** `open.first_line_job` — Sound-on delivery; write the line for the ear alone.  
`sf.first_line_both_ways`

### Front-Load the Payoff

**Also called:** no saving the ending  
**What it is:** The thing the piece promises is delivered early rather than held for the end.  
**Effect on the audience:** On short form the end may never arrive. A payoff behind forty seconds of build is a payoff most of the audience never receives.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Reels, shorts and vertical video, particularly on a feed where the first seconds decide distribution.  
**Best in:** formats: Micro-Doc Cold Open, YouTube Shorts Explainer | genres: Historical, True Crime, Investigative  
**Avoid when:** The piece is built on a single withheld reveal and the withholding is the whole structure. Then the hook must promise the withholding itself.  
**Example:** *"The fire that burned Nineveh to the ground is the only reason its royal library survived today."*  
**Yields to:** `sf.curiosity_gap_extreme` — The withheld reveal is the whole structure.  
`sf.front_load`

### The Contrarian Inversion

**Also called:** myth-smash, the counter-intuitive open  
**What it is:** Opening by directly asserting that an accepted common belief, standard historical advice, or official narrative is completely wrong.  
**Effect on the audience:** Triggers immediate cognitive dissonance; the viewer stops scrolling to defend their assumption or hear the contradiction justified.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Heritage historical facts, archaeological breakthroughs, debunking popular myths.  
**Best in:** formats: Historical Revisionist Reel, Video Essay Hook | genres: History, Science, Philosophy  
**Avoid when:** The contradiction is artificial or petty; fake controversy leads to instant bounce upon discovery.  
**Example:** *"Everything you learned in school about how the Assyrian empire collapsed is missing the real culprit: the river."*  
`sf.contrarian_inversion`

### The Micro-Scenario Open

**Also called:** the visceral plunge, instant immersion  
**What it is:** Dropping the viewer directly inside a high-stakes, hyper-specific sensory situation without introductory preamble ("Imagine standing on the walls of Nineveh at 3 AM hearing the river flood...").  
**Effect on the audience:** Bypasses analytical skepticism and activates emotional mirror neurons instantly.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Historical battles, atmospheric heritage recreations, psychological moments.  
**Best in:** formats: Immersive Historical Narrative, Cinematic Micro-Drama | genres: Drama, Epic, Ancient History  
**Avoid when:** Abstract statistical analyses or impersonal listicles.  
**Example:** *"Imagine standing on the ramparts of the Adad Gate at midnight, watching forty thousand Medes and Babylonians encircle your city."*  
**Yields to:** `sf.progress_bar_psychology` — Impersonal listicle; number the milestones instead.  
`sf.micro_scenario`

### Negative Framing (Loss Aversion)

**Also called:** the costly error, warning open  
**What it is:** Framing the core topic around avoiding a catastrophic blunder or hidden loss ("Stop reading Mesopotamian history like this...", "The one translation error that buried an empire...").  
**Effect on the audience:** Fear of missing out or suffering an error is twice as motivating as the promise of reward.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Craft advice, historical cautions, educational warnings.  
**Best in:** formats: Museum/Culture Guide, High-Retention Reel | genres: Art, Architecture, Cultural Heritage  
**Avoid when:** Gentle children's songs or celebratory cultural tributes.  
**Example:** *"Never visit the British Museum's Mesopotamian wing without knowing why these relief faces were deliberately scratched off."*  
**Yields to:** `sf.kids_rhyme_hook` — Gentle children's content; open warm, not fearful.  
`sf.negative_framing`

### The Extreme Curiosity Gap

**Also called:** the knowledge vacuum  
**What it is:** Stating a startling, verifiable truth while deliberately withholding the causal mechanism ("This clay tablet survived 2,600 years only because someone tried to incinerate it.").  
**Effect on the audience:** Creates mental tension that can only be resolved by watching the explanation.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Museum artifacts, historical paradoxes, archaeological mysteries.  
**Best in:** formats: Mystery / Archaeology Short, Investigative Reel | genres: Mystery, Archaeology, Lore  
**Avoid when:** Withholding essential clarity so heavily that the prompt makes zero sense.  
**Example:** *"Ashurbanipal built the largest library on earth, but he refused to let anyone touch this one specific cylinder."*  
`sf.curiosity_gap_extreme`

### The Taboo or Unspoken Reality

**Also called:** the declassified confession  
**What it is:** Opening with a fact that standard textbooks, mainstream media, or official institutions avoid mentioning.  
**Effect on the audience:** Gives the viewer the feeling of accessing privileged, illicit insider knowledge.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Suppressed history, controversial archaeological digs, diaspora narratives.  
**Best in:** formats: Untold History Short, Social Commentary Reel | genres: Historical Exposé, Dark Drama  
**Avoid when:** Defamatory ungrounded conspiracies without archaeological citations.  
**Example:** *"Historians rarely talk about what the royal weavers actually did during the siege of 612 BC."*  
`sf.taboo_unspoken`

### The Visual Paradox Open

**Also called:** cognitive collision  
**What it is:** Pairing an acoustic statement with an image that seems completely contrary to the words, forcing the brain to pause to reconcile them.  
**Effect on the audience:** Disorients the automatic scroll reflex through cross-modal sensory conflict.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Visual art, architectural contrasts, ancient ruins juxtaposed with modern tech.  
**Best in:** formats: Urban Archaeology Short, Geography Reel | genres: Heritage, Travel, Documentary  
**Avoid when:** Pure narration with static talking-head footage.  
**Example:** Showing a modern bustling Mosul street while the voice whispers: *"Right beneath these car wheels lies the palace of Sennacherib."*  
**Yields to:** `vertical.text_hook` — No contrary image available, static talking head.  
`sf.visual_paradox_hook`

### The 4-Beat Reel Architecture

**Also called:** ignition-complication-spike-button  
**What it is:** The universal structure for 45–60s video: Ignition (0:00–0:03), Complication (0:04–0:25), Spike (0:26–0:40), Button (0:41–End).  
**Effect on the audience:** Delivers a complete, emotionally satisfying narrative arc in under a minute.  
**Used for and where it works best:** All standalone vertical dramatic stories and reels. At 45 to 60 seconds all four beats have room to land, but complication and spike are two structural positions around **one** value turn: complication intensifies the starting value and the spike reverses it. **At 30 seconds — the common length of a paid social advert and of a cutdown — the four compress into three: hook, turn, close.** The hook keeps `0:00–0:03` unchanged, because its reason is the feed and not the runtime; complication and spike merge into one continuous rise with a single reversal at its top, roughly `0:04–0:22`; the close takes the remainder. One value turn either way, but at thirty seconds it arrives without a development passage in front of it.  

**Between 60 and about 90 seconds the four beats still hold, and only the middle grows.** A 75-second hype film or a 90-second brand story is not a different structure — ignition and button stay where they are, and the extra fifteen to thirty seconds go into the complication, usually as one more turn of events or one more piece of evidence rather than as slower versions of the same beats — not a second value turn: the reel's value still reverses once (`sf.value_turn_singular`). Above about 90 seconds stop using this card: the piece needs movements rather than beats, and `HOW-PHASE-1-WORKS.md` gives the long-form shape.
**Best in:** formats: Classic 4-Beat Narrative Short, Moral Vignette | genres: Parable, Folk Tale, Screenplay Short  
**Avoid when:** 10-second micro-memes or multi-hour livestream cuts. Below about 20 seconds this stops being a beat grid at all — a fifteen-second cutdown is one image and one line, and `time.shortform_compression` is the card for it.  
**Example:** 0:00 (The burning tablet) -> 0:12 (The siege begins) -> 0:28 (The wall crumbles) -> 0:42 (The tablet is dug up today).  
**Source:** assumption — the package's working figure, not a published standard; set the beat times against the rendered cut.  
**Yields to:** `time.shortform_compression` — Below twenty seconds it stops being a beat grid.  
`sf.four_beat_reel`

### One Rule of the Invented World

**Also called:** the one-rule reel, fantasy at reel length, the rule the ending spends  
**What it is:** The short-form reading of the fantasy genre. A reel has room to establish **one** rule of an invented world — shown, never explained — and the ending turns on that rule. The long-form version is the Fantasy and Epic kit in [`genres-world.md`](genres-world.md): its second required beat is this card's whole opening — one rule of the world demonstrated early, at someone's cost — and its world budget allows one new rule per sequence. A reel is one sequence, so it gets one rule. Everything else the kit asks for — the company, the subworld, the scale reveal, the old order ending — does not fit and is not attempted: the reel keeps beat 2 and spends it at the end.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer learns the world by watching it work, and then meets the ending as a consequence they could have predicted and did not — the pleasure of a trap closing rather than of a surprise. A reel that explains its magic spends its seconds on a lecture; a reel whose ending uses a rule it never showed feels arbitrary, because nobody could have seen it coming.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Any fantasy, fairy-tale or invented-world story told in a reel. Three moves. **Show the rule in the first beat** as one clean, single-variable instance — the demonstration craft is `expo.rule_by_demonstration` — ideally at someone's small cost, so the price arrives with the rule; where there is room for a second demonstration it shows the price (`expo.rule_cost_demo`), and in most reels the ending is where the price is paid. **Add no second rule**: a second invented rule halves the attention on the first and leaves the ending two things to turn on. **Turn the ending on the same rule** — used against the character who relied on it, or paid in full by the one who broke it — so the reel's single value turn (`sf.value_turn_singular`) is driven by the world rather than announced by a narrator. Invented names are held to the fewest the story cannot do without; `expo.proper_noun_budget` sets the budget.  
**Best in:** formats: 45-Second Micro-Story, Classic 4-Beat Narrative Short, Cinematic Micro-Drama, Tight Narrative Reel | genres: Fantasy, Folk Tale, Parable, Narrative Fiction  
**Avoid when:** The world is not invented. A myth, a scripture or a living folk tradition already has its rules, and they are the source's, not the writer's to cut down to one; a heritage subject answers to the record, not to this card. Avoid it too as the spine of a series: one planted rule serves one episode, and a run needs the long-form budget of one new rule per sequence. And **the rule is never stated in words** — if the ending needs a line of narration to explain why it worked, the demonstration in the first beat failed, and that is what gets rewritten.  
**Example:** 0:00 — a boy tells his mother he fed the goat, and as he walks off his shadow trails a step behind him; nobody in the yard looks twice. 0:25 — a stranger swears to the village council that the old bridge will hold the carts. 0:45 — the council thanks him and turns to go; the girl sent to fetch them looks down at the stranger, walking away in the noon sun, and at the shadow following a step behind him.  
**Yields to:** `expo.rule_by_demonstration` — the piece runs past reel length and carries more than one sequence: the Fantasy and Epic kit's budget of one new rule per sequence replaces this card's one, and each rule gets its own demonstration. `dial.exposition_correction` — the rule is abstract, statistical or legal, with no single stageable instance to show. `time.shortform_compression` — the piece is too short for a demonstration and a payoff both, below the length at which `sf.four_beat_reel` stops being a beat grid: it is one image and one line.  
`sf.fantasy_one_rule`
