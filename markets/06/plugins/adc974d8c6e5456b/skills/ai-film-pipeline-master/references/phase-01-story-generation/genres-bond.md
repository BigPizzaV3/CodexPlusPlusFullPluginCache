# Genres — bond

Entry point into the database. Each kit is a ranked short list; open the `cat-*.md`, `arcs.md`, `situations.md` or `structures.md` file named for that id prefix in `LOOKUP.md` for the full card.

A genre kit carries something a format kit does not: **required beats.** Genre in writing is not a marketing label, it is a set of obligations. Truby's position is that each genre demands fifteen to twenty specific plot events, and that a story carrying only one genre reads as formula — you need two or three others in supporting roles. Every kit below therefore has an ordered `Required beats` block. Skip a beat and the audience feels the hole even when they cannot name it.

The five kits here are the **bond** genres: stories whose central unit is a relationship rather than a person. In all of them the relationship, not the protagonist, is the thing that arcs.

## Love Story

**What the genre argues:** that a person cannot see themselves accurately alone, and that the only instrument accurate enough to show them is another person who wants something from them. The genre asks whether you can be known and survive it. Its peculiar structural fact — unique among genres — is that the hero's desire and the hero's opponent are the same human being, which is why every scene has to work as both courtship and combat.

**Required beats:**
1. The lover is shown inside a world that has already taught them a rule about love; someone else states the rule out loud.
2. The ghost surfaces — a named earlier betrayal or loss that set the fear running.
3. First sight. The gaze happens before the words, and only one of them knows it happened.
4. The meeting proper, staged so they are on opposite sides of something concrete.
5. The joust: a first verbal contest neither concedes. The couple's private grammar is established here.
6. A love adviser — friend, sibling, colleague — names the thing about the lover that the lover cannot see.
7. A second suitor arrives who is, on paper, the correct choice.
8. The scam: one of them approaches the other under a false pretext, a false errand, or a false name.
9. The first conversation with no witnesses, held somewhere the world cannot reach.
10. A public occasion — dance, wedding, funeral, party — where they must behave as if they are not what they are.
11. The first touch that is not accidental.
12. The kiss, and immediately a cost attached to it: an interruption, a witness, a lie moved closer to the surface.
13. The perfect love moment. Short, dramatised, never summarised — this is the promise the ending will be measured against.
14. The pretext of beat 8 collapses and lands back in the other's hands.
15. The breakup, staged in the location where the perfect love moment was staged.
16. Moral decline: one of them does something cheap — takes the job, sleeps with the second suitor, tells the family's version.
17. The battle of words, in which each names the other's real weakness accurately.
18. Double reversal — each sees their own failure, not only the other's, and one of them says so first.
19. The decision, made in public, at price, in front of the people whose opinion was the pressure.
20. Communion or farewell. The new equilibrium is shown as a habit, not a declaration.

**Supporting genres:** Comedy of Manners supplies the social machine that makes the obstacle plausible in a world where nothing forbids anything. Family Drama supplies the pressure that is not the lovers' own — the parent, the house, the inheritance. Tragedy supplies the version where the double reversal arrives correctly and too late.

**Rules:** Give the opponent and the beloved the same face — if you can remove the love interest from the obstacle, you have written two plots | Stage the perfect love moment as scene, never as montage or summary | Make the lie of beat 8 the exact lie the ghost of beat 2 taught them to tell | Let the second suitor be genuinely better in at least one measurable way | Write the battle of words so both accusations are true | Put the final decision in front of witnesses, because private courage costs nothing here | End on behaviour, not on a line | Withhold the declaration until after the decision, or cut it entirely

**Avoid:** The obstacle that is a misunderstanding a single sentence would clear — the audience spends the second act waiting for a phone call rather than watching a story. The second suitor written as an idiot or a cad, which retroactively makes the protagonist's hesitation stupid and drains the middle of the film. Summarising the happy stretch, so that the breakup has nothing to break. And the modern failure: no social force, so the only thing keeping two free adults apart is one of them being irrationally difficult for ninety minutes.

**The kit (ranked):**

1. **The Opponent Who Shares the Want** (Arc) `arc.shared_want_opponent`
   The genre's whole structural peculiarity. Build the beloved as the opponent first and the courtship second.
2. **The Relationship as Protagonist** (Relationship) `rel.relationship_as_protagonist`
   The thing that changes is the pair, not either person. Chart its arc before you chart theirs.
3. **Love** (Situations) `sit.tobias_love`
   The master pattern: the lovers must be separated by something real, and the separation must be dramatised, not asserted.
4. **Obstacles to Love** (Situations) `sit.polti_28_obstacles_to_love`
   Polti's inventory of what can legitimately stand between them. Pick one and make it structural, not attitudinal.
5. **What the Relationship Wants** (Relationship) `rel.relationship_want`
   Distinct from what either lover wants. Beats 13 and 20 are impossible to write without it.
6. **The Relationship Arc** (Relationship) `rel.relationship_arc`
   Plot the pair's value across the twenty beats and you will see instantly where the middle sags.
7. **Love Without Declaration** (Emotion) `emo.love_without_declaration`
   The declaration is the weakest available tool. Everything before beat 19 should be carried by behaviour.
8. **The Want/Statement Gap** (Subtext) `sub.want_statement_gap`
   The joust at beat 5 is a machine built out of this gap. So is every scene until the battle of words.
9. **Asymmetric Investment** (Relationship) `rel.asymmetric_investment`
   Never let both want it equally at the same moment. The imbalance is the engine; swap who holds it every few beats.
10. **The Value Turn** (Scene) `scene.value_turn`
    Love stories die of scenes where two people like each other for four pages. Every scene turns or goes.
11. **The Secret** (Character) `char.the_secret`
    Beat 8's pretext needs a secret underneath it that predates the meeting, or the collapse at 14 is mechanical.
12. **The Public Scene** (Scene) `scene.public_scene`
    Beats 10 and 19 are both public-scene problems: what can be said and what cannot, with an audience on the page.
13. **Understate at the Peak** (Emotion) `emo.understate_at_peak`
    The higher the emotional pressure, the smaller the line. Beat 20 in particular.
14. **The Value Landed** (Endings) `end.value_landed`
    Communion and farewell are both legitimate. What is not legitimate is an ending where the value never moved.

## Romantic Comedy

**What the genre argues:** that people are wrong about what would make them happy, and that the correction can only be administered by someone they find intolerable. Comedy supplies the diagnosis — the gap between what the protagonist says they want and what they visibly need — and romance supplies the cure. It is the only genre where humiliation is the mechanism of love rather than an obstacle to it.

**Required beats:**
1. Setup: the protagonist demonstrates, in action, the wrong thing they believe will make them happy — the job, the fiancé, the plan, the move.
2. An external clock is named: the wedding date, the deadline, the deportation, the bet, the lease.
3. Meet cute — first contact staged as a first defeat for one of them.
4. Each tells a third party why the other is impossible, and the audience hears both accounts.
5. The contrivance that forces proximity: shared flat, shared assignment, fake relationship, same train, same client.
6. The first argument, fought over something trivial that is transparently about something else.
7. A comic set piece where one is humiliated in front of the other — and survives it, which is where liking starts.
8. The friend or sibling chorus states the theme plainly and is ignored.
9. Midpoint hook: an event that binds them irreversibly — a shared lie, a shared crime, a night, a public announcement.
10. The pretence starts producing real feeling, and each notices it separately, in separate scenes.
11. The correct-on-paper partner presses their claim, reasonably.
12. The swivel: a decision taken for a bad reason — pride, fear, a line overheard out of context.
13. The dark moment. The private motive is exposed in public and the relationship reads as finished.
14. A scene alone with the friend where the protagonist is honest about what they want for the first time.
15. The grand gesture — public, costly, faintly ridiculous, and it must directly reverse beat 12.
16. Final image answering the opening image: the wrong thing from beat 1 is given up on screen, visibly.

**Supporting genres:** Comedy of Manners provides the rulebook the lovers keep breaking and the etiquette that makes the humiliation land. Buddy Love provides the friend-chorus and often the funnier relationship. Workplace or Family Drama provides the external clock, since modern rom-coms have no social prohibition to borrow.

**Rules:** Write the comic gap first — the stated want against the visible need — and derive the jokes from it | Make the meet cute a defeat, not a delight | Build the second act on a contrivance with rules, so the audience knows why they cannot simply leave | Let the rival be genuinely suitable | Put the dark moment in public | Make the grand gesture cost the protagonist the thing from beat 1 | Give the funniest character a want of their own or they become a joke-delivery appliance | Never let a joke stop the story to be a joke

**Avoid:** Jokes bolted onto a straight romance — the comedy has to come out of the characters' wrongness, or the film is a drama with interruptions. The best friend who exists only to receive information and be amusing. The dark moment triggered by a misunderstanding rather than by the protagonist's own decision, which makes the grand gesture an apology for nothing. And the grand gesture that costs nothing — a run through an airport is a location, not a sacrifice.

**The kit (ranked):**

1. **Comic Character Construction** (Comedy) `comedy.comic_character_engine`
   Build both leads as comic engines — a fixed wrongness that generates behaviour — and the second act writes itself.
2. **The Pair Who Cannot Leave** (Relationship) `rel.locked_pair`
   Beat 5 is this card. Rom-com collapses the moment either party could reasonably walk out.
3. **Status as the Engine** (Comedy) `comedy.status_engine`
   Every scene between them is a status exchange. Track who is up, and never let it sit still for two scenes.
4. **Escalation and the Comic Spiral** (Comedy) `comedy.escalation_spiral`
   The lie of beat 9 must cost more to maintain every scene. That escalation is the second act.
5. **Comedy of Manners** (Comedy) `comedy.comedy_of_manners`
   Supplies the rules that make the humiliation of beat 7 and the exposure of beat 13 actually hurt.
6. **The Polite Surface** (Subtext) `sub.comic_politeness`
   The rom-com's native dialogue mode: two people being courteous while conducting a war.
7. **Reversal of Expectation** (Scene) `scene.reversal_of_expectation`
   The meet cute, the set piece and the grand gesture are the same technique at three scales.
8. **Dramatic Irony** (Information) `info.dramatic_irony`
   The audience must know about the pretence before the victim does. That gap is where affection is generated.
9. **Love Without Declaration** (Emotion) `emo.love_without_declaration`
   Beat 10 has to be played entirely in behaviour, twice, in two separate rooms.
10. **Escalating Humiliation** (Tension) `tens.escalating_humiliation`
    Beat 13 needs to be genuinely bad. Comedy's darkness budget is higher than most writers spend.
11. **The Callback Payoff** (Comedy) `comedy.callback_payoff`
    The grand gesture should be a callback — the trivial thing from beat 6, returned at full weight.
12. **Interesting Beats Likeable** (Character) `char.likeable_vs_interesting`
    Both leads must be allowed to be genuinely wrong. Sanding them smooth removes the gap.
13. **Give the Supporting Character One Want** (Character) `char.supporting_character_want`
    The friend chorus at beat 8 needs its own agenda or it is furniture that talks.
14. **Opening Image, Answered** (Openings) `open.opening_image_answered`
    Beat 16 is a contract signed in beat 1. Write the pair of images before anything else.
15. **The Wrong-Sized Reaction** (Comedy) `comedy.wrong_reaction`
    The genre's most reliable single joke: a reaction disproportionate to the event, in either direction.

## Buddy Love

**What the genre argues:** that the self is incomplete and the missing piece is not a lover but a witness. Snyder's three requirements — an incomplete hero, a counterpart who supplies exactly what is missing, and a complication that makes the pairing costly — describe a love story in which the word love is never available, which is why the genre generates more subtext per page than anything else in this file. It asks whether you can need someone you did not choose.

**Required beats:**
1. The hero completes a task alone and does it measurably worse than they would with help.
2. The missing piece is named in one line — usually as the thing the hero refuses to need.
3. Forced pairing: an assignment, a journey, a custody, a contract, a sentence. Neither chose it.
4. Immediate friction over method rather than morals — they want the same outcome and disagree about the route.
5. First competence trade: each does one thing the other demonstrably cannot.
6. The founding event — the thing they will refer to for the rest of the story, and afterwards.
7. A private language appears: a nickname, a signal, a joke with no explanation offered.
8. An outsider insults one of them and the other defends them before thinking about it.
9. The complication is revealed: the secret orders, the money, the illness, the third party — whatever makes the pairing expensive.
10. Midpoint: they succeed at something together that neither could have done alone, and both know it.
11. One learns the other has been withholding beat 9.
12. The split — spoken as relief by both, staged as loss.
13. Each attempts the task alone and fails in the precise way the other used to cover.
14. The rescue: one goes back for the other with no strategic reason to.
15. The confession, conducted sideways — about logistics, a debt, a jacket, a car. Never about feeling.
16. New equilibrium: the pair continues in a changed form, or one is gone and the survivor is carrying the other's habits.

**Supporting genres:** Crime or Action supplies the assignment that locks the pair together and the clock. Comedy supplies the friction of beat 4 and makes the withheld affection bearable. Coming of Age is the frequent third, because the incomplete hero is usually incomplete in an age-specific way.

**Rules:** Make the pairing structurally binding — orders, handcuffs, a shared child, a shared debt — never mere convenience | Write each one's deficiency as the other's competence, precisely paired | Ban the word love and every synonym for it | Give them a founding event and refer back to it at least three times | Stage the split as relief and shoot it as bereavement | Let the rescue at beat 14 be strategically stupid | Put the confession inside a practical task | Give the pair one habit the audience can recognise from behind

**Avoid:** The pairing held together by nothing but the plot's convenience, so the audience keeps asking why either stays. Two characters with the same competence, which produces bickering instead of exchange. The confession played straight, which incinerates in one page everything sixteen beats built. And the third act that resolves the complication of beat 9 rather than the relationship — the complication was never the subject.

**The kit (ranked):**

1. **The Pair Who Cannot Leave** (Relationship) `rel.locked_pair`
   The genre's foundation. If either could walk out and does not, you have a sentiment, not a structure.
2. **The Relationship as Protagonist** (Relationship) `rel.relationship_as_protagonist`
   Both individual arcs are subordinate. The pair is the character the audience is actually tracking.
3. **The Professional Pair** (Relationship) `rel.professional_pair`
   The most common vehicle: a working relationship made to carry a private one in its subtext.
4. **The Rivalry That Is a Friendship** (Relationship) `rel.rivalry_as_friendship`
   Beat 4's friction must read as affection to the audience while reading as hostility to the characters.
5. **The Founding Event** (Relationship) `rel.founding_event`
   Beat 6. Without a shared past the pair has nothing to lose at beat 12.
6. **The Unspoken Agreement** (Relationship) `rel.unspoken_agreement`
   The private language of beat 7, and the thing beat 11 breaks.
7. **Love Without Declaration** (Emotion) `emo.love_without_declaration`
   The whole genre runs on this card. Beat 15 is its hardest test.
8. **The Debt Between Them** (Relationship) `rel.the_debt`
   Buddy pairs are held together by ledgers, not by feelings. Who owes whom, and does either say so.
9. **Who Defends Whom** (Relationship) `rel.who_defends_whom`
   Beat 8 written as a card. The reflex defence is the genre's most efficient affection beat.
10. **The Load-Bearing Contradiction** (Character) `char.load_bearing_contradiction`
    Each half needs one internal contradiction the other half keeps colliding with.
11. **The Mirror Character** (Arc) `arc.mirror_character`
    Design the counterpart as the hero's deficiency inverted, point by point, then hide the symmetry.
12. **The Straight Man** (Comedy) `comedy.straight_man`
    Assign it deliberately, then take it away at least once — a fixed straight man flattens the pair.
13. **What the Hands Are Doing** (Subtext) `sub.hands_business`
    Beat 15 lives or dies on business. The confession happens over an engine, a wound, a meal.
14. **The Change of Register** (Relationship) `rel.register_change`
    The pair's register shifts at beats 10, 12 and 15. Make each shift audible on the page.
15. **Repetition with Variation** (Dialogue) `dial.repetition_variation`
    The running exchange that means one thing at beat 7 and something else entirely at beat 16.
16. **The Value Landed** (Endings) `end.value_landed`
    Continuation or loss — either is legitimate, but the pair's value must have measurably moved.

## Family Drama

**What the genre argues:** that you do not choose the people with the most power over you, and that leaving does not end the relationship, it only changes its distance. Family drama is the purest study of the opponent who loves you — the antagonist who wants your happiness and is nevertheless the thing standing in front of it. Its question is whether people who cannot be escaped can be forgiven.

**Required beats:**
1. A ritual is convened — meal, holiday, funeral, anniversary — and everyone's seat is assigned without discussion.
2. The founding event the family tells about itself is narrated aloud, and the attention is on one listener's face.
3. The absent member is named: dead, estranged, imprisoned, abroad, not invited.
4. The returning member arrives and the household visibly rearranges.
5. First skirmish over a trivial object or a logistic — who drives, who cooks, who has the key.
6. A private two-hander in which one member says the true thing quietly to one other person.
7. The family's shared secret is referenced obliquely in front of somebody who does not hold it.
8. Money or property enters: an inheritance, a debt, a sale, a care home, a business.
9. Generational reversal — a parent asks a child for something, or a child grants a parent permission.
10. Midpoint: the secret is told to the wrong person, or told in the wrong room.
11. Coalitions shift. Two members who have never allied, ally.
12. A member does the exact thing they have spent the story condemning in another.
13. The ritual of beat 1 is reconvened and fails: the meal ends early, someone does not come, the guests leave.
14. The explosion scene, in which everything is said accurately and nothing is solved.
15. Someone leaves the house.
16. A private act of repair by a peripheral member — a task, not a speech.
17. New equilibrium: the family continues in a changed configuration, and one seat is different.

**Supporting genres:** Tragedy supplies the inherited fault nobody in the family can see. Comedy of Manners supplies the etiquette that keeps beats 5 through 12 from collapsing into shouting. Coming of Age is the frequent third, because families are usually shown at the moment one member is trying to leave.

**Rules:** Give every family member a case that would win in isolation | Put the real information in the two-handers and the noise in the group scenes | Treat the ritual as structure, not backdrop — convene it, break it, and note who is missing both times | Make the money specific: an amount, a property, a signature | Never resolve the explosion scene; resolve the story somewhere else and smaller | Write the absent member as an active force | Let the repair be performed by someone with no stake in winning | End on furniture, not on a statement

**Avoid:** The monster parent, which converts a family drama into a rescue story and lets every other character off. The explosion scene deployed as the climax, after which the film has forty minutes and nothing to do. Grievances stated rather than demonstrated in behaviour — a family drama where people explain their childhoods to each other is a therapy transcript. And the reconciliation ending, which almost always means the writer lost their nerve: families more often reach a workable distance than a resolution.

**The kit (ranked):**

1. **The Beloved Antagonist** (Antagonist) `anta.beloved`
   The genre's defining structure. The obstacle loves the protagonist and is not wrong about them.
2. **Parent and Adult Child** (Relationship) `rel.parent_and_adult_child`
   The axis the genre turns on, and the one that reverses direction at beat 9.
3. **Siblings and Position** (Relationship) `rel.sibling_order`
   Birth order assigns roles the adults are still playing at fifty. Beats 5 and 11 run on this.
4. **The Group with a Shared Secret** (Relationship) `rel.group_secret`
   Beats 7 and 10. Decide exactly who holds it and who is outside it before writing a word.
5. **The Founding Event** (Relationship) `rel.founding_event`
   Beat 2 — the story the family tells about itself, and the one member for whom it is false.
6. **The Ghost** (Arc) `arc.the_ghost`
   Family drama is the genre where the ghost is a person still in the room, or a chair where they sat.
7. **The Interrupted Ritual** (Scene) `scene.interrupted_ritual`
   Beats 1 and 13 are the same scene twice. The failure of the second is the story's real climax.
8. **High-Context Indirection** (Subtext) `sub.high_context_indirection`
   Families speak in shorthand carrying thirty years. This is the genre's native dialogue mode.
9. **Shared History as Generator** (Subtext) `sub.shared_history`
   Where the subtext comes from: everything already said, still in the room, never repeated.
10. **The Absent Antagonist** (Antagonist) `anta.absent`
    Beat 3. The person not at the table frequently exerts more pressure than anyone at it.
11. **Opposition Inside the Ally** (Antagonist) `anta.opposition_in_ally`
    Beats 11 and 12. The coalition partner who becomes the obstacle without changing their mind.
12. **Theme Through the Ensemble** (Theme) `theme.through_the_ensemble`
    Distribute the moral argument across members so nobody has to state it.
13. **The Wound as a Rule of Conduct** (Character) `char.wound_as_rule`
    Every family member's damage should show as a policy, not as a confession.
14. **Talking Past Each Other** (Dialogue) `dial.talking_past`
    Two members in the same conversation, having two different ones. Beat 14 is built from this.
15. **The Transaction Scene** (Scene) `scene.transaction_scene`
    Beat 8. Inheritance, sale, care home — the family's values on a table with a number attached.
16. **The Quiet Ending** (Endings) `end.quiet_ending`
    Beats 16 and 17. After the explosion, the only honest ending is small and practical.

## Ensemble

**What the genre argues:** that no single life contains enough of the truth, and that meaning is produced by the friction between lives that barely touch. The ensemble is a structural argument as much as a dramatic one: it says the subject is bigger than any one protagonist can carry. Its question is what a group owes the individuals inside it — and its risk is that a story distributed across ten people belongs to nobody.

**Required beats:**
1. The hub is established in the first sequence: the place, institution, event or disaster that will hold everyone.
2. Each principal gets one entrance that states their want in action, inside a minute.
3. The hierarchy is displayed once, physically — who sits where, who speaks first, who is served last.
4. The shared arrangement is named: the founding event, the contract, the rota, the debt, the rule.
5. Two strands touch for the first time without the characters registering the connection.
6. A pressure arrives that costs the group collectively — layoff, siege, storm, verdict, deadline.
7. The group splits into subgroups, and the pairings are deliberately the ones the audience has not yet seen alone.
8. Each strand reaches a small victory or defeat, at staggered intervals, never simultaneously.
9. Midpoint convergence: at least two strands meet and the characters now know they are in the same story.
10. The outsider asks the question that forces the group to state its rule out loud.
11. A coalition breaks. Somebody changes sides for a reason the audience can defend.
12. The group secret reaches a member who was outside it.
13. Every principal gets one scene in which they are unambiguously the most important person on the page.
14. The crisis requiring the group to act as one — and one member refuses.
15. Convergence climax: every strand in a single location or a single event.
16. The dispersal — characters shown leaving in different directions, at different speeds.
17. Final image on the hub itself, changed or emptied.

**Supporting genres:** Social Drama supplies the pressure of beat 6 and the reason the group exists at all. Family Drama supplies the internal ranking, since ensembles organise themselves into family roles whether or not anyone is related. Comedy of Manners supplies legibility — status is the fastest way to tell nine people apart on a page.

**Rules:** Build the hub before you build a single character | Give every principal one want, stated in action, and never a second | Never introduce more than three people in one scene | Assign each principal a physical signature and a verbal one, and keep both consistent | Stagger the strand climaxes, because simultaneity flattens them | Track screen time on a grid and treat a disparity as a structural fact, not an accident | Move the coalitions at least twice | Give every principal one scene they own outright | End on the hub, not on a character

**Avoid:** The ensemble where everyone agrees, which is a crowd rather than a system and produces scenes with no opposition in them. Strands that converge only in the last ten minutes, so the first ninety are four short films sharing a title. Characters distinguished by biography rather than by want — an audience cannot see biography. And the fatal one: distributing screen time evenly out of fairness, which guarantees nobody gets enough to matter.

**The kit (ranked):**

1. **The Ensemble as a System** (Relationship) `rel.ensemble_as_system`
   Design the group as a mechanism with inputs and pressure points before you name anyone.
2. **The Ensemble Grid** (Character) `char.ensemble_grid`
   The practical instrument: want, deficiency, ally, opponent and function, one row each.
3. **Keeping the Group Scene Legible** (Relationship) `rel.ensemble_scene_legibility`
   Nine people in a room is a craft problem with known solutions. This card is those solutions.
4. **Writing More Than Five** (Relationship) `rel.more_than_five`
   The techniques that take over once the cast passes the number a reader can hold at once.
5. **The Pair Matrix** (Relationship) `rel.pair_matrix`
   Beat 7. List every possible pairing, mark the ones you have never staged, and stage those.
6. **Rotating the Spotlight** (Relationship) `rel.rotating_spotlight`
   Beat 13 as a scheduling discipline. Which scene belongs to whom, decided in advance.
7. **The Shifting Coalition** (Relationship) `rel.shifting_coalition`
   Beat 11. Alliances that move are what makes a group a plot rather than a cast list.
8. **The Ensemble Where Everyone Agrees** (Relationship) `rel.everyone_agrees`
   The anti-card. Read it early, then audit every group scene against it.
9. **The Group Under Pressure** (Relationship) `rel.group_under_pressure`
   Beat 6. External pressure is the only reliable way to make a group reveal its actual ranking.
10. **Theme Through the Ensemble** (Theme) `theme.through_the_ensemble`
    The genre's reason for existing: one question, answered differently by each strand.
11. **Multi-POV Architecture** (POV) `pov.multi_pov_architecture`
    In prose the strand structure is a POV structure. Decide the pattern and hold it.
12. **Scene Ownership** (POV) `pov.scene_ownership`
    Every scene belongs to exactly one person. Ambiguity here is what makes ensembles feel soupy.
13. **Displaying the Hierarchy** (Relationship) `rel.group_hierarchy_display`
    Beat 3. One physical arrangement does the work of a page of introductions.
14. **The Outsider in the Group** (Relationship) `rel.outsider_in_the_group`
    Beat 10. The newcomer is how a group's rules get spoken without exposition.
15. **The Physical Signature** (Character) `char.physical_signature`
    With this many people, the reader needs a handle for each one by the second appearance.
16. **The Handoff Ending** (Endings) `end.handoff_ending`
    Beats 16 and 17 — an ending passed from character to character rather than resolved in one place.
