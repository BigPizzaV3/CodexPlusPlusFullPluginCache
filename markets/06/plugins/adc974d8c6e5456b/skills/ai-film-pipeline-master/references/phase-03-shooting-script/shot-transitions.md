# Shot-to-Shot Transitions

67 cards. These decide a shot: how it moves, how it cuts, and how it sits in time.

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, what to avoid, and the prompt written out in full.

---

### Name What Is Between Them

**Also called:** the transition selector, what goes in the gap, who makes the join  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `trans.` shelf. It does not describe an effect. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **what is actually between the two shots, when you had to decide it, and what the join is claiming happened** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were not invented: they were read off the forty-five `Yields to:` conditions the cards carry, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id.  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so the transition that does reach the screen was chosen against the material, the shoot day and the claim being made, rather than picked out of the effects folder because it was the one nearest the top.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf**. Question one splits the file five ways; questions two and three leave a shortlist of three to six. **And before any of that, settle which shelf you are on.** `trans.` decides *how you get from this shot to the next*; `cut.` decides *why the cut falls where it falls*. The line runs at the frame boundary: **if the join has a duration of its own — even a few frames of mix, cover or camera move — it is a `trans.` card; if your answer can be written as a single frame number, you are on `cut.` and this map will sell you an interval you do not need.** That line is not a preference, it is where every edge between the two shelves already sits, and the traffic is almost entirely one way: a transition that cannot be afforded collapses into a cut. **This shelf is the one that gets given up.** So if you are unsure, price the interval first — and if you cannot say how long the join lasts, you were never on this shelf.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** **The piece will not tolerate a visible join at all.** That is the single most repeated condition on this shelf and it is not a question this map can answer, because its answer is a card on the neighbouring shelf. If the honest reading of your project is *serious drama, realist documentary, or paid work a client will read as expensive*, most of the file is already out and the map is sorting a remainder rather than a shelf. Also avoid it when you are placing a run of joins rather than one join: this map answers *what goes between these two shots*, and most of the cards here record in their own `Avoid when:` that the damage begins at the **second** use — a question the map does not ask and cannot see. And **distrust axis one whenever your honest answer is its fifth value**: a layer put on afterwards can be put on anything, so that answer eliminates almost nothing and leaves two questions to do all the work.  
**Prompt:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

---

**The axes**

**Axis one — What is actually between the two shots?**
Asked first, because it is the condition that decides whether the rest of the questions are even available. The cards state it as a fact about the material, never as a taste: *"The whip was not designed in the shoot"* (`trans.whip_pan`), *"The darkness was not planned in the shoot"* (`trans.through_black`), *"Bright daylight, where no frame is dark enough"* (`trans.dark_frames`), *"No wide cover, so the change has no time to happen"* (`trans.walk_past`), *"The shot has no logical light source for a flare"* (`trans.flare_wipe`), *"The sky carries distinctive clouds or birds"* (`trans.sky_pan`), *"The ground texture differs on the two sides"* (`trans.ground_tilt`). Each one says the same sentence in a different costume: *the thing that was going to make this join is not in the picture, so choose a join made of something else.*

**Axis two — When did you have to decide: at the edit, or on the day?**
Thirteen conditions turn on this, and they are the ones that cost money when they are answered late: *"The two framings cannot be made to align"* (`trans.match_dissolve`), *"Realist drama, or two poses that will not align"* (`trans.morph`), *"A low-resolution image the push would expose"* (`trans.into_screen`), *"Serious documentary, or two shots at equal brightness"* (`trans.luma_fade`), *"A wide horizontal frame, where the tilt is weaker"* (`trans.whip_tilt`), *"A phone, where more than two panels stop reading"* (`trans.comic_panel_wipe`), *"Archive where invented motion would betray the record"* (`trans.still_to_motion`). Some joins can be laid on any pair; some need the pair to already share a property you cannot add later; some needed a thing to be standing in front of the camera, moving at the right speed, on the day.

**Axis three — What is the join claiming happened?**
Asked last, because it is the only axis that reaches the cards where neither the time nor the place moves and the picture itself changes what it is made of: *"Serious work; the dissolve says 'time passed' unironically"* (`trans.wipe_clock`), *"Serious work needing 'new chapter' without the paper"* (`trans.page_turn`), *"When the card only repeats what the picture shows"* (`trans.title_card`), *"When the end should snatch the picture, not say goodbye"* (`trans.fade_out`), *"The literal blur flashback now reads as dated soap"* (`trans.defocus`), *"Archive where invented motion would betray the record"* (`trans.still_to_motion`). A join that says *time passed* when no time passed is the commonest lie on this shelf, and it is the only one the audience catches every time.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `trans.match_dissolve`, and it is measured rather than asserted — which matters here more than on most shelves, because the obvious answer and the measured one are different cards.**

Three cards send a reader to `trans.match_dissolve` and they give three unrelated reasons: `trans.walk_past` (*"No wide cover, so the change has no time to happen"*), `trans.morph` (*"Realist drama, or two poses that will not align"*) and `trans.into_screen` (*"A low-resolution image the push would expose"*). **Three edges, three conditions — a card reached three times for three different reasons is a floor.** And the reasons agree with what the card is: it is the cheapest join on the shelf that still does the shelf's work, because it needs no object on set, no camera move planned in advance and no layer bought afterwards. It asks only that the two shots share a shape, which is the one thing you can still arrange after the shoot by choosing which two shots.

**`trans.dissolve` looks like the default and the graph says it is not.** It takes more inbound edges than any other card here, and **all four fire on one condition**: the register got serious, so drop the device. `trans.additive_dissolve` (*"Serious documentary, realist drama"*), `trans.luma_fade` (*"Serious documentary"*), `trans.wipe_clock` (*"Serious work; the dissolve says 'time passed' unironically"*) and `trans.light_leak` (*"Serious documentary or contemporary drama"*) are four cards making the same retreat. In-degree here measures **how often this shelf is too loud for the film**, not what it falls back on. It is the shelf's retreat, and a retreat and a floor look identical from behind.

**The one against it is `trans.through_wall`.** Nothing routes to it; it is the target of no `Yields to:` anywhere in the package; and it is the only card on the shelf that declares outright that the camera is not a person. Every other join here is one the viewer could in principle have experienced — an object crossed in front of their eyes, they blinked, they turned, they waited. This one passes through solid matter, and its own `Avoid when:` names the cost exactly: it works against you when you want the camera to stay a human eye inside the scene, *the exception is a film that has already declared the camera an unbound narrator*. That exception is not a loophole, it is a whole grammar, and nothing on this shelf points at it. For heritage work in particular it is the join that takes a viewer through a reconstructed wall into a room nobody has stood in for three thousand years, which no dissolve can do because a dissolve says time passed and this says *the wall is not the boundary you thought it was*. Its near cousin against the grain is `trans.live_to_animation`, likewise never yielded to, and the only other card here where the join changes the medium rather than the picture.

**The unasked**

**Nearly two-thirds of this shelf is the target of no `Yields to:` field anywhere in the package.** They can be chosen but nothing ever sends you to them. By id:

`trans.additive_dissolve` · `trans.blinds` · `trans.clap` · `trans.clone_reveal` · `trans.colour_flip` · `trans.comic_panel_wipe` · `trans.cube` · `trans.defocus` · `trans.dissolve_chain` · `trans.drone_descent` · `trans.fade_colour` · `trans.fade_in` · `trans.fade_white` · `trans.film_burn` · `trans.flare_wipe` · `trans.framerate_drop` · `trans.freeze_photo` · `trans.hand_cover` · `trans.infinite_zoom` · `trans.into_screen` · `trans.iris` · `trans.jump` · `trans.live_to_animation` · `trans.luma_fade` · `trans.mask_split` · `trans.mirror` · `trans.page_turn` · `trans.pupil` · `trans.reverse_forward` · `trans.scale_jump` · `trans.smash_zoom` · `trans.spin` · `trans.still_to_motion` · `trans.through_object` · `trans.through_wall` · `trans.water` · `trans.whip_tilt` · `trans.wipe` · `trans.wipe_clock` · `trans.wipe_star` · `trans.zoom_through`

**And the shape of that list is the finding, not its length.**

**First: every card where the camera passes through something is on it.** `trans.through_object`, `trans.through_wall`, `trans.pupil`, `trans.zoom_through`, `trans.into_screen`, `trans.water`, `trans.mirror`, `trans.smash_zoom`, `trans.infinite_zoom`, `trans.scale_jump` and `trans.drone_descent` all yield outward and nothing yields inward. The reason is structural, not an oversight: a pass-through is not an alternative to anything, because it needs a **particular object in a particular place** and the reader comparing two joins is comparing two things that could be done to any pair of shots. **The pair graph is a graph of joins you can apply; the pass-through family is a graph of joins the location has to grant you.** That is why axis two exists and why it asks *when did you have to decide* rather than *what does it look like* — it is the only question on this card that can see that family at all.

**Second: the shelf has a basement everyone leaves and nobody is sent to.** `trans.wipe`, `trans.wipe_clock`, `trans.wipe_star`, `trans.blinds`, `trans.cube`, `trans.page_turn`, `trans.iris` and `trans.mask_split` are all unasked, and every one of them carries an outbound edge, most of them to the same card on the neighbouring shelf. **Nothing in this package is ever an argument for a star wipe.** A region with outbound edges and no inbound edges is not a region of the shelf, it is a one-way exit, and a reader who arrives there arrived by browsing rather than by deciding. It is left in the map because the package carries no line excluding it and because a piece openly quoting that era is a real brief — but it should be entered on purpose, not fallen into.

**Third, and it is the new card this shelf wants.** `trans.fade_in` is unasked and it can never be otherwise: it is the only join in the family with no outgoing shot, and a pair field compares two answers to the same slot. That exposes the larger gap. **Twenty-five of the cards on this shelf put their real warning on the second use, not the first** — *if every cut in the film is a dissolve it means you never decided anything*, *repeated slides make the whole piece look like a template*, *do not repeat it more than twice in one video*, *do not repeat it often, the device wears out fast*. That is more than a third of the shelf saying the same thing, and **the shelf has no card for it**. Only `trans.dissolve_chain` and `trans.ken_burns` describe a run of joins rather than a single one, and neither is yielded to by anything. The card this shelf wants is the one that asks *how many of these are already in this piece* — and until it exists, every card here answers a question about one join while recording, in its own small print, that the problem was never the first one.

### Dissolve / Cross Dissolve

**Also called:** mix, cross fade  
**What it is:** The first shot fades out while the second fades in over it, so for a moment both images live in the same frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** It says "time passed" or "these two things are connected". The duration is the meaning: six to twelve frames is barely visible, twenty four frames reads as a short passage of time, and beyond forty eight it becomes reflection and memory.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Passage of time, memory, the end of a chapter, and documentary work between archive images. In heritage work it is the default move between a carving and the real place. In interviews it lets you join two answers from the same guest without a jump.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Series, Feature Film, Interview / Video Podcast, Video Essay, Wedding / Event Film, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Music Video | genres: Drama, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Romance, Melodrama, Tragedy, Nature / Wildlife, Science / Educational, Faith / Biblical Epic  
**Avoid when:** In an action scene a dissolve kills the energy instantly. And if every cut in the film is a dissolve it means you never decided anything, and the transition has become laziness.  
**Prompt:** `editor: cross dissolve over twenty four frames, aligning the strongest shapes of both shots so the overlap reads as one image`  
**Yields to:** `cut.hard` — Action scene, where a dissolve kills the energy.  
`trans.dissolve`

### Slow Dissolve

**Also called:** long dissolve, lap dissolve  
**What it is:** A dissolve running from two to five seconds, so both images stay superimposed long enough that the overlap itself becomes a third image.  
**Effect on the audience:** It creates a dream, a memory or a death. Time loses its edges and the viewer feels both images belong to the same mind.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The poetic flashback, the moment of dying, the last image of the film, and the opening of a reflective documentary. In heritage work the face of a statue dissolves slowly onto a living face, or ruins dissolve onto the city when it still stood. Design both compositions so the eyes align at the midpoint of the dissolve.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Feature Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Experimental / Art Film, Music Video, Short Film, Video Essay, Wedding / Event Film | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Drama, Tragedy, Faith / Biblical Epic, Psychological / Arthouse, Surreal / Absurdist, Romance  
**Avoid when:** If the two compositions fight each other the midpoint becomes visual mud and looks cheap. In short form and vertical it is suicide: five seconds of dissolve is half the video.  
**Prompt:** `editor: dissolve over three to five seconds, matching the eye positions of both shots so the superimposed midpoint is its own composition`  
**Yields to:** `trans.dissolve_fast` — Short form or vertical; five seconds is half the video.  
`trans.dissolve_slow`

### Fast Dissolve

**Also called:** soft cut, short mix  
**What it is:** A short dissolve of six to twelve frames, so brief the viewer barely registers it, closer to a soft cut than to a true dissolve.  
**Effect on the audience:** It softens the join without announcing a passage of time. It absorbs the jolt between two shots whose framing or exposure do not match.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Between two angles of the same interview, between calm nature shots, and in documentary montage where a hard cut lands too violently. It is the cleanest fix for a jump in an interview when you have no cutaway. In calm advertising it delivers a premium feel without slowing anything down.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Interview / Video Podcast, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Corporate / Training, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Video Essay, News Report / Reportage, Wedding / Event Film, Commercial / TVC | genres: Verite / Observational, Science / Educational, Nature / Wildlife, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Drama, Historical / Biopic, True Crime, Family  
**Avoid when:** On every cut it makes the whole piece soft and characterless. In drama it is a weak compromise: decide, either a hard cut or a real dissolve.  
**Prompt:** `editor: dissolve over eight frames only, just enough to take the edge off the join without reading as a time change`  
**Yields to:** `cut.hard` — Drama, where a soft cut is a weak compromise.  
`trans.dissolve_fast`

### Additive Dissolve

**Also called:** add dissolve, bright dissolve  
**What it is:** A dissolve where the brightness of both images adds up instead of averaging out, so the midpoint blooms bright before the new shot settles.  
**Effect on the audience:** It delivers light, energy and dream. The bloom at the midpoint reads as an ascent or a spiritual flare, not as ordinary passing time.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The glowing flashback, happy memory scenes, luxury advertising for perfume and jewellery, and title sequences. In music video it fits the bridge, and in heritage work it carries the crossing from the present into a glorious past.  
**Best in:** formats: Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Fashion Film, Music Video, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Feature Film, Wedding / Event Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Trailer / Teaser | genres: Romance, Fantasy / Epic, Faith / Biblical Epic, Melodrama, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Musical, Family, Drama  
**Avoid when:** If both shots are already bright the midpoint blows out and all detail disappears. In serious documentary and realist drama it plays as overblown and artificial.  
**Prompt:** `editor: use an additive dissolve so the midpoint blooms brighter than both shots, and keep it under one second to protect the highlights`  
**Yields to:** `trans.dissolve` — Serious documentary, realist drama, or two already-bright shots.  
`trans.additive_dissolve`

### Match Dissolve

**Also called:** morphing dissolve, shape dissolve  
**What it is:** A dissolve between two shapes matched in size and position: a young face dissolving into the same face grown old, a tree dissolving into the same tree in winter.  
**Effect on the audience:** The change happens in front of the viewer's eyes, so it feels like one thing transforming rather than one shot replacing another.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Years passing across a face or a place, and the comparison between two states of the same location in heritage documentary, the temple today and the temple in reconstruction. It is one of your strongest heritage tools: shoot on the same lens, the same height and the exact same angle. In advertising it is the before and after.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Video Essay, Feature Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Corporate / Training, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Science / Educational, Nature / Wildlife, Drama, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Family, Faith / Biblical Epic  
**Avoid when:** If the angle or the lens differs even slightly the shapes will not align and the dissolve reads as an error rather than a device. It usually wants both shots locked off, because any wobble breaks the match; the exception is a matched moving pair travelling the same path, or a shot stabilised in the edit before the dissolve.  
**Prompt:** `editor: dissolve between two locked off shots framed identically, so the shape stays still while the material of the image changes`  
**Yields to:** `cut.graphic_match` — The two framings cannot be made to align.  
`trans.match_dissolve`

### Dissolve Chain

**Also called:** chained dissolves, montage dissolves  
**What it is:** A run of shots joined by one dissolve after another with no hard cut at all, usually over music or narration.  
**Effect on the audience:** It melts a whole stretch of time into one mass. The viewer stops counting shots and feels a single continuous mood.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The journey, travel, season, healing or years passing montage. In nature and heritage documentary it sits under calm narration. In wedding and travel films it is the classic shape for the whole day in one minute.  
**Best in:** formats: Wedding / Event Film, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Corporate / Training, Music Video, Feature Film, Video Essay, Title Sequence / Opening Credits | genres: Romance, Nature / Wildlife, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Family, Drama, Science / Educational, Faith / Biblical Epic, Melodrama  
**Avoid when:** Beyond about thirty seconds the chain becomes sleep. And do not use it when the shots are not related in mood; a dissolve cannot repair a weak selection.  
**Prompt:** `editor: chain six to ten shots with one second dissolves and no hard cuts, keeping the exposure and the colour consistent across the chain`  
`trans.dissolve_chain`

### Fade In

**Also called:** fade up, fade from black  
**What it is:** The image rises gradually out of black, or out of any colour, over one to three seconds.  
**Effect on the audience:** It says "we begin". It gives the viewer a beat to breathe and enter the new world without a shock.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The first shot of a film or an act, the opening of a documentary, and the exit from a title sequence. In heritage work a slow fade up on the site with the music entering builds respect before a single word. In episodic television it resets the viewer's attention after a break.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Drama Series Episode, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Short Film, Limited Series, Corporate / Training, Documentary Series | genres: Drama, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Faith / Biblical Epic, Tragedy, War, Nature / Wildlife, Mystery / Whodunit  
**Avoid when:** In reels and short form it usually costs you the viewer, because the first frame is what holds the scroll; the exception is a subscribed audience that has already committed, where half a second of fade sets a tone. And opening every scene with a fade makes the whole episode breathe for no reason.  
**Prompt:** `editor: fade up from black over one and a half seconds, timing the full exposure to land with the first beat of the music`  
**Yields to:** `vertical.cold_cut` — Reels, where the first frame holds the scroll.  
`trans.fade_in`

### Fade Out / Fade to Black

**Also called:** fade down, fade to black  
**What it is:** The image sinks gradually until the screen is fully black, usually with the sound coming down with it.  
**Effect on the audience:** It says "something has ended". It is a large piece of punctuation: the end of a chapter, of a life, or of an idea.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of an act, the end of a film, the beat after a death or a farewell. In documentary it separates the big chapters. The difference from a cut to black: the fade gives the viewer time to say goodbye, the cut snatches the picture away.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Short Film, Documentary Series, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Soap Opera / Telenovela | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Melodrama, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), War, Faith / Biblical Epic, Romance, Mystery / Whodunit  
**Avoid when:** Used between every two scenes the film becomes a series of endings and loses all momentum. In suspense it gives the viewer a rest you do not want them to have.  
**Prompt:** `editor: fade to black over two seconds and let the sound fall with the picture, holding two seconds of black before the next scene`  
**Yields to:** `cut.to_black` — When the end should snatch the picture, not say goodbye.  
`trans.fade_out`

### Fade to White

**Also called:** white out, fade up to white  
**What it is:** The image fades gradually to a white screen instead of a black one, often with the sound continuing or turning into a ringing tone.  
**Effect on the audience:** White is excess, not absence: death as a crossing, a dream, a birth, a revelation or a loss of consciousness. It also reads as heaven, and as a blast whiting out the frame.  
**Used for and where it works best:** A spiritual or hopeful ending, the entrance to a memory, the beat of fainting or anaesthesia. It suits faith films, fantasy and religious heritage work. In advertising it is a clean exit into a white card carrying the logo.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Commercial / TVC, Short Film, Music Video, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Product Video / Motion Design, Drama Series Episode, Anime (Japanese 2D) | genres: Faith / Biblical Epic, Fantasy / Epic, Drama, Romance, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Melodrama, Sci-Fi, Supernatural Horror, Family  
**Avoid when:** On a sad ending it delivers deliverance rather than loss, which is the opposite of what you want. And long full white on modern screens punishes the eye.  
**Prompt:** `editor: fade to pure white over one and a half seconds, keeping a single sustained sound through the white before the next scene opens`  
**Yields to:** `trans.fade_out` — A sad ending; white delivers deliverance, not loss.  
`trans.fade_white`

### Fade to Colour

**Also called:** colour out, fade to red  
**What it is:** The fade goes to a saturated colour instead of black or white: red, blue, sand yellow, green.  
**Effect on the audience:** The colour carries the meaning: red is violence, rage and blood; blue is cold, grief and a cold death; sand yellow is memory, desert and deep time.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of an act in a film with a clear colour language, and the beat of rage, bleeding or drowning. In heritage work, fading to clay or sand before moving to another era. In music video and trailers it punctuates between blocks.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Music Video, Trailer / Teaser, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Heritage / Historical Documentary, Experimental / Art Film, Commercial / TVC | genres: Horror, Thriller / Suspense, War, Psychological / Arthouse, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Fantasy / Epic, Surreal / Absurdist, Action, Gothic / Vampire  
**Avoid when:** If the colour has no relationship to the film's language it reads as an added effect. One or two colours across a whole piece is usually the ceiling, because each new colour weakens the meaning stored in the first; the exception is a work built on a declared colour map where every chapter owns its own colour.  
**Prompt:** `editor: fade to a saturated colour card over one second, hold it briefly, then fade up into the next scene from the same colour`  
**Yields to:** `trans.fade_out` — The colour has no place in the film's language.  
`trans.fade_colour`

### Dip to Black

**Also called:** dip to colour, quick black  
**What it is:** A fast fade to black and an immediate rise back to picture in one motion, all inside a second, so it plays as a short black blink between two shots.  
**Effect on the audience:** It separates without stopping. Lighter than a full fade and heavier than a cut, it says "wait, look, something changed".  
**Used for and where it works best:** Between segments in documentary or explainer work, between hours or days inside the same scene, and between trailer blocks. In episodic television it is the small internal separator. In vertical it works only if it is very short, eight frames is enough.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Documentary Series, Explainer, Corporate / Training, Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Video Essay, News Report / Reportage, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | genres: Science / Educational, True Crime, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Drama, Political Thriller, Mystery / Whodunit, Verite / Observational  
**Avoid when:** As a habit between every two facts it becomes a tiring stutter and looks like a template. And in a tense scene any black bleeds the pressure out.  
**Prompt:** `editor: dip to black for eight to twelve frames total, down and back up in one move, with the sound continuing through the black`  
**Yields to:** `cut.hard` — A tense scene, where black bleeds the pressure out.  
`trans.dip_black`

### Luma Fade

**Also called:** luminance transition, luma dissolve  
**What it is:** The transition is driven by pixel brightness: the darkest parts of the image leave first and the brightest last, or the reverse, so the second shot appears through the light.  
**Effect on the audience:** A transition softer and richer than a dissolve, where the light itself appears to be carrying in the new shot.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Music video, advertising and reels, especially between a night shot and a day shot, or between two practical light sources. It is beautiful with candles, fire, neon and the sun. In heritage work it carries you from a torch inside the temple to daylight in the courtyard.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Fashion Film, Trailer / Teaser, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Wedding / Event Film, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Musical, Romance, Fantasy / Epic, Drama, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Sci-Fi, Cyberpunk, Gothic / Vampire  
**Avoid when:** If both shots sit at the same brightness it will not work and you get a dirty dissolve. And avoid it in serious documentary, it reads as a social media effect.  
**Prompt:** `editor: apply a luma based transition so the dark areas drop out first and the new shot emerges through the highlights, over about twenty frames`  
**Yields to:** `trans.dissolve` — Serious documentary, or two shots at equal brightness.  
`trans.luma_fade`

### Iris In / Iris Out

**Also called:** iris wipe, circle wipe  
**What it is:** A circle closes down onto one point in the frame until everything is black, or opens out from a point until it fills the screen.  
**Effect on the audience:** It concentrates attention on a single point and then closes on it. It carries the flavour of silent cinema and classic cartoons, so it reads as playful or nostalgic.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of a cartoon episode or kids content, parody comedy, and anything imitating an older era. Star Wars used it as part of its borrowed nineteen thirties serial language. In kids video it is a gentle ending a child understands immediately.  
**Best in:** formats: 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Kids Content, Meme / Comedy Edit, Feature Film, Short Film, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Stop Motion, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Drama Series Episode | genres: Comedy, Family, Preschool / Early Learning, Fairy Tale, Adventure, Dark Comedy / Satire, Slice of Life, Western  
**Avoid when:** In serious drama and modern documentary it is completely dead and reads as unintentional parody. Use it only when the whole style agrees with it.  
**Prompt:** `editor: close a circular iris down onto the subject over one second until the frame is black, then open the next scene the same way`  
**Example:** Looney Tunes, the iris closing on the ending card.  
**Yields to:** `trans.fade_out` — Serious drama or modern documentary.  
`trans.iris`

### Wipe

**Also called:** linear wipe, bar wipe  
**What it is:** A line travels across the screen, horizontal, vertical or diagonal, swapping the image behind it, so the new shot pushes the old one out of frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** It announces clearly that we have moved to another place or another time. It is frank, confident and theatrical, and it never tries to hide itself.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Classic epics, kids content, comedy and anything imitating a nineteen seventies style. Akira Kurosawa built half his language on the wipe and Star Wars took it from him. In heritage work it fits only when the style is openly theatrical rather than realist.  
**Best in:** formats: Kids Content, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Feature Film, Meme / Comedy Edit, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Corporate / Training, Explainer, Drama Series Episode, Stop Motion | genres: Adventure, Family, Comedy, Samurai / Chanbara, Fantasy / Epic, Western, Fairy Tale, Preschool / Early Learning, Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** In serious drama and modern documentary it plays as cheap and dated. And once you use one, commit to it as a language, because a single wipe in the middle of a cut driven film looks like a mistake.  
**Prompt:** `editor: wipe with a hard edged line travelling left to right over twenty frames, no feather and no drop shadow`  
**Example:** Seven Samurai, the wipes between sequences.  
**Yields to:** `cut.hard` — Serious drama, or one wipe inside a cut-driven film.  
`trans.wipe`

### Clock Wipe

**Also called:** radar wipe, radial wipe  
**What it is:** A line pivots from the centre of the screen and sweeps around like a clock hand, revealing the new shot as it turns.  
**Effect on the audience:** It says "time passed" as literally as possible, because the shape is a clock. It is so literal that it reads as childlike or as parody.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Educational kids content, simple explainers, and parody imitating old television. In children's videos about the day, the week or the clock it is both readable and useful. In comic advertising it works as a joke about waiting.  
**Best in:** formats: Kids Content, Explainer, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Meme / Comedy Edit, Corporate / Training, Stop Motion, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form | genres: Preschool / Early Learning, Family, Comedy, Science / Educational, Fairy Tale, Dark Comedy / Satire, Slice of Life  
**Avoid when:** In any serious professional work this transition is dead and destroys credibility instantly. Use it only when children are the audience or parody is the point.  
**Prompt:** `editor: sweep a radial wipe from the twelve o clock position around the centre over one second to reveal the next shot`  
**Yields to:** `trans.dissolve` — Serious work; the dissolve says "time passed" unironically.  
`trans.wipe_clock`

### Push

**Also called:** push transition, slide push  
**What it is:** The new shot enters from one edge and physically pushes the old one out, so both images travel together like slides.  
**Effect on the audience:** It gives direction, movement and order: right means forward, left means back, up and down mean levels.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Presentations, corporate video and explainers, and moving between blocks in reels. In comparison content, before and after or option one and option two, it delivers a clear spatial meaning. In heritage work it suits maps: pushing from one city to the next across the same map.  
**Best in:** formats: Corporate / Training, Explainer, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Product Video / Motion Design, News Report / Reportage, Video Essay, Kids Content, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Commercial / TVC | genres: Science / Educational, Family, Comedy, Political Thriller, Preschool / Early Learning, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** In narrative film and cinematic documentary it looks like a slide deck and breaks the illusion. In drama it is one of the clearest marks of amateur editing.  
**Prompt:** `editor: push the new shot in from screen right over fifteen frames, moving both images together at the same speed`  
**Yields to:** `cut.hard` — Narrative film or cinematic documentary.  
`trans.push`

### Slide

**Also called:** slide over, cover transition  
**What it is:** The new shot slides in over the old one and covers it, while the old shot stays still underneath without moving.  
**Effect on the audience:** Lighter than a push because only one layer moves. It gives a sense of stacking content rather than replacing a place.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Reels, teaching content and list videos, number one, number two, number three, and product video showing options. In vertical, a slide from the bottom up imitates the viewer's own thumb movement, so it feels native.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Explainer, Product Video / Motion Design, Corporate / Training, Kids Content, Video Essay, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Commercial / TVC, News Report / Reportage | genres: Science / Educational, Comedy, Family, Preschool / Early Learning, Slice of Life, Political Thriller  
**Avoid when:** In cinematic storytelling it looks like an app interface, not a film. And repeated slides make the whole piece look like a template.  
**Prompt:** `editor: slide the new shot up over the previous one across twelve frames, keeping the underlying shot static`  
**Yields to:** `cut.hard` — Cinematic storytelling, where it reads as an app interface.  
`trans.slide`

### Mask and Split Screen Transition

**Also called:** masked transition, split screen wipe  
**What it is:** The new shot enters inside a shape, a line, a circle, a letter, the outline of a building, instead of a rectangle, or the screen splits between two shots before one of them takes the whole frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** It makes the transition part of the design rather than a join. It also ties content to form, especially when the shape itself means something in the story.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Title sequences, advertising, music video and modern documentary. In heritage work the new shot can be revealed inside the shape of an arch or inside a cuneiform wedge. For comparisons and phone calls, half the screen each and then a cut to one of them.  
**Best in:** formats: Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Commercial / TVC, Music Video, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Video Essay, Product Video / Motion Design, Corporate / Training, Trailer / Teaser, Explainer | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Crime / Procedural, Political Thriller, Musical, Science / Educational, Thriller / Suspense, Drama  
**Avoid when:** If the shape has nothing to do with the content it becomes software showing off. And complex shapes are unreadable on a small screen.  
**Prompt:** `editor: reveal the incoming shot inside a meaningful shape mask that grows to fill the frame over twenty frames`  
**Yields to:** `trans.push` — Comparisons on a phone, where a complex mask is unreadable.  
`trans.mask_split`

### Title Card Transition

**Also called:** intertitle, chapter card  
**What it is:** A text card on a plain background placed between two scenes: a chapter name, a date, a place, a line or a single word.  
**Effect on the audience:** It divides the film into chapters and gives the viewer order and rest. The text itself can also be an ironic comment or a frightening prophecy.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Documentary and heritage work, the year and the place before every chapter, chapter driven features, and true crime. In reels and explainers one large word between blocks holds the viewer watching without sound. The typeface and its movement must belong to the world of the film.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Series, Feature Film, Video Essay, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Explainer, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Trailer / Teaser, News Report / Reportage | genres: Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), True Crime, Dark Comedy / Satire, Drama, Science / Educational, Political Thriller, War, Mystery / Whodunit  
**Avoid when:** Too many cards and the film becomes something you read rather than watch. And text that explains what the picture already shows insults the viewer.

### Ken Burns Move on Stills

**Also called:** pan and scan on stills, photo move  
**What it is:** A slow zoom or slow drift inside a high resolution still, so a motionless photograph becomes a moving shot. It carries the name of the documentarian Ken Burns.  
**Effect on the audience:** It gives life and time to a dead image, and makes the viewer's eye travel inside it instead of reading it in one glance.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Historical and heritage documentary with no footage, archive photographs, manuscripts and reliefs. The rule is that the move starts or ends on the detail the narration is talking about, the face, the hand, the inscription. In family and wedding films the same applies to old photographs.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Documentary Series, Video Essay, Wedding / Event Film, News Report / Reportage, Corporate / Training, Explainer, Title Sequence / Opening Credits | genres: Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), True Crime, Science / Educational, Family, War, Faith / Biblical Epic, Political Thriller, Nature / Wildlife  
**Avoid when:** If every photograph moves in the same direction at the same speed the film goes flat after five images. And pushing more than about twenty percent destroys the resolution.

### Freeze to Photo

**Also called:** freeze frame to still, snapshot transition  
**What it is:** The motion freezes on one frame, and then that frozen frame becomes a photograph: a white border appears around it, it prints, or it is pulled off the screen.  
**Effect on the audience:** It turns a living moment into a memory. The viewer moves instantly from "this is happening" to "this happened and it is over".  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of a film or an act, character introductions in titles, and documentary moving from a reenactment into the real archive photograph. ### Natural Wipe / Object Wipe

**Also called:** whip wipe, body wipe, foreground wipe  
**What it is:** An object crosses in front of the lens and completely fills the frame, a shoulder, a column, a car, a cloak, a wall, and on the other side of it we are somewhere new.  
**Effect on the audience:** The transition looks entirely natural, as if the camera travelled and never cut. It is the strongest free transition in cinema.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The best transition in AI video by a distance: ask the model for an object passing in front of the lens in the final second of the clip, and the same at the head of the next one. In live shooting you simply have someone cross very close to the lens at the end of the take. It is the backbone of long single takes and crowded scenes.  
**Best in:** formats: AI-Generated Clip, Feature Film, Music Video, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Commercial / TVC, Short Film, 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Trailer / Teaser, 360 / VR | genres: Action, Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Fantasy / Epic, Adventure, Musical, Historical / Biopic, Sci-Fi  
**Avoid when:** If the crossing speed differs between the two shots the illusion breaks immediately. And the object must fill the entire frame; a sliver of light at the edge exposes the join.  
**Prompt:** `a figure passes very close across the lens and fills the frame completely in the last half second, same direction and speed at the start of the next shot`  
`trans.object_wipe`

### Whip Pan Transition

**Also called:** swish pan, snap pan transition  
**What it is:** The camera whips horizontally so fast the image becomes streaks, the change happens inside the blur, and the camera settles in a different place.  
**Effect on the audience:** Energy, playfulness and speed. The viewer feels the camera teleported, and the transition itself becomes part of the film's mood.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Comedy, advertising, reels, cooking and travel video, and the signature style of Edgar Wright and Wes Anderson. In fast documentary it links two locations in the same city. In AI video, ask for a very fast horizontal camera move at the tail of one clip and the head of the next, in the same direction.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Commercial / TVC, Music Video, Feature Film, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Meme / Comedy Edit, Trailer / Teaser, AI-Generated Clip, Kids Content | genres: Comedy, Action, Rom-Com, Musical, Adventure, Dark Comedy / Satire, Spy / Espionage, Family, Heist  
**Avoid when:** It differs from cutting inside a whip: there you cut in the blur, here the transition is designed in the shooting itself. In heavy drama and heritage documentary it plays as light and juvenile.  
**Prompt:** `fast horizontal whip pan to the right that blurs the frame into streaks, then settles on a new location moving in the same direction`  
**Yields to:** `cut.in_whip` — The whip was not designed in the shoot.  
`trans.whip_pan`

### Whip Tilt Transition

**Also called:** vertical whip, snap tilt transition  
**What it is:** The same idea as a whip pan but vertical: the camera snaps up or down at high speed, the image smears vertically, and the change happens inside the blur.  
**Effect on the audience:** It reads as a move between levels rather than between places: ground to sky, feet to face, floor to floor.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Vertical video and reels are its natural home, because the frame is already vertical and a tilt fills the screen. In advertising: from the shoe to the face in one strike. In heritage work: from the base of the ziggurat to its summit, and on to another place.

**Avoid when:** If the direction reverses between the two shots, up then down, the viewer feels the error. And in a wide horizontal frame it is weaker than a whip pan.  
**Prompt:** `fast upward whip tilt that smears the frame vertically, continuing upward at the start of the next shot before it settles`  
**Yields to:** `trans.whip_pan` — A wide horizontal frame, where the tilt is weaker.  
`trans.whip_tilt`

### Zoom Through Transition

**Also called:** fly through, push through transition  
**What it is:** The camera drives forward hard toward something, a window, an eye, a door, a map, a fire, passes straight through it and emerges somewhere else.  
**Effect on the audience:** It delivers an unbroken journey and ties the two places with a causal link: we entered here and came out there.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Heritage documentary, punching through an old map into the living city, advertising, title sequences and fantasy. In AI video it is an excellent transition because models handle forward push well: ask for a continuous push into the detail until it fills the frame, and start the next clip already moving forward.

### Smash Zoom Transition

**Also called:** crash zoom transition, cross zoom

**Used for and where it works best:** Reels, music video, sports advertising and fast montage. It sits on a beat or a strong word. In comedy it works as an exclamation mark. ### Spin Transition

**Yields to:** `trans.roll` — Drama, or a large screen where a fast spin nauseates.

### Roll Transition

**Also called:** dutch roll transition, tilt over  
**What it is:** The frame tilts progressively until it is turned over or nearly so, the change happens at the peak of the tilt, and the new shot starts tilted and levels out.  
**Effect on the audience:** Calmer than a spin and heavier in meaning: it reads as a reversal of fortune, a loss of balance or an entry into another world.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Moving into a dream, a memory or madness, slow music video, and fantasy worlds. In psychological horror a slow roll makes the viewer lose faith in the room before it even changes. In AI video, ask for a continuous roll in the same direction across both clips.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Feature Film, AI-Generated Clip, Experimental / Art Film, Trailer / Teaser, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, 3D CG Animation | genres: Psychological Horror, Surreal / Absurdist, Fantasy / Epic, Psychological / Arthouse, Sci-Fi, Horror, Drama, Supernatural Horror  
**Avoid when:** If the direction of the roll reverses between shots the illusion breaks. In documentary and news it destroys credibility on the spot.  
**Prompt:** `camera rolls slowly to a steep angle through the end of the shot, and the next shot begins at that same angle and levels out`  
**Yields to:** `trans.dissolve_slow` — Documentary or news, where a roll destroys credibility.  
`trans.roll`

### Defocus Transition

**Also called:** blur transition, rack out transition  
**What it is:** The shot drifts out of focus until it is a haze of colour, the change happens at the peak of the blur, and the new shot pulls back into focus.  
**Effect on the audience:** It reads as losing consciousness, entering a memory, drunkenness or sleep. Blur is the closest visual equivalent of losing mental focus.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Flashbacks, dreams, fainting, illness, and the crossing from reality into imagination. Also intoxication. It suits medical documentary and psychological drama. With generated clips, ask for a gradual defocus in the last second and a focus pull at the head of the next one.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Short Film, Documentary Feature, AI-Generated Clip, Anime (Japanese 2D), Commercial / TVC, Trailer / Teaser, Soap Opera / Telenovela | genres: Psychological / Arthouse, Drama, Melodrama, Romance, Medical Drama, Psychological Horror, Surreal / Absurdist, Supernatural Horror, Fantasy / Epic  
**Avoid when:** Daily soaps wore it out as the flashback signal, so a literal use looks dated. And if the two shots differ in exposure the seam shows at the midpoint.  
**Prompt:** `slow focus pull until the frame is a soft colour haze, then the next shot racks back into sharp focus from the same haze`  
**Yields to:** `trans.dissolve_slow` — The literal blur flashback now reads as dated soap.  
`trans.defocus`

### Through Black / Natural Black

**Also called:** pass through darkness, natural black transition  
**What it is:** The camera enters a full shadow, a tunnel or a dark doorway until the frame is naturally black, and emerges in another place or another time.  
**Effect on the audience:** A magical transition that is physically justified, so the viewer accepts it without objection and feels the journey never stopped.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Building long single takes, and caves, temples, tunnels and corridors. In heritage work you enter the shadow of the temple gate and come out in the courtyard in another era. In AI video it is the cleanest join available: make the last frame fully black and the first frame fully black.

**Prompt:** `camera moves into a doorway shadow until the frame is completely black, then emerges from the same darkness into a new space`  
**Example:** 1917, the long take stitched through dark spaces.  
**Yields to:** `trans.dark_frames` — The darkness was not planned in the shoot.  
`trans.through_black`

### Dark Frame Stitch

**Also called:** invisible transition in dark frames, shadow stitch  
**What it is:** In the edit you find the darkest frame at the tail of one shot and the darkest frame at the head of the next and cut there, without the darkness having been planned in the shoot.

**Avoid when:** If the cut point is not at the top of the arc the landing looks wrong and the viewer feels a stumble. In serious content it plays as childish.  
**Prompt:** `the subject jumps upward and the clip ends with them airborne, and the next clip begins with the same body descending into a new location`  
**Yields to:** `cut.match_action` — Serious content, where the jump plays as childish.  
`trans.jump`

### Clone Reveal Transition

**Also called:** duplicate reveal, split self transition  
**What it is:** The camera moves or a body crosses the frame, and on the other side the same person appears twice or more in the same shot with no visible cut.  
**Effect on the audience:** A pleasant visual shock: the viewer knows it is a trick, enjoys it anyway, and replays the clip to work out how it was done.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Reels, music video, advertising and inner dialogue content where a person talks to himself. Shoot on a locked camera with identical light and move the mask with the crossing body. In AI video you can simply ask for two figures with the same face in one shot.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Short Film, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Meme / Comedy Edit, AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Micro-Drama, Experimental / Art Film | genres: Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Psychological / Arthouse, Sci-Fi, Surreal / Absurdist, Rom-Com, Fantasy / Epic, Slice of Life  
**Avoid when:** If the camera drifts or the light changes the mask shows as a visible line. And do not use it when the story needs no duplication; it becomes a stunt.  
**Prompt:** `locked camera as a figure crosses the frame and reveals a second identical figure already standing in the same room`  
`trans.clone_reveal`

### Colour Flip on the Beat

**Also called:** colour invert transition, grade flip  
**What it is:** The grade changes completely on a single frame on a musical beat: greyscale to full colour, warm to cold, or the colours invert.  
**Effect on the audience:** It delivers a visual hit without a cut. The viewer feels the world itself changed rather than the shot.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Music video, reels, advertising and title sequences, and the turn of a psychological state in drama, from grey into colour when the depression lifts. In heritage work it takes you from archive black and white into colour when the scene is reconstructed.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Commercial / TVC, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Trailer / Teaser, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Feature Film, Product Video / Motion Design, Lyric Video | genres: Musical, Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Romance, Fantasy / Epic, Superhero  
**Avoid when:** With no musical or narrative reason it looks like someone playing with the grade. And do not use it more than twice in one piece.  
**Prompt:** `editor: change the grade completely on one frame on the beat, holding the same framing and the same movement across the change`  
`trans.colour_flip`

### Frame Rate Drop Transition

**Also called:** frame rate shift, stutter transition  
**What it is:** The frame rate drops suddenly just before the cut, from twenty four to twelve or six per second, so the motion stutters, and it returns to normal in the new shot.  
**Effect on the audience:** The stutter reads as a glitch, a dream or an old memory. In fast action it increases the violence, because the eye loses the smoothness of the motion.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Music video, action, horror and anime inspired content, where stepped frames are part of the animation language. In heritage work it imitates the motion of early film before entering the archive. In reels it stamps a visual signature on the beat.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Anime (Japanese 2D), Trailer / Teaser, Feature Film, Experimental / Art Film, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Meme / Comedy Edit | genres: Action, Horror, Psychological Horror, Musical, Cyberpunk, Surreal / Absurdist, Shonen Battle (anime), Martial Arts / Wuxia  
**Avoid when:** When the content needs smoothness, dance, nature, luxury advertising, the stutter ruins the feel. In documentary it looks like a technical fault.  
**Prompt:** `editor: step the frame rate down to six frames per second for the last half second, then cut and return to full frame rate`  
`trans.framerate_drop`

### Reverse Then Forward

**Also called:** rewind transition, reverse snap  
**What it is:** The shot runs backwards for a fraction of a second, the movement retreating, then it cuts and continues forward into a new shot.  
**Effect on the audience:** It feels like rewinding a tape or a brief step back in time, and it grabs attention because the eye knows the motion is wrong.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Music video, reels and comedy where you rewind to replay a moment, and teaching content where you step back and repeat. In trailers a fast rewind precedes a reveal. In AI video you can simply reverse the clip in the edit without generating anything new.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Meme / Comedy Edit, Explainer, Trailer / Teaser, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Kids Content, Commercial / TVC, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Comedy, Musical, Dark Comedy / Satire, Sci-Fi, Surreal / Absurdist, Family, Thriller / Suspense, Action  
**Avoid when:** If the shot contains water, smoke or long hair the reverse is instantly obvious. And in serious drama it breaks the illusion.  
**Prompt:** `editor: reverse the last twelve frames of the shot, then cut forward into the new shot at normal speed`  
`trans.reverse_forward`

### Zoom Into a Screen or Frame

**Also called:** into the television, into the photograph  
**What it is:** The camera pushes toward a television, a phone or a hanging photograph until it fills the frame, and then what is inside it becomes the real scene.  
**Effect on the audience:** It creates two layers of reality and links them with one movement, telling the viewer that what was inside the image is now the world.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Documentary moving from an archive photograph into a reenactment, heritage work moving from a relief into the living scene, and memory drama. In advertising it takes you from the phone screen into the world of the app. The rule is to match the angle inside the image with the shot you cut to.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Docudrama / Reenactment, Commercial / TVC, Feature Film, Explainer, Video Essay, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, AI-Generated Clip, Drama Series Episode | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Drama, True Crime, Science / Educational, Psychological / Arthouse, Family, Faith / Biblical Epic, Mystery / Whodunit  
**Avoid when:** If the angle inside the image does not match the incoming shot the illusion breaks. And you need a high resolution image, otherwise the push exposes the pixels.  
**Prompt:** `camera pushes into the framed image until it fills the screen, then the scene inside it comes alive at the same angle`  
**Yields to:** `trans.match_dissolve` — A low-resolution image the push would expose.  
`trans.into_screen`

### Cube Spin / 3D Flip

**Also called:** cube transition, 3D page flip  
**What it is:** The screen turns like the face of a three dimensional cube and the next shot appears on the other face. It comes from the effect packs of old editing software.  
**Effect on the audience:** It announces loudly that this is amateur editing. Today it no longer reads as a transition, it reads as a date stamp.  
**Used for and where it works best:** It has no place outside deliberate parody: a meme, content imitating early two thousands video, or a joke inside a video about editing itself. If you use it, it must be obvious that you are laughing at it rather than using it seriously.  
**Best in:** formats: Meme / Comedy Edit, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Explainer, Kids Content | genres: Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Surreal / Absurdist, Slice of Life  
**Avoid when:** Any serious use costs you the credibility of the entire video in one second. In paid work it is usually indefensible, because the client reads it as the cheapest option in the menu; the exception is a piece openly quoting nineties software, where the cheapness is the joke.  
**Prompt:** `editor: rotate the frame like a three dimensional cube face to reveal the next shot, only as a deliberate retro joke`  
**Yields to:** `cut.hard` — Any serious use; it reads as a date stamp.  
`trans.cube`

### Page Turn / Page Peel

**Also called:** page peel, page curl  
**What it is:** The frame curls over like the page of a book to reveal the next shot behind it, sometimes with a shadow and a bend in the paper.  
**Effect on the audience:** It says "new chapter" extremely literally. It has become the signature of nineties presentations and old family albums.  
**Used for and where it works best:** It only works when the story genuinely lives inside a book: kids content reading a tale, a wedding album piece with a deliberately old feel, or knowing parody. In children's content the page is real in the story, which makes it acceptable.  
**Best in:** formats: Kids Content, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Wedding / Event Film, Meme / Comedy Edit, Explainer, Stop Motion | genres: Fairy Tale, Preschool / Early Learning, Family, Comedy, Fantasy / Epic  
**Avoid when:** In drama, documentary and professional advertising it is completely dead. And if you use it in kids content, make paper part of the whole design rather than a lone effect.  
**Prompt:** `editor: curl the frame over like a turning page to reveal the next shot, only when the story itself lives inside a book`  
**Yields to:** `trans.title_card` — Serious work needing "new chapter" without the paper.  
`trans.page_turn`

### Star Wipe

**Also called:** star transition  
**What it is:** A star shape grows or shrinks at the centre of the screen and reveals the next shot inside it.  
**Effect on the audience:** It became a joke in the nineteen eighties. Any serious use reads instantly as unintentional parody.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Memes, parody, very playful kids content and comic award segments. If you use it in children's content, surround it with music and design from the same playful world, never inside a serious teaching piece.  
**Best in:** formats: Meme / Comedy Edit, Kids Content, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | genres: Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Preschool / Early Learning, Family, Surreal / Absurdist  
**Avoid when:** Any serious use in documentary, drama or advertising destroys credibility. Reaching for it just to vary your transitions is where it goes wrong, because it drags a whole era in with it; the exception is a piece that is deliberately quoting that era.  
**Prompt:** `editor: expand a star shape from the centre to reveal the next shot, only as an intentional retro gag`  
**Yields to:** `cut.hard` — Any serious use in documentary, drama or advertising.  
`trans.wipe_star`

### Blinds and Checkerboard Wipe

**Also called:** venetian blinds, checkerboard transition  
**What it is:** The image breaks into horizontal slats or squares, and each piece flips or disappears to reveal the next shot beneath it.  
**Effect on the audience:** It shreds the image into pieces and kills any illusion or emotion. It reads as an old slide presentation rather than a film.  
**Used for and where it works best:** It has no place outside parody or content imitating old office presentations. If the video is itself about the nineteen nineties or about amateur editing, it works as a joke.  
**Best in:** formats: Meme / Comedy Edit, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Explainer, Corporate / Training, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | genres: Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Surreal / Absurdist, Slice of Life  
**Avoid when:** Any serious use demolishes the credibility of the video instantly. In paid work do not open that folder at all.  
**Prompt:** `editor: break the frame into slats or squares that flip to reveal the next shot, only as a deliberate retro joke`  
**Yields to:** `cut.hard` — Any serious use; paid work above all.  
`trans.blinds`

### Live Action to Animation Transition

**Also called:** rotoscope handover, drawn takeover, style shift transition  
**What it is:** A transition inside a shot or on a cut where the filmed image becomes a drawn one, by tracing lines over the live frame then pulling the photograph out from underneath. It can happen on the character alone or across the whole frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer feels reality being left behind for memory, dream or myth. The change carries the meaning: this is no longer something that happened, it is something being told.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Entering a mythic passage inside a heritage documentary, a childhood memory in drama, and a title sequence that moves from the cast to drawing. In advertising: the real product turning into a simple drawing to explain how it works inside.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Explainer, Documentary Feature, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Short Film, Video Essay | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Mythology / Ancient Epic, Historical / Biopic, Psychological / Arthouse, Fantasy / Epic, Magical Realism, Drama, Family  
**Avoid when:** It usually fails when the drawing style sits too far from the photograph, so the change reads as a jump rather than a passage. The exception is when the jump is declared on purpose, announcing entry into a different world with different rules.  
**Prompt:** `live action to animation transition, the filmed figure walking forward while the background lines redraw themselves around her, the photograph fading under the drawing, matched movement across the change`  
`trans.live_to_animation`

### Still to Motion Transition

**Also called:** the photograph comes alive, frame wakes up  
**What it is:** The shot opens as a completely still photograph, then one element or the whole scene begins to move from inside that same frame. It is the reverse of the freeze to photo transition, which ends in stillness instead of starting there.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer accepts the image as a document from the past, and then it moves and history itself seems to wake. The effect is strong and emotional because it breaks a rule they had already accepted.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Opening a heritage documentary on an archive photograph of a site or a family that then comes to life, a title sequence for a period series, and the animation of a painting or a relief. In advertising: the static product image starting to rotate or to open.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Documentary Feature, Commercial / TVC, Trailer / Teaser, AI-Generated Clip, Product Video / Motion Design, Video Essay, Music Video | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Drama, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Tragedy, Faith / Biblical Epic, Family, Magical Realism  
**Avoid when:** The original image is usually betrayed by movement that could not have existed when it was taken, and the documentary loses its standing. The exception is when the animation is declared as an interpretation rather than a record, said plainly in the narration or on a card.  
**Prompt:** `the shot opens as a completely static archive photograph for two seconds, then the smoke and the standing figures begin to move inside the same frame while the border of the print stays visible`  
**Yields to:** `trans.ken_burns` — Archive where invented motion would betray the record.  
`trans.still_to_motion`

### Scale Transition

**Also called:** powers of ten move, micro to macro join, scale ladder  
**What it is:** A continuous move that changes the scale of viewing by a large jump: from a grain of sand to a desert, or from a galaxy to an eye. It is built by joining successive zooms or pull outs at matching points so the whole thing reads as one move rather than a cut.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer feels their position inside a system larger than themselves. The jump between the two scales produces a quiet awe that no single shot delivers.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Science and educational openings, documentaries about nature or the universe, and the end of a film that leaves the protagonist small inside their place. In heritage work: from a small carved sign on a tablet out to the whole city, so the viewer understands that the detail belongs to a civilisation.  
**Best in:** formats: Explainer, Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Video Essay, 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Trailer / Teaser, Multi-Screen Installation | genres: Science / Educational, Nature / Wildlife, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Contemplative Science Fiction, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Historical / Biopic, Space Opera, Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** It usually turns into a technical display when the jumps multiply, tiring the eye and losing the meaning. The exception is when the enumeration is the subject, a piece about the ladder of scales itself.  
**Prompt:** `continuous scale transition, start on a single grain of sand filling the frame then pull back in one unbroken move through the dune, the desert and the coastline, matched motion at every join`  
**Example:** Powers of Ten, Charles and Ray Eames, 1977.  
`trans.scale_jump`

### Comic Panel Transition

**Also called:** panel wipe, frame in frame panels, webtoon scroll join  
**What it is:** A transition where the new shot enters as a bordered panel that pushes the previous one aside or settles beside it, the way panels sit on a comic page. It differs from the page turn, which imitates a sheet of paper flipping, because here the panels stay visible together for a beat.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer feels they are reading rather than watching. Time becomes discrete and controlled, and having more than one panel on screen allows a comparison inside a single instant.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The opening of a superhero series, an anime episode that pushes a fight into manga language, educational kids content, and turning a comic or a webtoon into video. In reels it serves three consecutive steps shown inside one vertical frame.  
**Best in:** formats: Anime (Japanese 2D), Comics / Webtoon / Photo Essay, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Kids Content, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Trailer / Teaser, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Explainer | genres: Superhero, Shonen Battle (anime), Action, Comedy, Family, Adventure, Preschool / Early Learning, Fantasy / Epic  
**Avoid when:** The panels usually stop reading when there are too many of them and each one shrinks on a phone until nothing is visible. The exception is a large screen, or holding to two panels at a generous size.  
**Prompt:** `comic panel transition, the incoming shot slides in as a bordered panel from the right while the outgoing one stays visible on the left, thin black panel borders, both readable for one beat before the panel fills the frame`  
**Yields to:** `trans.slide` — A phone, where more than two panels stop reading.  
`trans.comic_panel_wipe`

### Infinite Zoom Construction

**Also called:** nested zoom, endless zoom, recursive zoom join  
**What it is:** A run of connected zooms, each joined to the next at a small detail inside the image, so the camera appears to travel inward without end. Every picture contains the picture that follows it.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer loses their sense of scale and surrenders to the movement. The repetition produces a dreamlike or an obsessive feeling depending on the rhythm and the colour.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Music visualisers, experimental titles and interstitials in music video, and explaining a nested idea such as the layers inside an archaeological mound. In generative work it is one of the easiest structures to build, because each image is generated outward from a small crop of the one before it.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Visualizer, Music Video, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Experimental / Art Film, AI-Generated Clip, Explainer, Multi-Screen Installation, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) | genres: Surreal / Absurdist, Psychological / Arthouse, Sci-Fi, Magical Realism, Cosmic Horror, Fantasy / Epic, Science / Educational, Cyberpunk  
**Avoid when:** It usually tires the eye and makes some viewers dizzy when it runs long and accelerates. The exception is locking the speed to the pulse of the music and leaving short rests between the turns.  
**Prompt:** `infinite zoom construction, continuous push into a small bright detail in the centre of each image which opens into the next full scene, seamless joins, constant speed, no cuts`  
**Yields to:** `control.comfort_flashing` — Dizziness is a comfort pass, not a craft choice.  
`trans.infinite_zoom`
