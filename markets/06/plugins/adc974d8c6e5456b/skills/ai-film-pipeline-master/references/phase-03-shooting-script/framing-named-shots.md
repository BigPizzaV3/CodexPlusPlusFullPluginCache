# Framing, Relational and Named Shots

61 cards. Also in the other stage: a shot has a frame too. These decide a frame: what is in it, how it is lit, and what it is made of.

Every card carries what it is, what it does to the audience, where it works best, what to avoid, and the prompt written out in full.

---

### Name the Subject Before the Shot

**Also called:** the framing selector, subject before shot, what is this frame about  
**What it is:** The card at the head of the `frame.` shelf. It does not describe a look. It asks the three questions the shelf's own pair graph asks over and over — **is this frame about a relation, about one party, or about no party at all; does the lens have a clear line to it; and what is the frame refusing to give** — and hands back a named shortlist for each answer. The axes were not invented: they were read off the forty-four `Yields to:` conditions the cards carry, and the conditions that repeated became the questions. Every card cited here is cited by id.  
**Effect on the audience:** None. This card never reaches the screen. It exists so the framing that does reach the screen was chosen against the scene's relationships, its sightlines and its withholdings, rather than picked because it has a famous name.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Answer the three questions **in order and out loud, in one sentence each, before you open the shelf**. The first answer eliminates roughly three-quarters of the file; the second and third leave a shortlist of two to six. **This shelf answers a different question from the shot-size shelf and does not replace it** — a named framing says what is in the picture and how it is arranged, a shot size says how much of the subject the picture holds, and you spend both on the same shot. Twenty cards here name a size in their own prose or prompt and none of them offers a size as an alternative, which is the boundary stated by the inventory itself.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** **The decision in front of you is coverage, not a frame.** There this map is dangerous rather than merely thin: a wide master, a tight master, a triangle of setups and a shot-reverse pair are alternatives to one another at one moment, and half of them live on this shelf and half on the shot-size shelf, with no pair field anywhere joining the two halves. Ask the coverage question against both shelves at once or you will choose from half the candidates believing you saw them all - `frame.coverage_plan` is the card that asks it. Also **distrust axis two whenever your honest answer is its first value** — forty of the fifty-eight reachable cards live there, so on a clean unobstructed sightline this map has two questions left and is doing a third of the work it does on a layered one. And **it will not settle a tie it did not create**: three cards here each claim in their own prose to be the first or core answer for hidden faces, and the map routes all three to the same cell on purpose.  
**Prompt:** `[Selector — produces no prompt. It returns card ids. The chosen card carries the prompt.]`

---

**The axes**

**Axis 1 — Is this frame about a relation, about one party, about no party, or is it not one frame at all?**
Asked first, because it is the condition the shelf repeats most: sixteen of the forty-four `Yields to:` lines turn on the count of parties and what the frame does to the rank between them — *"The relationship itself is the subject"* (`frame.single`), *"The dialogue belongs to only two of them"* (`frame.three_shot`), *"An emotional beat; a group frame cools it"* (`frame.group_shot`), *"The scene is one person's solitude"* (`frame.crowd_shot`), *"The two must read as equals"* (`frame.depth_two_shot`), *"The viewer has to take a side"* (`frame.profile_two_shot`), *"One side has to read as stronger"* (`frame.fifty_fifty`), *"Both carry equal weight in the beat"* (`frame.favoring_two_shot`), *"The group has no relationship yet"* (`frame.slow_walk_group`), *"Several people introduced at once"* (`frame.hero_shot`), *"The three have no clear motives"* (`frame.leone_triangle`), *"Total isolation; any trace cancels it"* (`frame.dirty_single`).

**Axis 2 — Does the lens have a clear line to the subject, and if not, what is standing in the way?**
Twelve conditions turn on the interposed layer, and they are stated as facts about the object rather than as feelings about the picture: *"The framing element is architectural, not organic"* (`frame.natural_frame`), *"The element should enclose, not cut across"* (`frame.foreground_occlusion`), *"The object has no bearing on the outcome"* (`frame.over_the_object`), *"The scene must deliver clear information"* (`frame.glass_reflection`), *"The reflecting surface is an actual mirror"* (`frame.reflection`), *"The split is a post effect, not a real mirror"* (`frame.mirror`), *"The matte covers too much of the image"* (`frame.masked_pov`), *"The beat needs direct intimacy; glass cools it"* (`frame.through_window`), *"Text too small to read on a phone"* (`frame.screen_shot`), *"You want the hidden look but nobody is hiding"* (`frame.keyhole`), *"Warm or romantic scene, raking reads as surveillance"* (`frame.raking_ots`).

**Axis 3 — What does the frame refuse to give?**
Nine conditions are about withholding and its price, not about composition: *"The beat needs a facial performance"* (`frame.backs_to_camera`), *"The reveal is weak; show the thing"* (`frame.spielberg_face`), *"Faces stay hidden but performance is still needed"* (`frame.silhouette`), *"The shot has to identify who it is"* (`frame.hands_only`), *"Dialogue; overhead loses every eyeline"* (`frame.top_down_table`), *"A scene that needs sympathy and contact"* (`frame.profile_shot`), *"Actor cannot hold silence; fast comedy rhythm"* (`frame.listening_shot`), *"Serious emotional scene; cleverness steals the performance"* (`frame.object_pov`). It is asked last because it is the only axis that separates the withholding block at all, and because most of the shelf answers it *nothing*.

*A card appears once per axis. The answer is the intersection of the three shortlists, and it is normally two to six cards wide.*

**Two live collisions this map routes and does not settle.**

**Hidden faces in heritage work: `frame.silhouette`, `frame.back_follow` and `frame.hands_only` each claim primacy in their own prose** — *the first solution*, *the first answer*, *the core solution*. All three land in the same cell: one party, clear line, face refused. Three cards cannot all be first and nothing in the package ranks them. What the graph does say, and it is half an answer rather than a whole one: `frame.silhouette` yields to `frame.hands_only` when *performance is still needed*, so silhouette is not first where the beat has to be acted; and `frame.back_follow` is the target and the source of no edge at all, so nothing in the package has ever compared it with either. **Discriminate on what still performs** — the outline and the stance, the stride and the shoulders, or the work in the hands — and record which you chose, because the choice is currently the reader's and not the package's.

**The coverage collision, and it crosses the shelf boundary.** `frame.coverage_triangle` and `frame.shot_reverse_shot` prescribe a scene covered from a triangle of setups and cut in pairs. On the shot-size shelf, `size.wide_vs_tight_master` prescribes the opposite in as many words — cut between two masters *instead of leaning entirely on singles* — and `size.master` holds the whole scene in one setup. Those are four alternatives at one moment, split across two shelves, with no pair field joining them. Neither shelf's map can see the other half, so `frame.coverage_plan` asks the question across both and names every answer by id.

**The default, and the one against it**

**The default is `frame.single`, and it is measured rather than asserted.** Eight cards send a reader to it, more than to anything else here, and the trap that the most-yielded-to card is only a hatch does not catch it: the eight edges fire on four different conditions, not one. Isolation sends three — `frame.ots` (*"Character must feel completely alone"*), `frame.crowd_shot` (*"The scene is one person's solitude"*) and `frame.dirty_single` (*"Total isolation; any trace cancels it"*). Emotional temperature sends three — `frame.group_shot` (*"An emotional beat; a group frame cools it"*), `frame.through_window` (*"The beat needs direct intimacy; glass cools it"*) and `frame.object_pov` (*"Serious emotional scene; cleverness steals the performance"*). Contact sends one, `frame.profile_shot` (*"A scene that needs sympathy and contact"*), and identification sends one, `frame.hands_only` (*"The shot has to identify who it is"*). A card reached eight times for one reason is a door out of the shelf; this one is reached eight times for four reasons, which is what a fallback looks like. It is also one of only four cards here carrying no format or genre restriction at all.

**The one against it is `frame.empty_frame_hold`.** Nothing routes to it, it routes to nothing, and it is the only card on the shelf where the frame contains no subject whatever and the refusal is the whole content. Every other withholding card still puts a body in shot and then takes something away — `frame.silhouette` keeps the outline, `frame.back_follow` keeps the stride, `frame.hands_only` keeps the work, `frame.backs_to_camera` keeps the shared look — which is a subtraction the audience can feel being performed on someone. `frame.empty_frame_hold` never makes the promise, so it never breaks it, and the thing it withholds is the person rather than a part of them. Its only stated cost is that emptiness with no charge behind it is a dead shot — a cost paid by the shot before it, not by this one. Its near cousin against the grain is `frame.fourth_wall`, which inverts the founding assumption of every other card here, that the camera is not a party to the scene, and is likewise the target of nothing.

### Over-the-Shoulder Shot

**Also called:** OTS, over shoulder  
**What it is:** The camera sits behind one person's shoulder and looks at the other. The shoulder and the back of the head stay in the foreground, usually holding fifteen to thirty percent of one side of the frame.  
**Effect on the audience:** It ties the two people into one frame and puts the viewer inside the conversation instead of watching from outside. The foreground shoulder also works as proof that someone is listening.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Any two-hander: the confrontation, the interrogation, the negotiation, the confession. The real control is how much shoulder you give: more foreground shoulder crowds the person opposite, less of it hands them independence, so move that ratio across the scene as a live power meter.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Drama, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Legal Drama, Romance, Mystery / Whodunit, Political Thriller, Gangster, Shonen Battle (anime)  
**Avoid when:** When you want the character to feel completely alone, the shoulder breaks the isolation. In vertical 9:16 the shoulder eats half the width, so shrink it or switch to a single.  
**Prompt:** `over-the-shoulder shot, foreground shoulder soft and out of focus on the left third, the other character sharp and centered, eye level, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.single` — Character must feel completely alone; vertical 9:16.  
`frame.ots`

### Clean OTS vs Dirty OTS

**Also called:** clean single out of the OTS, dirty over  
**What it is:** Dirty means the foreground shoulder is present in the frame. Clean means the shoulder has slid out or been dropped, leaving the person alone in the frame from more or less the same angle.  
**Effect on the audience:** Moving from dirty to clean inside the same scene reads to the viewer as the relationship breaking, even when nothing in the dialogue has changed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat where the relationship breaks: the separation, the betrayal surfacing, someone pushed out of the group, the decision taken alone. A confession works the same way. Shoot the same dialogue as a dirty pair and a clean pair and let the edit switch exactly on the line that breaks it. In melodrama and family drama this is the strongest silent move you own.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Vertical Micro-Drama, Anime (Japanese 2D), Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography, Wedding Photography | genres: Drama, Melodrama, Romance, Tragedy, Crime / Procedural, Coming of Age, Thriller / Suspense  
**Avoid when:** Do not mix clean and dirty at random inside one scene with no meaning behind it, the viewer feels the inconsistency and blames the edit. And do not open on the clean, because then you have no break left to play.  
**Prompt:** `clean single at the same over-the-shoulder angle, the other person no longer in frame, subject alone against the wall, static camera`  
`frame.clean_dirty_ots`

### Raking Shot / French Over

**Also called:** raking over-the-shoulder, deep raking angle  
**What it is:** An over-the-shoulder taken at a very acute angle: the camera slides far around behind the shoulder so the foreground head is nearly in profile and the far face is seen at a narrow angle. The shoulder takes a bigger share and the distance between the two reads as longer.  
**Effect on the audience:** It makes the scene feel tense and observed, because the viewer is looking from a position nobody would naturally stand in. It also squeezes the far person between the shoulder and the frame edge.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The interrogation, the table scene with an uneven balance of power, the moment of the lie. In noir, crime and spy work it makes the room itself feel hostile to one person. In series, apply it to one character only inside a scene so it says clearly who is cornered.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Short Film, Vertical Micro-Drama, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography, Wedding Photography | genres: Noir / Neo-Noir, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Mystery / Whodunit, Political Thriller, Spy / Espionage, Psychological Horror, Drama  
**Avoid when:** In a warm or romantic scene it reads as surveillance. And do not apply it to both characters in the same scene, it cancels its own meaning.  
**Prompt:** `raking over-the-shoulder, camera far around behind the foreground head now nearly in profile, the other face compressed against the frame edge, eye level, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.ots` — Warm or romantic scene, raking reads as surveillance.  
`frame.raking_ots`

### Two Shot

**Also called:** 2S, flat two shot  
**What it is:** Two people in the same frame, at roughly the same distance from the camera, both legible. Usually a medium or medium wide.  
**Effect on the audience:** As long as both are in the frame, the relationship is live. The viewer reads the distance between them as a measure of emotional closeness.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The head of any dialogue scene before you go into singles, romance, friendship, partnership, two people working a task together. It is also the shot that lets you delay the cut: hold both of them longer than the viewer expects, then break to singles exactly when the relationship breaks.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Kids Content, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography | genres: Romance, Rom-Com, Comedy, Drama, Family, Slice of Life, Crime / Procedural, Coming of Age  
**Avoid when:** When the beat belongs to one person's interior, the two shot splits the attention. In vertical 9:16 a side by side two shot shrinks both faces badly, so swap it for a depth two shot.  
**Prompt:** `medium two shot, both characters sharing the frame at equal distance, clear gap between them, eye level, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.depth_two_shot` — Vertical 9:16 shrinks side-by-side faces badly; and `frame.single` — the relationship has separated.  
`frame.two_shot`

### Depth Two Shot / Stacked Two Shot

**Also called:** stacked two shot, foreground and background two shot  
**What it is:** One person close to the camera and one further back, both roughly on the same sight line. Perspective makes the near figure far bigger than the one behind.  
**Effect on the audience:** Instant hierarchy with no dialogue: the one in front is bigger and heavier, the one behind is small and under watch.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The interrogation, the boss and the employee, the king and the servant, the parent and the child confessing. Move the two figures in depth during the scene and flip the sizes when the power flips, it is the cleanest way to say the weaker one has taken over. In vertical 9:16 it is the best two shot you have, because both faces stay large.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Vertical Micro-Drama, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Drama, Noir / Neo-Noir, Political Thriller, Historical / Biopic, Psychological Horror, Gangster  
**Avoid when:** When you want the two to read as equals, depth forces a hierarchy on them. Watch the focus too, one face will go soft unless the lens is wide enough to hold both.  
**Prompt:** `stacked two shot, one figure large in the foreground facing away, the other small and sharp in the background, deep focus, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.profile_two_shot` — The two must read as equals.  
`frame.depth_two_shot`

### Profile Two Shot / Face-Off

**Also called:** face to face two shot, perpendicular two shot  
**What it is:** Both characters in side profile facing each other, with the camera perpendicular to the line between them so you see both faces edge on.  
**Effect on the audience:** A completely even confrontation. Neither has the advantage in frame, so the viewer judges the words rather than the angle. The same geometry reads as an evenly matched love.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat before the kiss, the beat before the punch, the negotiation between equals, the father and son at the moment of peace. In romance it reads as intimacy, in action and samurai it reads as balance before the explosion. Use it for one shot only in a scene so it stays an event.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Music Video, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Commercial / TVC, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography | genres: Romance, Drama, Action, Samurai / Chanbara, Martial Arts / Wuxia, Thriller / Suspense, Tragedy, Melodrama  
**Avoid when:** When you want the viewer to take a side, because it is neutral by definition. And do not run it as a dialogue master, a profile hides half of every expression.  
**Prompt:** `profile two shot, both faces in full side profile facing each other, camera perpendicular to their eyeline, shallow background, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.favoring_two_shot` — The viewer has to take a side.  
`frame.profile_two_shot`

### 50/50 Shot

**Also called:** fifty fifty, split frame two shot  
**What it is:** A tight face to face framing: both profiles sit on the two edges of the frame with the gap between them in the middle, splitting the image into two equal halves. It differs from the profile two shot by being closer and tighter, with the faces touching the frame edges.  
**Effect on the audience:** Compressed tension. The empty gap in the middle becomes the subject, and the viewer waits to see which of them crosses it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The confession between two people standing close, the goodbye, the whispered threat, the moment one hands the other news that changes everything. In drama, thriller and music video it works because the symmetry burns into memory. In vertical 9:16, rotate the idea: one face high in frame, one low.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Music Video, Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography | genres: Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Romance, Crime / Procedural, Noir / Neo-Noir, Tragedy, Psychological / Arthouse, Seinen (mature anime)  
**Avoid when:** In a long dialogue, because the symmetry freezes fast. And when one side has to read as stronger, this framing forces them into equality.  
**Prompt:** `tight 50/50 two shot, two profiles pressed against the left and right frame edges, empty space between their faces, symmetrical composition, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.depth_two_shot` — One side has to read as stronger.  
`frame.fifty_fifty`

### Three Shot

**Also called:** 3S, triangle three shot  
**What it is:** Three characters in one frame, usually staged as a triangle rather than a flat row: two forward and one back, or one raised in the middle between the other two.  
**Effect on the audience:** The spacing between the bodies is the message. Two pressed together and one apart says the one apart is the outsider or the opponent.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The alliance or betrayal inside a group, the family at the table, the crew before a decision. Move one of them a step closer or further during the scene and let the alliance shift with no dialogue. In sitcom and crime this framing replaces a whole scene of exposition.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Short Film, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Anime (Japanese 2D), 3D CG Animation, Kids Content, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Crime / Procedural, Comedy, Drama, Family, Heist, Gangster, Slice of Life, Fantasy / Epic  
**Avoid when:** Do not line them up flat in a row, it looks like a school play. And when the dialogue is between two of them, the third in frame pulls attention unless the silence is the point.  
**Prompt:** `three shot staged as a triangle, two characters close together in the foreground and the third standing apart behind them, even lighting, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.two_shot` — The dialogue belongs to only two of them.  
`frame.three_shot`

### Group Shot

**Also called:** ensemble shot, team shot  
**What it is:** Four or more in one frame, staged at different heights and depths so every face reads. Usually a wide or medium wide from a height that opens up the rows.  
**Effect on the audience:** It introduces a social unit: this is a family, this is a crew, this is a gang. It also speeds up every later scene because the relationships were understood in one image.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Introducing the crew or the family, the dinner scene, the council meeting, the final image after they survived. In series, fantasy and heist films it is the this is our people shot. In heritage documentary, the villagers or the workshop before you go in on each person alone.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, 3D CG Animation, Anime (Japanese 2D), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Kids Content, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Heist, Fantasy / Epic, Family, Drama, Comedy, War, Adventure, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Superhero  
**Avoid when:** With a crowded cast in a vertical frame the faces get crushed. And do not lean on it for emotional beats, a group frame cools an individual moment down.  
**Prompt:** `group shot of six characters staged at different depths and heights, every face visible, wide lens, eye level, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.single` — An emotional beat; a group frame cools it.  
`frame.group_shot`

### Crowd Shot

**Also called:** mass shot, sea of faces  
**What it is:** Dozens or hundreds in the frame as one texture, with no individual lead. It can be shot from above or from inside the crowd itself.  
**Effect on the audience:** It turns people into one mass with a single will: an army, a protest, an audience, a pilgrimage. It delivers scale and strips individuality at the same moment.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The speech or the announcement in front of a crowd, the army before the clash, the city celebrating or fleeing, the festival in a documentary. The strongest use is a crowd and then a cut to one face inside it, because the contrast hands that face all the meaning.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Documentary Feature, Documentary Series, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Concert / Live Event, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, News Report / Reportage, Trailer / Teaser, 3D CG Animation, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography | genres: War, Historical / Biopic, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Political Thriller, Disaster, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Dystopia / Post-Apocalyptic, Zombie, Musical  
**Avoid when:** A whole sequence of crowd with no single close face, because nobody bonds with a mass. And do not use it when the scene is about one person's solitude, the crowd swallows them.  
**Prompt:** `crowd shot, hundreds of people filling the entire frame as one moving mass, torchlight, slightly elevated camera, static`  
**Yields to:** `frame.single` — The scene is one person's solitude.  
`frame.crowd_shot`

### Single / Clean Single

**Also called:** clean single, solo shot  
**What it is:** One person in the frame with no trace of anyone else: no shoulder, no hand, no shadow. Any size, as long as the frame is clean.  
**Effect on the audience:** Isolation, or absolute focus. The viewer has nowhere else to look, so the face becomes the scene.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The emotional peak for each character, the moment of decision, the confession, hearing the bad news. In documentary interview it is the default. In drama, arrive at it after a run of over-the-shoulders so the switch itself says this person has separated from the room.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** When the relationship is the subject, cutting to singles too early kills the scene. And do not shoot an entire dialogue in singles, you get an assembly instead of a scene.  
**Prompt:** `clean single, one character alone in frame with no one else visible, medium close-up, plain background, eye level, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.two_shot` — The relationship itself is the subject.  
`frame.single`

### Dirty Single

**Also called:** dirty frame single, foreground element single  
**What it is:** One person is the subject, but a piece of someone else or something in the foreground clips the edge of the frame: a shoulder, an arm, the back of a head, the edge of a chair.  
**Effect on the audience:** It keeps the sense of the room and of presence. The subject is alone in focus but not alone in the world, so the scene stays connected.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Most naturalistic dialogue in series and drama: it buys you the intimacy of a single without cutting the thread of the relationship. In horror and thriller an unreadable foreground element creates anxiety, because the viewer cannot tell what it is or how close it is.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Limited Series, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Vertical Micro-Drama, Anime (Japanese 2D), Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography | genres: Drama, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Horror, Mystery / Whodunit, Romance, Verite / Observational, Noir / Neo-Noir  
**Avoid when:** When you want total isolation, any trace in frame cancels it. And keep the foreground element from becoming sharper or brighter than the face.  
**Prompt:** `dirty single, subject sharp in the center with an out of focus shoulder clipping the right edge of frame, shallow depth of field, eye level`  
**Yields to:** `frame.single` — Total isolation; any trace cancels it.  
`frame.dirty_single`

### Favoring Two Shot

**Also called:** favoring shot, two shot favoring  
**What it is:** A two shot that is deliberately unequal: the frame is built so one person owns the space, the focus and the light, while the other is present but secondary. On set it is called a two shot favoring whichever character carries the beat.  
**Effect on the audience:** It lets you live in one setup and still play a relationship, while telling the viewer whose moment this is. It is an editing economy and a dramatic decision at the same time.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The scene where only one of them changes and the other receives: the doctor and the patient, the detective and the witness, the mother and the daughter. In series and soap it cuts the number of setups you need without losing meaning. Flip which side is favored in the next scene when the power flips.  
**Best in:** formats: Drama Series Episode, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Feature Film, Limited Series, Vertical Micro-Drama, Short Film, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography, Wedding Photography | genres: Drama, Medical Drama, Legal Drama, Melodrama, Crime / Procedural, Romance, Family  
**Avoid when:** When both carry equal weight in the beat, favoring one of them cheats the other. And do not favor the same person all scene if the power is meant to move.  
**Prompt:** `medium two shot favoring the woman, she holds the left two thirds of frame in sharp focus and key light, the man smaller and dimmer at the right edge, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.two_shot` — Both carry equal weight in the beat.  
`frame.favoring_two_shot`

### Choosing the Coverage Plan

**Also called:** the coverage decision, how will this scene be covered, master or singles
**What it is:** The decision taken before the first setup of a dialogue or group scene: how the whole scene will be covered. The package holds its answers on two shelves, and they are alternatives at this one moment - one master that holds the scene (`size.master`), a wide and a tight master cut against each other (`size.wide_vs_tight_master`), or a triangle of setups on one side of the line (`frame.coverage_triangle`) cut as shot and reverse (`frame.shot_reverse_shot`), with `size.one_size_tighter` stepping the pair closer on the turn. The shot-size shelf and this one each hold half of them and neither map can see the other half. This card asks the question across both.
**Effect on the audience:** The plan decides what the viewer is allowed to watch. A scene held in a master keeps the room and the distance between people in view the whole time; a scene cut in pairs trades the room for faces and hands the editor every reaction; two masters keep the relationship whole without chopping it into singles.
**Used for and where it works best:** Any dialogue or group scene, answered before the location is lit, because every later setup inherits it. Ask where the scene lives. **In the room** - the geography, the distance, a location that is itself telling the story: hold it in `size.master` and cut inside it only where the scene demands. **In the relationship, over a long scene**: cut between two masters rather than leaning on singles, as `size.wide_vs_tight_master` argues. **In the reactions** - a face the viewer must read at the moment a line lands: build `frame.coverage_triangle`, cut in pairs with `frame.shot_reverse_shot`, and step down with `size.one_size_tighter` on the turn. Most scenes are a mix, and the plan names which stretch is which.
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Short Film, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Documentary Feature, Interview / Video Podcast, Vertical Micro-Drama | genres: Drama, Comedy, Romance, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Family, Legal Drama, Melodrama
**Avoid when:** The plan is being chosen by the schedule. A triangle cut in singles is the cheapest way to guarantee that a scene cuts, which is a reason to shoot it and not a reason the scene needs it - a relationship chopped into singles by default is the commonest way this decision goes wrong without anyone having made it. And when the scene is designed to play as one unbroken take there is no coverage to plan; that is a move, not a plan.
**Prompt:** `coverage plan: a wide master for the geography, a tight master for the long middle of the scene, and a two-setup triangle only for the reactions that must be read on the turn`
**Yields to:** `sig.oner` — The scene is designed to play as one unbroken take, so there is no coverage to plan.
`frame.coverage_plan`

### The Coverage Triangle

**Also called:** triangle principle, the director's triangle  
**What it is:** The camera layout for a two-hander: you pick three positions forming a triangle on one side of the eyeline, the apex holding the master or neutral shot and the two corners holding the over-the-shoulder and its reverse.  
**Effect on the audience:** It guarantees every cut in the scene works and the viewer's geography never breaks, because all three positions sit on the same side of the line.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Plan any dialogue scene as a triangle before you reach the location: an apex for the master and two corners for the pair. Then move the whole triangle to the other side when the scene turns a corner. In AI video this is the key move: describe the same room and the same light from three defined positions and you get coverage that actually cuts together.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Soap Opera / Telenovela, Short Film, AI-Generated Clip, 3D CG Animation, Anime (Japanese 2D) | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** Do not leave one leg of the triangle on the other side of the line, that cut will flip the geography. And do not jump to the opposite triangle on a hard cut, cross over on a move or through a neutral shot.  
**Prompt:** `three camera setups for this dialogue, a wide master from the apex and two matching over-the-shoulder reverses from the corners, all on the same side of the eyeline`  
**Yields to:** `angle.neutral_axis` — Crossing to the opposite triangle needs a neutral shot.  
`frame.coverage_triangle`

### Reverse Shot

**Also called:** reverse, the answering shot  
**What it is:** The shot that looks in the opposite direction to the one before it, taken from the same side of the line. If the first looks screen right, the reverse looks screen left, and its size and angle match the first.  
**Effect on the audience:** It completes the space in the viewer's mind: they see two halves and assemble one continuous location that never existed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** After any looking shot: a person looks, then the reverse on what they are looking at. In documentary and reportage it is the shot that saves you when you had one camera: film the interviewer from behind after the interview, matching size and angle. In AI video, spell out the same lighting and the opposite look direction in the prompt so the pair actually cuts.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** When its size or height does not match the first one, the pair reads broken. And do not shoot it from the other side of the line unless disorder is the point.  
**Prompt:** `reverse shot matching the previous setup, same size and height, the character now looking screen left, identical lighting, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `angle.crossing_the_line` — Shooting the other side when disorder is the point.  
`frame.reverse_shot`

### Shot / Reverse Shot Pair

**Also called:** shot reverse shot, the dialogue pair  
**What it is:** The basic structure of dialogue: a shot on the speaker, its reverse on the listener, then back and forth. The pair has to match in size, height, lens and the shape of the light.  
**Effect on the audience:** It makes the cutting invisible and lets the viewer forget the camera exists. Any mismatch inside the pair is felt as a fault even when the viewer cannot name it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Nearly every dialogue scene ever shot. The real power is breaking the match on purpose for one beat: make one character's reverse a size tighter or a touch lower when the power turns, and the viewer feels the change before they understand it. In interrogations, trials and family drama this is the engine.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** When the whole scene is nothing but the pair, with no two shot and no master, the viewer loses the room. And do not cut on every line, let some replies play on the listener's face.  
**Prompt:** `matched shot reverse shot pair, both medium close-ups at the same height and lens, key light from the same side of the room, static camera on each`  
**Yields to:** `frame.listening_shot` — Let a reply play on the listener instead.  
`frame.shot_reverse_shot`

### Driving Two Shot / Front Seat Two Shot

**Also called:** car two shot, front seat coverage  
**What it is:** Two people in the front seats of a car in one frame, camera mounted ahead of them or off the bonnet, with the street moving behind. The singles are shot from opposing sides on the same axis.  
**Effect on the audience:** Forced closeness without eye contact: the two are pressed together but looking at the road instead of each other, so the talk comes out easier and more honest.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The confession inside the car, the family argument on the road, the cop and his partner, the drive home after the funeral. In road movies and crime it is the most repeated dialogue setup there is, and in documentary it gives you a moving interview with no chair and no set.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Vertical Micro-Drama, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography | genres: Crime / Procedural, Road Movie, Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Comedy, Noir / Neo-Noir, True Crime, Coming of Age  
**Avoid when:** When you want an eye to eye confrontation, the car denies it by design. Watch for the camera reflected in the glass and for the light changing between the shot and its reverse.  
**Prompt:** `front seat two shot from the car bonnet, driver and passenger both in frame looking ahead, street lights sliding across the windscreen, locked camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.profile_two_shot` — Eye-to-eye confrontation the car denies by design.  
`frame.car_two_shot`

### Backs to Camera / Watching Together

**Also called:** over the backs, two figures from behind  
**What it is:** One or more figures seen from behind in the foreground, looking at something in the depth of the frame: a view, a fire, a sea, a body, a city. The faces are hidden and the look is shared with the viewer.  
**Effect on the audience:** It puts the viewer beside them instead of opposite them. Attention shifts to the thing being watched rather than to the reaction, so the beat becomes contemplation instead of response.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of a chapter, arriving somewhere new, standing in front of ruins or seeing the city for the first time, the last beat before leaving. In heritage documentary it is the cleanest way to hide faces and keep the emotion in the same shot.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Short Film, Music Video, Trailer / Teaser, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, 3D CG Animation, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Drama, Adventure, War, Fantasy / Epic, Survival, Tragedy, Mythology / Ancient Epic  
**Avoid when:** When the beat needs a facial performance, a back swallows it. And do not repeat it as a style, it turns into a poster cliche.  
**Prompt:** `two figures shot from behind in the foreground, standing still and looking out at the burning city below, faces not visible, wide shot, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.spielberg_face` — The beat needs a facial performance.  
`frame.backs_to_camera`

### The Profile Shot

**Also called:** side profile shot, ninety degree single  
**What it is:** One person seen fully from the side, the camera at ninety degrees to their face, so you get one eye and the line of the nose and chin.  
**Effect on the audience:** Closure. The character gives the viewer nothing and is watched rather than engaged with. At the same time it produces a sculptural, carved look, like a relief.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The beat of stubbornness or silence after a fight, the character thinking who will not answer, the person hearing a verdict. In heritage documentary it is the best portrait framing because it echoes Assyrian and Egyptian relief carving. In music video it delivers a cool, graphic portrait.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Music Video, Drama Series Episode, Fashion Film, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Drama, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Noir / Neo-Noir, Psychological / Arthouse, Tragedy, Seinen (mature anime)  
**Avoid when:** In a scene that needs sympathy, because the profile closes the face and blocks contact. And do not shoot a long dialogue in it, half the expression is lost.  
**Prompt:** `full profile single, camera at ninety degrees to the face, one eye visible, strong rim light on the nose and chin, dark background, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.single` — A scene that needs sympathy and contact.  
`frame.profile_shot`

### Reaction Shot

**Also called:** reaction, the receiving face  
**What it is:** The shot of the face receiving the news or witnessing the event, not of the person delivering it and not of the event itself.  
**Effect on the audience:** This is where the meaning lives. The viewer does not want to see the explosion, they want the face of the person who saw it, because that face tells them what to feel.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every pivot: hearing the news, seeing the body, the letter arriving, someone unexpected walking in. It is also the cheapest tool you own: you can skip filming the event entirely and carry it on a face plus sound, which is standard practice in horror, war and short films. And in any scene, stay on the listener longer than the viewer expects.  
**Best in:** formats: all | genres: all  
**Avoid when:** When you have not built an event worth reacting to, the face reads empty. And do not cut to the reaction too fast, give the face time to change.  
**Prompt:** `reaction shot, close-up on the listening face as the news lands, eyes shifting, no dialogue, soft key light, static camera`  
`frame.reaction_shot`

### The Listening Shot

**Also called:** hold on the listener, playing the scene off-face  
**What it is:** Staying on the listener's face for the whole of the other person's line or longer, instead of cutting to the speaker. It differs from a reaction shot because it is not waiting for an event, it plays the entire scene off the listener.  
**Effect on the audience:** It sends the viewer hunting through the face for the truth, so their mind works instead of receiving. A long silence held on one face builds more pressure than any line of dialogue.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The long confession, the death notification, the lawyer hearing the testimony, the mother hearing her son lie. The golden rule: hold the listener a beat longer than the viewer expects, that extra second is the depth. In documentary interview and limited series it is the strongest patience tool you have.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Limited Series, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Feature, Interview / Video Podcast, Short Film, Documentary Series, Anime (Japanese 2D) | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Legal Drama, Crime / Procedural, Melodrama, Psychological / Arthouse, True Crime, Medical Drama  
**Avoid when:** When the actor does not work in silence, an empty face exposes the scene. And do not use it in fast comedy, it kills the rhythm.  
**Prompt:** `hold on the listening face for the entire speech, medium close-up, no cutaway, micro expressions building, slow blink, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.shot_reverse_shot` — Actor cannot hold silence; fast comedy rhythm.  
`frame.listening_shot`

### Point of View Shot

**Also called:** POV, subjective shot, first person shot  
**What it is:** The camera becomes the character's eyes: it sees what they see, moves with their head and sits at the height of their gaze.  
**Effect on the audience:** The strongest identification tool in cinema. The viewer stops watching the character and becomes them.  
**Used for and where it works best:** It has to arrive as a sandwich: a shot of the character looking, then the POV, then the reaction shot, and without that order it does not read. In horror use it for the victim searching the dark, or for the monster closing in, in interactive documentary and games for entering a space, and in Reels as a hook because the viewer feels the hand is their own.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, 360 / VR, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Vertical Micro-Drama, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Horror, Thriller / Suspense, Survival, Found Footage, Action, Slasher, Adventure, Psychological Horror  
**Avoid when:** When you cannot shoot the looking shot or the reaction, it becomes an orphan shot with no owner. And do not hold it long, an extended POV tires the eye.  
**Prompt:** `first person POV shot, camera is the character's eyes, subtle head motion and breathing sway, hands entering the lower frame, handheld`  
**Yields to:** `frame.objective_view` — No looking shot or reaction to own it; and `frame.stalker_pov` — the owner of the look must stay unknown.  
`frame.pov`

### Unmotivated POV / Stalker POV

**Also called:** voyeur POV, watcher shot  
**What it is:** A point of view with no revealed owner: the camera watches the character from behind foliage, a door or a car, and we never cut to the face doing the watching.  
**Effect on the audience:** Pure dread. The viewer knows someone is watching and the character does not, so they are holding information that frightens them and can warn nobody.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The killer or the stalker's first presence before the reveal, the empty house sequence, the woman walking back to her car at night. In slasher and thriller it plants the threat without spending anything on it. Keep the motion light and unsteady so the viewer feels a body breathing behind the lens.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography, Wedding Photography | genres: Horror, Slasher, Thriller / Suspense, Psychological Horror, Mystery / Whodunit, Crime / Procedural, Found Footage, Supernatural Horror  
**Avoid when:** Repeat it too often in one film and the viewer adapts and stops being scared. And do not use it for a benign watcher, the framing turns them into a threat whether you meant it or not.  
**Prompt:** `voyeuristic POV from behind branches in the dark, leaves clipping the edges of frame, the woman small and unaware in the distance, slow handheld drift`  
**Yields to:** `frame.pov` — A benign watcher who must not read as threat.  
`frame.stalker_pov`

### Object POV

**Also called:** inanimate POV, from inside the object  
**What it is:** The camera takes the position of an object instead of a person: inside the fridge, from the bottom of the glass, from inside the box, from where the television sits, from the head of the nail.  
**Effect on the audience:** It breaks the ordinary and makes a routine action strange, funny or sinister. It also delivers an image nobody sees in life, which is why it sticks in memory.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Drinking or eating in a commercial, opening the box or the fridge in comedy, the object about to be broken or stolen in a crime scene. In advertising, product video and Reels it is the fastest way to get an image nobody has seen before at no cost.  
**Best in:** formats: Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Feature Film, Short Film, Meme / Comedy Edit, 2D Animation (Western / Cartoon), AI-Generated Clip, Kids Content, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Crime / Procedural, Thriller / Suspense, Horror, Surreal / Absurdist, Family, Slice of Life  
**Avoid when:** In a serious emotional scene a clever framing steals attention from the performance. And do not repeat it, the trick spends itself the first time.  
**Prompt:** `object POV from inside the refrigerator looking out, shelves framing the edges, the man opening the door and reaching in, cold light, locked camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.single` — Serious emotional scene; cleverness steals the performance.  
`frame.object_pov`

### Masked POV

**Also called:** binocular POV, scope shot, viewfinder POV  
**What it is:** A point of view shaped by a black matte that names the instrument: two circles for binoculars, a circle and crosshair for a rifle scope, a rectangle with data for a camera, a narrow slit for an armoured door port.  
**Effect on the audience:** It tells the viewer who is looking and with what intent, because the mask itself carries the meaning: surveillance, sniping, recording, hiding.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The stakeout before the raid, the sniper choosing a target, the camera operator recording inside a docudrama, the thief watching the house. In spy, war and found footage it is the cheapest way to deliver intent with no dialogue.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Short Film, Docudrama / Reenactment, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography | genres: Spy / Espionage, War, Thriller / Suspense, Crime / Procedural, Action, Found Footage, Mystery / Whodunit, Survival  
**Avoid when:** When the matte covers most of the image you lose the scene itself. And do not fake camera data overlays in a serious documentary, it costs you the audience's trust.  
**Prompt:** `masked POV through binoculars, two overlapping black circles framing the view, distant figures crossing the courtyard, slight handheld sway, shallow focus`  
**Yields to:** `frame.pov` — The matte covers too much of the image.  
`frame.masked_pov`

### Natural Frames

**Also called:** organic framing, foliage frame  
**What it is:** The same idea as the frame within a frame but built from non architectural elements: branches, tall grass, rocks, smoke, a moving curtain, a crowd, a standing figure.  
**Effect on the audience:** It adds depth and layers, and it makes the camera feel hidden inside the world rather than standing outside it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The exterior that feels empty: put a branch or tall grass in front of the lens and it becomes a composition. In nature and heritage documentary it is how you turn an open location into an image with a foreground, a middle and a background. Walking through a forest or a market, let elements sweep past the lens so the viewer feels they are moving with the characters.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Feature Film, Music Video, Short Film, 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip, Wedding / Event Film, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Nature / Wildlife, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Adventure, Romance, Drama, Folk Horror, Survival, Historical / Biopic  
**Avoid when:** When the foreground element moves more than the subject, the eye goes to it. And do not cover more than a third of the frame unless hiding is the point.  
**Prompt:** `natural frame, out of focus branches and long grass filling the left and bottom edges, the rider sharp in the clearing beyond, telephoto, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `comp.frame_within_frame` — The framing element is architectural, not organic.  
`frame.natural_frame`

### Doorway Frame Shot

**Also called:** door frame shot, threshold shot  
**What it is:** The subject is framed by a doorway, with the camera in one room and the person in another, so the door splits the world into an inside and an outside.  
**Effect on the audience:** The threshold itself carries the meaning: who is inside, who is outside, who decides to cross. It also delivers a sense of watching or waiting.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The moment of entering or of being refused: the mother standing in the doorway, the guest not invited in, the officer arriving with the news. In westerns, family drama and horror it is the shot of a decision. Shoot it from inside the dark room onto a lit figure so the door becomes a bright rectangle in blackness.

**Avoid when:** When the scene has no idea of crossing or of a barrier, the door is just decoration. And watch the exposure gap between the two rooms so the face does not disappear.

### Keyhole Shot / Peep Shot

**Also called:** peeping shot, through the crack  
**What it is:** The scene is seen through a narrow opening: a keyhole, a gap between two planks, the slit of a wardrobe door, the fingers of a hand. Most of the frame is black and the visible area is tiny.  
**Effect on the audience:** Sharp tension, because the viewer sees less than they want and knows the watcher is both hidden and exposed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The child hiding who sees what they should not, the victim inside the wardrobe with the killer in the room, the servant overhearing his master's secret. In horror and period thriller it is a peak shot. Hold it a little longer without cutting so the danger comes through the hider's breathing.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Short Film, Drama Series Episode, Trailer / Teaser, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography, Wedding Photography | genres: Horror, Thriller / Suspense, Slasher, Historical / Biopic, Gothic / Vampire, Psychological Horror, Mystery / Whodunit, Crime / Procedural  
**Avoid when:** When the opening is so tight the event cannot be read, you lose the information. And do not use it for the look alone, somebody has to actually be hiding.  
**Prompt:** `keyhole shot, the frame mostly black with a narrow vertical slit of light revealing two figures arguing in the lit room, shallow focus, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.stalker_pov` — You want the hidden look but nobody is hiding.  
`frame.keyhole`

### Through-the-Window Shot

**Also called:** window shot, shooting through glass  
**What it is:** The camera is outside and the subject inside, or the reverse, with glass between them. The window is an inner frame and a transparent barrier at the same time.  
**Effect on the audience:** Closeness with separation. You see the person but cannot hear or reach them, which reads as pity or as surveillance depending which side you shoot from.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The goodbye at the airport or the train, the person waiting in a cafe in the rain, the child looking out, the police watching. In drama and romance let rain on the glass work as an emotional layer. In crime, shoot from outside so the viewer becomes the watcher.

**Avoid when:** When the camera or the crew shows up in the reflection. And do not use it for a beat that needs direct intimacy, glass cools everything down.  
**Prompt:** `shot through a rain streaked window from outside, the woman sitting alone at the cafe table, droplets sharp on the glass and her face soft behind it, static camera`

`frame.through_window`

### Through-Glass-With-Reflection Shot

**What it is:** Shooting through glass at an angle that keeps the reflection visible, so two layers read at once: the person behind the glass and the street or a second person reflected over them.  
**Effect on the audience:** It builds a second meaning inside one shot: the person and their past, the person and who they are thinking about, the person and the city crushing them. No cut and no effects needed.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The scene of silence and thought, the shop window and the person who cannot afford what is inside, the passenger with the city sliding across their face, the documentary shot that ties a subject to their surroundings. ### Mirror Shot

**Also called:** mirror framing, reflection in a mirror  
**What it is:** The subject is seen in a mirror: either only the reflection, or both at once, the real body and its reflection in the same frame. It differs from the music video Mirror / Kaleidoscope, where the picture is mirrored onto itself in post: here the mirror is a real object inside the location.  
**Effect on the audience:** An internal split. The character is looking at herself, so the viewer understands there are two versions of her: the one she shows and the one she is.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Lying to yourself, getting ready before a confrontation, the moment after the crime, the character whose identity is shifting. In psychological thriller and crime, show the reflection first and reveal the real body with a small move, the reveal is the shot. In fashion film and music video a mirror gives you an elegant two shot out of one person.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Fashion Film, Short Film, Limited Series, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography | genres: Psychological / Arthouse, Thriller / Suspense, Crime / Procedural, Drama, Noir / Neo-Noir, Gothic / Vampire, Psychological Horror, Melodrama, Tragedy  
**Avoid when:** With no idea of split or identity in the scene, a mirror is just a trick. And plan the camera position so it does not appear in the glass, the answer is an oblique angle rather than shooting head on.  
**Prompt:** `mirror shot, the woman's face in the bathroom mirror sharp while her real head is soft at the edge of frame, two versions of her in one image, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `mv.mirror_kaleidoscope` — The split is a post effect, not a real mirror.  
`frame.mirror`

### Reflection Shot

**Also called:** reflected image, puddle shot, reflection in glasses, reflective surface  
**What it is:** The image is seen in a reflective surface that is not a mirror: water, a rain puddle, metal, a knife blade, a lens of glasses, a dark screen, polished marble. Sometimes only the reflection, sometimes both at once, the person and their copy.  
**Effect on the audience:** It adds a poetic layer and a slight distortion, so the image feels like a memory or a dream. On a blade or a pair of glasses it reads as threat, because the reflecting object is itself the danger, and when the person and their copy share the frame it reads as doubling and internal conflict.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Opening a scene on the reflection then tilting up to the real thing, walking a city after rain, the beat before the strike caught in a blade, the explosion in the witness's glasses, and the moment a character faces themselves before a decision or after a lie. In music video, luxury advertising and heritage documentary (a ziggurat mirrored in water, or an artefact reflected in display glass tying past to present) it delivers an image that stays. In Reels it is one of the fastest first second hooks there is.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Fashion Film, Trailer / Teaser, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography | genres: Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Crime / Procedural, Romance, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Noir / Neo-Noir, Surreal / Absurdist, Psychological / Arthouse, Action, Mystery / Whodunit, Psychological Horror, Gothic / Vampire  
**Avoid when:** When the reflection cannot be read, the shot becomes a pointless visual puzzle, and a whole scene built on reflections loses the viewer's bearings. Watch for the camera and the crew in the reflecting surface, and if it turns up in every scene it becomes a cheap symbol and loses its weight.  
**Prompt:** `reflection shot, the neon city and the walking figure mirrored in a rain puddle, camera low near the water, ripples distorting the image, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.mirror` — The reflecting surface is an actual mirror.  
`frame.reflection`

### Eyes in the Rearview Mirror

**Effect on the audience:** It gives you a pair of eyes without the rest of the face, so intent shows and everything else stays hidden. It also plants the feeling that something is behind the character, even when the road is empty.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The escape or the chase, the driver checking whether he is being followed, the in car conversation where one of them is lying, the glance back at the child in the rear seat. In crime, thriller and road movies it delivers suspense with no camera move and no dialogue.

### Screen Shot / Phone Screen Shot

**Also called:** phone screen insert, device shot  
**What it is:** A screen inside the frame: a phone, a laptop, a security camera, a television. Either the screen fills the whole frame, or it is seen in the character's hand together with her face.  
**Effect on the audience:** It delivers written or recorded information in a second with no dialogue. And when the face and the screen share one frame, the viewer gets the news and the reaction at the same time.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The message that lands at the worst moment, the call that goes unanswered, the security footage that breaks the case, the test result. In vertical micro-drama and Reels it carries the entire narrative. The rule: get the screen and the face in one frame whenever you can, because a screen alone stalls the scene.

### Silhouette Shot

**Also called:** silhouette, backlit figure  
**What it is:** The figure reads as solid black against a bright background: a sunset, a window, lit fog, fire, a white wall. No detail survives, only the outline.  
**Effect on the audience:** It turns a person into a symbol rather than an individual. It also makes the viewer fill in the blank themselves, which sometimes hits harder than a face.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The entrance of an unknown figure, the ending, the goodbye at the door, the person whose identity you are not ready to reveal. In heritage documentary and any piece where faces have to stay hidden it is the solution when the emotion lives in the stance and the outline, no face required; for a figure moving through a place take `frame.back_follow`, and where the performance is in what the hands do take `frame.hands_only`. It hides a face nobody can know, not a living person who must not be identified — that brief is handed over per `doc.anonymous_interview`. In music video and trailers it gives you a poster image.  
**Best in:** formats: Heritage / Historical Documentary, Music Video, Trailer / Teaser, Feature Film, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Drama, Thriller / Suspense, War, Horror, Fantasy / Epic, Noir / Neo-Noir

**Yields to:** `frame.hands_only` — Faces stay hidden but performance is still needed.

### Obstructed View / Foreground Occlusion

**Also called:** dirty foreground, shooting past an obstruction  
**What it is:** Something in the foreground blocks part of the subject: a person's back, a column, a curtain, a row of people, a machine. It differs from a frame within a frame because it does not enclose the subject, it cuts across and hides part of it.  
**Effect on the audience:** Anxiety and the urge to look. The viewer leans in to see past it, so they work at the shot instead of receiving it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Watching someone inside a crowd, the killer passing behind columns, the hospital seen past the nurses, the moment we are not meant to see in full. In thriller and observational documentary it makes the camera feel like it is really there rather than placed. In a violent beat, partial occlusion lands harder than showing everything.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Documentary Feature, Documentary Series, Short Film, News Report / Reportage, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Thriller / Suspense, Crime / Procedural, Verite / Observational, War, Drama, Horror, True Crime, Political Thriller, Survival  
**Avoid when:** When the obstruction covers the information the scene depends on, you trade the story for a look. And do not use it in advertising or product video, the customer has to see.  
**Prompt:** `obstructed view, the conversation glimpsed between passing bodies in the foreground, figures crossing and blocking the lens, telephoto, handheld`  
**Yields to:** `comp.frame_within_frame` — The element should enclose, not cut across.  
`frame.foreground_occlusion`

### Over-the-Object Shot

**Also called:** over the prop, object in the OTS position  
**What it is:** The same setup as an over-the-shoulder, but the foreground element is an object instead of a shoulder: a glass, a candle, a gun on the table, a statue, a keyboard, a plate of food.  
**Effect on the audience:** It makes the object a third party in the scene. The viewer forgets it is there and remembers it the second it matters, so the tension sits in the background the whole time.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The negotiation with the gun on the table, the family dinner with the empty chair, the interrogation with the file lying open. Put the object that will decide the scene in the foreground from the first shot, so the final cut to it feels inevitable rather than sudden. In crime, heritage work and documentary (an artefact in the foreground) it lands just as hard.

**Prompt:** `over-the-object shot, a revolver lying large and soft in the foreground, the two men talking sharp behind it, low camera on the table surface, static`  
**Yields to:** `frame.ots` — The object has no bearing on the outcome.  
`frame.over_the_object`

### Over-the-Head Shot / Top-Down Table Shot

**Also called:** overhead table shot, flat table view, top shot  
**What it is:** The camera hangs above a work surface and looks straight down on it: a kitchen counter, a games table, a desk, a workbench, or above a sleeping person's head. This record is about the framing choice, what belongs inside the frame, rather than about vertical angle as a concept.  
**Effect on the audience:** It turns the surface into a clean map: everything is legible at once with no perspective distortion. Combined with hands it makes the viewer feel they are the one doing the work.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Cooking, craft and explainer video, the craftsman's tools in heritage documentary, the card table in a crime scene, the insomniac lying awake. **Avoid when:** When you need faces and feeling, there are no faces from above. And do not use it for dialogue, the viewer loses every eyeline.

**Effect on the audience:** A hand lies less than a face, so the viewer believes it. It carries skill, fear or tenderness with no facial performance at all.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The craftsman in heritage documentary, the patient before surgery, the lovers' hands under the table, the man counting money. It is also the core solution when faces have to stay hidden and the performance is in what the hands do: let the hands carry the whole performance. ### Walk Into Frame

**Also called:** enter frame, step into shot  
**What it is:** The camera holds a composed empty frame and the character walks in from the edge and settles into position inside it.  
**Effect on the audience:** The arrival feels real, because the viewer saw the place before the person. The small wait before the entrance builds anticipation with no trick at all.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The head of any scene where the place carries value: the house, the grave, the temple courtyard, the empty office. In heritage documentary it is the best opening you have: let the location breathe for two seconds then let the figure step in, and you get an establisher and an entrance in one uncut shot. In Reels it hooks, because an empty frame makes people wait.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Music Video, 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Drama, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Thriller / Suspense, Mystery / Whodunit, Western, Tragedy, Verite / Observational  
**Avoid when:** In a fast sequence the wait kills the scene. And do not let the character wander in with no mark to land on, an entrance with no stop reads as lost.

### Hero Shot

**Also called:** hero framing, the introduction shot  
**What it is:** A shot designed to announce that this person is the lead: the body commands the frame, the light comes from behind or the side, the background serves them, and the stance is still and deliberate.  
**Effect on the audience:** It says pay attention, this one matters, before a single line. It also creates a visual memory the film can return to every time the character changes.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The lead or the villain's first appearance, the beat after a transformation, the last image before the ending, the trailer key art. In action, superhero and battle anime it is mandatory. In sports advertising and music video, save it for the chorus. Design it to work in silence: mute the sound and it should still say the same thing.

### Walk Away From the Explosion

**Also called:** cool guys don't look at explosions, hero walk out  
**What it is:** The character walks calmly toward the camera while the explosion or the fire goes off behind them in depth, and they never turn to look.  
**Effect on the audience:** Absolute composure. Not looking is the entire message: this person did that and does not care.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The end of an action beat, the hero leaving after wrecking the place, the trailer shot that sells the film. In action and thriller it works because it gives you slow movement and a large event in the same frame, and in advertising and music video it is the fastest hero image there is. It is also a known excess, so in dark comedy flip it: let them turn around and flinch.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Feature Film, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), 3D CG Animation, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Action, Superhero, Heist, Dark Comedy / Satire, Thriller / Suspense, War, Crime / Procedural, Shonen Battle (anime)  
**Avoid when:** In serious drama or anything realistic it snaps credibility instantly. And usually once in a piece, because a second one tells the audience it is a habit rather than a moment; the exception is a comedy built on repeating the same swagger.  
**Prompt:** `the hero walks steadily toward camera in slow motion as the building erupts in flame behind him, never looking back, low wide angle, camera slowly tracking back`  
**Yields to:** `frame.hero_shot` — Serious or realistic piece; the gag snaps credibility.

### Slow-Walk Group Shot

**Also called:** the hero walk, group slow motion walk  
**What it is:** A group walks in a line toward the camera or alongside it in slow motion, the camera low and retreating at their pace, each person locked in position in the row.  
**Effect on the audience:** A collective introduction: the group becomes a single unit and each member is given standing. It reads as confidence and as threat at once.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Introducing the crew or the gang, walking out of the building after the job, the team entering the arena. Quentin Tarantino fixed it in the opening of Reservoir Dogs and it has read instantly ever since. In sports advertising and music video it is the safest group image you can shoot.  
**Best in:** formats: Trailer / Teaser, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Feature Film, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Anime (Japanese 2D), AI-Generated Clip | genres: Heist, Crime / Procedural, Action, Gangster, Sports Drama, Comedy, Superhero, Musical  
**Avoid when:** When the group has no relationship yet, the shot promises something that does not exist. It is also heavily worn, so if you use it, put something of your own world inside it.  
**Prompt:** `low slow motion shot of five figures walking abreast toward camera, camera tracking back at their pace, wind in their coats, flat even light`  
**Yields to:** `frame.group_shot` — The group has no relationship yet.  
`frame.slow_walk_group`

### The Stare / Kubrick Stare

**Also called:** Kubrick stare, heads down eyes up  
**What it is:** A close-up or medium close-up with the head tilted down and the eyes raised into the lens or onto the victim, the brow shadowing the eyes.  
**Effect on the audience:** It announces that the character has crossed a line. Looking up from under the brow reads as madness or as premeditation, and it needs no dialogue at all.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The moment a character turns, the last frame before the crime, the villain's face in the trailer. Stanley Kubrick used it often enough that it carries his name, so it reads immediately in horror and psychological thriller. Work it with the light: key from above so the eyes sink into shadow.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography | genres: Horror, Psychological Horror, Thriller / Suspense, Crime / Procedural, Slasher, Dark Comedy / Satire, Gothic / Vampire, Seinen (mature anime)  
**Avoid when:** Before the character has actually turned, the look burns the surprise. Wrong for quiet drama and for family content.  
**Prompt:** `close-up, head tilted down and eyes raised straight into the lens, brow shadowing the eye sockets, hard top light, slow blink, static camera`  
**Example:** A Clockwork Orange and The Shining, the low-browed look straight into the lens.  
`frame.stare`

### The Leone Triangle / Three-Way Standoff

**Also called:** Mexican standoff framing, three-way duel  
**What it is:** A complete shot structure for a three way confrontation: a wide that shows the triangle between them, then a rotating cycle of cuts tightening on each man in turn, down to eye strips before the first shot is fired.  
**Effect on the audience:** It stretches time and makes the viewer calculate: who fires first, who is looking at whom. The repeating cycle at tighter and tighter sizes builds the entire tension with nobody moving.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The western climax, the last stand in crime and heist films, the three way confrontation in anime. Sergio Leone built it in the cemetery sequence of The Good, the Bad and the Ugly and it became the reference. Keep every look direction consistent across the cuts so the viewer can build the triangle in their head.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Short Film, Anime (Japanese 2D), Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Drama Series Episode, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography, Wedding Photography | genres: Western, Crime / Procedural, Heist, Gangster, Action, Samurai / Chanbara, Thriller / Suspense, Shonen Battle (anime)  
**Avoid when:** When the three have no clear motives, the cycle becomes a long piece of showing off. And do not break the eyelines, one wrong angle collapses the whole triangle.  
**Prompt:** `three-way standoff coverage, wide showing all three men spaced in a triangle, then tightening singles on each, ending on eye-only strips, all looks consistent, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.three_shot` — The three have no clear motives.  
`frame.leone_triangle`

### The Spielberg Face

**Also called:** the awe reaction, wonder face  
**What it is:** A shot of a face witnessing something enormous or terrifying, without showing the thing in the same frame. The expression changes slowly, the camera eases in, and light from whatever they are seeing plays across their skin.  
**Effect on the audience:** It makes the viewer build the thing in their imagination, which is always bigger than anything you could show. The face also tells them how to feel about it: wonder, fear or grief.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The big reveal: the first sight of the creature, the ship, the city, the body, the miracle. Steven Spielberg used it so consistently that it carries his name. The rule: hold the face longer than feels natural, then reveal the thing once the viewer is hungry for it. On a small budget this shot buys you a scene you never filmed.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Trailer / Teaser, Short Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, 3D CG Animation, Anime (Japanese 2D), Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography | genres: Sci-Fi, Fantasy / Epic, Adventure, Horror, Mythology / Ancient Epic, Family, Faith / Biblical Epic, Drama, Heritage (civilisation-focused)  
**Avoid when:** When the reveal is weak, the face promises what you cannot deliver. And do not repeat it every time, the viewer learns the trick and starts getting impatient.  
**Prompt:** `slow push in on the boy's face as he looks up at something unseen, awe spreading across his features, coloured light from off screen washing over him, no cut`  
**Yields to:** `frame.backs_to_camera` — The reveal is weak; show the thing.  
`frame.spielberg_face`

### The Beauty Shot

**Also called:** glamour shot, food beauty shot  
**What it is:** The shot where the subject appears at its absolute best: designed light, a chosen angle, the smallest of moves, every detail dressed. In food work it is the frame that makes the dish crave-worthy.  
**Effect on the audience:** Desire. The viewer is not reading information, they are feeling appetite or admiration, and that feeling is the product.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Food and drink commercials, the knife cut or the pour, the face in a fashion film, the bride in a wedding film, the frame that ends up on the cover. In advertising shoot it slower and closer than you think, and in Reels put it in the first second because it stops the scroll.  
**Best in:** formats: Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, Fashion Film, Music Video, Wedding / Event Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Travel / Real Estate / Hospitality, Corporate / Training, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Romance, Musical, Family, Slice of Life, Drama, Comedy  
**Avoid when:** When the subject is not genuinely ready, perfect light exposes the flaw instead of hiding it. And do not use it in serious documentary, it costs you the honesty.  
**Prompt:** `beauty shot, extreme close-up of honey pouring over the warm dessert, backlit steam, shallow focus, ultra slow motion, tiny camera drift`  
`frame.beauty_shot`

### The Slow-Motion Portrait Shot

**Also called:** slow motion portrait, hero portrait in slow mo, hero portrait shot  
**What it is:** A close or medium shot of one face in slow motion where the person does almost nothing: a turn, a blink, a breath, hair moving in the wind. There is no event, the face is the event.  
**Effect on the audience:** It lifts the person to the level of an icon. Slowed time gives every detail weight, and the viewer reads the face as a moving portrait rather than as a narrative shot.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The chorus in a music video, the player introductions in a sports commercial, the cast in a title sequence, a character introduction or a chapter ending in documentary: the craftsman's face, the athlete before the match, a crowd watching, and the faces in a heritage documentary before the narration starts. It is also the best vertical shot there is, because a face fills 9:16 completely. Shoot it at a high frame rate with side light so the features carve, and keep it to one strong shot rather than a whole sequence.  
**Best in:** formats: Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Title Sequence / Opening Credits, Sports Broadcast / Sports Film, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Fashion Film, Trailer / Teaser, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Documentary Feature, Documentary Series, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Musical, Sports Drama, Drama, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Romance, Action, Historical / Biopic, Verite / Observational, Tragedy, Nature / Wildlife  
**Avoid when:** In a narrative beat that has to deliver information, slow motion stops the story, and putting the whole video in slow motion loses the contrast that makes it work. Keep it out of hard news as well; it breaks the neutrality of a report.  
**Prompt:** `slow motion medium close-up portrait, she turns her head slightly and looks into the lens, hair lifting in the wind, side light carving the features, high frame rate`  
`frame.slowmo_portrait`

### Flat Lay / Knolling Shot

**Also called:** flat lay, knolling, overhead arrangement  
**What it is:** Objects arranged on a surface and shot from directly above. In a plain flat lay the arrangement is free and decorative, in knolling everything sits at right angles with equal spacing.  
**Effect on the audience:** Order and clarity. The viewer reads the whole set in an instant and senses an organised mind behind it, which transfers to the brand or the person.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Showing a set of products, tools or ingredients, opening a cooking video on the mise en place, laying out a craftsman's kit, comparing artefacts in a heritage documentary. It is also a great base for simple motion: a hand placing one piece, or the pieces assembling in stop motion. In Reels and explainers it delivers a lot of information in one second.  
**Best in:** formats: Product Video / Motion Design, Commercial / TVC, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Explainer, Corporate / Training, Stop Motion, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Kids Content, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Science / Educational, Family, Comedy, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Slice of Life, Preschool / Early Learning  
**Avoid when:** With too many objects at wildly different sizes, the arrangement turns into tidy clutter. And do not use it inside a narrative scene, there is no person and no action in it.  
**Prompt:** `overhead flat lay, the tools arranged at right angles with even spacing on a wooden table, soft top light, no shadows crossing, perfectly locked camera`  
**Yields to:** `frame.top_down_table` — Inside a narrative scene, needs a person and action.  
`frame.flat_lay`

### Back-of-the-Head Follow Shot

**Also called:** following shot, behind the head tracking  
**What it is:** The camera rides behind the character at shoulder level, following as they walk, so the back of the head and shoulders hold a large part of the frame while the world opens ahead of them.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer walks with them rather than facing them, which delivers direction and purpose. Hiding the face also builds anticipation: what will they see, and what will their face do when they turn.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Entering a new place, the walk down the corridor before the confrontation, crossing a market or a temple in heritage documentary, third person gameplay. It is also the answer for hiding faces in heritage work when the figure is moving through a place — for a figure read by its outline take `frame.silhouette`, for work done by hand `frame.hands_only`: all the emotion lives in the shoulders and the stride. End the shot on a turn or a stop so it has somewhere to land.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Game Cinematic / Gameplay, Drama Series Episode, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), 3D CG Animation, AI-Generated Clip | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Drama, Thriller / Suspense, Horror, Adventure, Historical / Biopic, Survival, Mystery / Whodunit  
**Avoid when:** Held too long with no payoff, the viewer tires of the back of a head. And keep the distance steady, drifting in and out reads as an operating error.  
**Prompt:** `following shot from behind the shoulders as she walks down the corridor, back of her head filling the lower frame, the doorway opening ahead, camera tracking at her pace`  
`frame.back_follow`

### Clean Plate

**Also called:** empty plate, clean pass  
**What it is:** A recording of the location with no people and no moving elements, on the same camera, lens, lighting and move as the real take. It is shot immediately before or after it.  
**Effect on the audience:** It hands the editor and the compositor a genuine background to erase anything unwanted: crew, a cable, a sign, a passerby, or to place a new element into the shot.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Any shot you will clean up or composite later: the street that has to be empty, the heritage site without tourists, the frame where a background or a title will be added. In heritage documentary it is essential, because sites are almost never empty. Record ten seconds only and change nothing in the setup.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Commercial / TVC, Product Video / Motion Design, 3D CG Animation, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Drama Series Episode, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Sci-Fi, Fantasy / Epic, Drama, Action, Mythology / Ancient Epic  
**Avoid when:** If the light or the angle changes between the take and the plate, it is useless. And do not shoot it at a different time of day, the shadows will not match.  
**Prompt:** `clean plate of the same shot, identical camera position lens and lighting, the location completely empty with no people or movement, ten seconds held`  
`frame.clean_plate`

### Look at Camera / Fourth Wall Shot

**Also called:** direct address, breaking the fourth wall  
**What it is:** The character looks directly into the lens, either in silence or speaking to the viewer. The camera admits it is a party to the scene.  
**Effect on the audience:** It breaks the contract between the viewer and the film. In comedy it becomes complicity and a shared joke, in horror and drama it becomes accusation or threat, because the character has seen you.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The character commenting on what just happened in comedy and sitcom, the presenter explaining in documentary and explainer, the last shot of a drama where the character owns what they did in front of you. In Reels and vlogs it is the default rather than the exception, because the whole platform is built on direct address. In horror, one look into the lens on the final shot is enough.  
**Best in:** formats: Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Explainer, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Feature Film, Mockumentary, Corporate / Training, Kids Content, Drama Series Episode, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Comedy, Dark Comedy / Satire, Horror, Drama, Rom-Com, Science / Educational, Family, Surreal / Absurdist, Psychological / Arthouse  
**Avoid when:** In naturalistic drama with no reason it reads as an actor mistakenly finding the lens. A single break in a piece that never set the rule up front usually lands as an accident, because the audience has no convention to place it in; the exception is when that one break is the shock the ending turns on.  
**Prompt:** `the character turns and looks straight down the lens in silence, medium close-up, holding the gaze for three seconds, static camera`  
`frame.fourth_wall`

### Hand-on-Shoulder Insert

**Also called:** touch insert, hand on the arm  
**What it is:** A close shot of the touch itself: a hand landing on a shoulder, an arm or a back, with no faces in frame or only a cropped one.  
**Effect on the audience:** The touch carries the entire relationship in one shot: comfort, control or threat, depending on its pressure and its length. The viewer reads it with no dialogue.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The condolence, the father holding his son before he leaves, the boss squeezing an employee's shoulder, the friend stopping his friend before the mistake. Place it between two face close-ups so it reads as a complete sentence. In heritage documentary and any piece with hidden faces it expresses a relationship without revealing anyone.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Short Film, Documentary Feature, Commercial / TVC, Anime (Japanese 2D), Vertical Micro-Drama, AI-Generated Clip, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Drama, Melodrama, Family, Tragedy, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Crime / Procedural, Romance, War  
**Avoid when:** With no relationship built, the touch lands with no weight. And do not hold it long, this shot works in a second or two.  
**Prompt:** `insert close-up of a hand settling firmly on his shoulder, fabric creasing under the fingers, faces out of frame, shallow depth of field, static camera`  
`frame.hand_on_shoulder`

### Interview Framing

**Also called:** off-axis eyeline framing, documentary interview frame  
**What it is:** The documentary interview frame: the subject in a medium close-up, sitting in one third of the image, looking at the interviewer who stands beside the lens rather than in it, which opens looking room on the other side.  
**Effect on the audience:** It makes the speech feel like a conversation rather than an address. The looking room tells the viewer there is another person in the room, so they feel they are sitting in on a conversation.  
**Used for and where it works best:** Every documentary interview, video podcast and heritage testimony. Pick the look direction to serve the cut: have one side of an argument look screen left and the other look screen right, and the viewer understands the split with no narration. And let the background say something about the subject, their workshop, their library, their home, never a blank wall.  
**Best in:** formats: Documentary Feature, Documentary Series, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Interview / Video Podcast, News Report / Reportage, Corporate / Training, Video Essay, Vlog / YouTube Long-Form, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism, Event Photography | genres: Verite / Observational, True Crime, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Historical / Biopic, Science / Educational, Political Thriller, Nature / Wildlife  
**Avoid when:** When the subject drifts into looking down the lens by accident it turns from an interview into an address. And do not center them exactly unless the formal frontal look is the intent.  
**Prompt:** `documentary interview framing, subject seated on the left third looking slightly off camera to the right, soft key from the window, workshop visible behind, static camera`  
**Yields to:** `doc.interrotron` — The formal frontal look into the lens is intended.  
`frame.interview_frame`

### The Objective Viewpoint

**Also called:** external viewpoint, neutral observer framing, third person camera  
**What it is:** A framing that belongs to no character's eyes: the camera stands where nobody stands and watches from a neutral distance. It is the direct opposite of the point of view shot, which welds the eye to one specific person.  
**Effect on the audience:** The viewer becomes a witness instead of a participant. Empathy recedes a little and a wider judgement takes its place, which is the difference between fearing with a character and fearing for them.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The moment of moral judgement in social drama and tragedy, observational documentary, and deadpan comedy where the joke needs distance to work. Choose it deliberately when you want the viewer to see the whole situation rather than live it from inside one person.  
**Best in:** formats: Feature Film, Documentary Feature, Limited Series, Short Film, Sitcom / Multi-Cam Comedy, Documentary Series, Recorded Theatre, Drama Series Episode, Video Essay, Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Social Drama, Tragedy, Deadpan Comedy, Verite / Observational, Drama, Psychological / Arthouse, Dark Comedy / Satire, Political Thriller  
**Avoid when:** It usually keeps the viewer cold in a scene that needs closeness, and the emotional beat is lost. The exception is when the coldness is the point, a violent moment meant to be witnessed rather than enjoyed.  
**Prompt:** `objective viewpoint, the camera stands apart from everyone at a neutral distance, static wide framing of the whole room, nobody looks toward the lens`  
**Yields to:** `frame.pov` — The scene needs closeness and identification.  
`frame.objective_view`

### The Bookend Shot

**Also called:** repeated frame, first and last image, mirrored shot  
**What it is:** One shot repeated with the same composition, lens and angle at the start and at the end of the piece, with a single deliberate change inside it. It differs from the bookend scene structure, which frames the story with two full scenes, because here a single shot returns exactly as itself.  
**Effect on the audience:** The repetition makes the difference visible. The viewer measures how far the story travelled from the gap between the two images, so they feel the change instead of being told it.  
**Used for and where it works best:** The ending of a short, an episode or a feature, and the close of a music video or a commercial. In heritage work: the same site at the head and the tail of the episode, once at dawn and once at dusk, so the passage of time reads with no narration.  
**Best in:** formats: Short Film, Feature Film, Drama Series Episode, Music Video, Commercial / TVC, Heritage / Historical Documentary, Limited Series, Trailer / Teaser, Vertical Short-Form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), Generated Keyframe Feeding Video, Poster / Static Design, Portrait Photography, Street Photography, Documentary Photography, Photojournalism | genres: Drama, Tragedy, Coming of Age, Heritage (civilisation-focused), Romance, War, Psychological / Arthouse, Melodrama  
**Avoid when:** The second shot usually feels redundant when nothing in it has changed, and it reads as lazy repetition. The exception is when total sameness is the meaning: everything changed except this place.  
**Prompt:** `bookend shot, identical framing lens and camera position to the opening shot of the piece, same doorway and same height, the only change is that the chair is now empty and the light is evening`  
**Yields to:** `scene.bookends` — The return is two full scenes, not one shot.  
`frame.bookend_shot`
