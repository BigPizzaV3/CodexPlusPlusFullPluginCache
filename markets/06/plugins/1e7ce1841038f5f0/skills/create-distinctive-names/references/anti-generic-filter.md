# Anti-Generic Filter

Reject or heavily penalize candidates that trigger several of these patterns unless the brief explicitly justifies them.

## Common synthetic-name patterns

- fashionable future/light/newness fragments;
- generic intelligence, mind, neural, quantum, nexus, matrix, lab, studio, systems, works, forge, flow, sync, hub, or platform language;
- classical roots selected only for “premium” atmosphere;
- random blends ending in interchangeable startup suffixes;
- one-letter vowel swaps of existing words;
- words that sound like a pharmaceutical, crypto token, generic SaaS, consultancy, or AI assistant without strategic reason;
- category metaphor chains such as orchestra → conductor → symphony → ensemble;
- vague cosmic, mythological, navigation, growth, architecture, creation, or transformation metaphors already dominant in the category;
- names whose entire case depends on a long explanation.

This is a diagnostic list, not a universal blacklist. A familiar word can still succeed when the strategic connection, category distance, execution, and availability are strong.

## Direction-distance test

For each proposed direction answer:

1. What source domain produced it?
2. What naming type and construction method produced it?
3. Which prior direction is it closest to?
4. Which two dimensions differ materially?
5. Would a neutral evaluator call it a new conceptual world or a synonym?

Fail the direction when questions 4 and 5 cannot be answered convincingly.

## Candidate rejection tests

Reject when:

- it closely resembles a known competitor or famous brand;
- pronunciation or spelling requires repeated correction without compensating value;
- meaning is misleading, culturally careless, derogatory, or embarrassing in a material market;
- it cannot extend to likely future offerings;
- it is merely descriptive and legally/strategically weak where distinctiveness matters;
- search results are overwhelmingly dominated by relevant existing uses;
- it violates the explicit rejection ledger;
- another candidate provides the same idea more cleanly.
