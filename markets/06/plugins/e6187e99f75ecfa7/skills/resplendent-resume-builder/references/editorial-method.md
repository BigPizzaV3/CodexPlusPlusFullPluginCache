# Professional editorial method

Read this reference for every resume, CV, cover letter, LinkedIn section, or application-response draft. The objective is not merely polished wording. It is an accurate, relevant argument in which every line earns its space.

## Build the evidence ledger

Before deleting, merging, or strengthening claims, identify the distinct facts available. A compact working ledger can contain:

| Fact | Candidate role | Method or constraint | Result or consequence | Source status | Best home |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What happened | led, owned, partnered, contributed, advised | how, for whom, under what conditions | verified outcome or honest qualitative effect | supplied, verified, unknown | summary, skill, role bullet, letter, or omit |

The ledger is an internal editing aid, not a required user-facing table. Use it to preserve unique evidence and prevent a synonym change from disguising duplication. Do not merge two claims if doing so would blur ownership, chronology, scope, or separate outcomes.

## Run the repetition audit

Compare the whole artifact, not only adjacent bullets.

| Repetition type | Test | Normal editorial action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact wording | Is the same phrase or sentence repeated? | Keep the strongest instance; delete or rewrite the other. |
| Semantic duplication | Do different words make the same claim with no new evidence? | Merge unique facts into one stronger statement. |
| Metric reuse | Is the same result used more than once without a new causal point? | Attach it to the clearest supported achievement once. |
| Verb monotony | Do several bullets begin with the same verb or synonyms that conceal similar content? | Fix the underlying evidence distinction first, then vary wording naturally. |
| Cross-section overlap | Do Summary, Highlights, Skills, and Experience repeat the same proposition? | Give each section a different job. Evidence normally lives in Experience; the Summary interprets it; Skills names only useful supported capabilities. |
| Cross-document overlap | Does a cover letter merely paraphrase a resume achievement? | Add reasoning, context, judgment, motivation, or relevance that the resume cannot show. |

Use **one fact, one best home** as the default. Deliberate reinforcement is justified only when a high-priority target requirement needs emphasis and the second use performs a different function. For example, the Summary may frame a verified transformation as the candidate's positioning while the Experience bullet supplies the method and result. Repeating the same metric in both places without new meaning is not reinforcement.

For each repeated claim, decide **keep, merge, delete, or reframe**. Preserve every unique, relevant fact. When the edit is consequential or the user asked to keep everything, show a short change note explaining what was consolidated and what survived.

## Edit like a resume editor

Apply these passes in order:

1. **Truth:** Trace claims to supplied or verified evidence. Separate candidate action from team action. Preserve uncertainty and confidentiality.
2. **Relevance:** Give the most space and prominence to evidence that predicts success in the target role. Compress older or less relevant material before shrinking type.
3. **Architecture:** Check section order, chronology, narrative progression, and whether the first half of page one establishes the candidate's target and strongest evidence.
4. **Evidence:** Replace duty descriptions and self-evaluations with supported action, context, method, constraint, and consequence.
5. **Economy:** Run the repetition audit; remove filler, throat-clearing, redundant skills, and detail that does not change the hiring argument.
6. **Line edit:** Use precise nouns and verbs, natural syntax, parallel bullet grammar, consistent tense, and field-appropriate terminology.
7. **Proof:** Check names, employers, titles, dates, numbers, punctuation, capitalization, spacing, hyperlinks, and agreement across related documents.

Do not erase necessary context merely to shorten the page. A concise line that becomes vague, overclaims causality, or hides the candidate's actual contribution is a worse edit.

## Sentence and bullet usefulness test

Label each unit internally:

- **Evidence:** adds a supported action, method, constraint, scale, or outcome.
- **Interpretation:** explains why evidence matters to the target role or reader.
- **Navigation:** provides necessary identity, chronology, section structure, or transition.
- **Repeat:** duplicates a fact or proposition without a new function.
- **Filler:** generic praise, enthusiasm, conclusion, or skill assertion that could describe many candidates.
- **Unsupported:** adds a claim that the evidence does not establish.

Keep evidence, useful interpretation, and necessary navigation. Merge, reframe, or remove repeats and filler. Remove unsupported claims or convert them to a focused confirmation question.

## Common editing mistakes

- Varying action verbs while leaving duplicate content intact.
- Treating a supported metric as permission to repeat it throughout the application.
- Cutting older experience indiscriminately instead of protecting relevant scope and progression.
- Filling Skills with interpersonal qualities already demonstrated in bullets.
- Turning every bullet into the same action-method-result sentence rhythm.
- Making a cover letter a more conversational copy of the resume.
- Scoring a repetitive draft highly because every individual sentence is clear.
