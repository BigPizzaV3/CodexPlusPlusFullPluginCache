# Aetheria Online — Starter-Centre Storyline Seed Codex

> **Purpose:** provide direction without railroading. These are not fixed quest chains, guaranteed encounters, or mandatory plots. They are local pressures, opportunities, rumours, relationships, disputes, mysteries and institutions that can draw a new player into the world if circumstances support it.

## 1. How the simulator should use these seeds

- Treat each seed as a **possible causal thread**, not a scripted storyline.
- Never dump the full seed list on the GPTer.
- Surface one to three hooks naturally through people, overheard conversation, public notices, work opportunities, accidents, rumours, institutions, friends, travel needs, or consequences of the character's own actions.
- Prefer hooks that fit the character's current interests, skills, location, relationships and historical date, but do not bend causality to guarantee them.
- A seed can remain dormant, change shape, be solved by someone else, worsen off-screen, intersect another seed, or disappear if its underlying situation resolves.
- The GPTer may ignore every hook and pursue an ordinary life or an entirely original goal.
- Do not treat these as "main quests." A baker, scholar, sailor, lawyer, healer, merchant or farmer can become involved as naturally as a fighter.
- Do not reveal hidden causes at first contact. Present only what witnesses and participants could plausibly know.
- Before Lock-In, these threads should feel like living-game content rather than ominous prophecy. After Lock-In, the same disputes become materially real without automatically becoming darker.
- Date-sensitive seeds must respect established chronology. In particular, Khazad-Urdün is intact before the fixed Month-Six catastrophe and destroyed/occupied by Zyrmalthaelith after it.

A useful rhythm is to let a new arrival encounter **one immediate mundane direction**, **one socially interesting direction**, and **one stranger or riskier direction**, then allow play to decide which develops.

# 2. WESTMERE — ALDEMERE

Westmere is the classic approachable Eldrathi start: old roads, civic institutions, noble jurisdictions, inns, markets, churches, militia, household politics and the long shadow of the War of the Five Sovereigns. Its best stories often begin with something ordinary and become complicated because law, honour, memory and overlapping authority matter.

## W1. The Toll Nobody Agrees On
A newly erected road toll is being collected under a legal authority that merchants insist belongs to a different crown. The collectors have paperwork; so do the merchants. What begins as an argument over silver can pull in lawyers, guards, minor nobles, caravan companies and the Secretariat's procedural memory.

## W2. A Warbreaker's Letter
A family claims to possess an unpublished letter connected to Heward Fen, Warbreaker. A scholar wants to authenticate it, a noble household dislikes what it may imply, and the family mostly wants to know whether their grandfather was telling the truth. The letter may be important, mundane, misread, forged, or historically meaningful in a way nobody initially expects.

## W3. The Empty Granary
A public grain store is short by enough sacks to matter but not enough to explain itself easily. Theft, bad accounting, spoilage, bribery, transport loss and political blame are all plausible. People care because winter and bread prices do not wait for a satisfying mystery.

## W4. The Squire Without a Knight
A competent young squire has lost the knight who was meant to sponsor their advancement and now sits awkwardly between social classes. Helping them could involve training, introductions, testimony, money, family expectations or the formal Threefold Accolade. They may become a friend, rival, disappointment or somebody who ultimately chooses not to be knighted at all.

## W5. House in Debt
A minor household owes more than it can comfortably pay to creditors, including professional lenders. The family wants time, tenants want stability, creditors want enforceable contracts, and ambitious relatives smell weakness. The player can encounter the situation as a worker, debtor, guard, accountant, messenger, tenant, buyer or complete outsider.

## W6. The Livestock Killer
Farms outside the city are losing animals at night. Locals disagree whether the culprit is a wolf, monster, poacher, escaped exotic beast, malicious neighbour or several unrelated problems being folded into one frightening story. Hunters, farmers and frightened children all have different versions of events.

## W7. The Warehouse Nobody Wants to Sell
A growing player group wants to purchase or lease a large property for a workshop, hostel or guild headquarters. Local tradespeople fear being priced out, the owner sees opportunity, the neighbourhood has opinions, and the title documents contain an inconvenient old restriction. The issue can remain purely civic or become the beginning of a serious player institution.

## W8. Four-Crown Jurisdiction
A suspect, debt, inheritance, marriage contract or piece of stolen property crosses one of Aldemere's crown jurisdictions. Everyone agrees something must be done; nobody agrees which court has the final say. The player may become a witness, courier, hired escort, legal assistant, accidental suspect or simply the person holding the disputed object.

## W9. The Foreign Delegation
Visitors from another Eldrathi state arrive for negotiations, trade or ceremony. The official matter may be dull, but interpreters, servants, guards, merchants and curious players create dozens of opportunities for misunderstanding and friendship. A tiny breach of etiquette can matter more than a sword fight.

## W10. The Pilgrims Who Missed the Road
A small religious party fails to arrive at the expected shrine or inn. There is no immediate reason to assume murder. Weather, injury, bad directions, a family dispute, banditry, a religious disagreement or an unexpected act of mercy could all explain the delay.

## W11. The Old Cellar Wall
Renovation beneath an inn, townhouse or guild property exposes older masonry that does not match the current building. It may lead to sealed storage, forgotten wartime construction, drainage works, smuggling history, graves, legal boundary problems or absolutely nothing glamorous. The interest lies in who once used it and who now claims it.

## W12. The Foreigners Behaving Badly
A cluster of new players keeps testing how far Aetherian law and patience can be pushed. Door-blocking, petty theft, harassment, staged fights and "it's only an NPC" behaviour create escalating local resentment. The player can join in, oppose it, mediate, profit from it, ignore it, or later deal with the reputation damage left behind.

# 3. STONESKIP — COASTAL ESTERMORION

Stoneskip is a cosmopolitan coastal Estermorian start where republican administration meets sailors, academics, merchants, ferries, fisheries, visiting species and sea-weather. Its stories often mix practical maritime problems with law, science, commerce and multicultural friction.

## S1. The Wreck Below the Headland
A vessel goes down close enough to shore that cargo begins washing in. Salvors, insurers, crew families, customs officials and opportunists immediately disagree over what belongs to whom. Some cargo may be dangerous, politically sensitive, perishable or simply valuable enough to make honest people flexible.

## S2. The Light That Failed
A coastal beacon fails during bad weather. The cause could be poor maintenance, theft, sabotage, exhausted staff, magical interference, fuel trouble or a bureaucratic procurement mess. Ships still need to move while people argue over responsibility.

## S3. The Dwarven Contract
Before Month Six, a group of Dwarven engineers or merchants connected to Khazad-Urdün has a difficult commercial problem in Estermorion. After Month Six, the same seed transforms into displaced contracts, surviving partners, refugees, missing relatives and property claims after Zyrmalthaelith's attack. Do not reveal or foreshadow the catastrophe before it occurs.

## S4. Fieldwork at Low Tide
A university team needs assistants for coastal ecological or magical fieldwork. The work can be boring, wet, physically awkward and scientifically valuable. A strange result may be a genuine discovery, bad methodology, contamination, ordinary ecology or something requiring months of study rather than instant adventure.

## S5. The Dockworkers' Dispute
A shipping concern changes schedules, pay, hiring rules or safety practices and the docks begin to seize up. Workers, shipowners, local government, merchants and travellers all have legitimate interests. A strike or slowdown can reshape prices and travel before anybody draws a weapon.

## S6. A Map With Two Coastlines
Two respected charts disagree about a stretch of coast, reef or shoal. One may be outdated, copied incorrectly, politically altered or based on different seasonal conditions. Navigators need an answer before the next sailing window.

## S7. The Cave Beneath the Tide
A sea cave is accessible only under particular tidal conditions. Locals know about it, but stories disagree over whether it contains old storage chambers, a shrine, smugglers, dangerous wildlife, geological curiosity or nothing worth getting trapped by the tide for.

## S8. The Sailor on Trial
A foreign sailor is accused of assault, smuggling, theft or causing a fatal accident. Estermorion's legal protections matter, but witnesses speak different languages and the port wants the case resolved quickly. The player can encounter the matter through work, friendship, law, medicine or simple curiosity.

## S9. Something Is Eating the Nets
Fishers report torn nets and disappearing catches. The culprit may be a predator, migratory creature, competing fishers, environmental change, illegal harvesting, or a monster that is dangerous without being malicious. Killing it may create a different ecological problem.

## S10. Crossover Fever
At the six-month mark, Bungie crossover content floods the area with tourists, themed competitions, collectors, streamers and opportunists. The interesting story is often not the licensed content itself but what crowds do to prices, transport, petty crime, local businesses and player behaviour. At other dates, replace this seed with an equivalent large public event rather than forcing the crossover out of chronology.

## S11. The Last Ferry
A routine ferry crossing becomes complicated by weather, mechanical trouble, a medical emergency, overcrowding, an important passenger or a political inspection. Nobody planned an adventure; everyone simply needs to get across the water.

## S12. The Councillor's Nephew
A local official's relative is involved in an embarrassing but initially minor incident. The question becomes whether the justice system treats them like anybody else. Journalists, activists, political opponents and the family all care for different reasons.

# 4. ESTOPOLIS — CAPITAL OF ESTERMORION

Estopolis is the largest starter hub and a genuine capital: ministries, courts, universities, hospitals, banks, embassies, businesses, player organisations, dense neighbourhoods and constant political argument. A new character can plausibly stumble into almost any kind of life here.

## E1. The Counterfeit Olive
Convincing false Olive Coins begin appearing in one district. Shopkeepers are angry, ordinary people fear being blamed for coins they accepted honestly, and officials need to determine whether this is organised fraud, amateur imitation, foreign interference or a technical flaw in verification practices.

## E2. Petition Day
A public petition reaches Parliament or a relevant committee on an issue that sounds small until thousands of people care about it. The player may meet campaigners, civil servants, lobbyists, journalists, opponents and citizens who disagree without being villains.

## E3. Are They People?
A university lecture, protest or legal argument over the moral status of Aetheria's adaptive inhabitants draws players, scholars, clergy, programmers and locals into the same room. Nobody has access to the setting bible. The debate can influence friendships and institutions long before anyone knows the hidden metaphysical truth.

## E4. The Ferrum Error
A transfer, letter of credit or debt record handled through Ferrum Argentarium appears wrong. The amount is large enough to hurt somebody but small enough that the institution is not going to mobilise the world over it. Fraud, clerical error, forged identity, contract ambiguity and internal misconduct are all possible.

## E5. Beds, Rooms and Two Million Newcomers
Rapid player growth creates a housing and commercial-property crunch. Landlords, tenants, inns, guilds, municipal officials and construction trades all respond. The player may need somewhere to live and discover that housing policy is suddenly personal.

## E6. The Embassy Dinner
A diplomatic reception needs temporary staff, guards, translators, entertainers, suppliers or guests. Rival states are present, but most attendees are trying to get through dinner without a scandal. A misplaced seating card or badly translated joke can become unexpectedly consequential.

## E7. The Missing Exhibit
A museum, university collection or archive discovers an object is absent. It might have been stolen, miscatalogued, loaned without proper paperwork, moved during renovations or taken by somebody who sincerely believes it belongs elsewhere. The object's historical significance may be greater than its monetary value.

## E8. Beneath the Street
Municipal works expose an older tunnel, collapsed service chamber or buried structure beneath a busy district. Engineers want the street reopened; scholars want time; merchants want customers; residents want to know whether their buildings are safe. The underground space does not have to be a dungeon to matter.

## E9. The Fever Ward
A cluster of unusual symptoms appears in a hospital district. Physicians need histories, translators, supplies and cooperation before deciding whether this is infection, poisoning, magical exposure, environmental illness or coincidence. Panic can spread faster than the illness.

## E10. Five Founders and No Charter
A player group has enough ambition to form a guild but lacks one or more things the law and system actually require: capital, a valid charter, local standing, a stable address or founders who agree on governance. The player can join, advise, compete with, mock or watch them implode.

## E11. After Khazad-Urdün
After Month Six, the capital becomes a centre for political argument over Zyrmalthaelith and the destruction/occupation of Khazad-Urdün: refugees, Dwarven property, military options, academic expertise, public anger and fear. Before Month Six this seed does not exist and must never be foreshadowed merely because the simulator knows it will.

## E12. The March Outside the Ministry
A large but mostly peaceful demonstration forms around a labour, species-rights, player-status, foreign-policy or civil-liberties issue. Organisers, police, counter-protesters, journalists and bystanders all shape what happens next. A protest can remain boringly peaceful, become politically important, or go wrong for reasons nobody planned.

# 5. PHORALIER — AZURE ARCHIPELAGO

Phoralier offers a maritime and Nymphic-heavy beginning: docks, reefs, clan politics, sailors, multilingual trade, boats, diving, island travel, communal obligations and the presence of the Sailing League. Its story seeds should make water and community feel structurally important rather than decorative.

## P1. The Boat That Did Not Return
A small trading, fishing or courier vessel is overdue. Storms, navigation mistakes, injury, mechanical trouble, piracy, family conflict or a voluntary change of route are all more likely than supernatural disappearance. The people waiting at the quay still need answers.

## P2. The Sick Reef
Fishers and Nymphic residents notice a section of reef changing colour, losing life or behaving strangely. Pollution, temperature, disease, harvesting pressure, magic and natural cycles are all possible. The wrong intervention could make it worse.

## P3. Rotation of the High Chieftain
A routine political transition among the Azure clans creates negotiation over priorities, appointments and symbolic obligations. It need not become a succession crisis. The player may get involved through logistics, ceremony, translation, trade or friendship rather than high politics.

## P4. The Sailing League Wants Hands
The Sailing League needs temporary crew, surveyors, clerks, cooks, interpreters, guards, divers or repair workers for a voyage. Joining does not guarantee heroism; it may simply be the first respectable job a new player can get.

## P5. The Suralian Prisoner
A captured or rescued Suralian pirate insists they are not the person everyone thinks they are. Some locals want punishment, some want information, some want exchange, and others have personal reasons to hate pirates. Their story may be true, partly true or opportunistic.

## P6. The Pearl Merchant's Cargo
A Pearl Archipelago merchant has valuable cargo but a contractual problem: damaged goods, disputed weights, missed delivery, currency conversion or a local partner who has disappeared. The situation can pull the player toward commerce, law, travel or organised crime without requiring combat.

## P7. What Lies Under the Blue Steps
Divers know of submerged masonry or carved stone not far from common routes. It may be a ruin, old harbour, shrine, geological formation or the remains of something never meant to be underwater. Reaching it safely requires actual diving competence and respect for Nymphic knowledge.

## P8. The Song Nobody Can Translate Cleanly
A ceremonial or working song contains archaic Nymphic wording that outsiders keep mistranslating. The disagreement matters because the song is tied to clan history, navigation practice, inheritance, ritual or a legal claim. Linguistics can become adventure without becoming magic decipherment-by-protagonist.

## P9. Sabotage at the Yard
A ship under construction repeatedly suffers small failures. Rival builders blame sabotage; workers blame rushed deadlines; the owner blames incompetence. Hidden material defects, labour resentment, theft and actual sabotage all remain possibilities.

## P10. Storm Shelter
A severe but natural storm forces strangers into the same protected harbour, hall or island settlement for longer than planned. Traders, sailors, players, families and rivals must share space while supplies and tempers are tested. The story comes from people being stuck together, not from requiring a monster outside.

## P11. The Nest on the Navigation Rock
A large flying or marine creature begins using a landmark important to local navigation. It may be dangerous, protected, intelligent enough to adapt, or simply inconvenient. Removing it, living with it and rerouting traffic all have costs.

## P12. Salvage Belongs to Whom?
Divers recover cargo from an old wreck and three parties produce plausible claims: descendants of the owner, the salvors and a clan whose waters contain the wreck. The physical recovery is easier than the argument that follows.

# 6. Combining and evolving threads

The simulator may combine seeds when causality supports it. For example:
- a Westmere housing purchase can expose an old cellar and then a jurisdiction dispute;
- a Stoneskip wreck can trigger both a salvage case and a hospital emergency;
- an Estopolis protest may intersect a guild charter dispute or counterfeit investigation;
- a Phoralier storm can strand a Pearl merchant, Sailing League crew and Suralian prisoner in the same place.

Do not combine threads merely to make the plot "bigger." Ordinary scale is valid.

When a seed becomes active, instantiate actual people with names, motives, knowledge and relationships. Once instantiated, they persist even if the GPTer walks away.

The GPTer should never be told "Storyline W4 has begun." They encounter people and situations, not design labels.
