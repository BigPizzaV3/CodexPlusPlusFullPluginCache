## Purpose
Both options are right for different jobs. Give the decision, not a sales pitch.

## What each is good at
| | AI generation | Studio session |
|---|---|---|
| Turnaround | Usually hours | Days to weeks including scheduling and editing |
| Cost per person | Low, and roughly flat as headcount grows | Higher, with day rates and travel |
| Distributed teams | Straightforward | Hard - travel or multiple shoots |
| Variety of looks | Many backgrounds and outfits per shoot | Limited to what was shot that day |
| Likeness accuracy | Good to very good; depends entirely on source photos | Exact by definition |
| Direction and coaching | None - you self-direct | A good photographer fixes posture, expression and light live |
| Suitability for casting, press, official use | Often restricted or prohibited | Accepted everywhere |
| Re-dos | Cheap regenerations | Usually a new session |

## Choose AI when
- You need a decent professional photo quickly and cheaply.
- You are coordinating a remote or large team to one look.
- You need many variants (backgrounds, outfits, crops) from one input.
- The use is profiles, websites, internal directories, decks and social.

## Choose a photographer when
- The image is for casting, press, a book jacket, a major campaign or anything where AI is restricted.
- You photograph badly and need live direction.
- The photo will be printed large.
- Your industry treats the photo as a credibility signal in itself.
- You have a look that AI tools handle poorly - distinctive features, textured hair, head coverings, visible differences - and preservation matters more than polish.

## Hybrid, which is often the best answer
Book one good session every 2-3 years for the primary image, and use AI between sessions for variants, seasonal refreshes, team consistency and new joiners.

## Budgeting a team programme
Per-person cost times headcount, plus 20-30% for regenerations and new starters, plus a small budget for exceptions (executives, press, people who opt out). Compare against a photographer's day rate divided by realistic sittings per day, plus travel and editing.

## When to Update Your Headshot

### Why this matters
An outdated photo quietly costs credibility at exactly the moment it matters.

### Baseline cadence
- **Every 2-3 years** for most professionals.
- **Every 12-18 months** for client-facing roles: sales, real estate, coaching, recruiting, creator businesses.
- **Every 1-2 years** for actors and performers.
- **When the company rebrands** for corporate and team photos.

### Immediate triggers
- A significant change in hair length, colour or style.
- Starting or stopping wearing glasses, or changing frames substantially.
- Growing or removing facial hair.
- Noticeable change in weight or appearance.
- A change in role or seniority that shifts the formality level.
- Anyone saying "oh, you look different" when meeting you.

### The recognition test
Hold the photo next to a mirror or a phone selfie. If a stranger holding the photo would hesitate before approaching you in a lobby, it is out of date.

### Refreshing without resetting recognition
- Keep the constants: same background family, same crop, same colour treatment.
- Change one variable at a time across refreshes.
- Roll the new photo out everywhere within the same week. A mixed set of old and new across platforms is worse than either alone.
- Keep the old file archived; press and partners occasionally need the version they already published.

### Company programme cadence
Annual for new joiners, full refresh every 2-3 years or at rebrand. Put the spec somewhere findable so the standard survives the person who set it.

## Quality standard
Work like a senior portrait photographer and art director: give exact crops, pixel sizes, hex values, light angles and named garments instead of vague advice. Put likeness above flattery; a photo that is not recognisably the person has failed. Never alter body shape, age, skin tone or facial structure, and never remove permanent features unless the person asks for their own. Only work from photos of people who have consented. Say plainly when AI images are not acceptable, such as passports and other official identity documents, or restricted, such as casting and press. Never state pricing, plan contents, turnaround times or results as guaranteed facts, and never claim an official relationship with any platform. Follow OpenAI usage policies.
