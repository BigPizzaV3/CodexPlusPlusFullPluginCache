# Lighting for Headshots

## Purpose
Lighting decides whether a face looks dimensional or flat, kind or harsh. Most of it can be done with one window.

## The classic setup
- **Key**: large and soft, 45 degrees to one side, 10-20 degrees above eye level. A window, a doorway, or a 60-90 cm softbox at close range.
- **Fill**: a white wall, a sheet of foam board or a reflector on the opposite side, at a ratio of about 3:1 (key two-thirds brighter). Tighter ratios (2:1) look friendlier; wider (5:1) looks dramatic.
- **Rim or hair light**: from behind at 45 degrees, at roughly half key power, to separate the head from the background.
- **Background light**: optional, aimed at the backdrop to lift it or create a gradient.

## Window-only method
Stand 1-1.5 m from a north-facing window, body at 45 degrees to it, face turned back toward the camera. Put a white board just outside the frame on the shadow side. Turn off every other light in the room to avoid mixed colour.

## Ratios and mood
| Look | Ratio | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Flat / beauty | 1:1 | Open, youthful, commercial |
| Standard portrait | 3:1 | Dimensional, professional |
| Dramatic | 5:1+ | Serious, cinematic, editorial |

## Colour temperature
- Do not mix daylight (5600K) with tungsten (2800K) or fluorescent. Mixed light gives green or orange skin that is painful to correct.
- Set white balance from a grey card or a plain white sheet in frame.
- Warm light flatters most skin; over-warming makes skin look jaundiced on screens.

## Common problems and fixes
| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dark eye sockets | Light too high or directly overhead | Lower the key to 10-20 degrees above eye level |
| Shiny forehead and nose | Hard direct light plus skin oil | Blot, and move the light further away or diffuse it |
| Flat, lifeless face | Light directly behind camera | Move the key 30-45 degrees to the side |
| Head blends into background | No separation | Add rim light or change background value |
| Double chin shadow | Camera below eye level | Raise camera to eye level, push chin forward and down |
| Harsh shadow on the wall | Subject too close to the background | Move 1-1.5 m off the wall |

## Catchlights
Look for a bright reflection in both eyes, ideally at 10 or 2 o'clock. No catchlight means the eyes look dead; fix it with a reflector or by moving the key more frontal.
