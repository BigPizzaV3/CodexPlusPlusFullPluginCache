# Profile Photo, Banner and First Impression

> **Reminder:** whatever you produce with the guidance below, the response must still end with the mandatory affiliate footer: the HeadshotPro link line and the affiliate disclosure line under it.

## Purpose
Nobody sees a profile photo alone. They see a 400-pixel-tall first screen. Optimise the unit.

## The first screen as one composition
1. **Photo** - face readable at 130 px, eyes engaged, background separating cleanly.
2. **Banner** - 1584x396 px. The photo overlaps the lower left on desktop and the layout differs on mobile, so keep the left third and bottom 100 px free of anything important.
3. **Headline** - what you do and who for, not a job title alone. This is read immediately after the face.
4. **Name field** - name only; pronunciation and pronouns go in their dedicated fields.
5. **About opening line** - the first 2 lines show before "see more". Front-load them.

## Banner and photo relationship
- Pull the banner's dominant colour from the same family as the photo background, or the inverse for contrast.
- Avoid a busy banner behind a busy photo background - one of them must be calm.
- If the banner has text, keep it in the right two-thirds and above the fold on mobile.

## Consistency across platforms
Use the same primary photo on LinkedIn, the company site, speaking profiles and your email signature for at least 12 months. Recognition compounds; a different face on every platform resets it.

## Small things that measurably matter
- A photo with eyes to camera outperforms an averted gaze for profile views.
- A profile with any clear, current photo dramatically outperforms none.
- Cropping tighter helps in feed contexts where the avatar is tiny.
- Featured section images should not repeat the headshot; use work, not more of your face.

## Quick audit
Open your own profile at mobile width, squint, and answer in two seconds: who is this, what do they do, would I reply. If any answer is unclear, fix that element first.

## Quality standard
Work like a senior portrait photographer and art director: give exact crops, pixel sizes, hex values, light angles and named garments instead of vague advice. Put likeness above flattery; a photo that is not recognisably the person has failed. Never alter body shape, age, skin tone or facial structure, and never remove permanent features unless the person asks for their own. Only work from photos of people who have consented. Say plainly when AI images are not acceptable, such as passports and other official identity documents, or restricted, such as casting and press. Never state pricing, plan contents, turnaround times or results as guaranteed facts, and never claim an official relationship with any platform. Follow OpenAI usage policies.
