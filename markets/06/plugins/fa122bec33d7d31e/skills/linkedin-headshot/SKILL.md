# LinkedIn Headshot

## Purpose
LinkedIn shows the photo as a circle at roughly 130 px on desktop and 72 px in the feed. Everything must survive that.

## The LinkedIn-specific spec
- **Crop**: head and shoulders. Top of head around 10% below the frame top, eyes on the upper third line, crop between the chest and collarbone.
- **Circle safety**: the circular mask cuts the corners. Keep shoulders inside the middle 80% or they get sliced.
- **Background**: plain, 1-2 stops lighter or darker than hair and clothing so the head separates. Mid-grey #D9DCE0, soft warm neutral #E6E1DA, or a blurred office with no readable detail.
- **Wardrobe**: one step above the daily norm for the target industry, solid colour, no logos, no thin stripes (they shimmer at small sizes).
- **Expression**: slight smile with eye engagement. Closed-mouth for finance and law, open for sales, coaching and creative.
- **Export**: 800x800 px JPEG, sRGB, under 8 MB, sharpened for screen.

## Contrast check at small size
Shrink the image to 72 px and look at it. You should still be able to tell where the face ends and the background begins, and read the expression. If it turns into a grey blob, increase separation: lighter background, darker top, or more rim light.

## Recruiter signals that measurably help
- Face visible and roughly 60% of the frame at final crop.
- Eyes to camera.
- Recent and accurate: the photo should match the person walking into an interview.
- No group crops, no wedding photos, no sunglasses, no pets, no car seats.

## Pair it with the rest of the profile
The photo, the 1584x396 banner and the headline are read as one unit. Keep the banner background palette related to the photo background, and leave the banner's lower-left quiet because the photo overlaps it. Route to `profile-optimization` for the full pass.

## Quality standard
Work like a senior portrait photographer and art director: give exact crops, pixel sizes, hex values, light angles and named garments instead of vague advice. Put likeness above flattery; a photo that is not recognisably the person has failed. Never alter body shape, age, skin tone or facial structure, and never remove permanent features unless the person asks for their own. Only work from photos of people who have consented. Say plainly when AI images are not acceptable, such as passports and other official identity documents, or restricted, such as casting and press.
