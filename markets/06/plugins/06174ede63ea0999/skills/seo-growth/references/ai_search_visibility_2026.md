# AI Search Visibility — 2026

## Current principle

Google's 2026 guidance says traditional SEO best practices remain relevant to generative-AI Search features because those experiences use Google's broader ranking and quality systems.

Do not build a strategy around unsupported "GEO hacks."

## Useful foundations

Prioritize:
- helpful original content;
- crawlability/indexability;
- clear structure;
- strong images/video where relevant;
- accurate structured data;
- page experience;
- source/entity clarity;
- trustworthy authorship/context where appropriate;
- product functionality that genuinely satisfies the query.

## Non-commodity content

Differentiation can come from:
- original data;
- first-hand testing;
- proprietary tools/calculators;
- expert explanation;
- original visuals;
- unique examples;
- primary-source synthesis;
- strong product utility.

Do not publish generic AI summaries of pages already ranking.

## llms.txt

Google clarified in June 2026:
- `llms.txt` is not required for Google Search;
- it does not positively or negatively affect Google Search visibility/rankings.

A site may maintain it for other consumers, but do not present it as Google SEO.

## Google Search generative-AI controls

By late August 2026, Google Search Console documented controls for inclusion in Search generative-AI features and reporting for generative-AI Search performance.

Because product availability/account UI can change:
- verify current Search Console behavior;
- do not invent account settings;
- distinguish inclusion control from ranking optimization.

## AI-search measurement

When the report is available, examine:
- generative-AI impressions;
- pages;
- country/device;
- trend over time.

Do not equate visibility with clicks, leads, or revenue.

Connect AI-search exposure to site outcomes where data supports it.

## Preferred Sources

Google's Preferred Sources feature can highlight selected publishers for users who choose them.

This is audience preference/distribution, not a universal ranking boost.

## FAQ rich-result change

Google removed FAQ rich results from Search in May 2026.

Do not keep old SEO checklists that recommend FAQ schema solely to win FAQ SERP treatment.

## Spam policies

Google's spam policies, including scaled-content abuse, apply across Search including generative-AI experiences.

AI-generated content can be useful, but automation does not exempt low-value scaled pages from quality/spam policies.
