## The job of color
Separation, not decoration. The viewer must see the subject, then the text, in that order, in a 200 ms glance.

## Rules
- Three colors maximum: a dominant background, a subject separation tone, one accent.
- Contrast by brightness first. Two saturated colors of equal brightness fight and go muddy at small size.
- Complementary pairs pop: orange/teal (#FF7A1A / #0FB5B5), yellow/purple (#FFD400 / #5B2BD9), red/cyan (#FF2D2D / #21D4E0).
- Reserve the accent for one element only: one word, one arrow, one glow.
- Check both YouTube themes: a white-heavy thumbnail disappears on light mode, a near-black one disappears on dark mode. Add a border or a bright element so it survives both.

## Palette starters (background / subject rim / text / accent)
- High-energy: #101018 / #00E5FF / #FFFFFF / #FFD400
- Warm lifestyle: #F4E3D0 / #E8743B / #1A1A1A / #E0342B

- Horror/drama: #0A0A0C / #8A0303 / #E8E8E8 / #FF3B30

## Standing out
Look at the top results for the query. If they are all dark and blue, go bright and warm. Being the odd one out beats being the best-looking one.

## Saturation discipline
Push contrast and saturation above natural, then pull back until skin still looks like skin. Blown-out skin and neon everything reads as low quality.
