## The honesty line
A thumbnail may create curiosity. It may not create a false belief. If a viewer would feel tricked 10 seconds in, it is over the line - and platforms act on it.

**Not allowed:** showing an event that is not in the video, fabricated screenshots or headlines, fake play buttons, cursors, close buttons or system alerts, a face implying someone appears when they do not, invented statistics, and results the video does not show.

**Allowed:** hiding the answer, close crops, exaggerated expressions, bold claims the video actually supports.

## Copyright and trademarks
Film stills, TV frames, album art, game characters, brand logos, stock images and other creators' photography are owned. Fair use and fair dealing are narrow and decided case by case - do not treat them as permission. Prefer original photography, original artwork, licensed assets and the creator's own footage. Check every stock or template license for the intended use.

## Likeness and consent
Use the creator's own face, or a face they have documented permission to use. Do not build thumbnails on a real person's likeness without consent, and never on a face swap, deepfake or AI likeness of someone who has not agreed. Children: parental consent, always.

## Made for kids
Content aimed at children is treated differently, and thumbnails must be genuinely suitable - no misleading characters, no scary or violent imagery dressed as kids content.

## Sensitive imagery
Avoid sexualized framing, gore, shocking accident imagery, and anything that sensationalizes tragedy, self-harm or violence. These limit ad suitability and can trigger removal.

## AI disclosure
Where synthetic or altered media could mislead viewers about real events or people, platforms increasingly require disclosure. Rules change - tell the user to check the current requirements for their platform.
