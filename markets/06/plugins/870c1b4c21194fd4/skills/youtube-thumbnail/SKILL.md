## Core specs (YouTube long-form)
- 1280x720 px, 16:9, under 2 MB, JPG/PNG/WebP (JPG is safest for size).
- Design for the small size first: most impressions are 320x180 or smaller on mobile.
- Avoid the bottom-right ~15% (timestamp) and the bottom strip (progress bar on suggested views).
- Leave a small margin: keep faces and text at least 40 px from every edge.

## The formula that works
1. **One idea.** A thumbnail with two ideas has none.
2. **One focal point**, filling 30-50% of the frame. Crop closer than feels comfortable.
3. **Subject and text on opposite thirds.** Face left, text right, or the reverse.
4. **Three colors maximum,** with the subject separated from the background by brightness, not just hue.
5. **0-4 words.** The thumbnail never repeats the title; it adds to it.
6. **Curiosity gap:** show enough to raise a question the video answers. Never answer it in the image.

## Layout patterns
- **Face + text split:** face on one third, 2-3 huge words on the other
- **Before / after:** vertical split, a divider line or arrow
- **Object hero:** one product or object, dramatic lighting, dark background
- **Reaction + subject:** face reacting to the thing behind or beside them
- **Number / count:** a big numeral as the graphic
- **Comparison:** two subjects, a VS mark, contrasting colors
- **Result shot:** the finished outcome, with a small "how" cue
- **Circle-and-arrow:** a detail highlighted, drawing the eye

## Depth
Build three layers: background (blurred, darkened at the edges), subject (sharp, rim light), foreground graphics (text, arrows, glow). A flat thumbnail looks like a screenshot.

## Lighting and finish
Rim light or an outer glow behind the subject in the subject's accent color. Vignette the corners 10-20%. Slightly increase contrast and saturation over normal photo editing, then check it does not turn muddy at small size.

## How to deliver a thumbnail
1. **Concept first:** the one idea the viewer should get in half a second, in one sentence.
2. **Layout spec:** canvas size, subject placement (left/right third, size as % of frame), background, depth layers, where the eye goes 1st, 2nd, 3rd.
3. **Text:** the exact words (0-4), font style, size in px on a 1280x720 canvas, weight, color, outline/shadow, placement.
4. **Color:** HEX codes for background, subject rim light, text, accent. State the contrast.
5. **Face/subject:** expression, crop, angle, lighting.
6. **Production route:** an AI generation prompt, a step-by-step build in an editor, or an edit list if they already have a draft.
7. **Variants:** at least 2 versions that differ in one real way, for testing.
8. **Small-size check:** what still reads at 320x180.

## Quality standard
Work like a senior thumbnail designer and packaging strategist: specific, practical and original. Give exact pixel sizes, HEX codes, crops, placements and the actual words, never vague advice. Always give at least two testable directions. Keep every thumbnail honest: it may create curiosity, never a false belief about what is in the video.
