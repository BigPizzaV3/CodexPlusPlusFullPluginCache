        Prepare your examples. Communicate your value. Have a better

 The purpose of this guide
 Preparation should make you clearer - not more scripted. This guide is designed to help you
 research the opportunity, prepare evidence from your experience, communicate with discipline and
 evaluate the role with the same care the company is evaluating you.


     A practical guide for candidates who want to prepare without sounding rehearsed.

02 | RESEARCH WITH PURPOSE



Know enough about the business to have a point of view.
Research is not interview trivia. It helps you understand what the company cares about, tailor your
responses and ask questions that reveal whether the opportunity actually makes sense for you.

    BUILD THE BUSINESS CONTEXT                              GO DEEPER WHERE IT MATTERS
    Review the company website, leadership, products,       For public companies, review earnings materials and
    markets, recent news and public messaging.              leadership commentary. For technical or
    Understand the industry, major competitors,             manufacturing businesses, look at product literature,
    customer types, market pressures and the issues         technical papers, patents or innovation activity when
    likely to affect the role.                              relevant. Use credible trade publications for outside
                                                            context.




Turn research into better interview preparation
     Strategic priorities: What is the company trying to grow, fix, integrate, launch, improve or protect?
     Role connection: Why might this position matter to those priorities? What business problem could
      the person in the role help solve?
     Leadership context: Who are you meeting? What do their backgrounds suggest they may care
      about or ask about?
     Questions: What did your research make you genuinely curious about? Those questions are usually
      stronger than generic ones.

    Use research to think - not to perform research at the interviewer.
    Reference what you learned when it is relevant. Ask how a strategic priority affects the role. Connect
    an industry trend to the work. The strongest signal is not the number of facts you can recite; it is the
    quality of the conversation your preparation allows you to have.

05 | INTERVIEW FORWARD



Interview for the open role - not your prior ones.
Your history is the evidence. The new role is the point. Strong candidates translate what they have done
into what they can contribute next.

Make the bridge explicit
    Understand the new work. Know the responsibilities, business needs, level, stakeholders and
     success measures of the position you are pursuing.
    Select the most relevant evidence. You do not need to showcase everything you have done.
     Highlight the experiences that best demonstrate readiness for this role.
    Translate scope. If your past environment was larger, smaller, more centralized, more
     entrepreneurial or used different systems, explain the capability underneath the context.
    Show adaptability. Different organizations use different methods, systems and structures.
     Demonstrate that you can learn and adjust rather than insisting your prior way is the only way.
    Address transitions without defensiveness. If the role changes industry, function, scale or title,
     explain why the move makes sense and what transfers.

Beware the “same title, therefore same job” assumption
Two jobs with the same title can differ radically in scale, complexity, decision rights, customer mix,
team maturity or business expectations. Prepare for the actual work, not the label.

    A better connection sentence
    “The environment was different, but the part that translates directly is…” Then name the capability,
    decision pattern, stakeholder challenge or business result that matters here.

06 | COMMUNICATE WITH DISCIPLINE



Conciseness is a leadership skill.
Strong experience can lose its impact when the answer becomes too long, too detailed or disconnected
from the original question. One of the most common ways strong candidates lose an interviewer is by
over-explaining until the point disappears.

    KEEP IT CLEAR                                           READ THE ROOM
    Use STAR, PAR or the TRX evidence lens to keep your     Pause when you need a moment. Watch for verbal
    story structured. Give enough context to understand     and non-verbal cues that the interviewer has the
    the situation, then spend most of your time on your     information they need. Stop when you have
    action, judgment and result.                            answered the question and let them decide where to
                                                            go deeper.




A practical pacing target
Many examples can be communicated effectively in roughly one to two minutes. That is a guide, not a
stopwatch. Complex executive examples may need more context. The test is whether each detail is
helping the interviewer understand the answer.

When technical depth is part of your value
     Start at the level the interviewer can use. Add technical detail when they ask for it or when it is
      essential to the decision.
     Explain the business implication of the technical issue - cost, risk, quality, speed, customer impact or
      strategic consequence.
     Avoid assuming jargon demonstrates expertise. Clear explanation usually demonstrates mastery
      better.

    The goal is not to sound abbreviated.
    The goal is to make your experience easy to follow, easy to probe and easy to remember.

07 | KEEP THE TONE CONSTRUCTIVE



How you talk about difficult situations matters.
Interviewers learn about your professionalism and resilience from the way you describe past
employers, colleagues, conflict, setbacks and change. You do not need to sanitize reality, but you do
need to show judgment in how you frame it.

    STAY FACTUAL                                               OWN YOUR RESPONSE
    Explain what happened without turning the                  Focus on what you did, what you controlled, what
    interview into a complaint about another person or         you learned and how you would handle a similar
    organization. Specific facts are more credible than        situation now.
    broad judgments.



    SHOW RANGE                                                 LOOK FORWARD
    A story with conflict or failure does not have to end in   When discussing why you are considering a move,
    a perfect outcome. It can still demonstrate judgment,      spend more time on what you are moving toward
    resilience, learning and appropriate accountability.       than what you are trying to escape.




Avoid two extremes
     Overly negative: Blaming a manager, dismissing colleagues or reliving a grievance can become the
      story the interviewer remembers.
     Artificially positive: Pretending every difficult experience was wonderful can sound evasive.
      Candor and professionalism can coexist.

    A constructive framing
    “That environment was challenging because ____. My responsibility was ____. What I learned - and
    what I would do differently now - is ____.”

08 | MANAGING NERVES



Reduce uncertainty. Give yourself room to think.
Nerves are a normal response to a high-stakes conversation. They can make recall and sequencing feel
less fluent, especially when you are trying to retrieve a specific example quickly. Preparation cannot
eliminate every nervous moment, but it can reduce uncertainty and give you ways to regain your
footing.

    BEFORE | Build familiarity                               DURING | Create a little space
    Practice your examples without memorizing them           Pause before responding instead of filling every
    word-for-word. Know the meeting format, people and       second immediately. If you lose your train of thought,
    role. Use slow, intentional breathing to settle your     stop and restart. Return your attention to the
    pace. Give yourself enough time that arrival or          question rather than evaluating your own
    technology does not create additional pressure.          performance while you are speaking.




If your mind goes blank
     Ask for a moment to think. A thoughtful pause is better than an unrelated answer.
     Repeat or paraphrase the question to make sure you are solving the right problem.
     If one perfect example will not come, choose a relevant example and explain why it connects.
     If you genuinely do not have that experience, say so and describe the closest relevant experience,
      how you would approach it or what you would need to learn.

    You do not need a perfect interview.
    You need enough clarity and evidence for the interviewer to understand how you think, what you
    have done and whether the opportunity makes sense on both sides.

09 | MAKE YOUR VALUE CLEAR



Know what differentiates you before someone asks.
Setting yourself apart is not about a rehearsed sales pitch. It is about understanding the combination of
experience, perspective, results and approach you bring - and connecting it to what the organization
needs.

Do not confuse differentiation with perfection
A strong candidate does not need to match every line of a job description. What matters is whether the
combination of capability, experience, learning agility and motivation gives the organization a credible
reason to believe you can do the work.

 TRX perspective
 Your goal is not to prove that you are universally impressive. It is to make the evidence for this role
 easy to see.

10 | YOUR QUESTIONS MATTER



Use your questions to evaluate the opportunity, too.
Thoughtful questions demonstrate preparation and strategic thinking, but they also give you
information you need to determine whether the role, leadership team and organization are right for
you.
01 What are the most immediate challenges the person in this role needs to address?
02 How is success measured for this position?
03 What would you expect the person in this role to accomplish in the first six months?
04 What prompted the need for this role now?
05 How would you describe the team I would be working with and the way the team operates?
06 Where does this role have the most decision authority, and where does it need to influence across
functions?
07 What are the company’s biggest priorities or challenges right now, and how does this role connect
to them?
08 What tends to distinguish people who are highly effective in this organization?
09 Is there anything about my background or qualifications you would like me to clarify?
10 What are the next steps in the interview process?

 Tailor the questions to the conversation.
 Use your research. Ask about something specific you learned. Go deeper on an issue the interviewer
 raised. A prepared list is useful, but the strongest questions often come from listening well during
 the interview.

11 | AFTER THE CONVERSATION



Reflect, learn and adjust.
Each interview gives you information about the opportunity and about your own preparation. A brief,
objective review helps you improve as the process continues.

    REFLECT | What worked?                                 ADAPT | What will you do differently?
    Which examples landed clearly? Where did you feel      Notice where you became too detailed, where nerves
    confident and engaged? What did you learn about the    affected your answer or where a question caught you
    role, company and people that changes your view of     unprepared. Use that information to sharpen the next
    the opportunity?                                       conversation.




Capture the information while it is fresh
     Who did you meet and what did each person appear to care about most?
     What business or role challenges became clearer?
     What questions remain unanswered?
     What examples should you sharpen before the next round?
     Did anything materially increase or decrease your interest in the opportunity?

Follow-up
If a thank-you or follow-up is appropriate, keep it specific. Reference something meaningful from the
conversation, reinforce your interest honestly and clarify anything important that you wish you had
communicated more clearly. Avoid a generic note that could have been sent after any interview.

    If you are working with TRX Search on the role
    Send your recruiter a quick debrief while the conversation is fresh. The more specific you are about
    what you learned, what felt aligned and what concerns remain, the more useful we can be as the
    process continues.

QUICK PRE-INTERVIEW CHECK



Ten minutes before the interview.
☐ I know who I am meeting and the basic structure of the conversation.
☐ I have reviewed the role and the company’s current priorities.
☐ I can explain what genuinely interests me about this opportunity.
☐ I have several relevant examples available - including one involving a challenge or lesson learned.
☐ I can clearly separate my contribution from the broader team or company result.
☐ I know the results, scale or business impact of the examples I am likely to use.
☐ I can explain why my experience translates to this role even where the environment differs.
☐ I have 3-5 thoughtful questions, but I am prepared to follow the conversation instead of reading a list.
☐ My technology, environment or travel plan is handled.
☐ I am prepared - not scripted.



One final reminder
 A strong interview is a conversation with evidence.
 Listen carefully. Answer the question. Make your ownership clear. Explain the decisions that
 mattered. Show the result. Be willing to reflect on what you learned. Then use your questions to
 decide whether the opportunity makes sense for you, too.
