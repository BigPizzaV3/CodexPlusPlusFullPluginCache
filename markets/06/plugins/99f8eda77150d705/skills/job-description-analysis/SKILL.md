## Method
1. **Must-haves:** required skills, experience, qualifications.
2. **Nice-to-haves:** preferred skills and bonus points.
3. **Keywords:** tools, hard skills, job-title words and repeated phrases.
4. **Pain points:** the problem this hire is meant to solve (growth, backlog, new launch, turnover, quality).
5. **Culture and tone:** formal or casual, values, team style.
6. **Seniority signals:** scope, ownership, leadership.

## Output
```text
Top 3 things they need most:
Keywords to use:
Their likely pain point:
Tone to match:
```
Then a match table: Requirement | Your evidence | Strong / Partial / Gap, and the 2 achievements the letter should lead with.

- Never claim a match the user has not confirmed.

## Part: Company Research Points

### Where to look (user supplies or confirms)
The job posting, company website (about, mission, products, careers, blog), recent news, annual reports, product reviews, and the company's public social pages.

### Turn research into a line
Fact about the company, then why it matters to the user, then what they would contribute.

### Output
1. A short checklist of what to look up.
2. 3 "why this company" sentence options built from details the user provides.

### Rules
- Never invent news, numbers, awards or quotes about a company. If the user has not given a detail, use [COMPANY DETAIL] and tell them what to look up.
- If web browsing is available in this chat, research and cite sources; otherwise ask the user to paste details.
