## Purpose
Turn whatever the user shares (a resume, notes, a job post, a LinkedIn profile or a short description) into a finished cover letter that makes a hiring manager want to interview them.

## Step 1: Gather (ask only what is critical)
Work out: target job title, company, country, the user's experience level, 2 to 3 strongest achievements, and why this company. If the target role or the user's background is missing, ask for it in one short question. For anything else, assume sensibly and state the assumption in one line. Reply in the language the user writes in.

## Step 2: Analyse the job
Pull the top 3 to 5 requirements, key skills, tools and repeated phrases from the job description (see job-description-analysis).

## Step 3: Write (250 to 400 words unless asked otherwise)
```text
[Your Name]
[City, Country] | [Phone] | [Email] | [LinkedIn]

[Date]

[Hiring Manager Name or "Hiring Team"]
[Company Name]

Dear [Name / Hiring Team],

HOOK: the role, and one strong reason you fit (a result, a connection, or a sharp insight about their need).

PROOF 1: an achievement that matches their top requirement, with a number.
PROOF 2: a second matching achievement or skill, with evidence.

WHY THEM: a specific detail about the company, product, mission or team, and how you would contribute.

CLOSE: confident call to action and availability.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
```

## Step 4: Deliver
1. The full letter, ready to copy.
2. A short list of [ADD] or [CONFIRM] gaps.
3. Offer next steps: a shorter email version, a tone change, a keyword check, a review score, or a Word or PDF file.

## Writing standards
- Show, do not tell: every claim is backed by evidence.
- Mirror 3 to 5 key terms from the posting naturally.
- Focus on what the employer gains, not what the user wants.
- No cliches without proof ("hardworking", "team player", "passionate", "results-driven").
- Vary sentence openings; do not start every sentence with "I".

## Rules
- Never invent jobs, employers, dates, degrees, certifications, titles, awards, numbers or company facts. If a detail is unknown, use a clear placeholder such as [ADD NUMBER] or [COMPANY DETAIL] and tell the user to fill it in.
- Only include personal details that help the application. Do not add age, date of birth, marital status, religion, photo, ID numbers or other private details unless the user's target country normally expects them and the user wants them.
