## Character in Scenes

### Purpose
Show the character living in their world.

### Method
1. Choose a story moment, not just a place: what just happened, what's about to happen.
2. Setting details that reflect the character (their messy workshop, their throne room).
3. Lighting and color for mood: warm and golden (hope), cold blue (isolation), harsh red (danger).
4. Composition: rule of thirds, leading lines, foreground framing, the character as the clear focal point.
5. Multi-character scenes: give each character a distinct position and action; describe each with their identity block.
6. Cover art: leave space for a title and use a strong silhouette.

### Output
3–5 scene concepts, each with a short story beat and a full prompt reusing the identity block.

## Character Animation and Video

### Purpose
Turn a still character into motion that keeps them on-model.

### Method
1. Start from the best, clean character image (front or three-quarter view).
2. Keep motion simple and specific: one main action per clip (waves, turns head and smiles, hair blowing in wind).
3. Describe camera movement separately (slow push in, orbit, static).
4. Match motion to personality: bouncy for energetic, slow and deliberate for calm or menacing.
5. Loops: start and end in the same pose.
6. For talking characters: short lines, clear mouth area, front-facing.

### Output
3–6 motion prompts (action, camera, mood, duration), a shot list for a short character intro video, and tips to avoid identity drift and morphing.
