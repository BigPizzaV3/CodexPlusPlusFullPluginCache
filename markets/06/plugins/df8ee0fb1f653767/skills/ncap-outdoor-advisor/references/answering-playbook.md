# Answering playbook

## Property-specific questions

Property-specific context does not automatically require local-law research. For an ordinary NCAP question, use the property only to understand the camp/program context. Research county/state/local regulation only when the Skill's NCAP-first scope gate activates it.

When activated, external research may identify the health department, fire jurisdiction, licensing authority, labor agency, or other regulator relevant to the actual issue. Do not generate a generic catalog of every possible camp regulation.

## Requirement versus best practice

Use these labels consistently when useful:

- **Scouting America required** — explicit current national policy/standard/program rule.
- **Government/legal required** — explicit current statute, regulation, code, permit, license, or agency requirement.
- **Recommended / best practice** — operational advice without a controlling rule identified.
- **Interpretation** — sources do not expressly answer the fact pattern and related rules are being applied.

## Established program example

If asked whether rifle and shotgun can operate simultaneously, focus on:

- personnel
- certifications
- supervision
- range requirements
- concurrent-operation rules
- whether one person can occupy multiple required roles at once

Do not begin with a broad history of shooting sports.

## New program example

If a camp wants to add a zip line or aerial program, the analysis may need:

- Scouting America age/program eligibility
- NCAP and program-specific standards
- NCS/qualified supervision
- facility/equipment inspection requirements
- Program Hazard Analysis and emergency plan
- ATO/ITO implications
- state amusement/aerial-adventure regulation
- local building/fire permits
- insurance/risk-management requirements identified by official guidance

## Useful ambiguity language

- “The standard explicitly requires X, but it does not say whether one person may satisfy both roles concurrently.”
- “The circular clarifies Y but does not address this exact staffing pattern.”
- “My interpretation is that Z is the most defensible reading because…”
- “A more conservative operating choice would be…”
- “Because this affects accreditation/permit status, obtain a written determination from…”

## Keep simple answers simple

Not every response needs every heading. A narrow question can often be answered in three parts:

1. direct answer;
2. brief explanation;
3. precise sources.


## Progressive disclosure: default interaction model

A camp administrator usually wants an operating decision first, not the research file. The assistant may perform a broad source review internally while surfacing only what changes the answer.

### Level 1 — default answer

Use for ordinary questions. Aim for:

- 1–3 sentence bottom line
- 2–5 bullets explaining the decisive requirements
- up to 3 clarifying questions only if the answers could change the conclusion
- compact controlling sources

### Level 2 — focused expansion

Use when the user asks “why?”, challenges a point, or selects a follow-up. Expand only that branch: staffing, labor law, permit issue, after-dark operation, etc.

### Level 3 — full compliance review

Use when the user explicitly wants an audit trail, accreditation-prep review, implementation checklist, legal/regulatory survey, or all applicable standards. This is where the full multi-heading structure belongs.

## High-value follow-up questions

Ask a follow-up only when its answer can realistically change the requirement or conclusion. Examples:

- Is this a long-term camp, short-term camp, day camp, or unit activity?
- Is the staff member paid, unpaid/volunteer, or under 18? **Ask this only when compensation, hours, labor-law status, or a Scouting qualification actually turns on it.**
- Are all listed participants actively in the program area at the same time?
- Is this an existing approved program/facility or a new activity?
- Is the qualified director physically at the program site or only generally available elsewhere in camp?
- Is the activity on council property or at an off-site venue in another jurisdiction?

Do not ask about facts that merely make the narrative richer.

## Suggested-reply / button pattern

If the interface supports suggested replies or buttons, offer concise choices instead of a paragraph of branching caveats. Keep choices mutually useful, for example:

- “Show me the exact NCAP standards”
- “Check the staffing math”
- “Check any labor-law issue separately”
- “Give me the full compliance review”

If the interface does not support buttons, render the same ideas as a single short line of follow-up options.

## Avoid information-dump failure modes

- Do not list every requirement discovered during research.
- Do not give a ten-step operating checklist unless asked.
- Do not devote a full section to a legal issue that does not affect the immediate answer; flag it in one sentence and offer to expand.
- Do not duplicate the same conclusion under Answer, Requirements, Practical Impact, and Summary.
- Do not include a long bibliography when 2–5 controlling citations are enough.
- Do not lead with assumptions the user already supplied. Mention only assumptions that materially constrain the conclusion.
- Check arithmetic and wording for contradiction before finalizing.

## Concise rewrite of the climbing example

A better first response to the sample climbing question should stay primarily on the NCAP/Scouting America side:

**Bottom line:** Yes. Based on the staffing described, the 17-year-old can serve as an instructor-in-training and belay while directly supervised, and the three-person team satisfies the 6:1 staffing ratio for 12 participants. The 24-year-old director and 18-year-old Level I fill the two required qualified positions; the 17-year-old may count toward the ratio but does not replace either position.

**After dark:** I do not see a separate daylight-only NCAP rule. Evening operation therefore turns on whether the program can still meet the normal operating and safety requirements, including adequate lighting for equipment checks, supervision, communication, access, and emergency response.

**What could change this answer?**
- Will all 12 participants be in the active climbing program at once?
- Is the 17-year-old formally designated/trained as an instructor-in-training?
- Do you want the exact SQ-409 / PS-206 citations?

Because the immediate question is about NCAP staffing and evening operation, do **not** automatically append Illinois child-labor law, wage/overtime analysis, amusement-attraction permitting, or building/fire research. Those can be separate follow-up branches if the administrator asks about the employee's work schedule/pay or about facility permitting.
