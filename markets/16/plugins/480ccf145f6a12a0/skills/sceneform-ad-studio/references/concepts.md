# Ad concepts

A concept is the brief the ad's own writer works from. It decides what the
ad says; the template or hook decides how it looks. One concept, one angle.

## Template

Write the `concept` as short labelled lines:

```
Angle: problem-solution
Audience: runners training for their first marathon, 25 to 40
Message: the bottle keeps water cold through a three-hour long run
Proof: double-wall steel; 24 hours cold (from the product page)
Offer: 15% off the first order with code FIRST15
Call to action: Shop the bottle
Show: the bottle in hand at mile 20, condensation on the steel
Tone: plain and confident, like a coach
```

Leave out any line the brief gives no facts for. A line you would have to
make up is a line to drop.

## Angles

Each ad in a set takes a different angle, so a test compares ideas, not
wording.

| Angle | What the ad shows |
| --- | --- |
| Problem-solution | the pain in the first beat, the product ending it |
| Transformation | the end state the buyer wants |
| Demonstration | the product doing its one job, up close |
| Feature zoom | one differentiating detail, large |
| Comparison | before and after, or this against the usual way |
| Social proof | real reviews, ratings, or customer counts, only when the user or kit supplies them |
| Curiosity | an image or line that needs the ad to explain it |
| Lifestyle | the life the product fits into |
| Urgency | a real deadline or limited stock, only when one exists |
| Founder story | the person behind the brand, in their words |
| Unboxing | the reveal, the packaging, the first touch |
| Behind the scenes | how it is made |

## Claims

- Use only claims the user or the BrandKit supplies, word for word. Never
  strengthen, combine, or round them.
- With no supplied claims, describe what anyone can see or feel: the
  material, the mechanism, the texture. No numbers, rankings, or results.
- A generated creator is a demonstrator, never a customer: no invented
  testimonials, purchase stories, ratings, or before and after results.
- Prices, discounts, and codes come from the user.

## Copy

- A headline is at most about seven words and says one concrete thing.
- Specific beats grand: "Cold for 24 hours" over "Stay refreshed".
- These words read as filler and lower trust: game-changer, holy grail,
  obsessed, elevate, seamless, effortless, unlock, supercharge,
  revolutionary, "changed my life", "you NEED this". Replace each with the
  concrete fact it stands in for.
- Write in the brand's `voice`, and quote a `voice_exemplars` line from the
  kit when the tone is unclear.

## Video ad concepts

The production agent writes the script and shots from the concept, so the
concept carries the story:

- **The hook.** The first second already shows or says the most surprising
  thing. Name it in the concept ("opens on the bottle frosting over in the
  sun"). Openers like "Hey guys", "So", "OK so", and "Stop scrolling" waste
  that second.
- **One turn.** The problem, then one "but then", then the resolution. Two
  turns do not fit in 15 seconds.
- **The product as the turn.** It enters about halfway, as what changes the
  situation.
- **The call to action inside the ending,** spoken or shown, not as a
  separate outro.
- **The `setting_brief`** names one place the audience recognises ("a
  cramped studio apartment kitchen at 7am"), without a shot list.

## Casting

When the ad shows a person, pick the Persona by what the product changes:

- skincare and makeup: a bare or lightly made-up face;
- hair care: hair down, with its natural texture;
- sleep and energy: a little tiredness around the eyes;
- fashion: fully styled, in the brand's register.

A Persona's portrait without the product or props works best; the product
comes in through `product_ref`.

## Checklist

- One angle, and it differs from the other ads in the set.
- Every claim, price, and code traces to the user or the kit.
- The headline idea is concrete and under about seven words.
- No word from the filler list.
- A video concept names its hook, its one turn, and its ending.
- The size matches the placement.
