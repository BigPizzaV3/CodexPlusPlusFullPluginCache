# Medical terminology lesson: origins, word-building, context

## Non-negotiable parts of a term lesson
1. **Term, sense and pronunciation:** English term, part of speech, domain-specific Vietnamese equivalent. Provide IPA (UK/US where relevant) only when reliable. Mark whether meaning differs in general English versus medicine; note alternative terms.
2. **Etymology:** historical language/source and original meaning **only if verified or high-confidence**. Etymology is a historical claim: do not infer it merely by noticing English-looking chunks. If unknown, say `Nguồn gốc lịch sử chưa xác minh` and proceed without speculation.
3. **Morphology / word formation:** explain affixes, roots, compounds, borrowing, abbreviations where actually applicable. Show a simple diagram `dys- (difficulty/abnormal) + -phagia (eating/swallowing) → dysphagia`. Explicitly note that this is a modern pedagogic analysis, not automatically the entire historical derivation; some terms are not productively segmentable.
4. **In context:** at least 2 concrete, idiomatic, clinically realistic original English examples, each paired with fluent Vietnamese. Prefer contrasting registers: scientific/clinical note, clinician-patient communication or OET. Label examples `Ví dụ minh họa (giả định)` and do not imply real patient facts.
5. **Usage:** common collocations, related morphological family, confusing terms and one false friend when useful. Distinguish similar diagnoses and linguistic meanings carefully; clinical diagnostic claims require verification.

## Model mini-lesson (illustrative, not a dictionary citation)
**Dysphagia** /dɪsˈfeɪdʒiə/ (noun): khó nuốt; not the same as odynophagia (painful swallowing).
- **Nguồn gốc:** from Greek elements associated with difficulty and eating/swallowing; refine exact historical pathway only after verifying a dictionary.
- **Cấu tạo để ghi nhớ:** `dys-` (khó/bất thường) + `-phagia` (ăn/nuốt) → dysphagia (khó nuốt).
- **In context — clinical note:** “The patient reports intermittent dysphagia to solid foods.” → “Người bệnh cho biết thỉnh thoảng khó nuốt thức ăn đặc.”
- **In context — patient communication:** “Do you have difficulty swallowing liquids as well?” → “Anh/chị có khó nuốt cả chất lỏng không?” (Uses the accessible paraphrase *difficulty swallowing* rather than jargon.)
- **Collocations:** oropharyngeal dysphagia; esophageal dysphagia; progressive dysphagia. Do not equate difficulty swallowing with painful swallowing.

## When a word is not Greek or Latin
For words like *feedback*, *follow-up*, or *handover*, describe actual English compounding/phrasing rather than invent a Greco-Latin decomposition. For modern eponyms, acronyms, and brand names, distinguish naming history from morphemes; if unverified, say so.

## Interactive vocabulary learning
After teaching, optionally offer a one-question recall prompt (e.g. choose usage or paraphrase) and give feedback on the user's response. Never require quizzes; user's request comes first.
