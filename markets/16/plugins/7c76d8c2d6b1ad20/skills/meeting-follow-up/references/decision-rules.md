# Decision and commitment rules

## Decision status

Classify each item as:

- `CONFIRMED`: the group or authorized participant clearly settled the choice.
- `TENTATIVE`: the choice is provisional or depends on a condition.
- `PROPOSED`: an option was suggested but not accepted.
- `REOPENED`: a previous decision is under renewed discussion.
- `REVERSED`: a later confirmed choice superseded an earlier decision.
- `UNCLEAR`: the source does not establish whether a decision was made.

“Maybe,” “we could,” “what if,” or “let's consider” signal a proposal. “Agreed,” “we decided,” or “we'll proceed with” may support a confirmed decision only in context; check for objections, conditions, later changes, and who had authority. Do not infer unanimous agreement from silence.

## Record a decision

When supported, capture the decision, status, decision-maker or agreeing participants, reason, effective date, evidence, source location, and confidence. Omit unknown fields or label them `NOT_PROVIDED`/`UNCLEAR`; do not infer decision authority or rationale.

Distinguish an operational choice from a formal approval. Casual acknowledgment does not automatically authorize spending, scope, legal terms, publication, or other consequential actions. Clearly flag conditional or pending approval.

## Commitments

Record a commitment when a participant clearly promises an action, deliverable, update, or response. Keep the person, commitment, deadline, recipient, evidence, and confidence when available. A question such as “could we send this?” or “should someone call?” is not a promise. A general team aspiration is not an individual commitment.

## Conflicts and revisions

If a later statement explicitly changes a confirmed decision, use the latest confirmed state and note the earlier state when it matters. If chronology or agreement does not resolve the conflict, show both statements and mark the decision `UNCLEAR` or `REOPENED`; do not pick a winner.
