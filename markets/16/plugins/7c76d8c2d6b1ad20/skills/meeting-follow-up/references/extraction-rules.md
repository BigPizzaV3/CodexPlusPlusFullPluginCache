# Extraction rules

## Read and segment the full source

Use the complete material available in the conversation or attached documents. Identify source boundaries, meeting date, participant names, speaker labels, and major topics only when supplied. A transcript converted from a recording is supported; the skill does not process raw audio or recordings.

Group discussion by topic. Keep statements attributed to the speaker when that distinction matters. Separate facts stated by a participant from another participant's interpretation or assumption. Do not infer that the loudest or most senior person is the decision-maker.

## Classify each item

- **Discussion point:** information exchanged, without a decision or accepted task.
- **Proposal:** an option or idea being considered, not yet agreed.
- **Decision:** a clearly settled choice, with status and evidence.
- **Action item:** a concrete task the meeting expects someone to complete.
- **Commitment:** an explicit promise or undertaking by a participant.
- **Open question:** a material question that remains unanswered.
- **Blocker/dependency:** an obstacle or prerequisite stated in the source.
- **Risk:** a possible adverse outcome identified in the source; do not invent risks merely because they are common in that meeting type.

Do not duplicate the same content in several sections unless a concise summary and detailed action log serve different purposes. Preserve distinct tasks when deliverables or deadlines differ.

## Evidence and provenance

For important decisions, commitments, owners, and deadlines, include a compact evidence note when the source provides a useful timestamp, page, section, speaker label, or short excerpt. Evidence should let the user trace the result without overwhelming the recap. Do not fabricate timestamps, page numbers, speaker identity, or transcript wording.

If two participants describe different facts, attribute both rather than choosing one. If a later statement updates an earlier one, note the revision when it affects an action, scope, or date.

## Context-specific extraction

- Sales calls: distinguish prospect statements from sales interpretations. Extract goals, needs, objections, buying criteria, stakeholders, budget only if explicitly discussed, timeline, promised follow-ups, and next meeting.
- Client/project meetings: separate requested changes from approved scope; capture deliverables, responsibilities, dependencies, approval state, and blocked work.
- Standups: organize each participant's completed work, current work, blockers, and requests for help; then show shared dependencies and urgent items.
- One-on-ones: summarize only relevant topics and commitments. Avoid unnecessarily repeating sensitive personal, performance, or career details.
- Brainstorms and workshops: retain ideas as proposals until a decision or task is explicitly agreed.
