# Assessment rules

Use pass, gap, partial and unknown for business checks, with an observation and a next step. “Observed working” is limited to the exact behavior checked. Report evidence coverage as known checks / total checks, not a performance percentage. Lighthouse is a tool score, not a business grade.

No composite score by default. If explicitly requested, define the rubric before assigning grades, include unknowns and coverage, publish weights, and suppress a composite below a declared coverage threshold. Do not borrow proprietary vendor scores or present invented industry percentiles.

Prioritize in dependency order: inquiry delivery and measurement; friction and service-page relevance; controlled acquisition test; customer follow-up. Adapt this sequence to the business. A business booked beyond capacity may need qualification and scheduling before more demand.

For each recommendation: state the evidence, why it matters, the work, an owner role, a timeframe or prerequisite and a definition of done. Avoid unsupported lift percentages and false precision in effort/impact scores.

Opportunity model: additional relevant visits × inquiry rate × qualification rate × close rate × average job value = expected booked revenue before costs. Identify every assumed input. Show sensitivity, not just an optimistic point. Do not equate search volume with visits or revenue with profit. Use actual margins, acquisition costs and capacity to set a budget.

The first pilot validates the report format and tooling against one business. It does not prove that unattended scraping, advertiser matching or business diagnosis works across every industry. Human/agent review remains necessary.
