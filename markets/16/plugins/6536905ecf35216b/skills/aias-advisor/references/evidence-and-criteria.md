# Evidence and rubric starting points

These are starting points from the existing adviser instructions. Adapt them to the learning outcomes, discipline, student stage and grading system. They are not compulsory artefacts or fixed weightings for every task.

| Level | Evidence to consider | Criteria to consider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 No AI | Work produced under controlled conditions | Accuracy, reasoning and technique, as appropriate to the independently demonstrated outcomes |
| 2 AI Planning | An annotated outline or topic map; sources with notes on what was rejected | Quality of framing, range and appropriateness of sources, development of the idea |
| 3 AI Collaboration | The disciplinary work plus a short record of what was kept, changed or rejected from AI output and why | Quality of the work and quality of the student's evaluation and integration decisions; weight both |
| 4 Full AI | The artefact, the approach to directing AI and evidence that important AI claims were checked | Sophistication of direction, subject knowledge used in judging outputs, quality of the result |
| 5 AI Exploration | A proposal, proportionate process record and artefact | Originality, disciplinary insight, ethical reasoning and how the approach was reached |

Before drafting a rubric, establish the institution's grading system, relevant outcomes and constraints. If asked to proceed with assumptions, state them. Explain why each criterion assesses an intended outcome and how the proposed evidence supports it.

Keep process requirements proportionate to the task. They should reveal judgement rather than create burdensome logs or claim to authenticate authorship. Do not reward prompt quantity, access to paid tools or superficial declarations instead of disciplinary learning.

## When proposing a revised task

Offer the pieces the educator requested. A complete redesign can cover:

1. Task purpose and learning outcomes.
2. Chosen AIAS task type and any secure conditions.
3. What students do and what AI contributes.
4. Evidence students produce, including meaningful checkpoints where appropriate.
5. Criteria aligned to the work and judgement being assessed.
6. Access or implementation requirements and assumptions to resolve.

Use existing formats and constraints where supplied. Do not invent institutional policies, deadlines, grade weights or tool entitlements. Separate the official level statement from local task-specific wording.
