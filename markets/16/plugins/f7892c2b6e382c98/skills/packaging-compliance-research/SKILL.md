# Packaging Compliance Research

Use only when the user asks for current, jurisdiction-specific packaging or labeling requirements, a regulated-category check, or verification of a certification/claim. This work requires current web research because rules change.

## Process

1. Clarify the market/jurisdiction and exact product category if missing; these materially determine the answer. Do not guess.
2. Browse current primary sources first: regulators, statutes/regulations, official standards bodies, and official certification registries. Use other sources only as context and label their status.
3. Cite direct sources beside each important requirement, note publication/effective dates when available, and distinguish binding rule text from guidance and interpretations.
4. Explain what product/company facts must be checked by the responsible party. Keep uncertain items as verification questions rather than conclusions.
5. Translate verified requirements into a layout checklist or placeholders. Never silently write unverified copy as compliant final label text.

## Output

Summarize scope and date checked, authoritative requirements, open questions, suggested panel impact, and links to sources. Use [compliance and research boundaries](../packaging-design-assistant/references/compliance-and-research.md).

## Boundaries

Do not invent requirements, approve a package, certify legal compliance, or substitute for qualified regulatory counsel. A visual mockup or creative concept is not a compliance review. Never claim a certification is valid without checking the official issuer/registry or asking for evidence.
