# Packaging Concepts

Use when the user wants initial packaging directions, ideation, package format recommendations, shelf positioning, or a family of product variants.

## Inputs

Start with the supplied product/category and task. Use optional brand, audience, channel, price tier, package type, dimensions, claims, existing assets, and manufacturer constraints where given. Do not force a full questionnaire; call out only assumptions that matter.

## Process

1. Summarize the product facts and unknowns in one short line.
2. Recommend a structure only as a concept, considering protection, use, shipping, sales channel, sustainability evidence, cost cues, and user constraints.
3. Create three distinct directions when ideation is open-ended. Vary composition, type, image style, hierarchy, color logic, and structural presentation instead of renaming one layout.
4. For each direction specify concept name, design idea, focal element, product/variant treatment, material/finish ideas, shelf/ecommerce behavior, production notes, and unknowns.
5. Keep facts and claims tied to supplied or verified information. Use placeholders for missing mandatory content.

## Output

Use a concise comparison followed by useful panel details. Follow the concept schema in [concept and panel rules](../packaging-design-assistant/references/concept-and-layout-rules.md). Add a handoff section listing facts or supplier specifications still needed.

## Checks and boundaries

Keep product name/type identifiable; maintain a clear hierarchy; make concepts different at a glance; keep decorative ideas away from information that must remain legible. Do not invent package dimensions, product benefits, certifications, ingredient/nutrition data, or manufacturing feasibility. Do not claim consumer preference or retail performance without testing.
