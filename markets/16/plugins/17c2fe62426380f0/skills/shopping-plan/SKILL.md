name: shopping-plan
description: Turn a room design, restyling plan, render or inspiration image into a prioritised shopping plan with a buying order in phases. Use when the user asks what to buy for a look, wants a shopping list or budget breakdown for a room, asks what to buy first, or wants to spread the cost of a makeover over time.
---

Produce a buying plan the user can follow over weeks or months: every piece that makes the look, ranked by how much it changes the room, grouped into phases so the room looks deliberate after each one.

The user's explicit instructions win over the defaults here. If they give a total budget, the plan fits inside it.

## 1. Establish the source

The plan needs a look to work from. Use, in this order: a restyling plan already made in this conversation, an image the user shares (their render, an inspiration photo), or a description. If there is none, ask for one. Do not invent a look to shop for.

Ask what they already own that stays, if it is not clear. Never put something they already have on the list.

## 2. List the pieces

Go through the look and list each piece of furniture and decor. For each:

- **Item** — what it is, in plain words, with material and colour.
- **Category** — seating, tables, storage, lighting, rugs, textiles, art or decor.
- **Priority** — 1 to 5. 1 changes the room most and is bought first.
- **What to look for** — the details that make it right for this look: proportions, leg style, finish, fabric, size relative to the room.
- **Price range** — only if the user wants costs. Give a low and a high figure in their currency and label the column as an estimate.

Assume a renter unless told otherwise: only furniture, textiles, lighting that plugs in, decor and removable items. No flooring, tiling, joinery or wiring.

## 3. Order by impact

Priority follows visual weight, not price. Usual order:

1. The largest surface you can change: rug, curtains, bedding.
2. The anchor piece: sofa, bed or dining table.
3. Lighting.
4. Secondary furniture: side tables, chairs, storage.
5. Art, plants and objects.

Break the order when the room demands it. A room with good furniture and bad lighting starts with lamps.

## 4. Group into phases

Three or four phases. Each has a name, the items in it, and a rough subtotal if costs were requested. After each phase the room should look finished at that level, not half-moved-in.

- **Phase 1** — the few things that set the look. Often textiles and one anchor piece.
- **Phase 2** — the remaining large pieces.
- **Phase 3** — lighting and secondary furniture.
- **Phase 4** — art, objects and the last 10 percent.

## 5. Present it

A table for the items, then the phases as a short list. Add the total range if costs were requested. Close with the one purchase to make first and why.

## Rules

- Estimates are estimates. Say so once, clearly. Do not present a figure as a shop price.
- Describe types of pieces. Name a specific product, brand or shop only if the user asks for that and you can look it up; never invent product names, links or stock.
- Sizes must suit the room. If the room's dimensions are unknown and a size matters (rug, sofa, table), ask or tell the user what to measure.
- Suggest second-hand for solid wood furniture, mirrors, lamps and frames when the user is cost-conscious.
- If a total budget cannot cover the look, say which pieces to drop or substitute, in order.
