# Renter swaps

Most looks are defined in photographs of rooms that were built for them: panelled walls, stone floors, coves of hidden light. A renter can have the look without any of that. Translate each built-in feature into something freestanding or removable. Each style sheet lists its own swaps; these are the general ones for when a sheet has none.

## Walls

- Wood panelling, fluted or ribbed wall panels -> a freestanding folding screen, a fluted-front cabinet, or a large textured artwork leaning on the wall.
- A stone slab feature wall -> stone on tabletops, a stone-topped console or a plinth, lit by a lamp.
- Plaster, limewash or clay render -> limewash-effect or matte clay-toned paint, where painting is allowed.
- Murals and colour-block walls -> removable wallcovering, or paint if the lease allows it.
- Mouldings, cornices and panel frames -> an oversized framed mirror and art hung in a tight grid. Do not suggest stick-on mouldings as if they were the real thing.
- Gallery walls -> removable hanging strips, or frames leaned on a picture ledge or the floor.

## Floors

- Stone, terrazzo, tile, chequerboard or painted floors -> a large rug in that tone or pattern over the existing floor.
- Dark stained or ebonised floors -> a dark rug or a dark rug border.
- Parquet or wide-plank timber -> leave the floor, bring the wood tone in through furniture.

## Built-in furniture

- Built-in shelving and library walls -> freestanding or modular shelving, painted inside in the accent colour if the look calls for it.
- Built-in seating, window seats, banquettes -> a freestanding bench with a fitted cushion.
- Handleless fitted cabinetry -> flush-fronted freestanding units, or removable wraps on existing fronts.
- A wall-hung console or floating vanity -> a slim freestanding console on legs.

## Lighting

- Cove lighting, recessed downlights, wall washers -> lamps set low behind furniture, plug-in picture lights, adhesive LED strips with cords in raceways.
- Hard-wired sconces -> plug-in sconces or a pair of table lamps.
- A statement chandelier on a new ceiling point -> a plug-in pendant on a ceiling hook only if hooks are allowed, otherwise an arc floor lamp.

## Architecture

- Arches, columns, beams, vaults, niches, fireplaces -> only where they already exist. Never invented. Echo the shape with an arched floor mirror, a round mirror or a curved piece of furniture.
- Exposed brick or concrete -> only where it already exists. Otherwise a dark, textured paint colour.

## Ceiling, light and floor: the hard lines

These are where a renter plan most often goes wrong.

- The ceiling stays exactly as it is: same plane, slope and height. No crown moulding, cornice, cove, dropped section, recessed spots or light strips.
- A chandelier or pendant hangs only from a ceiling light point that already exists. Every other light is a lamp or a plug-in sconce.
- The floor keeps its material and colour. Colour or pattern on the floor arrives only as a rug, with the existing floor visible around its edges.
- Bookcases and shelving stand clear of the ceiling and the corners, so they read as furniture and not as joinery.
- A sideboard or console replaces a media wall.
- Light switches, sockets, intercoms, fuse boxes and radiators stay where they are and stay reachable. Furniture may stand in front of a socket; nothing is built over one.
- Curtains can be added to any window, but the glass stays visible behind or beside them. A glazed wall or conservatory front stays glass.
- In kitchens and on dated fitted fronts, peel-and-stick wallcovering and vinyl wraps are fair game where the lease allows them. Say that they need a clean, flat surface and a test patch.

## Always safe

Rugs, curtains on tension rods or existing rails, lamps, freestanding furniture, plants, art leaned or hung on removable strips, bedding, cushions, throws, trays and objects.
