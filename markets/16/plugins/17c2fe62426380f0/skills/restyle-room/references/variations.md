# Variations and directions

Two different requests that sound alike. Decide which one the user is making.

## Siblings: "show me a few versions of this"

The user likes a look and wants options inside it. Someone who liked the base must like every version. Do not produce unrelated worlds.

Keep the essence: the core mood, the defining materials, the era or place the look is rooted in. Change one or two of these axes per version, never all of them:

- **Colour temperature** — warmer, cooler, more saturated or more muted.
- **Material emphasis** — more timber, more stone, more textile or more metal.
- **Density** — sparser and more restrained, or layered and more collected.
- **Lighting mood** — bright and daylit, or low, pooled and evening.
- **One hero gesture** — a statement piece, a wall colour, a rug, a plant scheme.

For each version give:

- A name of two or three words that a person could say out loud. Not "Variation 3".
- One line on what changed relative to the base.
- The concrete result: the actual furniture, materials, colours and light.

The versions must be tellable apart at a glance. If two would photograph the same, change one. Three is usually enough; five at most.

## Strangers: "I don't know what I want" or "give me ideas for a hotel feel"

The user has a room and at most a theme. Give directions that are as far apart as possible.

- With a theme, treat it as the axis and interpret it in radically different ways. "Five-star hotel" could be a glass tower suite, an Alpine lodge, a Tokyo ryokan, a Parisian grand hotel and a tropical resort.
- Without a theme, vary era, geography, material palette, colour temperature and mood.
- Each direction must feel like a different world from the others.
- Ground each one in this room: mention its real windows, proportions and fixed features.
- Prefer looks from the style index. Where a direction has no match there, describe it in the same terms the sheets use (palette, materials, shapes, light, signature moves, avoid) rather than naming a style and stopping.

Give three to five directions, each with a name, two sentences, a palette and the one move that defines it. Then ask which to develop into a full plan.

## Neither changes the room

No version or direction adds or removes windows, walls, doors, ceilings or built-in structure. Only furniture, textiles, lighting, decor and removable finishes vary.
