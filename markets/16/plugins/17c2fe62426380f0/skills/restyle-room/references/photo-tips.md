# Room photo tips

Give this advice when the user's photo is too tight, too dark or too distorted to plan from, or when they ask how to photograph a room for a redesign.

## The one rule

Stand in the doorway or a corner and get at least three walls in the frame. A shot into a corner shows two walls, which is not enough: the shape and layout of the room have to be readable. Floor and ceiling do not count as walls. Count only walls that are actually visible, not ones hidden behind a wardrobe.

## What a good room photo looks like

Judge a photo against a professional interior shot:

- **Camera height** — hip height, about 100 to 120 cm (40 to 47 in). Standing eye level looks down on the room; floor level looks up.
- **Verticals** — walls, door frames and window edges parallel to the edges of the photo. No leaning.
- **Level** — not tilted left or right.
- **Lens** — the normal lens. Ultra-wide bends the walls.
- **Light** — daylight, curtains and blinds open. A dark or blown-out photo is the most common reason a redesign fails.
- **Framing** — floor to ceiling if possible, and a good share of the room.
- **Foreground** — nothing large between the camera and the room. A chair back or a door edge filling a third of the frame ruins an otherwise good photo.
- **Clutter** — ordinary mess is fine. Extreme mess hides the room.
- **Orientation** — horizontal.
- **File** — the original from the camera roll.

## What does not work

- A single wall, an alcove or a corner: that is not a room.
- One piece of furniture on its own: show the room it sits in.
- Screenshots of photos, photos of a screen, and images with black or white bars around them: they lose resolution and add borders or glare.
- Watermarked or stock images.
- 3D renders, floor plans, sketches and drawings, when the aim is to restyle a real photograph.
- Exteriors and gardens.

## When the photo falls short

Still help with what is visible. Name the specific problems in a few words ("camera too high", "too dark", "ceiling cut off", "wardrobe blocks the left wall") and say exactly what a second photo should show.
