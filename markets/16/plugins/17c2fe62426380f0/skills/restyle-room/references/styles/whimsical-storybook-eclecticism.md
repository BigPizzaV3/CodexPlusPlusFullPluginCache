# Whimsical Storybook Eclecticism

Peacock blue, butter yellow, scallops and wit

**Mood:** joyful, witty, charming, inviting, imaginative

## Essence
A joyful and witty atmosphere created by a vibrant mix of saturated colors and playful, imaginative details, resulting in a unique, storybook-like setting.

## Palette
- Base: peacock blue (#2E6A8A), butter yellow (#E9C97C), crisp gallery white (#F2EFE8), mid-tone oak brown (#A9743F), soft verdigris green (#7E9B84)
- Accent: raspberry pink (#C64465), tomato red (#BE3B2C), cobalt blue (#2B4CA8), marigold (#E0A32E)
- Balance: One saturated hue, such as peacock blue or butter yellow, anchors each room on large surfaces like textiles or painted furniture. Accent colors are used sparingly and cheerfully, often in clashing combinations, on smaller objects and decorative details. The overall palette is warm and moderately to highly saturated.

## Materials
- brass (unlacquered)
- wicker (woven)
- rattan (woven)
- green marble (polished, veined)
- colorful stone (polished)
- glazed ceramic (glossy)
- velvet (plush)
- cotton (matte, crisp)
- papier-mache (matte)
- plaster (matte)
- antiqued mirror (reflective)

## Shapes
Dominantly comfortable, deep, and classic volumes often softened with whimsical and unexpected elements like scalloped edges, cartoon-like curves, and elegant cabriole forms. Thin, linear elements are used for delicate details.

## Texture and pattern
A rich and varied mix of textures, including plush velvets, crisp cottons, woven wicker, and smooth polished stone. Patterns are prominent and diverse, featuring large-scale painterly florals, bold stripes (like red-and-cream grain-sack), checkerboard and diamond motifs, and whimsical folk prints. Pattern scale varies from expansive to intricate.

## Light
Warm and inviting, often diffused by playful, characterful fixtures with pleated or scalloped fabric shades or glowing paper lanterns. Lighting creates pools of light and contributes to the room's narrative rather than providing even illumination.

## Composition
Asymmetrical and layered, creating a storied and cozy atmosphere. Key pieces in saturated colors or witty, surreal objects are strategically placed as focal points. Surfaces are often home to artfully arranged collections and decorative groupings, contributing to a sense of density and charm.

## Contrast
Strong contrast between saturated color fields and crisp white backgrounds, and between large-scale patterns and solid blocks of color. There is also tactile contrast between smooth and textured materials.

## Negative space
Moderately dense, with surfaces often displaying thoughtful groupings of objects and layered textiles. Empty wall and floor space is present but intentionally composed, contributing to a cozy, lived-in feel rather than sparsity.

## Signature moves
- Scalloped edges on textiles, soft furnishings, or woven pieces.
- A large expanse of butter yellow fabric, or the interiors of storage pieces painted in a saturated peacock blue.

## Avoid
- beige-on-beige minimalism
- industrial raw concrete and steel
- sombre moody monochrome
- matching furniture suites
- sparse gallery emptiness
- cold grey palettes

## The full-build version
The concrete pieces and moves of the look as a designer would build it with no constraints. Use it to choose actual pieces. Anything fixed to the building goes through the renter swaps below, and furniture that belongs to another kind of room is translated to this room's purpose.

### Key pieces
- **Silhouettes:** Classic comfortable English shapes — deep sofas, tufted ottomans, bentwood chairs — given fairy-tale twists like scalloped edges, cartoon curves and cabriole feet.
- **Signature pieces:** shell-shaped scalloped wicker chair; deep-buttoned ottoman in shocking pink; sofa in large-scale floral chintz; bespoke dresser with whimsical carved details; marquetry-lined cocktail bar nook; canopied bed with fabric valance
- **Arrangement:** Cosy, conversational and slightly dense, with built-in bookcases, window seats and layered rugs making rooms feel storied and lived-in.

### Art, objects and plants
- colour-organized book walls
- faience plates and ceramic curiosities
- plaster and papier-mache animal figures
- vintage glass and murano lamps
- potted geraniums and fresh dahlias
- framed naive and folk art

### Textiles and pattern
- large-scale painterly florals
- red-and-cream grain-sack stripes
- toile and folk-print tablecloths
- leopard velvet touches
- checkerboard and diamond motifs
- colour-blocked curtains with contrast leading edge
- Moroccan-checked vintage rugs

### Lighting pieces
- Playful bespoke fixtures — pleated and scalloped fabric pendants, tole flower lights, glowing paper lanterns — that read as characters in the room.

### Staging
- scalloped shell wicker chair
- blue-backed bookshelves stuffed with colour
- faux-bois mural or fireplace
- brass-detailed bespoke joinery
- one witty surreal object per room
- yellow floor-length curtains

## Renter-friendly swaps
What the look asks for -> what to do without construction.

- painted bespoke joinery, built-in bookcases and window seats -> a freestanding bookcase painted peacock blue inside, and a freestanding upholstered bench under the window
- faux-bois mural or fireplace -> a removable faux-bois wallcovering panel or a faux-bois painted freestanding cabinet
- lattice, painted-glass or moulded ceilings -> keep the existing ceiling; add a pleated or scalloped fabric pendant
- painted panelled doors -> leave the doors; the colour goes on furniture, curtains and lamps
- marquetry-lined cocktail bar nook -> a freestanding marquetry drinks cabinet
- herringbone parquet -> keep the existing floor and layer vintage rugs
