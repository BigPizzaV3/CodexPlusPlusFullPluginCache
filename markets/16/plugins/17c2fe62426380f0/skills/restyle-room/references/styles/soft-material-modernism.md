# Soft Material Modernism

Travertine, fluted oak, glass-globe lights and rounded upholstery

**Mood:** calm, tactile, unshowy, inviting, grounded

## Essence
A sense of calm and tactile warmth derived from soft, rounded forms, layered natural textures, and a palette of muted, mineral-inspired colors, fostering a playful yet grounded atmosphere.

## Palette
- Base: travertine sand (#D9C4A3), walnut brown (#6B4630), Ceppo grey (#9A9C98), plaster cream (#EDE4D3)
- Accent: terracotta (#B5654A), mustard (#C9A227), petrol blue (#5E7F92)
- Balance: A warm, tonal ground dominates through large surfaces like paint and rugs, integrating wood tones. Upholstery introduces one or two dusty, muted colors, while a single stronger, saturated note appears sparingly in a small object or feature. The overall saturation is low to medium.

## Materials
- honed travertine
- fluted oak
- Verde Alpi green marble, polished
- brushed copper
- woven rope
- bouclé textile
- iridescent glass

## Shapes
Predominantly low-slung, rounded, and soft-edged volumes, often with a pebble-like or organic quality. These substantial forms are often contrasted with slim, often rectilinear, metal-framed elements or fine vertical lines from fluted wood surfaces.

## Texture and pattern
A rich mix of natural textures from soft, chunky wool and bouclé textiles to smooth honed stone and tactually defined fluted wood. Patterns are typically small-scale, subtly geometric, or micro-patterns on upholstery, adding visual interest without dominating.

## Light
Soft, warm, and often diffused through sheer textiles, creating an even wash or gentle pools. Lighting fixtures feature glowing opal glass globes that emit a gentle ambient light.

## Composition
Asymmetrical arrangements of elements are common, with soft volumes often grouped loosely on overlapping rugs. Visual layering is achieved through open-frame objects or screens that imply division without creating solid barriers.

## Contrast
Moderate contrast between muted, soft hues and a single saturated accent. Strong contrast exists between smooth, honed surfaces and deeply textured fabrics or fluted wood, as well as between solid rounded volumes and slim metallic lines.

## Negative space
The room feels spacious with thoughtfully placed, often low-slung, substantial elements. Surfaces typically hold a few distinct, sculptural objects rather than many small items, maintaining openness.

## Signature moves
- Low, soft-edged upholstered volumes with rounded forms.
- Freestanding objects featuring fluted wood surfaces or iridescent glass.

## Avoid
- Plain white walls lacking texture
- Sharp-cornered or boxy furniture silhouettes
- Highly saturated primary colors
- Excessive high-gloss or ornate metallic finishes
- Heavy, ornate window coverings
- Artificial or overtly industrial materials

## The full-build version
The concrete pieces and moves of the look as a designer would build it with no constraints. Use it to choose actual pieces. Anything fixed to the building goes through the renter swaps below, and furniture that belongs to another kind of room is translated to this room's purpose.

### Key pieces
- **Silhouettes:** Low, rounded and soft-edged: curved modular sofas, tub chairs with wrapped backs, bolster-armed benches and pebble-shaped tables, set against slim metal-framed stools and timber consoles.
- **Signature pieces:** quilted shell armchairs; curved modular sofa in textured grey wool; iridescent glass side table; woven high-back chair; bench with bolster ends on a timber base; organic-shaped stone-topped coffee table; rope-wrapped outdoor dining chairs
- **Arrangement:** Loose, asymmetrical clusters of seating on overlapping rugs within open plans, with screens of rods or timber fins dividing space instead of solid walls.

### Art, objects and plants
- matt ceramic vessels in terracotta and black
- palms and ferns in terracotta pots
- large tapestry-like artworks
- fragments of classical plaster sculpture
- art books on low tables
- single stems in small stone vases

### Textiles and pattern
- small-scale geometric woven rugs
- chunky looped wool carpets
- waffle-weave bedcovers
- sheer linen curtains in sand tones
- micro-patterned upholstery weaves
- shaggy rugs in teal and rust
- inlaid stone floors of half-circle motifs

### Lighting pieces
- Opal glass globes on bronze or copper stems - singly, in pairs or gathered into a ring chandelier - with concealed cove lighting washing stone walls and ceilings; daylight is filtered through sheers and timber screens.

### Staging

## Renter-friendly swaps
What the look asks for -> what to do without construction.

- walls and floors clad in honed travertine, travertine arches and stepped benches -> travertine-topped tables, a travertine plinth or side table and limewash paint in a warm travertine tone; never add arches, stone cladding or stone benches
- fluted oak panelling, walnut fins and a fluted wall behind the sofa -> a low freestanding fluted-oak sideboard or chest of drawers; the fluting stays on furniture fronts, never on a wall, and never as a tall slatted panel, screen or headboard behind the bed or sofa. The headboard is low, rounded and upholstered
- slatted walnut ceilings and concealed cove lighting -> keep the existing ceiling; a ring chandelier of opal glass globes hung from the ceiling light point already in the photo, and floor or table lamps with opal glass globes
- terrazzo, inlaid stone and geometric parquet floors -> a small-patterned geometric rug over the existing floor
- green marble bar on fluted legs and built-in banquettes -> a freestanding console or table with a green marble top and a curved freestanding sofa
- walnut stair hung in a screen of copper rods -> leave stairs untouched; a copper- or bronze-finished floor lamp or side table carries the metal
