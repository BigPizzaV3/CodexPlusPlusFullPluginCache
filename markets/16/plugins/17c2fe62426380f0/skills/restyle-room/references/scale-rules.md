# Scale rules

Most furniture is made for an average room of about 12 by 14 feet (3.7 by 4.3 m). In a small room it crowds; in a big room it shrinks. The rules for the two are opposites. Use the units the user uses.

## Large rooms: bigger, longer, fewer

The mistake is filling the room with more pieces. It ends up looking like a furniture car park and still feels empty. Use fewer, longer pieces with space around them.

Long, horizontal pieces:

- Sofas 96 in (245 cm) or longer, or deep sectionals with a chaise.
- Low, wide media consoles of 70 to 90 in (180 to 230 cm) instead of tall units.
- One oversized artwork or a horizontal row, not small frames.
- Dining tables 8 to 10 ft (2.4 to 3 m) long, with bench seating.
- A console table behind the sofa.
- Coffee tables 48 in (120 cm) or longer, or two side by side.
- Curtains across the full wall, hung high and wide.
- A rug big enough that all the furniture sits on it, 9 by 12 ft (2.7 by 3.7 m) or more.
- Linear chandeliers or pendants in a row.

Negative space is the feature:

- Leave 30 to 50 percent of the wall space bare, especially behind seating.
- Float furniture away from the walls and create clear circulation.
- One large statement piece instead of many small competing ones.
- Keep sightlines to windows and doorways open.
- Cut the decor by about 40 percent.

Common mistakes: a standard 84 in (213 cm) sofa on a 20 ft (6 m) wall; several small rugs chopping up the floor; tall bookcases everywhere; every piece pushed against a wall with a dead zone in the middle; a gallery wall of tiny frames; copying the layout of a smaller room.

By room:

- Living: float the sofa into a U-shaped conversation zone; one 48 in (120 cm) or larger artwork or a triptych above it; floor and arc lamps in the corners.
- Dining: table 36 to 42 in (90 to 107 cm) wide; sideboard 60 to 80 in (150 to 200 cm) long; 36 to 48 in (90 to 120 cm) of clearance around the table; one large centrepiece.
- Bedroom: king bed with a wide headboard; two substantial nightstands; a bench at the foot; a rug extending 24 in (60 cm) past the bed on each side; one tall and one wide low dresser.

## Small rooms: light, leggy, close to the walls

- Apartment-sized sofas and compact sectionals.
- Mount the TV; mount whatever else can be mounted. For renters, use a slim console or a floor easel instead.
- Tall, narrow storage that draws the eye up.
- Furniture on visible legs, so more floor shows.
- Round coffee tables for easier movement.
- Curtains hung high and wide to fake width.
- A tight palette and few patterns.
- A mirror opposite the light source.
- One rug under the coffee table, not a patchwork.
- Multifunctional pieces: storage ottoman, sleeper sofa, drop-leaf table.
- Leave walkways of at least 30 in (75 cm). Measure first.
- One focal point only.
