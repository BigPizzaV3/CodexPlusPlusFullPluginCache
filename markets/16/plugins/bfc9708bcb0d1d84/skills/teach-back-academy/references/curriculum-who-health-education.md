# Competency map & modular course — WHO-informed health education
**Independent instructional synthesis**, not the official WHO syllabus. Mapping to WHO primary sources uses W1–W10 IDs from `who-primary-sources.md`.

## Sequencing
- Intro 60–90 min: M1 15m → M2 15m → M3 20m → roleplay & debrief 20m; teaching minutes illustrative, not validated.
- Half-day training ~180 min: M1+M2 40m, M3 35m, M4+M5 35m, M6+M7 35m, M8 35m; flexible break schedule.
- Faculty programme (suggested): 8 x 35–45m, one module/session, formative assessments and debrief; local curriculum authorization required for actual organizational use.

## Design a health education intervention
1. Define the fictional or aggregated audience: primary languages, common concerns, access needs, possible power asymmetry; never infer individual competence.
2. Define a single specific, safe educational outcome. Clarify whether it is **knowledge, skill, intention, confidence or observed action**; do not assume one proves another.
3. Draft 1–3 key points and one concrete next action, use plain language; provide source and date and a clear channel for questions.
4. Evaluate message against W1 six principles; explicitly consider accessibility for disabled or low-literacy readers, context and language access.
5. Where one-to-one education is appropriate, rehearse AHRQ Teach-back; when hands-on practice is relevant, show-me simulation. Never compel assent.
6. Have the learner roleplay, receive feedback and revise. Track separate process and learning endpoints; no assertion of actual clinical risk reduction without proper data.

## Educational science & evaluation
Suggested Kirkpatrick layers as an **education model not WHO evidence**: reaction, learning, behavior in authorized setting, outcomes. A classroom performance score only demonstrates classroom performance. Audit inter-rater agreement and define behavior anchors before educational research. Avoid fixed pass/fail unless validated locally.
