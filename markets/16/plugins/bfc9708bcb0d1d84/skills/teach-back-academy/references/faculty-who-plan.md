# Faculty training plan: WHO-informed health communication + AHRQ Teach-back
This is an original facilitation plan, not issued by WHO.

## 90-minute workshop (example)
| Min | Component | Learner output |
|---|---|---|
|0–10| Opening + health literacy barriers (W2) | One avoidable communication barrier |
|10–25| WHO six principles (W1) | Score short fictional message without WHO branding |
|25–40| Teach-back demonstration (AHRQ Tool 5) | Rephrase one non-shaming invitation |
|40–58| Three-way roleplay | 2–3 exchanges; observe one misconception |
|58–75| Medication safety engagement or community risk message (W7 / W8) | Revised dialogue or 70-word poster |
|75–85| Debrief and one redo | Specific evidence-based feedback |
|85–90| Reflection | One new skill + one local adaptation requiring approval |

## Faculty quality assurance
- Standardized patient actor sheet (visible/hidden); two raters practice using same 0/1/2 anchors and N/O.
- Report *simulated* scores, separate observer agreement and learner satisfaction; don't equate with clinical outcomes or real-world adoption.
- When creating a public education artifact for actual dissemination, label as **draft for local public health/clinical/communications review**, not an approved WHO message.
- Compare context: individual bedside education vs institution handouts vs public health emergency RCCE. Different sources/frameworks and different ethical constraints.
- Accessible materials: consider languages and readability, plain-language test with intended audience, auditory/visual needs, mobile format; do not presume 1 method fits all.

## Suggested small evaluation design
Pre: rewrite a short fictional message; Post: rewrite parallel message + observed 3-turn simulation. Report process completion, rubric a/b, and rater variability separately; avoid claims of validated outcome improvement. Consent and privacy rules apply if later conducted as real educational research.
