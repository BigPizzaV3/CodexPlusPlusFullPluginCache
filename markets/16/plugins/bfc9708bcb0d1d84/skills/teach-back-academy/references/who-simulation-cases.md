# WHO-informed simulation bank — entirely fictional education cases
Each scenario has learner-visible brief and confidential actor notes. Do not reveal actor key until debrief or completion. Never use actual patient details or protocol-specific dosing, thresholds or clinical prescriptions. Framework mappings W1–W10 in primary sources document.

## H1: Community message clarity (M1+M2)
**Visible:** Health educator explains how community members can find verified event announcements at a fictional community center. A handout is long, undated and has unclear contact details. Deliver a 60-word message and one comprehension check.
**Hidden:** Listener believes all forwarded group messages are official; says "dạ" until asked where to verify. Outcome: identifies an official channel and a way to ask a question. Sources: W1 W2.

## H2: Parent Teach-back at discharge teaching class (M3)
**Visible:** Educational mock handout: "Read the labelled instructions carefully; if it is unreadable, ask your instructor; do not guess missing steps." The goal is technique of explaining, not home treatment.
**Hidden:** Parent assumes missing steps can be approximated; embarrassed, verbally agrees without explaining. Sources: AHRQ Tool 5, not WHO.

## H3: Health literacy and access (M1+M4)
**Visible:** In fictional outpatient educational session the parent needs a translation and an accessible pictorial schedule. Ask needs without shaming; use qualified interpreter in simulation.
**Hidden:** Listener cannot read the current tiny-font handout; fears being judged. Strong performance adapts material, never assumes literacy from appearance. Sources W2 and AHRQ Tool 4.

## H4: Medication safety five moments (M5)
**Visible:** Learning exercise explaining WHO 5 Moments for Medication Safety (starting, taking, adding, reviewing, stopping), emphasizing questions to ask professionals. No real medicines/doses.
**Hidden:** Simulated participant thinks "stopping" means anyone can stop prescribed medicine alone; educator must correct: the framework is for questions and engagement with professionals, not unilateral changes. Source W7.

## H5: Shared decisions and caregiver worries (M4+M5)
**Visible:** Simulated guardian asks why an information sheet has several options for a nonmedical educational activity. Practice listening and inviting preference before an educational decision.
**Hidden:** Worries about transport and language; may be silent unless asked. Communication only; no clinical choice or informed-consent proxy. W4 W5.

## H6: Risk communication in a public health exercise (M6)
**Visible:** Fictional public health class runs a rumor-response exercise about a made-up community drill. Draft one truthful message, acknowledge what is unknown, request feedback, identify a source to check later.
**Hidden:** Participant misreads stale social posts; wants a definitive answer. Avoid making up public health facts or claiming a local emergency exists. W1 W8 W9.

## H7: Non-stigmatizing communication (M1+M6)
**Visible:** Review a fictional poster that calls people "ignorant" for asking questions. Rewrite without judgement, with inclusive formats.
**Hidden:** Simulated participant is afraid to disclose difficulty reading. W1 W2 W3.

## H8: Bilingual family workshop (M2+M7)
**Visible:** Design a Vietnamese–English informational handout on how to attend a simulated health education workshop (date, location, contact). Audit 6 WHO principles.
**Hidden:** Family assumes written message means only adults can ask questions; ask inclusivity and where to verify. W1.

## H9: Team read-back (optional, secondary)
**Visible:** A simulation educator gives fictional equipment-check sequence to a trainee. They practise closing loop verbally. No equipment applied to real care, no real medical device operations.
**Hidden:** Trainee skips a fictional label verification; responds better to short cue and read-back. WHO patient safety education context W6; technique not necessarily WHO-origin.

## H10: Patient information card redesign (M7)
**Visible:** A fabricated educational card uses five acronyms, tiny print, no date, and no next action. Simplify; run checklist.
**Hidden:** Reader believes it is mandatory legal instruction because it is official-looking. Do not claim official approval or fabricate official channels. W1 W2.

## Escalation
Level 1: one concept/one issue; level 2: subtle misunderstanding; level 3: dual barriers; level 4: multi-audience community message + verification; OSCE must preserve hidden key. Debrief starts only on "kết thúc" or explicit stop request.
