# Video Treatment

> A shoot-ready treatment a director or friend-with-a-camera can run with.
> *Content desk · Content & marketing · about 25 minutes*

## When to use

- A video is planned and needs a concept the team can follow.
- The artist wants to pitch a director or crew.
- A tight budget needs a smart plan.

## Ask first

Read the artist profile first (`artist-profile.md`, or the profile pasted in chat) and only ask for what is missing. Ask in one short batch.

- Song, its meaning, and the feeling the video should leave.
- Budget range and available gear, crew, and locations.
- Visual references (describe or link; originality matters).
- Shoot constraints: dates, permits, cast, daylight, travel.
- Where the video will live (YouTube, short-form cut-downs, visuals for shows).

## Workflow

1. Write a one-sentence logline and the core visual idea.
2. Define tone and look: palette, lighting, camera movement, pacing.
3. Break the song into sections (intro, verse, chorus, bridge) and describe each scene with action, location, and performance moments.
4. Build the shot list per scene (shot type, lens or movement, notes).
5. Plan production: locations and permission, cast, wardrobe, props, schedule (one-day and two-day options), and crew roles.
6. Size the plan to the budget with three tiers: DIY, mid, and full production. Show what changes at each tier.
7. Add the safety, permit, and release checklist (location releases, appearance releases, music rights, insurance if needed).
8. Plan the cut-downs: vertical clips and a teaser.

## Deliverables

- Treatment document.
- Shot list.
- Production schedule and call sheet outline.
- Three-tier budget framework.
- Release and permit checklist.

Save each deliverable as a Markdown file if file tools are available; otherwise present it in the conversation.

## Quality checks (run before handing over)

- [ ] Every scene maps to a section of the song.
- [ ] The plan fits the stated budget and crew.
- [ ] Locations and cast have permission or a plan for it.
- [ ] No elements imitate another video's distinctive scenes.

## Guardrails

- Do not copy another video's signature concept or imagery.
- Cost figures are the artist's quotes or labeled estimates.
- Never invent facts, numbers, credits, quotes, placements, or contacts. If the artist has not supplied something, ask or mark it `[CONFIRM]`.
- Label every estimate as an ESTIMATE and show the formula or assumption behind it.
- Never collect, solicit, or process Restricted Data: payment card data (PCI DSS), protected health information (PHI), government identifiers (Social Security numbers, ITINs, EIN digits, passport or driver's license numbers), or access credentials and authentication secrets (passwords, API keys, MFA/OTP codes, bank logins). The artist enters sensitive information directly on official sites.
- Gather only the minimum task-specific input required; never request full conversation history, raw chat transcripts, or precise user location data (street addresses or GPS coordinates).
- Do not display subscription plans, promote paid upgrades, sell digital goods/credits, or embed checkout flows.
- Draft first. Never claim anything was filed, registered, sent, posted, uploaded, or paid. The artist takes every outside action.

## Hand-offs

- `budget-planning`: price the shoot
- `upload-music-video-to-youtube`: publish it
- `create-lyric-video`: a cheaper first video asset
