# Evidence grading

Apply this rubric to every trend. It is the only definition of the grades; quote the condition that decided a grade when it needs explaining.

## Measures

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Level | The latest share of consumers showing the behaviour (survey), or the trend's share of its parent category's revenue (market data) |
| Momentum | Change over the look-back window. For survey shares, percentage points (pp). For revenue, users or volume, CAGR, compared with the parent category's CAGR. |
| Breadth | The number of markets in scope moving the same way. Use this only when two or more markets are in scope. |
| Corroboration | The number of independent sources pointing the same way. Market Insights, Consumer Insights and each non-Statista publisher count as one source each. |
| Commercial pull | Revenue, volume or production moves the same way as the attitude or usage measure |

## Momentum tests

Classify momentum before grading. A trend may have a survey measure, a market measure, or both.

| Test | Survey share (pp change over the window) | Market data (gap = trend CAGR − category CAGR; gap = trend CAGR if no category CAGR) |
|---|---|---|
| Flat | within ±1 pp | gap within ±1 pp |
| Positive | more than +1 pp | gap more than +1 pp |
| Strong | +3 pp or more | trend CAGR ≥ 2× category CAGR when the category grows; trend CAGR above 3% when the category is flat or shrinking |

With both measures: momentum is flat only if both are flat, and it is positive or strong if either one is.

Other conditions:

- **Not reversed:** the latest yearly change is above zero, or was not measured.
- **Breadth:** markets moving the same way ÷ markets in scope. It passes automatically when only one market is in scope.
- **Commercial pull:** yes, no, or not measurable. Only "yes" passes for Established.
- **Data points:** actual years in the series, counted after any wording break.

## Grades

Work through the grades from the top and assign the first one whose conditions are all met. A trend with fewer than 2 data points, or with momentum that is flat or not positive, is Not supported whatever else it has.

| Grade | Level | Momentum | Breadth (when 2+ markets) | Corroboration | Commercial pull | Data points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Established | ≥ 25% of consumers or ≥ 10% of revenue | Positive, and not reversed in the latest year | ≥ 2/3 of markets | ≥ 2 sources | Yes | ≥ 3 years |
| Emerging | ≥ 5% of consumers or ≥ 2% of revenue | ≥ +3 pp, or CAGR ≥ 2× the category CAGR | ≥ 1/2 of markets | ≥ 2 sources | Yes, or not measurable | ≥ 2 years |
| Weak signal | Any | Positive beyond the flat band | Any | ≥ 1 source | Not required | ≥ 2 years |
| Not supported | Any | Flat (within ±1 pp, or within ±1 pp of the category CAGR) or negative, or no time series | Any | Any | Any | Any |

## Direction

Measure direction on actual years, comparing the latest yearly change with the average yearly change over the window:

| Direction | Condition |
|---|---|
| Accelerating | The latest change is more than 25% above the average |
| Slowing | The latest change is more than 25% below the average |
| Steady | The latest change is within 25% of the average |
| Reversing | The average is positive but the latest change is negative |
| Rebounding | The average is zero or negative but the latest change is positive |
| Declining | Both the average and the latest change are zero or negative |

Report the forecast direction separately when it differs from the direction on actual years. Market Insights growth-rate charts state whether growth rises or falls up to the horizon.

## Edge cases

- **Wording break.** Consumer Insights lists wording changes under "Changes in wording and/or filtering" in each fetch description. Use only the years after the latest change to an answer you rely on. If that leaves one year, there is no time series: grade it Not supported, report the level, and add it to the watch list until a second wave arrives.
- **Overlapping fieldwork.** Survey years are fieldwork windows, and these can overlap. For example, the "2026" wave may start in October 2025. Read consecutive waves as consecutive, not as exactly 12 months apart.
- **Large but flat or declining.** Grade it Not supported as a trend. Report it under Implications as a mainstream behaviour, flat or declining.
- **Proxy level.** When the trend's own consumer share is unavailable, a related diet or attitude share may stand in, e.g. flexitarians for meat substitutes. Mark it as a proxy in the radar table.
