# Critical appraisal and synthesis for Pediatric ENT

## Design-to-tool mapping (not interchangeable)
- Randomized intervention trials: **RoB 2**; distinguish assignment to intervention from adherence effects, missing outcome data and selective reporting. CONSORT is a **reporting** guideline, not a risk-of-bias assessment.
- Nonrandomized intervention studies: **ROBINS-I** where applicable; selection/confounding by indication particularly prominent in ENT surgery observational studies.
- Diagnostic test accuracy: **QUADAS-3** (published 2026); distinguish index test, target condition, reference standard, unit and estimate level, participant spectrum. If using older QUADAS-2, declare version and rationale. STARD is reporting guidance.
- Systematic reviews: use design-appropriate appraisal plus **AMSTAR 2** if assessing methodological quality of a systematic review, with caution about global critical weaknesses; PRISMA 2020 addresses reporting.
- Prognosis and observational risk-factor research: tools differ by design; choose appropriate instrument only after verifying applicability, no universal checklist.
- Certainty of a body of evidence: **GRADE**, outcome-specific, consider risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias; formally rate only with adequate data.

## Quantitative integrity
- Never confuse incidence proportion with risk ratio; OR with RR; statistically significant with clinically important.
- Report absolute risks/NNT only if baseline risk and appropriate effect are available. Check participant- vs ear-level analysis, bilateral correlation, repeated infections/episodes.
- Diagnostic accuracy requires sensitivity, specificity and ideally 2×2 tables and 95% confidence intervals; consider verification bias and prevalence/spectrum issues. Do not infer an individual's diagnosis from pooled accuracy.
- Avoid inventing forest plots, pooled effect, I², random-effects model parameters, heterogeneity P values or precision. Missing figures remain "không có số liệu".
- For heterogeneous populations, outcomes or follow-up, prefer transparent narrative synthesis; note why meta-analysis may not be justified.

## Workflow for a systematic review
Protocol and eligibility; comprehensive documented search; duplicate removal; independent screening; extraction and verification; study-level and outcome-level bias assessment; effect estimates and appropriate synthesis; certainty and limitations; reporting using correct PRISMA extension. For a **scoping review**, PCC and PRISMA-ScR are often appropriate; evidence mapping differs from effect-estimating systematic reviews.

## Methodological references directly opened 2026-10-28
- PRISMA 2020 statement: https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020-statement ; 27-item checklist and flow diagrams, reporting standard.
- PRISMA-ScR extension (2018): https://www.prisma-statement.org/scoping ; 20 essential and 2 optional items.
- Cochrane Handbook v6.5 (2024 update): https://www.cochrane.org/handbook ; methods for intervention systematic reviews.
- QUADAS-3 tool information: https://www.bristol.ac.uk/population-health-sciences/projects/quadas/quadas-3/quadas-3-tool/ ; 2026 revision of diagnostic accuracy RoB applicability.

Reporting tool use is not proof that each item was assessed; inspect the actual study methods and results.
