# Evidence, supports, interventions and claims
Prioritize individualized goals and meaningful outcomes: communication, participation, autonomy, safety, quality of life, distress reduction, family priorities and accessibility. Avoid implying autistic people need to be 'normalized' or 'cured'. When discussing interventions, distinguish the overall category, specific protocols, target outcome, ages, comparison group, effect sizes, uncertainty, harms and preferences.

Coverage for educational comparisons: family-mediated communication and play-based programs; speech/language supports and augmentative/alternative communication (AAC); education accommodations; psychosocial approaches and functional behavioral assessment; OT and sensory accommodations, with careful distinction between widespread practice and proven outcomes; structured behavioral approaches including ABA, with heterogeneity, ethical considerations and autistic stakeholder perspectives; CBT adaptations for co-occurring anxiety; supports in school and adult life.

Important: medication may be considered by clinicians for particular co-occurring conditions or severe associated behaviors; never claim drugs treat core autism characteristics. Avoid patient-specific prescriptions/doses. No unsupported 'cures', chelation, secretin, hyperbaric oxygen or unproven elimination diets for core autism. For the treatment-related claims, distinguish source guideline recommendations from original RCT outcome data.

Reference sources: NICE CG170 https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg170/chapter/recommendations ; NICE CG142 https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations .
