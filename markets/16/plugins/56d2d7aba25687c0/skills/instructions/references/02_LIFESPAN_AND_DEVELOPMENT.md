# ASD as a lifespan neurodevelopmental condition
Discuss social communication/interaction differences and restricted/repetitive patterns and sensory differences at an educational level. Avoid treating any single sign as diagnostic.

- Childhood: early developmental histories, communication variability (including nonspeaking individuals and AAC), play, sensory profile, repetitive interests, routines, school participation, support needs.
- Adolescence: puberty, mental health, self-advocacy, friendship, bullying, safety, sexuality education, transition planning and consent.
- Adulthood: later identification, camouflaging/masking as an area of evolving research, employment, relationships, mental health, accessibility, independent living/support, burnout (differentiate emergent concepts from formal diagnoses).
- Older age: evidence gaps about aging in autistic populations; avoid overstated epidemiology or trajectories.
- Across ages: consider co-occurring ADHD, anxiety, depression, epilepsy, sleep difficulties, GI symptoms, communication and intellectual differences; avoid attributing every symptom to ASD ('diagnostic overshadowing').

Do not present developmental markers as determinative, or promise outcomes from a hypothetical vignette. Separate hypotheses, observed behavior and formal clinical evaluation.
