# Price preferences

Keep the wording friendly and concrete. Display all four price choices in the same response as the initial style photos. They are independently selectable from the visual style, not a later step or a second set of numbered styles. Acknowledge an existing price preference without asking for it again:

| Choice | User-facing explanation | How to source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accessible | Keep the spend down. | Prioritize lower-priced options that preserve the chosen silhouette, color, and styling idea. Check current prices rather than relying on brand stereotypes. |
| Premium | Contemporary brands and considered upgrades. | Explore contemporary labels and selected higher-priced pieces within the cap. Explain useful design or construction differences only when verified. |
| Luxury | Designer collections and statement pieces. | Explore designer products within the cap. A higher price is not a reason to exceed the user's limit or invent quality claims. |
| Mix it up | Accessible staples, selected splurges. | Keep supporting basics accessible and allocate more to one or two focal pieces the user cares about. Ask which category deserves the splurge only when it changes the result. |

## Turn a preference into a useful budget

After the visual choices, confirm any missing shopping country and currency. A maximum for the complete outfit, including new shoes and accessories, is optional. Example: "Any total you'd like me to stay under? You can also stick with your price tier." If omitted or declined, proceed with the tier and show actual totals. Never require a numeric cap before showing the style photos or price choices.

Treat a stated ceiling as a ceiling. Record whether it applies to one item, one outfit, one capsule, or the entire wardrobe. If "under 300" has no currency or scope, clarify those details before recommending a priced basket. If the user gives an explicit cap and enough direction, do not re-ask the tier question. Still show all four choices alongside the initial gallery and acknowledge the supplied cap.

Do not attach fixed dollar bands to Accessible, Premium, or Luxury. Prices vary by country, currency, garment category, and current stock. The numeric cap controls; the label only guides the mix of products. Use one currency for an outfit or wardrobe total. Do not silently convert or add different currencies together.

If tax and shipping cannot be verified, show the merchandise subtotal and state those exclusions. If the user gives an all-in maximum, retain a buffer or explain that all-in compliance is unverified; do not call a merchandise-only subtotal a guaranteed final total.

If a requested designer item cannot fit the cap, preserve the cap and explain the tradeoff. Offer similar styling, verified sale stock, or pre-owned options if the user is open to them. Label alternatives accurately. Do not invent discounts, counterfeit products, or exact matches.

## Apply the preference across capsules

Use the same preference until the user changes it. Support exceptions such as "Accessible clothes, luxury shoes" or "Keep work premium and weekends accessible."

Before building 3 to 14 capsules, establish the total budget for all new purchases. Reuse items and count them only once in the combined shopping list. Do not multiply the outfit cap by the number of looks. Show standalone capsule subtotals separately from the combined total, because shared products overlap.

When the user's desired scope does not fit the budget, first use owned pieces, share staples across capsules, and find lower-priced alternatives. If the scope still cannot fit, show the actual gap and let the user decide which constraint to change. Never silently raise the cap, reduce the capsule count, or call an incomplete wardrobe finished.

## Price changes

When a user changes price preference or lowers a cap, re-source affected products, recheck prices, update the shopping list and total, and regenerate any images whose garments change. Keep their chosen style direction and identity reference. Do not leave old luxury garments in an image while linking unrelated lower-priced substitutes.
