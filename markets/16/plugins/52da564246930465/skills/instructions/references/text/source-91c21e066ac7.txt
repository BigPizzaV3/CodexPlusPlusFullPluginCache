THE ESSENCE OF LEADERSHIP

First and foremost, you must develop into a true leader. With skills. The title of self-
proclaimed galactic commander or CEO or managing direction isn't what makes you a
leader. Leadership can differ from person to person, but at Catalyst88, we define it very
clearly. True leadership consists on three elements:



VISION:
Your vision for the future of the company and the steps you'll take to make it a reality. You
must have a crystal clear vision that you can clearly articulate with energy and a strong
feeling of dedication. Everyone in your company must be aware of it and believe in it. It
must become a shared vision. They have to see how it applies to their own role and future
at the company and why the things that they do matter.

It will go nowhere if you don't set the course and steer the ship. You won't get there
unless you have a clear vision for the future of your company and an equally clear vision
for the path you'll follow to get there.



ACTION:
What you do and how you do it are both actions. As a role model, the example you set
shows everybody how things are done.   You won't get there  until you  level up
your systemization  , increase your individual competence, and drive organizational
advancements while creating a clear picture of the route the company will take to get
there.

The way you do things and the passion you put into anything you do is what you build

problems as individuals as if they are our own personal problem to solve, and to do so
quickly!

IT’S PERSONAL
The first thing to understand about the process of finding your why is that each person’s
individual why is different. It is in fact intensely personal. You are the focus and nobody
else can judge if your answer is right or wrong or good or bad. Your own personal WHY is
a reflection of what feels right for you and indeed what resonates with your own gut
instincts about the things that you really want in your life.

Many entrepreneurs are not necessarily a slave to their own emotions and in fact they
often repeat the phrase it’s not personal it’s just business. THAT IS WRONG!

It is absolutely personal. The purpose of your business is to serve your life. Therefore if
you set yourself on a journey that is all about making sacrifices for the end result you are
likely to hate most if not all of the the journey.

--- PAGE 16 ---
Entrepreneurs  often  think  their  decisions must  first  serve  others  before  serving
themselves. That kind of thinking can often be perceived as noble and that perception
reinforces the idea of continuing a long term cycle of sacrifice.

For this process of finding your WHY you must challenge yourself to think of you first. The
reality is that once you understand what motivates you, what inspires you, what gives you
the most happiness and joy you then can return that same positive energy back to your
staff, your family, your customers and the world in general ten fold.

The cycle of sacrifice keeps chipping parts of ourselves away until decades later people
often look back and they regret many of the choices they made.

By making an affirmative decision to find your WHY you are deciding that you will find
your path to freedom and that path will carry those around you happily along for the
journey.

DON’T SHOW ME THE MONEY
The concept of financial freedom may be a component of your personal WHY, but it is not
the answer. Things like possessions, power, influence, status symbols and having money
are not the core components of your personal WHY. It is far more intangible and deeper
than those superficial success indicators.

Some entrepreneurs who read about the motivations to find your personal WHY make
mistake this process as some kind of hippy dippy routine to find your zen and shake your
chakras, but this has nothing to do with anything like that.

This is about a practical system to help guide your decisions toward the right type of
thinking so that you can find the answer for yourself.

will find that their own  initial instincts will reflect their inner voice to ask rhetorical
questions back like, “Who cares, what I want? I have to make payroll.” or “What I want isn’t
important right now, I need to get sales to grow.” or “I can worry about that later when I

YOU ARE THE MOST RELEVANT PART OF THIS PROCESS
Have you ever found yourself getting ready for bed and laying down just before your eyes
close and you find yourself feeling exhausted, yet fully satisfied with your day? It’s the
kind of feel you get after having an incredible day. You may even say to yourself, “If I died
right now I would die happy.” This is a dream day for sure even if it happened by accident.

This kind of feeling is what is missing from so many entrepreneur’s lives. That deep sense
of fulfillment comes from living your life and running your business the way that serves
your life in the most complete way possible.

All of us have core values that drive our behaviors and we have beliefs and principles that
help guide us day to day. The actions and behaviors we demonstrate day to day are what
make us who we are.

The most important thing to remember is that your own personal desires and dreams are
the things that will unlock your ability to have dream days more and more predictably. By
establishing your WHY you can start proactively living through intentional actions and
behaviors vs. randomly moving through each day.

YOUR PERSONAL VISION
Every business needs a vision to drive forward the “big picture” objectives of the
company even if the company is just starting out and that vision seems audacious or
even impossible in the present.

Just like a company you need to establish your vision of what you want and synthesize
that down to your personal WHY. If you want to create a personal legacy that is worth
remembering and establish a business that is sustainable beyond your personal day to
day management you must find those things that give you the greatest sense of direction
and purpose. These factors will lead to to your most effective productivity and give you a
level of stamina you never imagined possible. In fact people don’t realize that just like the
so called “runners high” there is a similar feeling that entrepreneurs get when they are “in
the zone.” This sense of momentum is unmistakable and incredibly powerful. AND best of
all it is a common side effect of knowing your personal WHY.

WHY ISN’T WHY SIMPLE?
Despite the seeming  simplicity  of  defining what our primary purpose  is most
entrepreneurs find themselves intractable stuck grinding through their day to day lives
and find  it not only difficult to suss out their WHY, but often they don’t believe that
knowing the answer will have an impact to their lives and to their businesses.

To further complicate matters the journey of self discovery can come across like some
kind of new age mumbo jumbo which is often a turn off to many entrepreneurs who are
obsessed with numbers, metrics and other forms of measurement. The instinct is often to
start thinking about your personal WHY and then dismiss this as a philosophical exercise
that has no tangible benefit nor any way to measure its effectiveness. These instincts are
part of our own self preservation systems that help us keep moving when we get mired
down  in something hard. So instead of procrastinating the discovery process an
entrepreneur  will rationalize that because of so many factors listed about that this
process is not important.

THE TRUTH WILL SET YOU FREE
Despite the very natural self preservation instincts that are hard wired into each and every
human the simple fact is that when you clearly understand your purpose only then, can
you act decisively to move towards the objective.

When you have your WHY you can use that as your benchmark for every single decision in
your life.

For example if you decide that part of your personal WHY is that you want to explore and
travel the world while working from your laptop and your best friend offers you an
amazing opportunity go to work at IBM or some other major company with them in a
cubicle for big money for the next 5 years you can easily see that is not compatible with
your WHY.

Another example: You have the chance to buy another business at a great deal. This
business is outside of your current industry. Your personal WHY has emphasized that you
want to be a domain expert in the Fitness Space. The business opportunity is to buy a
small chain of coffee kiosks. Unless there is some unknown connection between coffee
and fitness it seems likely that the answer to that opportunity is no thank you.

PRACTICALITY MEETS PHILOSOPHY
Although we have to challenge ourselves mentally to think through the influences in our
lives that are the most positive and the most negative; which is admittedly a largely
philosophical task. But there is no question that the answer has a specific and recurring
practical application for your daily life.

You must release the idea that this is some fad found in the latest self help book or some
gimmick about as enduring as the Pet Rock. This process will help you surface something
that is a core part of who you really are. It will help you articulate what is truly important
to you better than you imagined possible. When you continue on your journey of life you
will have a roadmap. You will know what you want. This can in fact change everything. For
the better.

RED HERRINGS ABOUND
Without a doubt the modern culture of accumulation pushes us in directions that lead to
a conventional definition of success. Having a really nice car must mean you are happy,
right? If you have a big boat then you are fulfilled, correct? If you have power, influence,

--- PAGE 22 ---
celebrity, possessions and other traditional wealth indicators then by the transitive
principle you must be happy. WRONG! All of these are superficial and not things that
ultimately lead people to a deep sense of personal joy and fulfillment. In fact the
accumulation of theses red herrings can often lead to a sense of emptiness that creates
deep depression in many entrepreneurs. That sense of hollowness is compounded by the
depression which is then highlighted with guilt because society says we should be happy
because we have those things.

There are countless examples entrepreneurs who have garnered every symbol of wealth
and traditional success and they still feel empty. Don’t let these false indicators drive your
WHY. The truth is that some of the positive side effects of finding your personal WHY will
often lead to accomplishing your financial goals. Having a beautiful estate, or a rare
painting or some other physical piece of property is fine and even enjoyable. Those
things, however, will not drive your self of personal accomplishment and satisfaction for
the long term.

IT IS POSSIBLE
By now you should be on board with the idea that the WHY is important. So the best news
is that you can follow a process to determine your WHY and to define it for yourself in the
most clearly articulated way imaginable. This process is, however, undeniably personal on
every level. There are no short cuts. Different people react in different ways to the
process. There is not right or wrong and there is no clear timeline. You must find your
own path and experience the journey for yourself to get the result. In other words don’t
try to delegate this to someone else. Nobody is qualified to do this for you. Once you
push yourself through the process rest assured the result will be a level of clarity and
certainty about your purpose that will help you accelerate your business and simply your
life overall. At the end of the process you will find your own unique life vision statement.

So often entrepreneurs are so great at being responsible and having a strong moral
compass that we subordinate our own desires, thoughts and needs to those around us.
We want to help and support those around us. It is a part of who we are. But over time we
often start equating sacrifice with progress and we chip away pieces of ourselves over
time without any real benefit.

--- PAGE 23 ---
During this process it is vital to give yourself permission to think of you first. Don’t think
about others for this specific purpose. Dream about what you really want and disregard
what other might think about it.

The objective is to break the day to day grind and reach for the impossible dream that
you want deep down. Don’t let fear or the judgement of others stand in the way of this
process. Stop projecting negative reactions of others  into the process and  start
imagining you being the very best person you can be. When you achieve this level of
personal fulfillment you will be in a position to support and help 10x more than you could
before. This is not a zero sum game. It is about expanding your capacity to achieve in
every aspect of your life.

Our instincts as entrepreneurs will most often forbid us from being self indulgent. We
rarely take time to celebrate and instead we challenge ourselves and those around us to
do better next time. For this process let go of the opinions of others. Release the image of
success placed by society. Embrace your own gut feel for what you want.

Equally important you must eliminate what you don’t want in your life. This too can be
challenging for an entrepreneur. We often decide that the burden of being a business
owner means that we have to just “suck it up” and press onwards. While that may be a
practical reality early on, it is vital that you allow yourself to determine what you do and
don’t like. What you are good at and what you aren’t. The by-product of your strengths are
positive things like fulfillment, happiness, joy, momentum and the like. The by-product of
your weaknesses are negative things like resentment, dissatisfaction,  anger, frustration
and more.

So you must fight your instinct that inherently says you have to do stuff you don’t like
because that’s life. You can in fact eliminate, even if it is over time, the stuff you don't like
to do.

For example  if you hate to make employee schedules but you find yourself slogging
through it because there is no alternative you can allow yourself to start thinking of ways
to eliminate that task from your role as a mangers.

--- PAGE 24 ---
A typical example for entrepreneurs is that they are great at vision and amazing at
ideation, but sometimes the details of documentation and follow through are annoying.
The net effect of that is that the execution is often poor, inconsistent and totally random.
The reality is that by creating a solution to the gap in execution an entrepreneur can be
empowered beyond  belief. And in a mind-blowing twist to entrepreneurs with this
mentality there are in fact people who thrive on details. Hard to believe, right? Yes there
are people and resources that will find their own incredible sense of fulfillment and joy
from things you hate to do.

The point is that in the modern world we can find resources and people to help in various
ways and that over time we can free ourselves from things we consider mundane and
unrewarding. Sometimes  this concept  is one that  is  difficult  for entrepreneurs to
understand. We often project our own mindset onto those around us. After all we love the
idea of creating a company and building a business so it’s logical that everyone loves that
right? Wrong. There are so many things that motivate people that are dramatically
different from person to person. This creates opportunity.

For this process to work you must release everything and focus on the things that you
want.

THE WORDS DON’T MATTER
Ok the words matter, but they really don’t matter if the final output from this process
doesn’t connect with you on a physical and emotion level. When you finish the process of
finding your why you should read your statement and know in your gut immediately that
this is you. It should give you a positive physiological indicator. You literally should FEEL
that  this  is  right. You should have an unprecedented sense of genuineness and
authenticity that emanates the simple act of reading your why aloud.

If you put down a statement that is anything less than that you are not there yet. A
common outcome from this sort of process is for people to subconsciously start to write
down phrases or statements that they think will sound good or that their family, friends or
the world in general would approve of. This is a natural reaction, but it is not helpful and
should be avoided.

--- PAGE 25 ---
So instead of focusing on what sounds good or what you think others might approve of
you must instead transcend above the words and think about the feelings and emotions
that your own personal WHY should evoke.

YOU ALREADY KNOW THE ANSWER
Here’s the dichotomy with this whole process: you actually already know the answer, but
you almost certainly can not yet articulate  it. Your WHY is a part of you. It is entirely
personal and it already makes you feel happy or sad based on the input around you day to
day. Therefore this is less about inventing a new idea and more about the process of
thought you must go through to identify your WHY. Think of your mind as a steam that
you are panning for gold in. In this case the gold are the emotional drivers of your
happiness. In order to maximize the results of this process you must trust that you will
have to patience to work through the entirety of it and forbid yourself for giving up. It is
often confusing and occasionally maddening during this process of discovery. Be patient.

WHEN DO YOU NEED A SYSTEM?

A vital concept when you start building systems is knowing when a function, problem, or
process deserves having a system build in the first place. Here are 3 key questions to ask
yourself before green lighting a new system:

HOW IMPORTANT IS THE RESULT?
Is it worthwhile to put forth the work to develop, implement, and manage a system to
achieve the desired outcome? Typically, anything that has a significant influence on the
consumer warrants the development of a system to achieve that impact.

IS THIS A REPEATING ACTIVITY?
If the outcome is a one-time or exceedingly rare occurrence, you may not require a
system. You'll probably be able to handle it once it arises. Even one-time events, if they
are significant enough, should have well-designed processes in place to achieve the
desired result. A new product launch, for example, is a major event that necessitates
meticulous planning and attention to detail. And, even if power outages are rare in your
area, procedures for dealing with them are well worth developing.

IS THERE A NEED FOR CONSISTENCY AND RELIABILITY?
While systemization can help almost every aspect of your company's operations, some
activities are simply not worth the effort, especially  if they have little impact on your
consumers or make no difference to your internal management process. Your focus on
building systems should be about creating the same predictable result regardless of who
is accountable for the process. Having inventory too late is widely recognized as not
awesomer, however, having inventory too early is equally problematic for other reasons
like cash flow, warehouse space, inventory turn ratios, etc… The point is that having
consistency and predictability is a better objective than simply thinking that everything
needs to be faster. Build your systems and concepts around the ideal of hitting the target
in the middle and minimizing the shots outside of the center. Systems can deliver that in
a predictable way when they are done right.

The Key Frustrations Process directs your attention away from placing blame on others.

KEY FRUSTRATIONS PROCESS
Like any new process this concept may seem unfamiliar or otherwise awkward. However,
this is one of the most powerful ways to quash problems again and again through the
organization by following a series of steps which ask questions until the systemic answer
becomes clear.

STEP 1 - DEFINE A SINGLE FRUSTRATION

Although you may be experiencing multiple frustrations at the same time, you can only
work on one at a time effectively, so choose one.

Write it down as a brief statement in straightforward language, as if you were speaking to
someone about it.

This  is a more crucial stage than  it appears at  first glance. By expressing yourself
incorrectly, you may find yourself heading in the wrong path, and possibly finding a
solution that does not alleviate your core issue at all.

--- PAGE 88 ---
For example, it's typical to express frustration in the form of a proposed remedy, such as
"I require an inventory system.”

That is not the source of frustration. This immediate diagnosis would force the creation
or modification of an inventory system, but that may not be the actual root cause.

This particular problem could be better stated as follows: “We keep running our of
inventory on key items.“

This 30,000 foot view eliminates the chance that you may go down the wrong path
needlessly but opening up many root causes that could lead to running out of stock.

What if you already have an inventory system and you’re still running out of stock?

The point is to make this process work start with a generic top level view of the issue and
work backwards.

STEP 2 - REDEFINE AS A SYSTEM FAILURE OR GAP

A systemic focus towards a frustration is the need for a system to eliminate a specific
problem in your business.  It makes no mention of persons or external causes as the
source of the issue.

Step 2 is completed if your frustration statement is already System-Directed.

If your frustration statement blames you for the problem, you have an INTERNAL
frustration. When you articulate a problem as something that you can not or will not do
this means that the obstacle is you.

To convert an INTERNAL frustration, you must first remove yourself from the equation.

You accomplish this by refocusing your attention on what isn't working rather than
your role in it.

EXTERNAL problems occur when you blame someone or something outside of yourself
for your frustrations.

They often express a desire to modify something over which you have no control.

Politics, time, the economy, and the weather are all unchangeable.

You can only change what you have control over, which is your business.

You concentrate on systems rather than people, and on what you can control rather than
what you can't. You reframe your frustrations so that they lead you to system solutions.

The question is not, "How can I get Quincy to quit his needless water cooler talk with my
other employees?" rather, "What is missing in my business structure that allows Jack to
participate in superfluous chatter with my other employees?"

when the Key Frustrations Process gets to the meat of the matter.
