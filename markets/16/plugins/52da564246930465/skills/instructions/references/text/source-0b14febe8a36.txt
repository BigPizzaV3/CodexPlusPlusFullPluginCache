--- PAGE 1 ---
Hey everybody - thanks for being here and thanks to Amber and the team for
putting on such a great event.


Please allow me to extend a special thanks to each sponsor for helping create
memorable experiences, learning, and lasting ROI for each one of us. Be sure to
take a moment and thank each sponsor personally as well, please.

I want to remind each of you that when we are in a place that is so different from
our typical routine and doing things that break the paradigm of normal we
expand our minds.

You see all of the efforts you make to come to faraway places like this gives us
the chance for a very high sensory experience - causing our brains to fire
synapses in a different order compared to our regular day-to-day lives.

Those new pathways being built in your brain are like lighting up your own brand
new personal electrical grid in parts of your brain you haven’t yet started using -
and those new connections can become your key to seeing patterns you have
not seen before, having novel ideas or breakthroughs, and generally just
unleashing a level of creativity that you can not get in a standard day at the
office.

I think we call all agree this ain’t no zoom call.


You may have noticed that last week I announced my retirement 2.0 - so, if you’ll
indulge me, today is going to be less process presentation and more fireside
chat with your ‘ol buddy Steve.


These three stories are simple but they illustrate lessons that have proven
valuable to me.

So firstly what’s Retirement 2.0?

Not the same as retirement.

I know that I can never stop using my brain to solve complex problems AND still
achieve happiness and fulfillment.

How do I know this?

I refer you to Retirement 1.0

⁃   2012 sucked - full disengagement,
⁃    Lots of vacations, too many, and guilty that I’m not happy.
⁃   So I’m now in the process of working out the bugs for 2.0
⁃        I’ll only work 35 hours, not 70
⁃        I’ll only focus on Network effect business, not product businesses -
⁃     Let’s be honest - I’ve moved mountains of products - the thrill of selling
     another container or even selling 100 containers is gone.

Ok let’s get into my three stories

Chapter One -


I had a Paper route in Germany when I was 14 - this is around 1984. George
Orwell had his vision of what 1984 would look like, but my own vision was
simpler and focus on me having some WAM. For the uninitiated WAM means
walking around money. And as a snot-nosed teenager I had some WAM.

My routine at that time was simple:

3:55 am wake-up call;
4:00 am grab the papers from delivery guy
And deliver papers by 6 am then leave for school at 6:15 am

This went on 365 days a year - like the US postal service come rain, come sleet,
or come snow I’m still delivering.

For your reference, The Stars and Stripes newspaper was a lofty four dollars and
fifty cents per month and of that, I get $1.00.

And for that dollar, On top of daily delivers I have to go to each home one or
more times to collect the money from them personally.

The upside of the collection was sometimes they would give me 5 dollars so I
could keep the extra 50 cents giving me a 50% increase on my AOV for that
customer for that month.

--- PAGE 3 ---
I had around 200 apartment homes to deliver papers in 120 minutes.

Each Home = 12 bucks per year - let's call that the lifetime value of the
customer.

But let’s break that down a bit further

That’s 3.3 cents per day I get paid for early am home delivery

- btw good luck finding a 14-year-old to do anything for 3 cents - even inflation
adjusted a 14-year-old won’t even make eye contact with you for a nickel.

Safety Tip: asking 14-year-olds to make eye contact with you for a nickel is not
ok.

Now, when you’re a paperboy at 4 am you wait for the delivery truck to
anyone…. anyone…Bueller…anyone….

YES, THE DELIVERY TRUCK DELIVERS THE PAPERS - thanks for playing
along.

Sometimes Jack Frost would make it Snow - and since school always takes
priority if the school bus shows up before the delivery truck then I go to school.

In those cases, after school, I then deliver the paper

The good news for a young paperboy is that during the Christmas season,
collection tops were better than average

- instead of occasional 50-cent tips here and there I would see tips of 5-10
dollars with more regularity.

For the numbers people in the audience: This had a material impact on my
customer LTV.


During one Christmas season while I was out doing collections this one lady was
mad as hell about the paper being delivered late in the day a few times that
month.


She yelled and I took it and I apologized and explained politely the situation,

--- PAGE 4 ---
She yelled more and I took it and I apologized further.


She yelled more and I started doing math in my head….

Is this abuse worth me coming here to collect?

Is her yelling at me for something I can not control worth it to me - for $1.00 a
month ?!


Remember that’s a high roller 3.3 cents a day with zero extra pay unless they tip
to show up and collect money only to be yelled at!?!

Then I went into the big picture - I deliver the Stars and Stripes - which happens
to be the ONLY English newspaper in Germany at that time. There was no
internet.

And then it hit me - If she doesn’t want to pay me I will simply stop coming to
this house every day and she can keep her four fifty.

So that’s said. I told her every bit of my lack of interest in having my ass handed
to me for 1 dollar per month. If she wanted an English newspaper she gets it
from me or not at all. I had no concern for my own volume control or the
potential for a one-star review. I let her have it.

Suddenly - Out of the shadows, Like the Batman, her husband leaped to the
door subtly sweeping back ‘ol yeller with one arm while somehow wrapping his
other arm around my shoulder and saying something like,
“Hey kid there’s no problem here. I want you to take this twenty-dollar bill &
have a very Merry Christmas. And we totally understand snow delays and you
won’t hear another peep out of us.”

I left that doorway realizing that I was in control of this valuable commodity and I
was doing them a favor by delivering it.

The lesson is to know what you have and its place in the market. And to Know
your own value. And always make sure the juice is worth the squeeze.

--- PAGE 5 ---
Just as important, although I did not recognize this at that time the framework to
think more strategically vs transactionally was taking shape. I urge you not to
forget that lesson for yourself.



Chapter 2 -


In 1988 I started at Idaho State University. I dropped out of college after one
semester and went into a partnership to buy a janitorial company while I was still
18.

After dropping out I was also earning sweat equity in a carpet cleaning &
restoration company with the same partner and we later opened a flooring store
within that same entity. So I was working hard.

Around 1992 a sales rep notified me of a flooring store for sale in a distressed
situation. He knew that although I was young, only 22 at that time, I was
someone who was capable and he wanted to make the connection to the
current owner.

Tragically, the owner had been diagnosed with stage 4 melanoma cancer from a
little spot on his back that he never saw. At the time of our discussions, the
founder of the store called Midlakes was Gerald Stauffer and he was already in a
hospice center.

At his request, we never met in person, but in our many phone conversations he
told me he had one simple desire - he didn’t want his personal guarantees or
Company debts to be a burden on his family.

I quickly came to understand his financial situation and identified the biggest
issues and went to work.

And once I saw I could make a go of it - I told him I would take care of
everything and he didn’t have to worry anymore.


I purchased the company for $1.00 + assumed debt of around 150k for a
company doing around 1 Million dollars of annual turnover and with “assets” of
paper value of around 150K. But they weren’t liquid - things like tenant

--- PAGE 6 ---
improvements, store racking, lighting, some flooring inventory and signage etc…
They had value to an ongoing operation, but not in liquidation. So in my mind, I
was getting this operation for free since the business would pay the debts from
cash flow. This was my first leveraged buyout offer.

I had high immediate success getting suppliers to release personal guarantees
and refinance each individual debt into a new note over 12-24 months with no
interest.

The suppliers understood the situation: Given the choice of fighting in some
estate bankruptcy for scraps or backing the play of this up-and-coming kid in
the industry they choose to make their bets on me.

However, One particular supplier, the biggest debt holder bar far with the
majority of the debt, was a holdout and being jerky. Apparently, they didn’t see
the charm in me that every else did.

So I got creative and went to their biggest competitor, who was also the guy
who turned me into the deal in the first place and I said if you pay off the debt to
that competitor I will make all my future purchases through them until the note is
paid off.

It was a win-win For them because not only did they get a key account from a
competitor they also earned my undying loyalty. -

Years later that same company would be my key partner to enter the container-
purchasing business.

The store grew and prospered over the next 5 or so years.

However, in late 1998, I was burning out. I was working often 7 days a week and
even on holidays to try to keep my little world turning.

We were doing ok - a top retailer in the area and a top 25 store nationally in a
specific franchise group. We were “successful” by most measures from the
outside.

And I remember hating waking up every day.

The pain of running a retail store and dealing with dozens of contractors who
install hardwood flooring, carpet, tile, and so forth in people’s houses was full-
time stress. It was endless firefighting.

--- PAGE 7 ---
Customers often wouldn’t pay on time and then have the audacity to go on
vacation with MY MONEY -

While at the same exact time I struggled to make payroll.

Sometimes we didn’t make it and occasionally payroll check or two would
bounce - all while those idiots were in Hawaii with my money. It was terrible.


I have always loved technology and in 1996 I started my first website
midlakes.com which was just brochure ware to support my retail store.

If you want to have a good laugh - Check the way-back machine for 1996 or
1997 captures of midlakes to see what a pile of shit it really was.

I was really desperate to figure out what I could sell online and I didn’t know
what product I could sell.

After tripping over the flooring samples for the millionth time I finally decided in
late 1998 to simply try to sell the stuff I had in my retail store since I couldn’t
think of something more creative and since books and DVDs were locked up
tight by Amazon even in the mid-nineties.

I decided to build a yahoo store and I listed some area rugs and doorway mats
initially.

And in October of 1998 once my first sale from Bulgaria came in and I was
hooked.

It was ordered by the customer online with their credit card on a secure
shopping cart and then the Yahoo store faxed the order to me.

And we took that little curled-up piece of paper and we celebrated.

Side story :
Nobody had the inventory for the rug the customer purchased.

We apologized by email and promised To send it asap and prayed they wouldn’t
cancel the order.

The rug showed up 3 weeks later and we rushed to the post office to send it out
while simultaneously paying MORE for the postage than we got in revenue and
we were thrilled.

--- PAGE 8 ---
The very next day we got a chargeback notice in the mail. It was a fraudulent
sale - but I didn’t care. A rational person would have quit immediately.

in Feb of 1999, the movie October sky came
Out  I remember feeling all the feelings of the protagonist.

You see the story of Homer Hickam was about a kid who was expected to just
work in the coal mine and grin and bear it because that’s life in West Virginia.

But the kid wanted to shoot rockets into space. The story of Homer Hickam is
worth a watch even today and it really resonated With me.

My version of rockets was selling stuff online and this retail store was my coal
mine.

After our online store started getting more and more faxes (some of which were
real orders and not fraud to Bulgaria)

I knew we were in the rocket business and by early 1999 I hired my first
developers to start building parsimony.

There are countless lessons in this story, but the first highlighted lesson is to
avoid personal guarantees like the plague.

And don’t forget that DEAL values and structures hold infinite possibilities. Don’t
let your own bias or someone else saying that’s impossible stand in the way.

And finally, the big lesson is to find your rocket ship and stop walking hunched
over in the proverbial coal mine. The visual image of those miners in the movie
with coal-stained faces who are literally not able to stand upright was the
perfect metaphor that illustrated how I felt working my retail operation.

Don’t work in a coal mine if you can build rockets.

Chapter 3 -


In late 2018 my regular doctor and I were arguing about cholesterol during my
annual physical. My doctor knows that I think he’s an idiot. He also thinks I am
an idiot.

We’re quite a pair!

Let it be known that I’m not the one that calls his business a “Medical Practice”.
If he just practicing on me then I’m not afraid to stand up for myself.

Anyway, After a heated discussion, We agreed that I would get a heart scan and
if it was clear he would stop trying to sell me Drugs as part of his upsell funnel.

So I did the heart scan and he said everything looked good.

But he mentioned in the background they did see some little spots in my lungs.

He suggested I go to a lung doctor, which I came to find out is a pulmonary
specialist.

This time I complied without any pushback.

But because he said it was probably nothing, I waited a few months.

The lung doctor reviewed some new scans and without being prompted; asked
if I have ever been to Arizona.

I said yes. She said have you ever been sick there?

I said sure - I’ve had a cold or flu when there a time or two.

She said maybe this is a remnant scar from having a certain cold or flu which
has this type of specific lung-scaring pattern and is unique to Arizona

The doctor went on to say that since taking a biopsy in the lungs is not
awesomer let’s just keep an eye on this and scan again in a year.

In Summary, she said It’s probably Nothing.

--- PAGE 10 ---
So I carried on. But After a few more months went by I noticed a lump in my
abdomen.

I called my doctor’s office and the regular guy I argue with was on holiday they
said to have one of his colleagues take a look.

A new doctor poked and prodded me a bit and said it was probably nothing but
I should get an ultrasound.

I asked if it was urgent she said no. So I did that after a few more months.

So it’s mid-2019 maybe august and I go in for an ultrasound

and they said - there’s a lump In your abdomen.

I said No shit - I’m the one who reported it and that’s why I’m here.

They said on lumps we always recommend a biopsy. I said fine but it seems like
that should have been happening on my first visit instead of making another
cash register ring at the scanning center.

But as always I asked if it is urgent because I’ve got some Travel On the books
with my kids to Europe.

They said no rush; it’s probably nothing.

So finally around the last part of august 2019 I go in for a biopsy. I could see
from the reaction of the ultrasound tech’s face as she scanned that it was more
than nothing, but she was in no position to make a diagnosis. The doctor did the
biopsy and called me the next day.

Steve there’s no easy way
To say this - but you have a metastatic melanoma.

I said what does that mean?

He said: You have cancer.

I hope none of you ever hear those words. They crashed on my ears like a
thousand cannons. It was like I was punched in the face by Mike Tyson except
somehow the blow was delivered by phone.

After a pause of unknown length I said ok - what do I do now?

--- PAGE 11 ---
He said I’ve got you an appointment tomorrow with the top melanoma
oncologist in the state at the cancer care center next door.

Btw - The casual way doctors use the term cancer and even the way they name
the clinics which write the word cancer in their signs, and business cards and
stationary is idiotic.

Those of us who’ve heard the cannon shot - we already know we have cancer
so there’s no need to have them making giant signs to remind us.

The word cancer is no fun. Even as I say it now it will make you squirm.

But the phrase YOU have cancer is a show-stopper.

Nevertheless - I put on my game face and went to the oncologist.

He said, “Steve you have cancer - and that sucks, but we don’t know what it
means yet so don’t go jumping to any conclusions.”

I carefully weighed his advice.

Then I said how long have I got doc?

He said you are jumping and in any case, I can’t answer that yet.

He went on to say we need to do more scans and we need to biopsy those
spots in your lungs.

Btw - For those that love horror movies ask me sometime later about how they
do a lung biopsy.

After a flurry of scans that required me to drink enough radioactive dye to kill a
small village and after living through the live horror show that is a lung biopsy
the doctor called me back in.

Steve what we have here is stage IV melanoma cancer. And that’s not what we
were hoping for.

I said it makes sense that I have the high score - I’m a natural
Overachiever.

I said how long have I got?

--- PAGE 12 ---
He said I don’t know. It depends. But you should get your affairs in order while
we see how the treatment plan unfolds.

He went on to say “It’s in your lungs and your abdomen and that means it can
be anywhere in your body.”

I said what’s the plan.

He said in this case we can’t operate. We could normally operate on your
abdomen, but not on your lungs so that’s not a viable plan.

Shortly after the staging diagnosis, he said the full body scans indicate we just
need to focus on the known areas of the lungs and abdomen.

He said we’re going to start chemo and then determine how effective this
cocktail of immunotherapy works on your own brand of cancer.

I told him if that is going to turn me into a zombie I’m not interested.

I never felt any pain or any effects of cancer and I certainly wasn’t interested in
spending my final days dying from the “cure.”

He said immunotherapy is a newer kind of chemo that basically tells your
immune system to work at super strength and the side effects were very mild for
most people.

I said ok when do we start.

He said tomorrow. That was in mid-October 2019.

I said I am leaving in 3 days to China and hosting a group of 20 people with me.
I will not to disrupt that trip.

He assured me I could still go even if I did have some chemo side effects.

When I returned home I did the normal “so you have cancer” internet searches
about a stage IV melanoma diagnosis and the picture didn’t look good. The 5
year survival rate in the preceding decade was around 20%. For those
conversion experts if 20% survive - 80% die.

My version of cancer was not awesomer.

--- PAGE 13 ---
After sharing with my family and my business partners I announced my
diagnosis on the awesomers podcast.

I was not seeking attention,
Thought and prayers,
 or suggestions on how to cope;

I shared because  I felt a duty to proactively let people know I will not be as
accessible in the near future as I was in the recent past.

During that episode I shared a simple message:

I didn’t volunteer for this mission, but I have no choice but to accept it and to
keep calm and carry on.

Despite my requests to the contrary, some people still offered unsolicited advice
which I both appreciated and ignored.

Some other people said, “oh whew melanoma - skin cancer that’s no big deal.” I
ignored that too.

Stage IV is not some pimple or mole that can be removed, but ignorance is not a
sin.

And for people closest to me who were scared I simply had to reassure them
that I felt fine and until I felt otherwise there was no point in losing our minds.

As Steve Jobs said death is the ultimate motivator. We all have limited time and
if we spend too many days doing things we don’t like we’re wasting that time.

Remarkably after two months of chemo, I could no longer feel the lump in my
stomach.

The fleet of doctors assured me that was a positive sign.

They said we should assume the treatment is having an effect on all of the
cancer cells and we should remain optimistic.

I restated what I always said - until I feel some negative effect of cancer or the
chemo I will simply keep calm and carry on.

I was able to complete the China Sourcing with Steve trip without a hitch
including some bonus Disney stops in Hong Kong and Shanghai with my kids.

--- PAGE 14 ---
I attended a couple of Other events that winter and enjoyed a trip from Rome to
Israel, Egypt, Jordan Oman and the UAE.

Then Covid happened

Despite covid, I maintained my monthly chemo schedule, and with each
treatment, which I called my monthly oil change, I would drive myself there and
back.

After each oil change, I got back to my daily routine and pushed through all of
the complexities that came with Covid and watching the world lose its mind.

By the summer we did some scans and they looked clear.

Finally, by October 2020 I said if my scans are clear and the blood tests are
good why should I keep doing chemo? One doctor said, “because the insurance
will still pay for it.”

I said that is not the right answer. We agreed that I would stop treatments and
go into surveillance mode.

Btw - My insurance company tried to raise my rate to 10,000 per month btw - so
I was forced to switch to a new company and luckily I was able to jam my last
chemo down the bastards at blue cross who tried to Jack up my rates due to
one year of chemo bills - after I paid in for 20+ years with basically zero claims.

One lesson my friends is that life is short. I was lucky that my treatment worked.

And I was super lucky the only side effect I have is my skin color changed a bit.

I do not consider myself cured and I do not call myself a survivor. I’m just a guy
who got lucky.
I’ve always been lucky and
I encourage you to find ways to create luck in your life.

Never stop building a life that is worth living.

Luck has a way of finding people who make deliberate decisions.

--- PAGE 15 ---
There’s no benefit to panic and when faced with a lightning bolt we must keep
calm and carry on.

Even the experts who said it’s probably nothing multiple times were wrong. So
don’t wait for some expert to tell you what’s right for you. You decide.

I don’t want you to believe that I never had dark days and nights- just like
anyone else —- wondering why me; why now?

Those questions, while natural, are pointless.

I knew intellectually that there was no point in melting down when I didn’t feel
sick. I knew my kids needed their father for every remaining day without them
carrying the burden of my diagnosis.

My job was clear enough and I took it as seriously as any undertaking in my life.

The mental checkup that Steve Jobs talked about was looking in the mirror each
night and asking if that was a day you are proud of. And if too many of those go
by when the answer is no -
Something’s got to change.

Jeff Bezos has the ultimate nerd phrase called “regret minimization framework”
which gave him permission to take risks based on his future self NOT regretting
the road he never took - even if he failed.

Each of you should ask yourself in the mirror tonight how was today, and then
repeat that often.

Everyone gets lighting bolts in the course of their life. Including some Of my
friends in this community who were meant to be here celebrating with us at
festivus - they had a lighting bolt last week that is both devastating and yet part
of life.

Ask yourself what your potential regrets might be as we head into 2023 and take
steps to minimize those.

The key lesson from story one is to know your value and don’t take shit from
anyone who doesn’t value you.

The lesson from story two is that if you can fly rockets don’t spend any more
time than absolutely necessary in the coal mines.

--- PAGE 16 ---
And the lesson from story 3 is no matter what you’re faced with you must keep
calm and carry on. Deal with the cold reality.
Make a rational plan to move forward.

And remember that no matter how bad you have it there are others who have
had worse.

When I looked around the chemo center I was the youngest person there and
some may say that’s unlucky, but it was clear to me that I was the luckiest in
that room because I felt no pain and had no side effects and have nothing
besides the IV in my arm that indicated that I had cancer.

In the strongest terms possible I urge you to Be grateful for the chance you have
to play in the game of life

and the gift of having a new roll of the dice each and every day.

Thank you for being here and I wish you the very best holiday and new year
possible!
