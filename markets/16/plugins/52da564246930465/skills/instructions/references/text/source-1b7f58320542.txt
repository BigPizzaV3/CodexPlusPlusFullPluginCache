TOMS SHOES


Brand Story: According to the website,
which includes The Toms Story, founder
Blake Mycoskie “witnessed the
hardships faced by children growing up
without shoes” while traveling in
Argentina in 2006. “Wanting to help, he
created Toms Shoes, a company that
would match every pair of shoes
purchased with a new pair of shoes for
a child in need,” the site says.
In a Nutshell: One for One.

--- PAGE 42 ---
WARBY PARKER


Brand Story: In its Story, eyewear retailer
Warby Parker says it was founded as a
rebellious upstart to tackle the problem of
expensive eyewear after one of its founders
lost his glasses on a backpacking trip and
couldn’t afford to replace them as a grad
student. What’s more, like Toms, Warby
Parker partners with nonprofits such as
VisionSpring to distribute a pair of glasses
to someone in need for each pair sold.
In a Nutshell: Good eyewear, good
outcome.

--- PAGE 43 ---
CHIPOTLE


Brand Story: Chipotle says it is in the
business of good food. It seeks to use
high-quality raw ingredients, classic
cooking techniques and distinctive
interior design to bring elements of fine
dining to quick-service restaurants. At
the same time, Chipotle also says it
seeks to cultivate a better world with
respect for animals, farmers and the
environment.
In a Nutshell: Food with Integrity.

--- PAGE 44 ---
BACKRUB


BRAND STORY: Google is a relatively young
brand, which was founded in 1995 and was
initially named Backrub. After a quick change
of name, it skyrocketed past the competition
and into the popular vernacular. Google is all
about advertising, yet it rarely advertises its
search capabilities on the screen. When it
does, it turns off the sales pitch and focuses
on human stories. Google commercials use
minimal branding, and rarely include a call to
action. What’s consistent with the brand is
quick simple information on demand.
IN A NUT SHELL: Clean fast reliable results.

Internal Team Pros

In-house marketing teams capture the soul of your brand
better than any agency can.
You can utilize the talent you have already.
In-house marketing teams can get things done faster.
More control over the outcome.
Better sales and marketing alignment.
Get faster results.

--- PAGE 62 ---
Internal Team Cons

 You’ll need to hire new staff members.
Hiring new employees means having to pay
additional salaries.
You need to train these staff members.
With more staff comes more responsibility.
There is no pause button if things don’t work out.

--- PAGE 63 ---
Pros of Hiring an Agency

Agencies do all the work for you.
 Agencies tend to be good at managing their
capacities.
 Agencies have a large pool of talent.
 Larger agencies are better at cost management.
 They manage their own staff.

--- PAGE 64 ---
Internal Team Cons

It’s difficult for agencies to capture the essence of
your brand.
The work can take longer to get started and fixed.
You will always be depending on an outside source.
It’s difficult to keep track of your return on investment.
There is no pause button if things don’t work out.
