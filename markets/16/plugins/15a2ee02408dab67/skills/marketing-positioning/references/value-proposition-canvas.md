# Value Proposition Canvas

Adapted into English from the supplied DELUCKS knowledge module.

Use the canvas to connect an offer with the audience's actual needs. Treat the fit as a working hypothesis until validated.

| Customer profile | Meaning | Useful questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jobs | Functional, professional, emotional or social progress | What does this person need to accomplish? What responsibility matters? |
| Pains | Friction, cost, effort, risk or frustration | What gets in the way? What creates stress or uncertainty? |
| Gains | Desired outcomes and signs of success | What would improve? What would count as a meaningful result? |

| Value map | Meaning | Useful questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Products/services | What the offer actually contains | What is delivered, to whom, and how? |
| Pain relievers | How the offer reduces a specific pain | Which mechanism reduces effort, risk or frustration? |
| Gain creators | How it supports the desired outcome | What progress becomes possible, and what evidence supports it? |

Map each relevant offer element to a specific job, pain or gain; flag features with no clear customer value and benefits with no mechanism or proof.

Illustrative B2B example: a service for internal communication may address scattered tools, slow coordination and opaque responsibilities. Desired gains might include clearer information, fewer avoidable meetings and reliable remote collaboration. An onboarding workshop or a small initial implementation could support those gains, but neither is automatically part of the user's offer.

When the user struggles to describe needs, begin with responsibilities and current work rather than demanding framework terminology. Ask what costs time, what creates risk and what a better situation would look like. Use the result to refine positioning and messages, not to invent guaranteed outcomes.
