# Source validation

Use the supplied DELUCKS five-criterion weighting as a transparent editorial heuristic. It is not an external trust metric, scientific measurement, probability of correctness or ranking factor. State the scope: individual claim/page, or a limited domain sample.

## Gather evidence first

Inspect the relevant content and, where available, authorship, publisher ownership, editorial policies, sponsorship disclosures, publication/update date, references, methods and the underlying evidence. Check the claim against an appropriate original source where needed.

Separate:
- Source type: original research, official specification/schedule, editorial article, review aggregation, company content, sponsored article, etc.
- Independence and conflicts: editorial control, affiliate revenue, funding or other incentives.
- Claim suitability: what this source can actually substantiate.
- Audience fit and outreach value: separate marketing judgments, not components of factual truth.

A manufacturer's specification can support its own stated specification; an organizer can establish an event date. Neither alone independently validates performance or broad comparative claims.

## Score only assessable criteria

Rate each criterion from 0 to 5, where 0 means observed severe deficiencies and 5 means strong supporting evidence. Unknown is not zero and not a neutral midpoint.

| Criterion | Weight | Consider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objectivity/independence | 3 | Editorial control, conflicts, balance and relevance to the claim |
| Relevant reputation/expertise | 2 | Demonstrable subject expertise, accountable authorship and track record |
| Currency | 2 | Whether the information remains appropriate for the claim; date alone is insufficient |
| Evidence/methods | 2 | Traceable sources, methods, sample limits and support for assertions |
| Transparency | 1 | Ownership, author, commercial disclosures, limitations and corrections |

Base score = 3 × objectivity + 2 × reputation + 2 × currency + 2 × evidence + transparency.
The range is 0–50. Show the five inputs or a brief auditable breakdown when the user requests a detailed source review.

If any criterion cannot reasonably be assessed, report "not assessed" for the total and explain the missing evidence. Do not fill uncertainty with 25/50, invent a traffic/authority metric, or normalize a partial score to look complete. Distinguish unknown from an observed absence (for example, an article making empirical claims without any traceable support).

| Score | Rating |
| --- | --- |
| 41–50 | ⭐ Very high |
| 31–40 | ✅ High |
| 21–30 | ⚠️ Moderate |
| 11–20 | 🚫 Low |
| 0–10 | ❌ Very low |
| Not assessed | ? Insufficient evidence |

Translate labels into the conversation/result language. Always describe the score as heuristic, with high/medium/low confidence based on access and evidence. A low score does not by itself prove that a publisher or person is fraudulent.

## Detect commercial influence without unsupported accusations

Confirmed advertorial requires evidence such as an explicit advertisement/sponsorship label, disclosed paid placement or verified advertiser editorial control. Clearly identify it.

Possible warning signs include vague authorship, affiliate links, one-sided promotion, weak topic fit, missing dates and replicated copy. These call for closer inspection; even several signs do not automatically prove sponsorship or falsehood. Missing dates are less material for stable claims than for current fees or event details.

Do not apply the former automatic default score or an arbitrary advertorial penalty. Reflect observed conflicts in the criteria and label the source type separately. Regardless of its numerical score, paid content is not independent endorsement.

## Use the score appropriately

- Prefer well-supported, relevant evidence for factual claims; a score alone never establishes correctness.
- Scores of 31 or above may help prioritize editorial evidence, subject to claim fit and corroboration.
- Treat scores of 21–30 cautiously; check the important claims against appropriate original or independent sources.
- Use lower-scored material as context rather than the sole basis for consequential comparative claims.
- Treat an unassessed source as unverified; do not present it as verified merely because it is on a famous domain.
- Preserve official sources for narrow facts they directly control, such as published schedules or terms, while noting their interests.
- Paid listings and sponsored opportunities may be relevant to a PR plan; evaluate audience fit, cost and transparency separately from editorial trust.

## Compare several sources

Compute the median, minimum, maximum and spread only over actual numeric scores; report the count assessed and excluded. For three scores, the median is the middle ordered value. Do not label a mean as a median or hide missing scores.

Finish with a claim-specific recommendation: suitable evidence, requires corroboration, background only or not assessable. Identify which missing source or check would materially change that judgment.
