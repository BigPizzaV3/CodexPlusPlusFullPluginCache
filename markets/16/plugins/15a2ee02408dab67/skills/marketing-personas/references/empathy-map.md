# Empathy Map

Adapted into English from the supplied DELUCKS knowledge module.

Use the map to explore the customer's context and possible emotions. Distinguish observed statements and behavior from imagined explanations. Do not pretend to know private thoughts.

| Perspective | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Sees | Which problems, alternatives, messages and trends are visible in daily work or life? |
| Hears | What comes from colleagues, managers, peers, media or other influences? |
| Thinks/feels | What concerns, hopes and uncertainties have been expressed or could be tested? |
| Says/does | What does the person state publicly, and what behavior is observed? |
| Pains | What frustrates, delays or adds effort or perceived risk? |
| Gains | What progress, reassurance or success is sought? |

Illustrative example: a manufacturing business leader might see rising costs, hear pressure to modernize, and publicly say that new tools are under review. Concern about disruption and a preference for a manageable first step are possible hypotheses, not confirmed inner thoughts.

Use interview prompts such as "What do you hear from your team about this problem?" and "What would make the next step feel manageable?" Ask about real situations before imagining stories. If a role-play or composite quote helps explain a hypothesis, label it as illustrative.

Connect insights to objections, trust signals, language, evidence and useful content formats. Do not use emotional insight to exploit vulnerability or stereotype a demographic.
