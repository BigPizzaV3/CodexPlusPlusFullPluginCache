# Slide guide: what each slide has to do

An investor reads a deck on a phone, headlines first, in about three minutes. Every slide has one job. If the headline alone doesn't do that job, the slide isn't finished.

For each slide: the job, what it must show, the usual mistakes, and a quick test.

## Cover and one-liner
- **Job:** say what the company does, for whom, in one plain sentence.
- **Must show:** company name, the one-liner, round and contact if it's sent cold.
- **Mistakes:** a tagline instead of a description ("Reimagining work"), a category label ("AI-powered platform"), no one-liner at all.
- **Test:** could a smart friend outside the industry repeat it back after one read?

## Problem
- **Job:** make the investor feel a real, specific pain before seeing the solution.
- **Must show:** who has the problem, what it costs them (money, time, risk), how they deal with it today.
- **Strong problems are:** common, urgent, expensive, frequent, or forced by a change (a law, a platform shift).
- **Mistakes:** a problem invented to fit the solution, three vague problems, "inefficient" with no number.
- **Test:** two lines maximum, a specific person, a specific cost.

## Solution
- **Job:** show why this is clearly better for that exact problem.
- **Must show:** what the user does differently, and the before/after in one comparison.
- **Mistakes:** feature lists, architecture diagrams, "seamless", "end-to-end", screenshots with no caption.
- **Test:** is the improvement obvious, ideally 10x on one thing that matters?

## Why now
- **Job:** explain what changed that makes this possible or necessary today.
- **Must show:** the shift (technology, regulation, behaviour, cost curve) and how fast it's moving.
- **Mistakes:** skipping it, or "AI is growing" with no link to this company.
- **Test:** could this company have been built five years ago? If yes, why didn't it work then?

## Market
- **Job:** show the prize is big enough to matter, and believable.
- **Must show:** bottom-up sizing (number of customers × what they pay), the first segment, and how it expands.
- **Mistakes:** top-down TAM from a research report, trillion-dollar numbers, TAM/SAM/SOM circles with no logic.
- **Test:** can the investor redo the math in their head?

## Product
- **Job:** make it real. Show it working.
- **Must show:** one or two screens or a short flow, captioned with what the user gets.
- **Mistakes:** a wall of screenshots, mockups presented as live product, roadmap mixed in with what exists.
- **Test:** is it clear what exists today versus what's planned?

## Traction
- **Job:** prove demand. This is often the slide that decides the meeting.
- **Must show:** the one metric that best proves people want it, over time, with the time axis visible.
- **By stage:** pre-seed shows engagement, waitlists with conversion, pilots or letters of intent, and founder-led sales; seed shows revenue or usage growth, retention and repeat use; Series A shows repeatable growth and unit economics.
- **Mistakes:** cumulative charts that hide flat growth, vanity numbers (downloads, signups) without retention, logos of free pilots presented as customers.
- **Test:** would the number still impress if the investor asked "per week, and how many came back"?

## Business model
- **Job:** show how money comes in and why it can be a lot.
- **Must show:** who pays, how much, how often; one line of unit economics if known.
- **Mistakes:** five revenue streams at pre-seed, pricing with no evidence anyone pays it.
- **Test:** price × customers in one line.

## Competition and insight
- **Job:** show what the founders know that others don't, and why they win.
- **Must show:** the real alternatives (including spreadsheets and doing nothing), and the one advantage that compounds (network effects, proprietary data, distribution, cost, brand).
- **Mistakes:** the 2×2 where the startup is alone in the top right, "no competitors", feature checkbox tables.
- **Test:** can the founder say the secret in one sentence?

## Go-to-market
- **Job:** show how the first 100 or 1,000 customers arrive.
- **Must show:** the specific channel, why it works for this customer, and early evidence it does.
- **Mistakes:** "viral", "social media", a list of every channel.
- **Test:** is there one channel with proof, not ten with hope?

## Team
- **Job:** answer "why these people?"
- **Must show:** what each founder has built or achieved that maps to this problem, and how long they've worked on it.
- **Mistakes:** logos of past employers with no achievement, a long advisor list, missing technical founder with no explanation.
- **Test:** one impressive, specific line per founder.

## The ask
- **Job:** say what the money buys.
- **Must show:** amount, runway, and the milestones it reaches that make the next round easy.
- **Mistakes:** a use-of-funds pie chart with no milestones, no amount.
- **Test:** "We're raising X to get to Y by Z."

## Whole-deck checks
- **Headline test:** read only the first line of every slide. It should read like a paragraph that tells the story.
- **Order:** the strongest proof comes early. If the best line is on slide 11, it belongs on slide 2 or 3.
- **Plain words:** no jargon, no unexplained acronyms, no copy that sounds machine-written (stacked adjectives, "revolutionizing", "seamlessly").
- **One idea per slide,** big type, numbers instead of adjectives.
- **Length:** around 10 to 15 slides for a first meeting; detail goes in an appendix.
