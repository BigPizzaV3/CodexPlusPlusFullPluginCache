## B. Fast CAT / journal club
1. **Question:** precise clinical/research question and population, with context.
2. **Study and source access:** design, full-text vs abstract, DOI verification status.
3. **Findings:** actual effect sizes and absolute measures with CI, primary outcome, harms. Cite table/page.
4. **Can we trust it?** two to four strengths, key risk-of-bias domains and unknowns, reporting completeness.
5. **Does it apply?** similarity of patient/setting, resources, baseline risk, preferences, feasibility; no personalized directive.
6. **Bottom line:** one carefully qualified conclusion and what evidence or analysis would change it.

## C. Deep appraisal
- Overview and source inventory.
- Explicit PICO/PECO/PIRD and design.
- Findings (results, precision, missingness and discrepancies).
- Risk of bias by outcome/domain with evidence-location table; label provisional where full information is absent.
- Statistical critique (effect estimand, analysis population, effect size and CI, sensitivity analysis).
- Clinical relevance/generalizability and comparison with verified external evidence when asked.
- Prioritized concerns: confirmed issue / likely concern / information missing.
- Recommendations for clarification, data checks and future research.
- Final confidence/uncertainties note.

## D. Journal peer-review draft
**To authors**
- Summary: question/design/main claims in neutral language, no invented results.
- Overall assessment: significance, novelty only with external search or explicitly conditional, strengths and core methodological limits.
- Major Comments (numbered): each includes manuscript location, evidence, implications, specific requested improvement, and whether issue is confirmed/uncertain.
- Minor Comments (numbered): clarity, reporting, terminology, table headings, units and reproducibility.
- Conclusions: constructive next revision priorities.

**Confidential comments to editor**, only if separately requested and if appropriate: optional editorial concerns not suitable for authors, distinguishing confidential information from evidence. Do not put allegations of misconduct without sufficient basis.

**Recommendation**: If user explicitly requests, offer one of Accept / Minor Revision / Major Revision / Reject with a transparent, conditional rationale. Journal editors decide outcomes. Do not supply a formal recommendation solely on an abstract unless limitations are acknowledged; do not invent a journal's editorial criteria.

## E. Journal fit
For a named journal, obtain verified scope, manuscript types, article requirements and any editorial instructions. Separate: topical fit, audience fit, format fit, methods soundness, registration/ethics, potential contribution. Avoid numeric acceptance probabilities or claims about indexing/impact factor without current sources.

## Evidence-anchored comment pattern
“**Major 2 – Table 3 / Statistical Methods:** Authors report X in Table 3 but Y in the abstract. This makes the primary effect estimate difficult to interpret. Please reconcile denominators and report [specified calculation]. **Evidence status:** confirmed discrepancy from provided text; clinical impact cannot be assessed until clarified.”

Never include the example's X or Y as if it were a real manuscript result.
