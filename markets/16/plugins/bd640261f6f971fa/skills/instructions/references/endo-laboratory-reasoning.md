# Endocrine Laboratory Reasoning — Educational Guide

## Interpret the question before the number
1. Name the hormone/biomarker, unit, sample type, collection time, and assay method if available. Convert units only when exact, trustworthy conversion is available.
2. Compare with the **laboratory's applicable age-, sex-, pregnancy-, and pubertal-stage-specific reference interval**, not assumed adult limits. Pediatric endocrine physiology changes during infancy and puberty.
3. Locate the expected feedback relationship (e.g. TSH–free T4, ACTH–cortisol, gonadotropins–sex steroids, IGF-1–GH axis) and distinguish primary gland effects from central or adaptive responses. Explain why a single test may be non-diagnostic.
4. Consider preanalytic/analytic issues: time of day, fasting status when relevant, sample handling, biotin interference, assay-specific cross-reactivity/heterophile antibodies, binding proteins, medication effects (e.g. glucocorticoids), acute illness, and test timing after dynamic stimulation/suppression. Do not imply every factor affects every test.
5. Explicitly list missing information and alternative explanations. If values, assay, units or ranges are absent, do not invent them.
6. For dynamic tests explain the purpose/logic of stimulation or suppression conceptually; **do not prescribe doses, preparation, cutoffs, or individualized protocols**.

## Teaching templates
- **Thyroid axis:** What does TSH imply relative to FT4? Are thyroiditis, Graves disease, illness and medications distinguishable with current data? What are the assay limitations?
- **Adrenal axis:** Cortisol interpretation depends on collection context and illness; distinguish physiology from an emergency. In actual crisis symptoms, prioritize urgent clinical care, not a classroom algorithm.
- **Growth/puberty:** Growth velocity, age-adjusted values, bone-age context and pubertal stage affect interpretation; no diagnosis from one isolated numerical result.
- **Glucose/diabetes:** For theoretical glucose/ketone data, distinguish screening/diagnostic definitions and clinical context; do not use unsupported thresholds. Flag severe hypoglycemia or suspected DKA in actual patients as urgent.
- **Calcium/phosphate:** Corrected/ionized calcium and albumin relationship, PTH, vitamin D, renal function and measurement context may matter; avoid fabricated reference intervals.

## Output format
**Data given** | **Physiology** | **Possible explanations (not diagnosis)** | **Confounders and missing data** | **How to verify with reliable source**.
