# IUL Educational Content Workflow

Use this optional specialist workflow only when the user asks for IUL or related life-insurance education. It is a content and explanation aid, not a product recommendation, illustration, suitability assessment, or substitute for licensed professional review.

## Entry and evidence

First establish what the user wants to create and what jurisdiction, product, carrier, audience, and approved source materials apply. Treat missing information as UNKNOWN. Use current primary sources for product mechanics and rules where available; cite them and state date and jurisdiction. Do not assume a product, company, compensation structure, customer profile, product design, or approved disclosure.

Keep CONFIRMED facts, HYPOTHESIS, ASSUMPTION, EXAMPLE, and UNKNOWN distinct. Fictional case studies and illustrations must be visibly labeled as examples. Never invent customer facts, premiums, benefits, rates, cash values, loan terms, tax outcomes, performance, costs, fees, or guarantees.

## Guided workflow

1. **Audience and question:** Clarify the intended audience, what they are trying to understand, and whether the asset is educational, informational, or promotional. Audience traits and needs remain hypotheses unless supported by evidence.
2. **Problem and context:** Describe the question or decision context without implying that everyone shares it or that IUL is the answer.
3. **Case narrative:** Use real, approved, permissioned facts only. Separate the person's situation, the issue they explored, options considered, decision process, and any verified outcome. If no real case is supplied, create a clearly labeled fictional teaching example with no fabricated testimonial or promised result.
4. **Source-based product explanation:** Explain only mechanics supported by a current approved policy document, illustration, disclosure, or authoritative source. Distinguish index crediting mechanics from direct market investment and explain that non-guaranteed values are not promises.
5. **Illustration review:** If the user supplies an illustration, explain only visible documented assumptions and values. Separate guaranteed from non-guaranteed values; identify charges, assumptions, access conditions, loans or withdrawals, lapse risks, and policy-specific constraints when present in the document. Do not calculate or project missing values. Recommend review by the qualified, licensed professional responsible for the illustration.
6. **Voice and format:** Adapt approved language to the user's chosen audience, channel, and language. Keep product explanations comprehensible, balanced, and non-alarmist. Do not turn education into pressure.
7. **Next step:** Use a voluntary, accurate CTA. A public question does not grant permission for private contact, lead capture, or sales follow-up.
8. **Quality check:** Verify every material statement against supplied/current sources, preserve uncertainty, avoid unsupported comparisons, and flag required carrier or compliance review.

## Output options

Depending on the request, produce a research brief, question guide, case-story outline, educational short-form script, caption, carousel structure, illustration walkthrough, FAQ, or funnel handoff. Include placeholders where source facts are missing and label them clearly.

## Professional and claims boundaries

- Do not make individualized financial, legal, tax, or insurance recommendations.
- Do not state or imply that IUL is suitable for everyone, guarantees investment-like returns, removes risk, or guarantees a particular outcome.
- Do not confuse an illustrated projection with a guaranteed value or actual customer result.
- Do not invent carrier rules, product terms, premiums, rates, tax outcomes, fees, or compensation disclosures.
- Keep referral, recruiting, and consumer education objectives separate unless the user explicitly requests a combined plan; make each audience and CTA clear.
- Require the user to verify product-specific and jurisdiction-specific claims with current approved materials and qualified compliance review before publication.

## Handoff

Route broader brand strategy to the brand skills, real customer research to `customer-discovery`, market facts to `market-research`, feasibility to `business-feasibility-validation`, content production to `content-creation`, funnel design to `lead-generation`, and optional opportunity/team development to `network-marketing-growth`.
