# Case Study Builder

Use when a user wants to turn a real project, customer experience, or documented result into a case study. A case study is only as strong as its source material and permissions.

## Readiness and source check

Before drafting, establish:

- Whether this is a real case, anonymized real case, composite, or fictional example.
- Which source notes, records, metrics, and approvals are available and what the AIOS actually inspected.
- Whether the subject has agreed to the intended use, naming, quotation, imagery, and channel; minimize sensitive details.
- What the case can substantiate and what it cannot. A single case does not establish typical performance or causation.

If real evidence or permission is missing, offer a source-gathering template or a clearly labeled demonstration instead. Never fill gaps with plausible details.

## Case-study structure

1. **Context:** relevant starting conditions and scope.
2. **Challenge:** the problem as documented or reported, with attribution.
3. **Approach:** actions actually taken, distinguishing the user's work from other contributors.
4. **Outcome:** only documented results, with dates, units, denominators, comparison basis, and limitations where applicable.
5. **Interpretation:** bounded explanation; distinguish correlation, reported perception, and demonstrated causation.
6. **Applicability:** who might find the approach relevant and what would need to differ in another context.

## Claim and evidence controls

- Preserve exact supplied figures; check arithmetic and units when possible. Label estimates and user-reported metrics. If a figure is unsupported or contradicted, flag it and suggest a sourced correction. Never publish a known-false figure as fact.
- Do not fabricate testimonials, quotes, customer identity, baseline, method, timeline, or results.
- Do not imply typical or guaranteed outcomes from one example. Explain selection and survivorship limits when relevant.
- For regulated or high-stakes claims, recommend qualified review and use only approved, current source material.

## Standard output

Deliver a case outline or draft, a claim-to-source table, permission and privacy status, limitations, and the remaining confirmations needed before publication. Any synthetic sample must be visibly labeled throughout as **EXAMPLE / SYNTHETIC — NOT EVIDENCE**.
