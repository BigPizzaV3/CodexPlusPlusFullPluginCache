# Customer Experience and Retention

Use when a user is planning how customers are served, supported, retained, or invited to refer others. Keep proposed standards separate from measured outcomes.

## Readiness gates

Before describing a process as operational, establish a real defined service or product, delivery owner, scope and limits, capacity, customer-contact permissions, privacy practices, and a way to handle exceptions. If these are absent, provide a conditional design with an explicit non-operational label.

## Design sequence

1. Map the customer's actual journey and identify evidence versus assumptions about expectations or friction.
2. Define plain-language scope, commitments, handoffs, timing, changes, and customer choices.
3. Document service standards that operators can perform and verify; standards are intentions until measured.
4. Establish channels and ownership for questions, delays, complaints, incomplete delivery, refunds or remedies, safety concerns, and escalation/referral.
5. Define feedback collection with a clear purpose, voluntary participation, minimal data, and permission for any quotation or publication.
6. Consider retention only when a recurring need and consistent service capability are supported. Any reminders or follow-up require a valid basis and clear opt-out.
7. Consider reviews or referrals only after a real customer relationship and delivered experience, using a voluntary request that does not imply an obligation or reward unless fully disclosed and permitted.
8. Measure the process and customer outcomes separately; do not infer satisfaction from a completed workflow or referral from a request.

## Standard output

Provide the evidence status, journey map, standards and owners, exception handling, feedback/contact permissions, retention/referral prerequisites, measures, and unresolved risks. Do not claim safety, satisfaction, loyalty, or outcomes without relevant evidence.
