# Comparison pages and alternatives pages

This is the format that Frontmost AI uses for its own comparison pages. The examples use invented businesses.

## Comparison page

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Title | "<Business> vs <Rival>: prices and features" |
| First paragraph | Two sentences. Each one says what one product includes and its entry price. Give no verdict |
| Table | The same rows for both products: each plan that exists, what each plan includes, and the limits |
| Source line | "<Rival> facts: <link to the rival's page>, checked <date>." |
| Choose <Business> if | Two or three lines. Each line is a fact from the table |
| Choose <Rival> if | Two or three lines. Each line is a true strength of the rival |
| Questions | Four or five questions that buyers ask about the two products, each with a direct answer |

Example of the two "Choose" blocks:

> **Choose Acme Invoices if**
> - You want no monthly fee. Acme Invoices charges 1% for each paid invoice.
> - You send recurring invoices.
>
> **Choose Billbright if**
> - You want time tracking in the same tool.
> - You send more than 200 invoices a month. Its flat fee is lower at that volume.

## Rules for facts about a rival

- Take each fact from the rival's own public page, opened today. Do not use your memory, and do not use a list on another site.
- Record the address and the date of each fact.
- Include the points where the rival is stronger. A page that hides them is not fair, and readers will see it.
- If two prices use different units or different currencies, say so. Do not convert them. Do not rank the products by cost unless the units match.
- State facts. Do not write about the rival's quality, its plans, or its customers' opinions.
- Use the rival's name as text. Do not use its logo or its screenshots without permission.
- For a legal question about a comparison, the person asks a lawyer.

## Alternatives page

- List four to eight real alternatives. Include the strong rivals.
- Say at the top who wrote the list: "We make <Business>. This is how the alternatives compare."
- Give each entry the same parts: what it is, who it fits, the entry price with its source, and one limit.
- Give the business one entry with the same parts, limit included.
- Put the entries in an order that you state, such as by entry price.

A reader, an editor, and an AI app can all see who published a list. A list that puts its publisher first with no stated reason loses their trust.

## Upkeep

- Check the rival's page again each month, and after each price change that you see.
- Change the "checked" date only after you opened the page again. A new date with no check is not an update.
- If a fact changed, change the table and each line that used the fact.
