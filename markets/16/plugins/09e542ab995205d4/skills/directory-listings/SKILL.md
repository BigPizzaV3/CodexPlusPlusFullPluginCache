# Directory listings

Directories, marketplaces, and business profiles are pages that a business can change by itself. AI apps cite them, and they repeat the facts that they find there. A listing with an old price or a second spelling of the name gives an AI app a wrong fact to repeat.

Use this guidance to find the listings, to make them agree, and to stay inside each platform's rules. You write the corrections. The person makes them.

## 1. Write the facts once

Make one record of the true facts. The person confirms each line. Save it as `business-facts.md`.

| Fact | Rule |
|---|---|
| Name | The real name, as it is on the sign, the website, and the documents of the business |
| Address or service area | The place where customers come, or the area that the business serves |
| Phone and website | One phone number and one website |
| Hours | The hours when a customer can reach the business |
| Category | The few categories that describe the main business |
| One sentence | "<Name> is a <category> for <buyer> in <place>." |
| Short description | Two or three sentences with the same facts |
| Products or services | Each one with its price, or with what sets the price |

Use the same sentence and the same spelling in each listing.

## 2. Find the listings

1. Take the Directory and Business profile rows from a `source-map`, if one exists.
2. Search the web for the business name with its phone number, and the name with its city.
3. Search for the directories that list the rivals for the buyer questions.
4. Check the usual profiles for the business type. For a local business: Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, and the trade directories in the search results. For software: the software directories and the marketplaces of the tools that it works with.

Give each listing one status: Claimed, Not claimed, No listing, or Duplicate. A listing is Not claimed when its page shows a link such as "Claim this business". If the page does not show which it is, ask the person.

## 3. Compare

For each listing, compare the name, the address, the phone, the website, the hours, the category, the description, and the prices with the record of step 1. Record each difference with both values.

## 4. Write the corrections

Read the platform's own rules before you write a correction. Quote the rule that applies.

Rules of Google Business Profile ([guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)):

- **Name.** Use the real name of the business. Add no keywords, no place names, and no taglines.
- **Address.** A business that goes to its customers hides its address and sets a service area. A P.O. box is not acceptable. A virtual office is not eligible, unless staff are there during business hours.
- **Categories.** Choose the fewest categories that describe the main business.
- **Description.** Describe the business. Add no links.
- **Facts.** Keep the hours, the phone, and the website current.

For each correction, give the listing, the field, the value there now, and the new value.

## 5. Reviews

Read the review rules of each platform first. They differ.

| Platform or rule | What it says |
|---|---|
| Google Maps ([policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)) | A business can ask customers for reviews. It cannot offer a reward for a review. It cannot ask only the happy customers, and it cannot discourage negative reviews |
| Yelp ([guidelines](https://www.yelp.com/guidelines)) | "Businesses should never ask customers to write reviews." |
| United States law ([FTC rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)) | Since October 21, 2024, a rule covers fake reviews, payment for reviews on the condition that they are positive or negative, and reviews by insiders who do not say who they are |

Then:

1. Where the platform allows a request, draft one neutral message. It asks each customer for honest feedback, and it offers nothing.
2. Draft a reply to each review, positive and negative. A reply to a negative review states the facts and offers a way to contact the business.
3. The person sends the request and posts the replies.

## 6. Who acts

- The person claims the listings, accepts each platform's terms, and enters each verification code. You do none of these.
- You write the corrections and the drafts.
- If a platform shows a listing that the person cannot claim, give the platform's own correction route.

## 7. Check again

Check the listings each quarter, and after each change of address, phone, hours, or price.

## Rules

- No evidence shows that a directory listing earns an AI mention by itself. Consistent listings keep the facts that AI apps repeat correct. Say so.
- Use only the facts that the person confirmed.
- A platform's rules can change. Open the rule page on the day that you quote it. If the page does not load, write "not checked today" next to the rule.

## Never suggest

- A listing at an address that the business does not have, or a second listing for the same place.
- Keywords or place names added to the business name.
- A fake review, a review by staff or family, a reward for a review, or payment to remove a review.
- Mass submission to hundreds of directories. List the business where its buyers look.

## Output

1. The record of facts, with each line that the person has yet to confirm.
2. A spreadsheet file, `listings-<date>.csv`: Platform, Listing Link, Status, Field, Value Now, New Value, Rule.
3. The review request, where it is allowed, and the review replies, as drafts.
4. The listings that the person must claim, each with the page where to start.
