# Original practice starters

These are independently authored teaching exercises, not official IELTS questions, past papers, predicted topics or calibrated tests. Use one at a time. Show the task first; reveal feedback, a key or a model only after the learner attempts it or explicitly requests one.

## Academic Task 1: synthetic data

The table shows the percentage of commuters using three travel modes in the fictional town of Norbridge. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words for full-task practice. For an overview drill, ask for two sentences and do not treat that drill as a full task.

| Mode | 2010 | 2015 | 2020 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Private car | 60% | 55% | 45% |
| Public transport | 30% | 32% | 35% |
| Bicycle | 10% | 13% | 20% |

Internal marking notes: each column totals 100%; cars remain highest but decline, while the other two increase. Bicycle share doubles. No causes are supplied; do not invent a policy explanation. Preserve these figures in any chart or model.

## General Training Task 1

You borrowed a book from a friend but damaged it accidentally. Write a letter to your friend. Explain what happened, describe the damage, and suggest how you will put the situation right. Write at least 150 words. Do not ask the learner for real identifying information; fictional names are sufficient.

## Task 2

Some people think employers should provide time during the working week for employees to learn new skills. Others believe employees should do this in their own time. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. Write at least 250 words for full-task practice. For planning practice, request only a position and two paragraph plans, then wait.

## Speaking Part 2

Describe a skill you learned from another person. Say what the skill was, who helped you, how you practised, and explain why it was useful. Offer one minute to prepare and a one-to-two-minute spoken response; if timing tools are unavailable, ask the learner to self-time. With text input, treat this as answer-development practice.

Part 3 follow-ups, one at a time: How does learning from another person differ from learning alone? Should workplaces set aside time for learning? Why might adults hesitate to learn something new?

## Mini Reading: True/False/Not Given

Passage: The Norbridge Community Library started a Saturday skills club in March. Sessions are free to attend, but participants must book because the room holds only twelve people. The first month offered bicycle maintenance and basic photography. A staff member collected feedback after each session. In April, the library added an evening photography session after several participants said they worked on Saturdays. The Saturday programme continued. The library has not yet decided whether to offer classes for children. Its next review will consider attendance records and participant comments. No information about the club's effect on library membership has been collected.

Decide whether each statement agrees with the passage: True, False, or Not Given.
1. Participants must reserve a place before attending.
2. The evening photography session replaced all Saturday sessions.
3. The bicycle-maintenance teacher had ten years of teaching experience.
4. The library has already decided to introduce classes for children.

Key, withheld until the attempt: 1 True (booking required); 2 False (Saturday programme continued); 3 Not Given (teacher experience not stated); 4 False (decision not made). Report only a score out of four and error explanations. Do not convert it to a Reading band.
