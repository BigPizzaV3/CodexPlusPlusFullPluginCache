# Rare-disease educational workflow

## Complete disease profile (rearrange for user needs)
1. **Identity**: preferred disease name, synonyms, syndrome vs spectrum, and identifiers from checked source (ORPHAcode, OMIM disease number, ICD codes only if truly applicable). Record taxonomy version/source; an Orphanet subgroup is not interchangeable with a single disorder.
2. **Definition and history**: the condition, what makes it rare, discovery if relevant and sourced.
3. **Epidemiology**: distinguish prevalence, incidence, birth prevalence, minimum case counts, and carrier frequency; show geographical/ancestral context, ascertainment bias and evidence quality. Do not estimate from an unsourced case series.
4. **Etiology and pathogenesis**: mode of inheritance, variants/gene(s), molecular and organ mechanism, phenotype heterogeneity, allelic disorders; distinguish definitive from candidate genes.
5. **Phenotype**: core vs possible features, age at onset, variable expressivity, complications, childhood/adult difference, penetrance if characterized, overlap with phenocopies. HPO terms if verified.
6. **Diagnosis (educational)**: syndromic features, differential, diagnostic criteria and their version, biochemical/pathology/genomic modalities, strengths/limitations of tests (e.g. coverage, VUS, missed variant classes). Avoid offering a personal diagnostic conclusion.
7. **Management overview**: supportive care, disease-modifying interventions, surveillance, multidisciplinary teams; separate evidence-based standard, regulatory-authorized therapy, off-label care, and investigational trials. No patient-specific plan or doses.
8. **Prognosis**: state uncertainty, selection/survivorship bias, variable natural history and benefits/harms of therapy.
9. **Sources and evidence gaps**: authoritative named references, publication/revision dates and applicable populations; finish with clearly labeled uncertainties.

## Hypothetical case comparison
- Present 2–5 hypotheses linked to observed phenotype, red flags, and distinctive tests *as concepts*, without ranking with unsupported percentages.
- In pediatric hepatology examples such as Wilson disease vs other hepatic/metabolic causes or Alagille syndrome vs alternate causes of cholestasis, tailor discussion to age of onset and syndrome phenotype; do not conclude from a single feature or a variant alone.
- If case is real, keep educational framing, avoid collection of identifiers and do not give patient-specific treatment decisions.

## Diagnostic evidence
- Sensitivity and specificity are assay-, phenotype-, population- and comparator-dependent; do not treat a published detection rate as universal accuracy.
- An estimated exome/genome diagnostic yield is study- and cohort-specific; only quote with source and denominator.
- Distinguish no pathogenic variant detected from disease excluded.
