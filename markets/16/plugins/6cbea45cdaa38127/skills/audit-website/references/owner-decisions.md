# Help the owner choose the next step

## Explain what matters

Use concrete visitor tasks: understanding the offer, finding a service, reading on a phone, using a keyboard, completing an enquiry, or accessing information. Explain both the observed problem and its practical consequence. If an effect on enquiries or sales is inferred, say so; do not quantify loss without suitable data.

Preserve strengths. Distinguish observations, reported symptoms, hypotheses, and untested areas. Use ordinary language first, then give the technical detail needed by whoever implements the fix.

## Repair or rebuild?

Return one of these conclusions with supporting finding IDs:

- **Targeted improvements are the sensible first step.** The evidenced problems are localized and appear repairable within the current site, such as unclear titles, an obstructing overlay, a broken link, or a few oversized assets. Do not recommend replacing a functioning website because of isolated defects.
- **Consider a broader redesign; verify the scope first.** Several important journeys or templates have demonstrated structural problems, and the business's stated needs support redesign. Explain what a new design must solve and what might still be repaired. Without access to the implementation, do not claim the platform makes repair impossible or that a rebuild is cheaper.
- **More evidence is needed before deciding.** Inspection is blocked, coverage is low, the owner's needs are unclear, or the decisive platform/maintenance constraints are not verified. State the minimum information needed for a reliable choice.

When evidence supports considering a rebuild, provide requirements rather than a predetermined vendor recommendation: pages and visitor tasks, mobile and accessibility expectations, content/offer clarity, performance goals with measurement conditions, required integrations, migration/redirect needs, and acceptance tests. Do not recommend a rebuild solely because a site is old, uses a different platform, has an unfashionable design, or receives a low partial score.

Compare repair and rebuild honestly. Existing search visibility, content, URLs, integrations, accessibility, ongoing costs, and maintenance are relevant. Estimates depend on access and scope; no invented price comparisons or guaranteed launch dates.

## Actionable handoff

Give the owner a short brief they can use themselves or hand to a designer/developer:

1. Website URL and stated primary visitor action.
2. Top verified finding IDs and affected pages.
3. First fixes, owner roles, effort assumptions, and dependencies.
4. Proposed repair/redesign decision and its uncertainty.
5. Acceptance tests and the evidence needed for a re-audit.

The owner chooses whether and where to share the brief. Merely generating it does not submit an enquiry or notify SiteIn72. Do not ask for personal contact details to unlock it.
