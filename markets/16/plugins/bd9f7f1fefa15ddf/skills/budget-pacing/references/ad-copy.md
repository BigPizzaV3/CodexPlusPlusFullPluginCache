# Ad copy

## Responsive search ads

- Use the full character budget: headlines close to 30 characters, descriptions close to 90. Count programmatically; accented characters cause off-by-one errors.
- At least eight headlines with real differences in angle. Fifteen only if each adds a new angle; cosmetic variations add nothing.
- Write every headline so it works in any position. Do not pin to control the message; rewrite or remove the asset that creates a bad combination. Pin only text that must appear for legal or compliance reasons.
- Ad Strength is a guide to asset variety, not a performance metric. Do not pause or rewrite an ad only because of its Ad Strength. Judge ads on conversions and cost.
- Fill both display paths, matching the keyword theme of the ad group.

## Grounding

- Every claim in an ad must exist on the landing page: prices, numbers, guarantees, awards. Do not invent claims.
- Numbers about money (prices, salaries, savings) need their qualifier: "from", "up to", "average", or a range. A bare number reads as a promise.
- Use the final URL that the ad group's working ads already use. A new URL starts without history and can break tracking.

## Competitors

- Bidding on competitor keywords can be legitimate; check the account's policy.
- Do not use competitor brand names in ad text. It risks trademark complaints and disapprovals.

## Style

- Action verbs in the imperative: "Book", "Compare", "Start".
- Consistent capitalization across one ad: all title case or all sentence case.
- Dynamic keyword insertion only where every keyword in the ad group renders correctly (acronyms and long phrases often do not).

## Testing copy

- Changes to ads reset part of their learning. Batch them.
- Compare ads on conversions per impression over enough volume, not CTR alone: a higher CTR from a vague promise often lowers conversion rate.
