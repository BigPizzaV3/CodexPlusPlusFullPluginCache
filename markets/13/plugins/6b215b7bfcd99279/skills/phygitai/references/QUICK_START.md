# PhygitAi Public Screening — Quick start (3 × 2 mode cards)

A first-time invocation must give a **brief definition, what the user can provide, what they'll receive, and a visible menu of six modes**. Always display the modes in **six separate rows or supported clickable actions**, not joined together in a single text paragraph. Choose:

| Mode | Best for |
| --- | --- |
| **Audit** | One project (G0–G5 + IBR) |
| **Compare** | Two projects side by side |
| **Inspect** | Source claims and missing records |
| **Detect** | Documented disagreements / authority gaps |
| **Explain** | Meaning of an existing finding |
| **IBR** | One decision's seven-field trace |

**Start with 2–5 sentences** about the product and any AI contribution; formal documents are optional. The preliminary response will identify documented claims, missing evidence, unclear decision authority and suggested next steps.

**Example:** “Audit my AI-assisted reading lamp; I have no prototype observations yet.”


**PhygitAi helps you review design decisions involving AI.** It is intended for design students, educators, researchers and product teams working on AI-assisted concepts or adaptive physical–digital products.

## 1. What does it look for?

PhygitAi examines the evidence available for: human goals and assumptions; AI's reported design input; who may approve or override a decision; prototype or user-observed behavior; documented differences between intended and reported outcomes; and approved revisions or follow-up testing. It never pretends to inspect your physical product or see records that you have not provided.

## 2. What do I provide?

A few sentences about your project are enough to start. Optionally include:
- Who the product is for and what it is supposed to accomplish.
- How AI was used in ideation, design, or any product behavior.
- Who approved or can override its decisions.
- Relevant requirements, meeting notes, trial results, logs, or feedback.

If something is not available, say so. PhygitAi will label the evidence gap instead of inventing it. Only share information you are entitled to disclose.

## 3. What will I receive?

A preliminary qualitative assessment separating documented claims from uncertainty, gaps in decision authority, recorded discrepancies where supported, and practical next evidence to collect. A complete Audit also uses six research stages (G0–G5) and the seven-field Intention–Behavior Record (IBR). This is **not** independent testing, legal analysis, a safety inspection, certification, or empirical validation.

## 4. Choose a mode

- `PhygitAi audit` — review one project and its supporting evidence.
- `PhygitAi compare` — review two designs side by side without scores or rankings.
- `PhygitAi inspect` — examine claims, references and missing records.
- `PhygitAi detect` — identify documented gaps or disagreements, never live faults.
- `PhygitAi explain` — interpret an existing assessment finding in plain language.
- `PhygitAi ibr` — map one design decision across seven record fields.

**Can't decide?** Use `@PhygitAi` and describe your project in two or three sentences; ask it to suggest a suitable mode.

## Example starter

> Audit my adaptive reading lamp. Our team reports that AI suggested automatic brightness changes. We have a prototype description but do not yet have sensor logs, documented override rules or user-test records.

Expected response: what the supplied description supports, what is not verified, any missing approvals or tests, and concrete next evidence to gather—not a claim that the lamp passed or failed.

## Data-handling and public scope

No additional PhygitAi login is needed. This is a public screening skill and does not upload evidence to an independent PhygitAi server. The ChatGPT platform's data-handling terms apply. Confidential research master notes, unpublished decision rules and an independent assessment backend are not bundled with the public screening edition.
