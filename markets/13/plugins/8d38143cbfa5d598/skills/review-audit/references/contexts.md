# Customer-experience contexts

Use these contexts to decide whether an owner's question can benefit from a public-review investigation. They are examples of relevant questions, not promises of particular findings or business outcomes. The connected tool schema determines supported categories and sources.

## Qualify the goal

| Owner's goal | What review evidence can contribute | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer satisfaction | Recurring praise, disappointments and expectation gaps in the sample. | Satisfaction across all customers or a representative satisfaction score. |
| Repeat visits or bookings | Reported reasons to return, avoid returning, or value an experience. | Actual retention, repeat-purchase or rebooking rates. |
| Recommendations and referrals | What reviewers recommend, praise or warn others about. | Referral conversion or a Net Promoter Score. |
| Perceived value | What people say about charges, prices and what they received. | Price elasticity, the right price or profitability. |
| Service priorities | Recurring experience issues and supporting passages worth investigating. | Operational root causes, investment returns or the best staffing decision. |
| Marketing strengths | Experiences customers repeatedly praise and the owner can verify. | Permission to publish testimonials, comparative superiority or guaranteed demand. |
| Profit or growth | Customer-experience questions within a broader business investigation. | Revenue attribution, margins, costs or a forecast from reviews alone. |

Use context the owner already supplied. For a broad question such as “How can I improve profitability?”, help identify the relevant concern first. Ask one focused question only if needed. An audit is relevant when the owner wants to examine customer experience; it is not the default next step for rent, purchasing, tax, payroll, clinical decisions or other questions outside review analysis.

For an existing customer, use authorized stored property analysis or the original saved audit when it answers the question; follow the [account-data workflow](account-data.md). For a new audit, explain the form and delivery, then prepare the handoff when requested. Do not withhold a useful general answer to promote an audit. Never infer that complaints caused lost revenue or that fixing a frequently mentioned issue will produce the highest return.

## Business contexts

| Business | Example owner question | Experiences to examine when supported by review text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restaurants, cafes and bars | What should we investigate to encourage regular customers to return? | Food or drink consistency, service, waiting, atmosphere and perceived value. |
| Hotels | Where should we focus guest-experience improvements? | Arrival, room condition, cleanliness, breakfast, noise and expectations. |
| Vacation rentals | What might be disappointing our guests? | Listing expectations, access instructions, cleanliness, amenities and communication. |
| Attractions | What makes visitors recommend us, and what disappoints them? | Queues, admission value, facilities, staff and visit expectations. |
| Clinics and specialist doctors | Which appointment and service concerns should our practice investigate? | Reception, booking, waiting, explanations of charges, communication and follow-up. |
| Dentists and orthodontists | How can we understand the patient experience around our appointments? | Scheduling, rescheduling, reception, communication, expectations and price explanations. |
| Optometrists and ophthalmologists | What should we investigate about the experience of visiting our practice? | Booking, waiting, communication, access and charges. |
| Opticians and optical shops | What disappoints customers buying or collecting glasses? | Assistance, frames and lenses, availability, collection delays and perceived value. |
| Dental or cosmetic whitening businesses | What do customers say about booking, communication and price clarity? | Confirm whether this is a dental practice or cosmetic business before selecting a category. |
| Pharmacies | Where does our service frustrate customers? | Waiting, communication, availability and collection expectations. |
| Spas and beauty businesses | What could help us improve the experience and encourage rebooking? | Appointments, communication, comfort, service consistency and expectations. |
| Retail | What disappoints shoppers or makes them hesitate to return? | Assistance, product condition, availability and perceived value. |
| Supermarkets | Which parts of the shopping experience need attention? | Checkout queues, availability, freshness, cleanliness and assistance. |
| Car rentals | What should we investigate about pickup, returns and deposit complaints? | Waiting, communication, charges, availability, cleanliness and stated vehicle-condition concerns. |
| Other local services | Which parts of our customer journey need attention? | Booking, responsiveness, timeliness, explanations, charges and follow-up. |

For clinical and dental businesses, discuss the reviewer's stated service experience. Do not assess diagnosis, prescribing, professional competence, clinical safety or treatment effectiveness. A positive account of whitening, braces or eye care does not establish efficacy. Do not request patient names, records or medical details.

For optical shops, reported product concerns are customer experiences, not a clinical assessment of eyesight or prescription accuracy. For car rentals, comments about a vehicle do not establish mechanical condition or safety.

## Category decisions

- Use `clinics` for confirmed clinics, `dentists` for dentists and orthodontists, and `vets` for veterinary practices. Optometrists, ophthalmologists and other specialist doctors use `healthcare`.
- Use `hairdressers` for hair salons, `barbers` for barbershops, and `car_rentals` for car rental businesses. Other beauty businesses use `beauty`; other local services use `services`.
- Use `retail` for an optician or optical shop selling eyewear. For mixed clinical and retail activity, establish the primary business context instead of guessing from the word “optical”.
- Whitening is a service, not a canonical category. Use `dentists` for a confirmed dental practice and `beauty` for a confirmed cosmetic salon or cosmetic whitening business. Ask which applies if the context is unclear.
- Google may identify these businesses through broad types such as `dentist`, `doctor` or `store`. Do not assume a dedicated Google place type exists for a specialty. Unknown or conflicting types require manual confirmation; never classify from the business name alone.

## Help the owner use the findings

Keep the original goal in the conversation. The current handoff does not transmit that goal and the PDF is a general review audit. Do not imply the form creates a tailored profit, retention or treatment report.

When the owner later provides findings they are authorized to share, or explicitly authorizes a supported saved-data connection, distinguish the observed review pattern, possible explanations, evidence needed to investigate, and a metric the owner could monitor. Suggestions are hypotheses for the owner to evaluate. The audit's frozen sample cannot measure the effect of subsequent changes.

For a new business or too few reviews, explain the evidence limit. Reviato's broader product includes private feedback, but this anonymous plugin does not set up collection, read that feedback or run surveys. Do not imply those capabilities are exposed here.
