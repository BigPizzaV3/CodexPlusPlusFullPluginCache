GRADE-LEVEL COMPLETE READING PASSAGES — LQT PRACTICE DEFAULTS
Grade 3: 2 complete reading passages. Grade 4: 2. Grade 5: 3. Grades 6, 7, and 8: 3 each. Lit Comp II: 4. These are LQT practice-design defaults, NOT official Georgia passage-count mandates. The number of passages is determined by grade, NOT reduced when the teacher requests media. Every passage must be accessible in full within the learner task, coherent, substantial, factual, and readable at the specified grade. Different passages must contribute distinct useful evidence and perspectives; do not pad with near-identical passages. Maintain intellectual rigor while calibrating vocabulary, sentences, paragraph length, source structure, and overall conceptual load.

Cite verified fact-checking sources separately when used; never call an original passage a direct excerpt or reproduce invented publisher details. Do not supply only source links, teasers or summaries in place of a complete passage.

Design questions only around content actually furnished or reliably established, not video titles, excerpts, or inaccessible transcripts.

REQUIRED SECTION ORDER — TEST-LIKE LEARNER PRODUCT
1. SCENARIO: explicit heading required, BEFORE any texts. Give a believable school/community/workplace research situation, learner role, authentic audience, purpose and reason to examine the sources. Make the scenario specific to the selected topic and grade. Do not replace the scenario with a generic topic introduction.
2. RESEARCH QUESTION: a meaningful question that calls for comparing or weighing the actual source evidence.
3. SOURCES: numbered full-length, readable passages and any educator-requested additional multimodal resources. Give source labels and clear attributions; passage counts are LQT defaults, not official passage quotas.
4. INTERPRETING TEXTS: three questions drawing on supplied sources, mixing selected response and technology-enhanced-style interactions (or print equivalents). The 2025–2026 design also includes one additional field-test item, which should be noted as an optional practice simulation only, not a scored requirement.
5. CONSTRUCTING TEXTS: either EXTENDED WRITING TASK (Grades 3, 5, 8, Lit Comp II) with explicit role, audience, purpose, source-use directions and first-person Writer's Checklist; or PEER REVISION TASK (Grades 4, 6, 7) with credible learner draft and revision items totaling eight points.
Always print the distinct heading "SCENARIO" at the top and use the format faithfully for both workflows. Never skip it.

Multimodal format remains flexible: infographic, chart, table, diagram, map, image set, audio, video, data display, or another meaningful combination. Never lock a medium to a grade.

WORKFLOW A: GRADES 3, 5, 8, 10 — EXTENDED WRITING ICT
Use the complete passage counts specified above, plus any teacher-selected multimedia. Create three source-based interpreting-text questions before writing, with an appropriate mix of selected-response and technology-enhanced-style items. Grades 3 and 5 use OPINION; Grades 8 and 10 use ARGUMENT. Include Research Question, Purpose, Audience, and directions to use source evidence.
Grade 3: manageable length, concrete context, accessible vocabulary, paragraph/developed short response.
Grade 5: greater independence, comparison, elaboration, reasons and source evidence.
Grade 8: clear claim, multiple-source evidence, reasoning, organization, counterclaim.
Lit Comp II: greater nuance, synthesis, attribution, reasoning, and counterclaim sophistication than Grade 8.
Create a grade/genre-specific first-person Writer's Checklist around Purpose and Organization; Evidence and Elaboration; Language Usage and Conventions. Do not add a model response or answer key unless requested.

WORKFLOW B: GRADES 4, 6, 7 — PEER REVISION ICT
By default, create assessment-style peer revision. If the educator explicitly requests supplemental extended writing, create that instead and label it supplemental practice.

ASSESSMENT-STYLE PEER REVISION PRODUCT
1. Create the scenario and grade-appropriate source set.
2. Include source-based Interpreting Texts items that build topic/evidence understanding.
3. Provide a constructed-text draft written for the scenario. The learner revises this draft rather than composing the full original response.
4. Create selected-response and technology-enhanced-style peer-revision items totaling exactly 8 points.
5. Align revision decisions to Purpose and Organization; Evidence and Elaboration; Language Usage and Conventions, and to context, structure/style, techniques, research/analysis, and grammar/usage/mechanics.
6. Do not impose a fixed question count when guidance establishes points rather than a universal count.

NON-NEGOTIABLE: AUTHENTIC GRADE-LEVEL DRAFT CALIBRATION
For Grades 4, 6, and 7, the provided draft must read like credible work from a learner at that grade level. Do NOT intentionally make the draft simplistic, obviously bad, childish, or packed with errors merely to create easy revision questions. The draft should contain genuine strengths as well as purposeful opportunities for revision. Not every paragraph or sentence should need fixing. Revision items should require judgment about whether and how a credible draft can be improved.

Use realistic revision opportunities such as uneven elaboration, imprecise wording, a transition that could be stronger, evidence that needs better explanation, organization that can be improved, an underdeveloped opposing idea, a sentence-level clarity issue, or a limited number of authentic usage/convention errors. Avoid stacking exaggerated flaws such as empty filler, obviously illogical claims, insulting counterclaims, repeated fragments, or multiple elementary errors unless the selected grade and purpose genuinely justify them.

GRADE 4 DRAFT CALIBRATION
The draft should sound like a capable fourth-grade learner, not an adult and not an intentionally weak writer. It should have a recognizable introduction/opinion, logical grouping, reasons supported with source information, some elaboration, linking language, and an attempted conclusion. Sentences may be relatively direct and vocabulary accessible. Include a small number of meaningful revision opportunities in organization, reason/evidence development, elaboration, linking, conclusion, sentence clarity, or conventions while preserving substantial grade-level strengths.

GRADE 6 DRAFT CALIBRATION
The draft should sound like credible sixth-grade argumentative writing. It should have a recognizable introduction and claim, organized body paragraphs, relevant evidence from provided sources, attempts to explain how evidence supports reasons, transitions, an appropriate attempt to acknowledge or address an opposing position when the task calls for it, and a conclusion. It may show uneven elaboration, imprecise phrasing, a weak evidence-to-reason connection, an underdeveloped counterclaim, a transition needing improvement, or limited authentic convention errors. Do not make every paragraph defective. Avoid deliberately juvenile filler such as "this is a big thing," sweeping claims such as "this proves it always makes life better," or dismissive counterclaim language such as "that idea is silly" merely to manufacture revision targets.

GRADE 7 DRAFT CALIBRATION
The draft should show a noticeable step beyond Grade 6 in control and sophistication while remaining believable seventh-grade work. It should include a focused claim, purposeful organization, relevant evidence integrated from sources, developing reasoning/elaboration, transitions and cohesion, appropriate style/tone, meaningful treatment of a counterclaim, varied sentence structures, and a conclusion. Revision opportunities should be subtler and may involve precision, depth of reasoning, evidence integration, cohesion, tone, counterclaim treatment, syntax, or conventions. Do not simply reuse a Grade 6 draft with harder vocabulary.

PEER REVISION ITEM DESIGN
Build questions from the authentic revision opportunities intentionally embedded in the draft. Include some choices where the original wording is already effective or where a proposed revision would make the writing worse, so learners must exercise evaluative judgment rather than assume every highlighted passage is wrong. Distractors should be plausible at the selected grade. The best revision should improve the draft while preserving the learner's intended meaning and grade-appropriate voice.

SUPPLEMENTAL EXTENDED WRITING FOR 4, 6, 7
Only when explicitly requested: Grade 4 uses opinion supported with reasons/source information; Grades 6 and 7 use argument supported with source evidence and counterclaim. Include Research Question, Purpose, Audience, and grade-appropriate Writer's Checklist. Label as supplemental extended-writing practice, not the end-of-year Section 1 format.
