# Lenses and evidence

## Sonic (best-sounding)

Likely sound quality from mastering engineer credits, matrix and runout stampers (early stampers, engineer marks such as "RL", "KP", "MASTERED BY CAPITOL"), pressing plant, format (e.g. 45 RPM, 180 g), audiophile reissue labels and the community rating against the album's average. A heuristic, not a measurement: a sonic score never proves one copy sounds better, and condition matters for any used copy.

## Collector (most collectible)

Originality (judged against the earliest pressing in the set, not necessarily the album's first release), label and catalogue provenance, demand (how many Discogs users have and want it) and the rating. Not a price or a value forecast.

## Reading a result

- `verdict` words: "strong sonic pick" / "strong collector pick", "solid pick", "weak evidence". Weak evidence means little is known, not that the pressing is bad.
- `evidenceCoverage` says how much of the evidence was present; thin coverage deserves a caveat.
- Surveys report coverage: versions listed, versions with demand data, pressings checked in detail. Say "best among N checked".
- `catalogClaims` (when present) are a model's reading of the release notes, with a certainty that is model certainty. Quote the cited sentence; never present them as fact.
- Prefer concrete signals ("mastered by Kevin Gray; early stamper -1J") over the number.

## Original or reissue

- "Always buy the original" is too simple. The original is the reference when its mastering is known to be the best (Van Gelder Blue Notes, UK Beatles), when the object itself carries the history, or when the collector lens is what the user asked for.
- A careful reissue is the better listening copy when the original is scarce, when the reissue label is known for its chain and sources (see [labels-and-series.md](labels-and-series.md)), or when the reissue was cut from a better tape than the original: it happens more often than collectors expect.
- Be wary of reissues that do not say their source, "180 g audiophile" wording without a credible mastering credit, and low-end reissues from labels with no track record.
- Whatever the pick, cite the catalogue number and one distinguishing detail (country, year, runout, label variant) so the user can find that exact version.
