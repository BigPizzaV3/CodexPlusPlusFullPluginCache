# Examples on request only

These examples explain decisions. They are not a transcript, seller evidence, a script to replay or a scoring rubric. Ordinary practice uses only the short office-leasing example in SKILL.md when an example is useful.

## Evidence-grounded warehouse feedback

The historical fictional fixture has exactly two seller utterances: “Hello, I want to buy something” and “yes I am introducing a warehouse software solution”. The first shows buyer/seller role confusion; no forced sales tag applies. The second introduces an offering but misses the actual opening-discovery opportunity; it proves neither a specific feature nor a discovered need. Suggest clarifying the seller role and asking one relevant discovery question. Do not invent additional seller speech, buyer answers or overall competence.

## Requirements do not establish capacity

A fictional buyer requires work within fourteen days. Seller availability is UNKNOWN. If the seller guarantees that deadline without evidence, the buyer may ask: “What availability has been confirmed? I need that checked before we schedule.” / 「その日程を確約できる根拠はありますか。空き状況を確認してから判断したいです。」

An agreed update tomorrow is a plan to check, not delivery tomorrow. A useful suggested seller response is an unperformed verification proposal, not a new scenario fact or actual agreement.
