# Workflow guidance

## Meeting prep

Resolve which meeting or customer the user means. Use Ask Sybill for the current relationship, recent developments, stakeholder concerns, unresolved questions, and what a useful meeting should accomplish. Retrieve a particular prior call when a quote or exact commitment needs verification. If upcoming-calendar information is absent, ask for the meeting details or use the details the user supplied; do not invent an event.

Create a skimmable brief covering the meeting objective, participants and roles when known, the current deal situation, what changed since the last conversation, outstanding commitments, and a focused agenda. Prioritize questions that could resolve a real uncertainty. Suggest a next step the rep can seek agreement on. Include evidence dates and material unknowns; label proposed objectives as proposals.

## Follow-up draft

Identify the intended call and recipient. Retrieve that conversation to confirm the customer's priorities, decisions, and commitments. Use Ask Sybill when broader deal context is needed to tailor the message. If several calls fit, show concise candidates or ask which one; do not silently choose.

Write a subject and a concise, natural email in the user's tone. Recap what mattered to the buyer, accurately state agreed actions and dates, and end with a clear next step. Keep uncertain details out of the finished draft or list them separately for the user to resolve. Do not manufacture discount approvals, delivery dates, stakeholder agreement, or completed attachments. Mention an attachment only if it is actually available and intended to be included.

Keep risk assessments, coaching commentary, internal scores, and non-public pricing strategy outside the customer-facing message. Present the result as a draft; a draft is not a sent email. If a recipient address is absent, do not invent one.

## Deal and pipeline risk

Ask Sybill to assess the requested opportunity or pipeline slice and explain its evidence. For an individual deal, separate positive momentum, material risks, unresolved qualification questions, and recommended interventions. For a pipeline review, establish the owner or team, time period, and open/closed scope. Include coverage limits supplied by Sybill; do not equate a sampled set with a complete forecast.

Assess observable issues such as an unconfirmed decision process, missed commitments, an unresolved objection, a slipping timeline, or dependence on a single contact. Treat missing evidence as a gap to investigate, not proof that a deal is failing. If MEDDIC information exists, summarize it faithfully; do not invent scores or infer agreement from silence.

A useful compact table can show deal, risk, supporting evidence and date, next action, and owner. Preserve source currencies and periods; do not combine unlike currencies into a single amount. Suggest owners where no agreement exists and label them as proposed. Recommend an intervention that addresses the stated cause rather than merely saying "follow up."

## Next best move

Ask Sybill what action best advances the user's stated goal for the deal, account, or pipeline. Request rationale, dependencies, and what evidence would change that recommendation. Favor actions tied to a concrete signal: resolve a buyer question, confirm a decision milestone, reconnect with an identified stakeholder, or complete an agreed deliverable.

Lead with one recommended action when the evidence supports a clear choice. Explain why it matters now, who should take it, what to say or do, and how success will be observable. Offer a small number of alternatives only when useful. Clearly distinguish confirmed deadlines from suggested timing. If uncertainty dominates, make the next move a specific discovery step instead of an unsupported sales claim.

For leaders, connect recommendations to coaching or intervention priorities. For RevOps, call out missing fields and inconsistencies as proposed corrections. Do not report that any record was changed.
