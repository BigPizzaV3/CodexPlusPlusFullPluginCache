# Coordinate work toward the founder's mission

Read the mission target, label, due date and qualification from confirmed profile/brief context. Activation means first meaningful product use; the mission is the overall target. Neither a recommendation rationale nor a revision count is measured progress. When the mission is underspecified, identify the smallest operational-definition gap while continuing independent preparation.

## Pick a useful next unit

1. Honor the founder's requested action. Otherwise use the focused plan, started work and observed blockers to choose one next unit, avoiding additional live work beyond the server limits.
2. State the connection: “This deliverable tests [uncertainty] or removes [blocker], so [mission-qualified event] can occur.” Do not optimize for vanity counts unrelated to the mission.
3. Define a bounded artifact or observation: a qualified shortlist and interview kit, one exact outreach draft, actual listing copy, one repaired onboarding instruction, a testable event definition or a reproducible cohort calculation. Complete it rather than returning a generic checklist.
4. Discover capabilities only for this deliverable. Browser availability is execution capacity, not business eligibility. Do independent work while a prerequisite decision, identity question or access setup is pending.
5. Save authorized work with the live revision/schema protocol. State prepared versus actually executed/measured progress. Compare new evidence with success and stop criteria, then propose one next unit rather than expanding contact volume or launching another experiment automatically.

Use a matching installed specialist when available. Individual import of this coordinator remains usable without the specialists: follow the live execution brief, live field contract and the patterns below. Do not assume a detailed reference exists for every catalog strategy.

## Less specialized strategies

- **Positioning/prerequisites:** Compare a reachable audience/problem claim against observed and contradicting evidence. Produce a concrete audience/value draft or clarify the unresolved founder decision. Do not manufacture a confirmed problem from a profile.
- **Onboarding/repair:** Link a real obstacle/reproduction to a specific revised artifact or authorized change and acceptance evidence. An untested repair proposal remains a draft; source work that requires product changes uses the founder's normal approval and development process.
- **Local pilots:** Confirm service area, real venue/buyer fit, capacity, logistics, dates, permission and fees. Prepare one feasible visit, retailer trial, stall or pickup offer. Online profiles do not substitute for field evidence; inventory plans do not become fulfilled orders.
- **Economics:** Expose observed inputs versus assumptions, unit/period/currency, variable costs, support/labor and sensitivity. Calculate contribution conservatively, then recommend a bounded test or defer when fulfillment costs cannot be supported. A spreadsheet scenario is not revenue.
- **Partnership/events:** Produce a mutual-value proposal or session package with audience, responsibilities, terms, capacity and follow-up scope. Draft agreements and invitations do not imply consent, booked sessions or attendance.

## Review loop

Present the artifact, its mission relevance, observed progress and what remains uncertain. Recommend continuing, repairing or stopping within the existing effort/window. “Wrap up” alone yields a review summary; an attempt-ending result requires explicit recording intent and completed/failed verdict. Do not ask again for an already explicit verdict or bounded action approval. The founder makes unresolved strategic decisions; tools enforce eligibility and write permissions.
