# Answer-option quality

Make correctness depend on knowledge, not visual clues. Apply these rules to selection questions and any incorrect cloze segment alternatives.

- Keep `answers[].value` and segment answer values concise. Remove unnecessary explanation from choices rather than appending an answer-revealing back. Backs are optional and omitted by default; any visible context must not disclose a cloze answer or identify the correct choice. Retain qualifications needed for accuracy.
- Match grammatical form, semantic category, specificity, level of detail, tense, plurality, capitalisation, punctuation, units, and qualifiers. The correct option must not be the only complete sentence, carefully qualified phrase, or polished option.
- Actually compare option word counts and approximate character lengths before saving. For phrases/sentences, aim for the same word count (within one word) and about 80–120% of the correct option's character length. For names, numbers, and single terms, match category, notation, and precision rather than padding to a character target.
- Reject sets where a correct option is visibly the only long/detailed choice. Shorten it by removing unnecessary explanation, or give distractors equally specific, natural wrong content. Across the deck, correct options must not routinely be the longest or shortest. If natural, accurate balance is impossible, change the question or use true/false.
- Each distractor should represent a distinct plausible misconception, confused concept, or wrong relationship, and must remain unambiguously incorrect for the exact question. Never use an acceptable alternative, synonym, partially correct statement, or absurd choice as a distractor. Mark every applicable selection option correct.
- Do not pad with filler, empty suffixes, or absolutes such as “always” and “never” used only on wrong options. Never sacrifice accuracy for length matching.
- Review whether correctness is revealed by length, extra detail, grammar, formatting, or wording copied only into the correct option. Rewrite while preserving correctness and the misconception being tested. Never fall back to plain flashcards.

For “How does Athena help Odysseus?”, avoid a detailed correct sentence beside “Storms” and “Captivity”. Use parallel options such as “She offers divine guidance and protection”, “She sends fierce storms and shipwrecks”, and “She enforces prolonged captivity and isolation”. The wrong options target confusion with Poseidon and Calypso; omit the explanation from visible prompt text when it would reveal the correct option.

Keep true/false labels as “True” and “False”. Do not alter correct ordering positions or matching associations to make them look balanced.
