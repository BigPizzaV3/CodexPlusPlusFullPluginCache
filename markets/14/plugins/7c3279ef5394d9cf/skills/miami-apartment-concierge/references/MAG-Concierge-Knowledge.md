## Neighborhoods Don covers
Approximate 1BR rent ranges. Prices change constantly and vary by building, floor, view, and specials. Don confirms current pricing.

- Brickell: Miami's high-rise financial district. Walkable, lots of restaurants and nightlife, Metromover access. 1BR ~$2,800–$4,000+.
- Downtown Miami: Towers near Bayside and Kaseya Center, Metromover access. 1BR ~$2,400–$3,500.
- Edgewater: Newer towers on Biscayne Bay, quieter than Brickell, bay views. 1BR ~$2,600–$3,800.
- Midtown / Wynwood: Arts district with murals, breweries, and shops. Walkable. 1BR ~$2,500–$3,500.
- Coconut Grove: Green and bayfront, with a laid-back pace, parks, and marinas. 1BR ~$2,800–$4,000.
- Coral Gables: Tree-lined streets, upscale shopping and dining, near the University of Miami. 1BR ~$2,500–$3,600.
- Doral: West of the airport, newer communities, more space for the money. A car is needed. 1BR ~$2,200–$3,000.
- Little River: Newer mid-rise buildings, more budget-friendly. 1BR ~$1,900–$2,700.
- Fort Lauderdale: Beach access and a more relaxed pace, about 30–45 minutes north of Miami. 1BR ~$2,200–$3,200.

Studios usually run a few hundred dollars less than a 1BR. 2BRs often run $800–$1,500+ more than a 1BR in the same building.

Tight budget: Don has newer buildings with simpler amenities that work for people moving to Miami as affordably as possible.

## Move-in costs (typical; each building differs)
- Application fee: usually $50–$150 per adult.
- Admin fee: often $200–$500, one time.
- Security deposit: from a deposit alternative (usually a smaller non-refundable fee) up to one month's rent or more, depending on credit.
- Upfront: many buildings collect first month's rent plus deposit at signing.
- Monthly add-ons: parking, amenity, trash, pest, and internet/cable packages are common. Ask Don for the all-in monthly number.
- Pets: one-time pet fee often $300–$500, plus monthly pet rent around $25–$75 per pet. Many buildings have breed or weight limits.
- Service and assistance animals are not pets. Pet fees and pet rent generally don't apply to them; requests go through the building's accommodation process.
- Specials: some buildings offer 1–2 months free on longer leases. Don knows where.

## Typical requirements
- Income: most buildings want gross monthly income around 3x the rent.
- Credit: many buildings look for roughly 650+. Lower scores may mean a bigger deposit or a guarantor.
- Lower credit, new to the U.S., or self-employed: guarantors, larger deposits, or proof of savings can help. Don knows which buildings are more flexible.
- Common documents: photo ID, recent pay stubs or an offer letter, and bank statements.
- Lease length: 12 months is standard. Some buildings offer shorter leases at a higher rent.
- Each building sets its own criteria, and Don confirms them before you apply.
