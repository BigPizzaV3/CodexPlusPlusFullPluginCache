# EasyCarnet's published carnet facts

Everything here is what EasyCarnet publishes on www.easycarnet.com. State only these facts, in these terms. Prices and
dates are never in this file: use the `quote` and `check_trip` tools for them.

## What a carnet is

- A Carnet or ATA Carnet (pronounced kar-nay) is an international customs and temporary export-import document. It is
  used to clear customs without paying duties and import taxes on goods that will be re-exported within 12 months.
  Carnets are also known as Merchandise Passports.
- The carnet travels with the goods and is shown to customs at each border.
- The front green cover carries the account the carnet is issued to, which need not own the goods. This account is
  the Holder on the carnet: the name printed in Box A and shown to customs.
- The back green cover carries the General List of the goods. The list cannot be amended, but the goods may travel in
  partial shipments.
- The carnet holds certificate sets of counterfoils and vouchers. Counterfoils stay with the carnet; vouchers are
  removed by customs officials. Yellow sets register the goods that will return home, white sets are used by foreign
  customs for importation and re-exportation, and blue sets are for transit, when the goods travel by land through a
  country between the country of departure and the next country of entry.
- There are two kinds: ATA Carnets, for the 90+ countries that accept them, and TECRO/AIT Carnets (in Canada, CPD
  Canada/Taiwan Carnets), used only for temporary imports into Taiwan.
- Taiwan is on EasyCarnet's list of destinations, but it does not take the ATA Carnet. For a trip that starts in the
  United States, Taiwan requires a separate carnet called a TECRO/AIT carnet rather than the ATA Carnet, and these
  rules apply to it: Taiwan accepts carnets only for Commercial Samples and Professional Equipment: exhibitions and
  fairs may not enter on carnets. Goods may enter in partial shipments, but all items must be re-exported together (no
  split shipment). For anything else about Taiwan, the person should contact EasyCarnet for specific guidance.
- For a trip that starts in Canada, Taiwan requires a separate carnet called a CPD Canada/Taiwan carnet rather than
  the ATA Carnet. It covers commercial samples, exhibitions and fairs, and some professional equipment, is valid only
  between Canada and Taiwan for a single entry, and each package must weigh less than 30 kg gross. The rules for a
  trip that starts in the United States do not apply to it. For anything else about Taiwan, the person should contact
  EasyCarnet for specific guidance.
- In the United States, the United States Council for International Business (USCIB) is the sole National Guaranteeing
  Association and Issuing Association for ATA Carnets, and has appointed two independent Carnet Service Providers,
  boomerang carnets and Roanoke Insurance Group, to issue ATA and TECRO/AIT Carnets. In Canada, the guaranteeing
  association is the Canadian Chamber of Commerce.
- EasyCarnet is a Carnet Preparer: it prepares and submits the carnet paperwork on behalf of the account it is filed
  under (with a Power of Attorney). EasyCarnet is not a law firm and not a licensed customs broker.
- EasyCarnet prepares and submits carnet applications online for trips that start in the United States or Canada.

## Who uses one

Film and video crews, touring musicians, photographers, service providers and exhibitors, and any business moving
equipment abroad for a while: cameras, lighting, instruments and audio gear, specialised equipment such as marine
gear, industrial tools and medical devices, and technology, jewelry and fashion shown at trade shows.

## Which goods qualify

- Most merchandise can be listed on a carnet, in three categories: Commercial Samples, Professional Equipment (tools
  of the trade), and Goods for Fairs & Exhibitions (limited to 6 months).
- Examples EasyCarnet lists: aerospace equipment, agricultural equipment (non-food), aircraft, antiques, art,
  audio-visual and broadcast equipment, prototype and race cars, computers and laptops, construction equipment,
  defense and military items, diagnostic and medical devices, electronics, film and video equipment, jewelry,
  livestock, measurement and research equipment, musical instruments, photography equipment, robots, security
  equipment, sports gear, telecommunications equipment, theatrical props, trade show booths, unmanned vehicles and
  aircraft, and wearing apparel samples.

## Which goods do not

- Consumable items such as agricultural products (food, seeds, fertilizer, pesticides), explosives, disposables and
  postal traffic cannot travel under an ATA Carnet.
- A carnet only covers goods that will be re-exported. Goods intended for sale cannot be on a carnet: they must be
  formally imported. Materials that will be used up abroad cannot be on a carnet either; only the equipment and
  reusable tooling can.
- Samples qualify when they are for display and presentation only. Samples to be left with buyers or sold must be
  formally imported.
- EasyCarnet has published nothing about personal effects: the person should ask EasyCarnet's team.

## How long it lasts, and changes after issue

- A carnet is valid for up to 1 year from its issue date, except for exhibitions and fairs, where it is valid for 6
  months from the issue date. Some countries have exceptions to these periods, including Korea, Malaysia, Mexico and
  Singapore.
- A carnet can be used many times, in many countries, while it is valid, except that for a trip from the United
  States to Taiwan, the TECRO/AIT carnet is valid only between the United States and Taiwan, and for a trip from
  Canada to Taiwan, the CPD Canada/Taiwan carnet is valid only between Canada and Taiwan for a single entry. Split
  shipments are allowed, except that Mexico, Bahrain, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates and Vietnam do not allow them,
  and on a carnet issued in the United States, goods that enter Taiwan in partial shipments must all be re-exported
  together.
- Items cannot be added to the General List once the carnet is issued. For an item left off the list, or listed
  items that stay at home, the person should ask EasyCarnet's team.
- A carnet cannot be extended. In some cases foreign customs may allow a replacement carnet; it must be applied for
  before the original expires.
- Customs abroad can set an earlier date: pay close attention to the "final date for re-exportation" they write on the
  carnet.

## At the border

Leaving home:
1. Go to customs before checking in for the flight or crossing the border.
2. On the first use, the account's representative signs Box J on the front green cover, and home customs stamps and
   signs Box H (this activates the carnet).
3. Home customs stamps and signs the yellow Exportation Counterfoil. Note any split or partial shipment.

Entering a foreign country: foreign customs stamps and signs the white Importation Counterfoil; the representative
fills out and signs the white Importation Voucher; customs stamps, signs and removes it.

Leaving that country: the same with the white Re-Exportation Counterfoil and Voucher.

Coming home (very important): home customs stamps and signs the yellow Re-Importation Counterfoil.

- Go to customs on every entry and every exit, before checking in.
- Customs officers check the carnet against the actual goods, and discrepancies can lead to fines or seizure of items
  not properly documented.
- Goods shipped as cargo: send the carnet by courier to the person receiving the goods; never send it unattended with
  the freight.
- After the last use, the carnet must be returned for cancellation to its issuer, whose address is printed on the
  carnet. This is the final step that reduces liability.
- A Standard carnet carries enough documents for 2 round trips; a Pro carnet for 3.

## If goods are not stamped out

If goods enter a country and aren't stamped out on the carnet in time, the account the carnet is filed under
can be billed that country's duties, taxes, penalties and fees.

For a lost carnet or a missing stamp, the person should contact EasyCarnet straight away.

## The security bond

- Every U.S. and Canadian ATA Carnet must be financially secured. The usual security is a surety bond (a carnet bond)
  equal to 40% of the value of the General List. India requires 55%; road vehicles require 100% when exported by a
  business and 150% by an individual; any vehicle going to China requires 150%.
- The carnet bond is not insurance. It guarantees payment of the duties, taxes and penalties a foreign customs
  authority charges if the obligations of the carnet are not met. It pays that authority; the debt does not go away:
  the account the carnet is filed under reimburses the surety.
- EasyCarnet's fee includes the surety bond premium. The surety may ask for financial information, such as
  tax returns or financial statements.
- Because the bond scales with the value of the goods, values on the list must be accurate, not inflated.

## Standard and Pro

- Standard takes 5 business days of processing; Pro takes the next business day. Pro is required when the carnet is
  needed within five business days. A business day is a day a carnet can be issued, and the daily cutoff is 1pm
  ET. The `check_trip` tool gives each tier's pay-by date for a needed-by date, and says when only Pro, or
  neither tier, can make it; a date sooner than either is a reason to call EasyCarnet.
- The price depends on the tier and the value of the goods: use the `quote` tool.
- Pro also includes: documents for 3 round trips (Standard: 2), unlimited corrections before submission, a dedicated
  account rep and premium phone support. A U.S. Pro carnet includes replacement coverage if the carnet is lost or
  stolen; a Canadian Pro carnet includes an ACE Manifest, advance notice of the cargo to US customs.
- Every carnet is reviewed by an expert, who calls to confirm the equipment list and shipping address. EasyCarnet
  supports the carnet by call, text or email for its full 12 months.
- Payment is required before processing. The person can get a full refund if they choose not to proceed before they
  press "Finalize and Submit" on the EasyCarnet website; once they do, processing begins and the carnet and the surety
  bond premium are non-refundable.

## The account name

The account name is "Your full legal name if you are an individual, your registered company name otherwise. It is
printed on the carnet, and the Power of Attorney must be signed in that name." Filing for a client? Choose their
account, not yours.
