# Card reference

Fidelity to the cards comes first. Follow an explicit Enchanted Tarot meaning for the relevant orientation where one is listed below; otherwise follow the original meaning for that orientation. Preserve difficulty and consequences throughout the narrative arc. Advice must not replace interpretation, and feedback must not change emotional tone at the expense of fidelity. After interpreting a difficulty fully, follow the warning and practical-response guidance in reading-instructions.md, keeping the response grounded in the cards without promising prevention.

Each drawn card's readingMeaning governs that orientation: an explicit Enchanted Tarot interpretation where defined, otherwise the existing historical orientation entry. Raw historical entries, later theme labels, image-note interpretations and correspondences do not override it. The question changes relevance and emphasis, not the governing meaning. Do not select another tradition or reversal technique to make the narrative more reassuring, darker, or neatly resolved.

Rider-Waite-Smith foundation. Original Waite meanings below are public-domain historical source text. Later RWS theme labels are short identifying excerpts from Joan Bunning, with a per-card source. Translate archaic language carefully; preserve positive and negative meanings without turning historical stereotypes, diagnoses, or fatalistic assertions into facts about a user. Visual descriptions of this custom deck are intentionally absent.

## Five of Wands

ID: wands-05-five

Enchanted Tarot upright meaning (governs this orientation): Disagreement, rivalry, and competing efforts disrupt coordination. Friction and repeated hassles consume attention and can obstruct useful progress. Competition can also be lively or productive when the context supports it, but its divisions and costs must remain part of the interpretation.

Interpretive limits: Do not reduce this card to ambition or productive challenge without addressing the conflict. It does not by itself establish catastrophe, violence, or malicious intent. In an aims position, involvement in a contest does not mean the reader wants chaos.

Historical distinction: This deck wording elaborates the conflict and competition already present in Waite's upright entry; it does not treat the reference to riches as a promise of gain.

Sources: [Joan Bunning, Five of Wands](https://www.learntarot.com/w5.htm)

Later RWS themes (supplementary): DISAGREEMENT; COMPETITION; HASSLES. [Joan Bunning](https://www.learntarot.com/w5.htm)

Original upright meaning: Imitation, as, for example, sham fight, but also the strenuous competition and struggle of the search after riches and fortune. In this sense it connects with the battle of life. Hence some attributions say that it is a card of gold, gain, opulence.

Original reversed meaning: Litigation, disputes, trickery, contradiction.

[Waite source](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Pictorial_Key_to_the_Tarot/Part_3)

Supplementary Golden Dawn correspondence: Saturn in Leo; suit element: Fire. [Book T](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Library/Mathers/Book-T.html)

## Six of Swords

ID: swords-06-six

Enchanted Tarot upright meaning (governs this orientation): A difficult departure or transition carries sadness and the effects of what is being left. Distance, assistance, or a change of circumstances can support gradual recovery, but arrival does not erase the burdens carried through the passage.

Interpretive limits: Do not promise happiness, complete recovery, or a trouble-free journey. Read movement literally only when the question supports travel or relocation; an emotional or mental transition is also possible.

Historical distinction: Enchanted Tarot uses the later RWS transition and recovery interpretation as its governing meaning, rather than limiting the card to Waite's journey, route, or intermediary.

Sources: [Joan Bunning, Six of Swords](https://www.learntarot.com/s6.htm)

Enchanted Tarot reversed meaning (governs this orientation): A transition or departure is obstructed, resisted, or incomplete. Unresolved difficulties can keep someone caught in the past, draw them back into trouble, or follow them into changed circumstances. Moving away may fail to bring the expected relief.

Interpretive limits: The difficulty is the troubled passage or inability to move on. Do not substitute an assured recovery or a conversation that supposedly fixes it. Do not infer a literal capsizing, accident, or inevitable disaster from the reversed boat image, or assume the person alone caused the obstruction.

Historical distinction: Enchanted Tarot explicitly selects the later RWS obstructed-transition interpretation. Waite's declaration, confession, publicity, and proposal-of-love entry is retained as historical reference, not an alternative to choose during a reading.

Sources: [Labyrinthos, Six of Swords Reversal Meaning](https://labyrinthos.co/blogs/tarot-card-meanings-list/six-of-swords-meaning-tarot-card-meanings)

Later RWS themes (supplementary): THE BLUES; RECOVERY; TRAVEL. [Joan Bunning](https://www.learntarot.com/s6.htm)

Original upright meaning: Journey by water, route, way, envoy, commissionary, expedient.

Original reversed meaning: Declaration, confession, publicity; one account says that it is a proposal of love.

[Waite source](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Pictorial_Key_to_the_Tarot/Part_3)

Later RWS themes (supplementary): RESOURCES; KNOWLEDGE; POWER. [Joan Bunning](https://www.learntarot.com/p6.htm)

Original upright meaning: Presents, gifts, gratification; another account says attention, vigilance; now is the accepted time, present prosperity, etc.

Original reversed meaning: Desire, cupidity, envy, jealousy, illusion.

[Waite source](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Pictorial_Key_to_the_Tarot/Part_3)

Supplementary Golden Dawn correspondence: Moon in Taurus; suit element: Earth. [Book T](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Library/Mathers/Book-T.html)

## Seven of Pentacles

ID: pentacles-07-seven

Later RWS themes (supplementary): ASSESSMENT; REWARD; DIRECTION CHANGE. [Joan Bunning](https://www.learntarot.com/p7.htm)

Original upright meaning: These are exceedingly contradictory; in the main, it is a card of money, business, barter; but one reading gives altercation, quarrel—and another innocence, ingenuity, purgation.

Original reversed meaning: Cause for anxiety regarding money which it may be proposed to lend.

[Waite source](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Pictorial_Key_to_the_Tarot/Part_3)

Supplementary Golden Dawn correspondence: Saturn in Taurus; suit element: Earth. [Book T](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Library/Mathers/Book-T.html)

## Eight of Pentacles

ID: pentacles-08-eight

Later RWS themes (supplementary): DILIGENCE; KNOWLEDGE; DETAIL. [Joan Bunning](https://www.learntarot.com/p8.htm)

Original upright meaning: Work, employment, commission, craftsmanship, skill in craft and business, perhaps in the preparatory stage.

Original reversed meaning: Voided ambition, vanity, cupidity, exaction, usury. It may also signify the possession of skill, in the sense of the ingenious mind turned to cunning and intrigue.

[Waite source](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Pictorial_Key_to_the_Tarot/Part_3)

Supplementary Golden Dawn correspondence: Sun in Virgo; suit element: Earth. [Book T](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Library/Mathers/Book-T.html)

## Knight of Pentacles

## Documented card relationships

The following are paraphrased thematic relationships from Joan Bunning. They are not fixed event predictions. Their upright baseline must be reconsidered when a card is reversed. Relationships not listed here require synthesis from individual meanings, not invented claims of a canonical combination.

- The Fool + Four of Pentacles (opposing): Openness to an unfamiliar path meets an insistence on preserving what is already secure. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj00.htm)
- The Fool + The Hierophant (opposing): Spontaneous exploration contrasts with an established rule or accepted practice. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj00.htm)
- The Fool + The Star (reinforcing): Trust and receptivity are emphasized by both cards. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj00.htm)
- The Fool + Judgement (reinforcing): A beginning is reinforced by a theme of renewal. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj00.htm)
- The Magician + The High Priestess (opposing): Deliberate outward action contrasts with inward receptivity and waiting. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj01.htm)
- The Magician + Seven of Cups (opposing): Concentrated intention conflicts with attention dispersed among possibilities. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj01.htm)
- The Magician + Eight of Pentacles (reinforcing): Directed effort and sustained concentration support each other. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj01.htm)
- The Magician + Eight of Wands (reinforcing): Initiative combines with movement and timely action. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj01.htm)
- The High Priestess + The Hermit (reinforcing): Both emphasize turning inward to seek understanding. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj02.htm)
- The High Priestess + Four of Swords (reinforcing): Quiet contemplation is reinforced by a pause from activity. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj02.htm)
- Strength + The Chariot (opposing): Patient, compassionate influence contrasts with forceful direction and control. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj08.htm)
- Strength + Nine of Wands (reinforcing): Endurance and the ability to withstand difficulty are emphasized. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj08.htm)
- Death + The Tower (reinforcing): Both bring substantial change that interrupts an existing condition. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj13.htm)
- Death + Five of Cups (reinforcing): An ending is accompanied by the experience of loss and farewell. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj13.htm)
- Death + Eight of Cups (reinforcing): Closure and the movement away from an existing situation are emphasized. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj13.htm)
- The Devil + Eight of Swords (reinforcing): Restriction and a diminished sense of freedom reinforce one another. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj15.htm)
- The Devil + Nine of Swords (reinforcing): A bleak outlook is intensified by anguish and despair. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj15.htm)
- The Devil + The Star (opposing): Hopelessness and constriction contrast with renewed faith. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj15.htm)
- The Tower + Temperance (opposing): Abrupt disruption conflicts with measured integration and containment. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj16.htm)
- The Tower + The Sun (reinforcing): Both can bring an illuminating realization, even when the experience differs sharply. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj16.htm)
- The Tower + Five of Pentacles (reinforcing): Disruption is accompanied by material hardship or insecurity. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj16.htm)
- The Star + Nine of Swords (opposing): Hope and serenity contrast with distress and guilt. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj17.htm)
- The Star + Ten of Cups (reinforcing): Positive feeling and a sense of blessing reinforce hope. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj17.htm)
- The Sun + The Moon (opposing): Clarity and confidence contrast with ambiguity and disorientation. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj19.htm)
- The Sun + The World (reinforcing): Accomplishment and recognition support a sense of fulfillment. [Source](https://www.learntarot.com/maj19.htm)
