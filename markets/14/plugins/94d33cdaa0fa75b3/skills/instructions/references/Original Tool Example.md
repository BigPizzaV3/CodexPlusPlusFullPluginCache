# Original Tool Example

This reference preserves the structure and style of the original "When Will I EVER Use This?" GPT.

## Example input
Grade level: 7th grade
Content: Knowing the parts of a plant cell and understanding how they function.

## Original output pattern
The tool first provided five real-world scenarios where plant-cell knowledge is useful:
1. Gardening or farming — understanding why plants need sunlight, water, and nutrients and what happens when they lack them.
2. Making or eating food — connecting fruits, vegetables, and plant-based foods to plant cells and processes such as chloroplast function.
3. Environmental protection — understanding how scientists study plant responses to pollution, climate change, drought, and other environmental conditions.
4. Animal care — connecting healthy plants to the diets of plant-eating pets and animals.
5. Medicine or skincare — connecting plant structures and substances to products developed from plants.

It then provided five classroom experiences designed to make the learning feel useful:
1. Plant Cell Chef Challenge — create a recipe-style explanation of what cell parts contribute to a healthy plant.
2. Grow Your Own Plant Lab — grow and observe a simple plant while connecting visible changes to cell structures and functions.
3. Cell City Analogy Project — model cell-part functions through an understandable system analogy.
4. Plant Cell in the News — connect current real-world plant issues to what is happening at the cellular level.
5. Microscope Detectives — observe, draw, label, and interpret real plant cells when microscopes are available.

The tool then asked the educator to choose one of the five classroom activities for full development.

## What to preserve
- Five real-world connections followed by five classroom applications.
- Accessible, learner-friendly explanations.
- Concrete relevance rather than abstract claims that the content is "important."
- An invitation to select one activity for further development.
