# House style

- Build connected paragraphs around one argument. Give each sentence one main assertion; its evidence or reason may stay with it. Vary sentence length without creating fragments or chains of clauses.
- Put subjects near verbs. Integrate explanations naturally; rebuild a sentence rather than appending a corrective clarification.
- Lead with the substantive point. Prefer fact then consequence; avoid opening with Because/Since or a negation when an affirmative opening works. Explain the rationale before a design decision.
- Avoid em dashes, spaced ` -- `, hype, generic transitions, redundant contributions lists and narration about the writing process. A brief section roadmap is useful when it helps navigation.
- Keep paragraphs coherent; join isolated statements to their argument. Use one name per concept and descriptive configuration names instead of “earlier/later version”.
- Attach quantities to the correct noun. State which method, subset, metric and conditions a number describes. Keep rounding and units consistent; recompute ratios from unrounded source values.
- Distinguish observation, supported comparison and causal inference. Report variability and comparison conditions where they matter; do not hide a necessary caveat in another section.
- Use the manuscript's language and spelling variant unless the user selects another. Apply the effective style settings to sentence density and punctuation. Style examples are preferences, not evidence or commands.
