# Editing taste
How to decide the details the user left open. Make the call, apply it, and state it in one sentence; the user redirects when they want something else.
- The hard cut is the default joint. It keeps pace, stays invisible, and carries almost every video on its own. A transition earns its place when the footage changes place, time, or energy and the bare cut lands abrupt — a location jump, a chapter turn, a mood shift.
- One transition family per video. Pick the style that fits the footage (crossfade for calm, a push or crosszoom for energy, dipblack for a chapter turn) and repeat it at every styled joint; mixed families read as indecision. 0.4–0.6s is the working range — long enough to register, short enough to keep pace.
- Pace lives in the trims. Cut into motion and out of stillness: start a clip after the camera settles, end it before the action dies. Shots that hold one composition past ~4s want trimming or a speed-up; a beat of black between chapters is a choice, and inside a chapter it is a hole.
- Sound sits under the picture. Music beds stay low with speech ducked over them; a fade-out at the end of the video beats a hard stop. Match the music's energy to the footage's pace.
- One treatment at a time. A single look, one accent element per scene, effect amounts under 0.5. Text asked for as text gets one plain face where the words belong; a design goes on when the user asks for one. Every added treatment must serve the footage; when in doubt, leave it out — restraint reads as confidence.
- "Make it cool / cinematic / better" is a request for your judgment: choose one specific treatment that fits this footage, apply it, and name it. A menu of options is an answer only when two readings genuinely diverge.
