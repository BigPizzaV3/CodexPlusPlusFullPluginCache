# Talking-head videos
A raw recording of someone speaking to camera, made into the video the person asked for — any length, any frame. The ask sets the scope: "tighten my takes" is steps 1–3, "add captions" is the caption step, "make this into a finished video" is everything below. Each step is how that part is done well; take the steps the ask needs, in this order, and make every judgement call inside them yourself (which take, which layout, which graphic), naming the calls in a line or two when you finish. Work in the project's frame; every position below is a fraction of it, read against the layout on screen.

**1. Read the recording.** A separate clean recording of the voice (a mic or recorder file beside the camera file) is bound to the camera video with sync_audio before anything else: every clip of that video then plays the clean sound, and every cut, measurement and transcript reads it. subtitles_generate gives the transcript with word timings. watch_video the recording end to end, noting each take with note_source: what it says, and where the speaker's eyes are. A long recording is swept coarse first and watched closely where the takes are (watching-and-cutting skill).

**2. Keep the last good take.** People re-record a line until it lands, so the transcript repeats itself. Group the cues into lines; where a line was said more than once, keep the last complete take, and reach back to an earlier one only when the last has a defect in the words — a stutter, a clipped or wrong word, a restart mid-sentence. Cut the restarts, self-corrections, the "okay"s between takes and the muttered asides; find_filler catches the ums and stutters.

**3. Cut tight.** split_at and delete_item the waves, close the gaps, then refine_speech_cuts on every recut clip — pace fast for a short or a punchy piece, natural for a longer one that should breathe. Pauses longer than the pace's beat come down to it; short natural pauses stay. Every picture cut is a hard cut, and each joint gets a J-cut in the sound: set_split_edit with lead 0.05 on the incoming clip, so its first sound arrives a breath before its picture. listen_audio across every joint: each first and last word arrives whole, and no joint leaves dead air — a bad joint is fixed by cutting earlier or leading more, never by adding silence. The speaker's own footage holds still — no zooms, punch-ins or moves on it; the layout changes carry the variety.

**4. Camera on eye contact.** The camera shows while the speaker looks into the lens. Where they look away mid-line (reading notes, a glance off), the camera leaves at the last on-lens frame — split at the nearest word boundary before the turn — and a full visual covers the rest. A take read off to the side the whole way through is a full visual. Re-watch around each head turn at interval_seconds 0.5 to find the frame.

**5. Three layouts, planned first.** Write the plan as runs tiling the whole cut before building any graphic.
- Camera: the speaker filling the frame — set_framing fill, panned to hold the face.
- Split: the speaker in a rounded card with their head rising out of its top edge, the graphic filling the rest of the frame behind; the card's side follows the frame's shape unless the layout wants another. The speaker's cut stays on track 0 with its sound; over the run, the graphic (an image or a screen recording) goes on track 1, and the run's footage again on track 2 — add_overlay_video of the same source, then update_overlay_video to the run's start and trim, muted — with set_camera_card on that copy. Titles, shapes and stickers draw over every video row, so on a split they stay in the part of the frame the card and the head leave free.
- Visual: a graphic over the whole frame — an image or recording on a track above the speaker, or element cards over a full-frame shape.
The opening line (the hook) is a split. The call to action and everything after it is camera, as is a line said straight to the viewer — an opinion, a punchline. Screen recordings, web pages and product walk-throughs are visuals. A single screenshot, a logo, a number or a short list is a split. A run holds one idea and two neighbouring runs never share a layout; all three appear. Pace the runs to the piece: in a short no run lasts much over ~8s, and a longer video lets a run hold its idea as long as it takes.

**6. Graphics.** One look across the video: one background, one face, one accent color, held from the first graphic to the last. Each graphic has one dominant subject and at most one supporting cue. Show the thing being talked about — the person's own screenshots and screen recordings first (from media), then stock_search, or generate_image for an illustration or a clean card. The captions carry the words, so a graphic holds no labels restating the narration; a headline at most.
- Facts: check_facts every figure, date and release the narration states before it goes on screen. Something that happened shows through the person's own screenshot of its source; a source they gave no screenshot of is named in the closing message with the page the check found. A figure that disagrees with its sources is reported there too.
- Motion: lay a visual's cards out as one world (titles, shapes, stickers grouped with group_items, then placed across and beyond the frame), and move a camera over it with set_group_camera — a glide (sine.inOut, zoom change ≤ 0.04), a step to the next card (0.5s power3.inOut, starting 0.25s before the word that names it), a push onto the card being talked about (1.2–2s power2.inOut, up to ~1.6×), a pull back to show the whole, and at most one whip per run (out over 0.45s power3.in, in over 0.6s power3.out). motion_blur on the camera streaks its moves and leaves every frame at rest sharp. Each run's first frame is the camera at rest.
- Focus: as the camera pushes, the card being read stays sharp and its neighbours take set_overlay_keyframes keys of blur ~14 and opacity ~0.7; a word the viewer has to read is never blurred.
- A graphic's content keeps clear of the caption band and, on a split, of the card and the head.

**7. Cover, captions, call to action.**
- Cover: a short opens on its title — add_title on the first frame only (start 0, end 0.04), bold and large, dark on a light rounded add_shape box in the upper part of the frame — written from the hook when the person gave none. A longer video skips it unless asked.
- Captions: when the cut is final, subtitles_generate again so the captions come from the finished speech. set_caption_look style punchy, a few words per caption (words_per_cue 2 in a short, more in a longer piece), white with a dark outline. Position follows the layout, each caption placed with update_cue x/y inside project.safeZones: on a split, in the free band between the graphic's content and the card; on camera, the lower band under the face; on a visual, the band its content leaves free; on the outro, clear of the call to action. A caption never covers the face, the card edge or a visual's content.
- Call to action: on the outro, a comment box in the lower part of the frame — a white rounded add_shape, the keyword from the call-to-action line as an add_title typed in with a typewriter entrance and a blinking caret (set_overlay_animation caret), then a small Post button (shape and title, grouped) pressed with hit_style press once the keyword is typed. Nothing else on it.

**8. Sound.** set_clip_sound preset studio on the voice. Sound effects come from stock_search kind sound, one per meaningful visible event: a soft click on a button press or tap (the Post press included), a light keyboard tick on typing, a soft pop when a card or device lands, a chime on an answer or confirmation, and a low whoosh only on a camera whip, its loudest moment on the whip's midpoint (at most one per run, varied). Layout cuts, glides, steps and pushes get no sound. A riser of ~2–3s ends exactly on the join where the hook hands to the first point. Music is generate_music instrumental — modern, minimal, warm, 100–120 BPM, no lead melody, length "song" for a video over a minute — trimmed to the cut with a 0.8s fade-out at the end and one flat level throughout (no duck). measure_level sets every level from numbers: the music's integratedLufs 10 LU under the voice's; clicks, pops and chimes peaking ~10 dB under the voice's truePeakDbtp, whooshes ~9, ticks ~16, the riser ~14.

**9. Check before handing it over.** capture_frame both sides of every joint (the new take shows from its first frame), the first frame of every split (the head is there), and a frame in each run (captions clear of the face and the graphic, content inside its zone, a short's first frame a clean cover). listen_audio every joint and every effect: each lands on its visual event, the riser ends on the hook's join. measure_level with mix true confirms the whole video. Fix what fails and check again.
