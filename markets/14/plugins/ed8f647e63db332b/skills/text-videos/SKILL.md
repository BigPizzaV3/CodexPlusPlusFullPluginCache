# Text-driven videos: lyrics, kinetic type, quote cards
A video whose picture is words. Three pieces: the frame behind them (set_background, or the user's footage), the words themselves, and the clock that says when each line lands. For the device list — fifty ways to make a run of text worth watching — read the text-creativity skill.

**Design is something the user asks for.** "Add the text", "put what he says on screen", "add text from the audio" is a plain job: get the words and their times — the cues already in editor_state, a source's transcript, listen_audio, or subtitles_generate when the user wants a caption track — then place them with add_text_sequence and no `look`. That lays one readable face at one spot with a short fade, no cards, no moves, no tilts, and no repaint of the frame. Then align_to_audio: the times you placed came from a transcript, and that pass measures them against the mix's own waveform and moves each edge onto the speech. Reach for a look, cards, moves, or a variation only when the ask carries a design in it: a lyric video, kinetic type, a named style or vibe, "make it fun", a reference to match. A cut that comes back restyled when the user asked for words on screen is the failure this default exists to prevent.

**Two ways to carry the words.** A caption track is one styled row of text that follows a voice — it holds word timings, and one call restyles every line. Elements are separate titles and cards on the timeline — per-line face, per-line placement, per-glyph motion, a color card behind each line — which is what kinetic typography and lyric cards are made of. Captions for a song you want to sing along to; elements when each line is its own picture. Both burn into the export, and both play their words as they are said: word_highlight on the caption track, the words slot of set_overlay_animation on a title. Two families to pick from — an emphasis travels along a line that is fully up (the word lights, swells, or takes a card), and a build assembles the line as it is spoken (words appearing one by one, or a faint line going solid word by word).

**The looks.** A look is opt-in, and it is not one line's styling — it is the design of a whole run. It sets the frame color, the cards it cycles, the type, the entrance, the caption equivalent, AND the ensemble the run walks across its length: the faces it rotates through, the entrances, where successive lines land, and what each line does while it holds. So one add_text_sequence call already produces a composed, varied run:
- plain (Plain): One clean sans line low in the frame, white with a shadow, a short fade in and out. The look for text the user asked for without asking for a design — same face, same place, same entrance on every line, no cards, and the frame color untouched. Frame untouched, no cards; sf 72px; fade in; none by default; captions clean.
- lyric-card (Lyric cards): One line at a time on a full-frame color card, the color changing line to line. The loud, readable default for lyrics and punchy talking-head text. Frame #111114, cards #111114/#FFFFFF/#FF5500; archivo-black 104px; pop in; bold by default; captions punchy + karaoke.
- karaoke-glow (Karaoke glow): The whole line stays up and each word lights as it is sung. The look for singing you can follow — needs word timings, so it rides a caption track. Frame #0B0B12, no cards; montserrat 84px; fade in; bold by default; captions highlight + karaoke.
- neon-club (Neon club): Near-black frame, tall condensed caps, an electric accent. Dance, hip-hop, anything with a night to it. Frame #07070C, no cards; bebas 132px; streak in; bold by default; captions neon + karaoke.
- marker-note (Marker note): Handwriting on paper, ink arriving as it is written. Personal, confessional, singer-songwriter, or a note-to-camera. Frame #F4F1E8, cards #F4F1E8; caveat 118px; typewriter in; bold by default; captions typewriter.
- serif-mood (Serif mood): A wide-set serif holding still on black, fading between lines. Ballads, poetry, closing credits, anything slow. Frame #000000, no cards; dm-serif 76px; fade in; bold by default; captions minimal.
- type-out (Type out): Mono on a dark screen, each line typed in. Explainers, code, anything that should read like a terminal. Frame #101317, no cards; mono 62px; typewriter in; bold by default; captions typewriter.
- over-footage (Over footage): No cards at all — heavy outlined white type over whatever is playing. The look for words on the user's own video. Frame untouched, no cards; anton 96px; rise in; bold by default; captions hook + karaoke.
- kinetic-scatter (Kinetic scatter): Letters fly in and settle, the card color shifting under them. Busy, energetic, made to be watched twice. Frame #1B1036, cards #1B1036/#2563EB/#DB2777; poppins 98px; scatter in; bold by default; captions bubble + karaoke.
- ransom-note (Ransom note): Every phrase in a different face, tilted, scattered across the frame. Chaotic, hand-cut, pop-punk — the look when the ask is "make it wild". Frame #F5F2EA, no cards; archivo-black 110px; pop in; bold by default; captions punchy + karaoke.
- stacked-caps (Stacked caps): Huge condensed caps climbing the frame, one phrase per rung. Rap verses, manifestos, title sequences that want weight. Frame #0A0A0A, no cards; anton 150px; rise in; bold by default; captions block + karaoke.
- pastel-pop (Pastel pop): Soft candy cards and a round friendly face, everything bouncing. Vlogs, cozy songs, anything sweet. Frame #FFF1F2, cards #FFE4E6/#DBEAFE/#FEF9C3/#DCFCE7; poppins 96px; bounce in; bold by default; captions bubble + karaoke.

**Composing a run (this is the part that decides whether it reads as creative).** add_text_sequence takes the whole run in one call and walks the look's rotas by index, so no two neighbouring lines land the same. Steer it:
- `variation`: bold (default) walks faces, tilts, palette and moves; subtle keeps one face and varies placement and entrance; none lays every line identical — only for a run you are about to hand-compose yourself.
- `layout` overrides where lines land: center — Every line on the look's anchor. Still, formal, karaoke. stack — Lines step up and down the middle column, so a phrase builds vertically. alternate — Lines swing left and right of center — call and response. corners — Lines walk the four corners of the safe area. Busy, poster-like. drift — A slow diagonal walk across the frame and back. Ballads, long holds. scatter — A fixed scatter around the frame that never lands twice in a row. Chaotic energy. ladder — Each line one rung lower than the last, then back to the top. Lists, countdowns, verses.
- `emphasis` per line: hero is markedly bigger and takes the look's hero move, whisper is small and quiet. Mark the hook, the title line, the punchline. Two or three heroes in a verse; a run where everything is a hero is a run where nothing is.
- `section` per line ("verse", "chorus", "bridge"): a new label restarts the type and color rotas, so a chorus does not look like the verse before it. Label every line of a song — it is the cheapest way to make three passages read as three passages.
- `move` per line: what it does WHILE it holds, separate from the entrance. The rota gives each line one already; name one when a specific line wants a specific move.
- Anything you set per line — font, x, y, rotation, in_style, color, size — wins over the ensemble.
Never lay a run at variation "none" and then repair it with a hundred update_overlay calls. Compose it in the call.

**A lyric video, from an audio track and the words.** The user has the song and the lyrics.
1. The song is in the cut (a soundtrack clip). `inspect_project` confirms it and gives the duration.
2. sync_lyrics with the lyrics text, one line per screen. It transcribes the cut, aligns the user's words to what it recognized, and writes the user's text — verbatim — onto a caption track with per-word times. Singing defeats recognizers; that is why the wording comes from the user and only the clock comes from the transcript.
3. Dress it: set_caption_look with a look id (karaoke-glow for words lighting up, neon-club, serif-mood…), or accent_mode on its own to pick what each sung word does — pop swells it, box puts a card behind it, build has the line appear a word at a time, fill leaves it faintly up and solidifies each word as it lands — or add_text_sequence from_captions to turn the same timed lines into full-frame cards. Going the element route, re-send the lines yourself with emphasis and section on them rather than from_captions — from_captions carries the timing but knows nothing about which line is the hook.
4. Check it: sync_lyrics already snaps its lines to the mix, and align_to_audio re-runs that pass any time (on the caption track, or on the elements of a run with target elements). Then seek + capture_frame at three or four lines that landed on DIFFERENT rota steps, and listen_audio across one of them. A single line still sitting early or late retimes with update_cue.
With no lyrics text, transcribe with subtitles_generate and work from the cues it wrote.

**Words over the user's footage.** Same flow, with the over-footage look (or any look with no cards) and `background: false` — the clips keep playing and the words ride above them. Aim them off the caption band when captions are also on, and prefer layouts that stay out of the middle of the shot.

**Words on plain color.** A cut with nothing on track 0 is a complete video: set the background, place the run, export. Cards are how the color changes line to line — the sequence paints one per line on its own row behind the words, so they stack instead of sliding apart.

**When the words are off the audio.** "The subtitles are late", "sync the text to the audio", "it drifts by the end" — that is align_to_audio, and it is the only honest answer to it. It renders the cut's audible mix, reads a 10ms energy envelope to find where speech really starts and stops, and moves each caption or element edge onto the speech edge it belongs to; an edge with nothing audible near it keeps its time. Re-transcribing instead throws away the user's own wording, and retiming cue by cue with update_cue is guessing at what the waveform already knows — reach for update_cue for the one line the pass could not place. The pass reports `evidence: false` when the mix gave it nothing to read (a bed of music with no quiet in it, digital silence, or speech that does not line up with these times at all); say that plainly rather than reporting a fix, and check by ear with listen_audio.

**Timing.** One line per screen beats two. A line needs about 0.4s minimum to read; 1.5–2.5s is the normal beat for a spoken line, and a sung line takes whatever the vocal takes. Lines never overlap on a row — the sequence tool lays them end to end from the times you give it, each exactly where you put it. A label per shot of a fast cut can be as short as its shot (one frame and up); under 0.2s it cuts in and out with no entrance.

**Fixing a run.** update_overlay retimes or recolors any line, set_overlay_animation swaps its entrance, set_overlay_keyframes gives it a path. Reach for those to fix a handful of lines. When the whole run is wrong, delete it and re-compose with different look, layout, or variation — that is one call against a hundred.
