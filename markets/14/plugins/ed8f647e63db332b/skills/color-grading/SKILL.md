# Color grading
Grade like a colorist: read the image, correct it, then style it — in that order.

**Read before you write, look after.** Call read_color_stats on the clip before grading (never grade blind): by default it reads the base rendering — the footage through its source conversion, before any LUT or grade. After a move, read it again with graded:true to confirm the numbers went where you meant, and capture_frame to look at the result — faces, whites, and the frame as a whole — before calling the grade done. The quantiles tell you where the tones sit: p2 well above 0 = lifted/milky blacks, p98 well below 255 = no real whites (flat), a p50 far from ~118 = exposure off. warmth > 1 reads warm, < 1 cool; meanSat under ~0.15 reads muted, over ~0.35 vivid. The clip's current color state is in editor_state (colorPreset, grade, lut, curves/wheels/hslBands); each asset's sourceColor says what its code values mean.

**Source color first.** Log and HDR footage (apple-log = Apple Log, apple-log-2 = Apple Log 2, hlg = HLG, pq = PQ) is detected from the file. Log footage is graded in scene light and then rendered for the display, so exposure moves real stops and bright areas roll off smoothly; HDR footage is converted to display values first. When a clip reads flat and grey and its sourceColor is rec709, the header did not say — set_source_color with the right profile fixes the whole clip in one move. A camera maker's Log-to-Rec.709 LUT is the other route: set_source_color rec709 on that asset, then set_color_lut, so the picture is converted once.

**HDR delivery.** The project's color space (editor_state project.colorSpace; set_project_color: sdr, hlg, pq) decides what the clips convert to. In an HDR project log and HDR footage keeps its range, SDR footage and every title or sticker sits at reference white (BT.2408, 203 nits), and the grade works in the HDR container; the export is 10-bit HEVC Main 10 or ProRes tagged Rec.2020 HLG or PQ. "Export in HDR" or "make this HLG/PQ" is set_project_color; the export then carries it.

**Correct next.** Balance and tone before any look: auto (set_color_grade auto:true) is a computed starting point — exposure to middle gray, contrast stretch, gray-world white balance — then nudge. Neutralize a cast with temperature/tint before styling warmth on top, or the style fights the cast. Every value 0 is neutral and fields patch, so small moves are cheap; centred sliders run ±50, fade/sharpen/clarity 0..50.

**One model, layered.** A clip's color is one pipeline: the source conversion, then its LUT (scaled by amount), then the manual adjustments that balance the shot, then the preset's look on top. On log footage the adjustments and preset run in scene light and the LUT on the rendered picture. set_color_preset never touches the manual layer and vice versa; intensity (amount) scales only the preset. Preview, thumbnails, and export render the same numbers.

**Which tool for which job.**
- set_color_lut — a film look from a LUT: the built-in ones (Portrait (warm, glowing skin, a touch brighter; flattering on people); Natural (gently warm, true-to-life color, clean whites); Airy (bright and light, lifted midtones, warm with a little more color); Vivid (saturated, warm slide-film color with strong contrast); Heritage (rich reds, restrained greens, crisp whites; classic slide film); Crisp (neutral color, deeper midtones, clean whites); Pop (saturated and dense, deep reds, darker midtones); Street (warm, punchy everyday film color, bright whites); Summer (hot reds and magentas, saturated, darker midtones); Cross Process (strong cyan-blue cast, saturated; surreal); Snapshot (faded instant film: lifted blacks, muted, cool-green shadows); Warm Fade (warm, lifted blacks, bright highlights; sunny faded film); Nostalgic (muted and softly faded, slightly cooler; old prints); Dusty (dark, dense amber-brown, muted greens); Press (black and white, even tones, bright whites); Documentary (black and white, dark and contrasty); Infrared (black and white, very bright, glowing highlights and foliage); Retro Mono (black and white, hard contrast, deep blacks)) and the user's .cube/.3dl files, all in editor_state.luts; amount 0..1 blends it. "none" removes it. Pick by what the look does to the picture, and start around 0.7 on footage that is already graded or strongly colored.
- set_color_preset — a fast, coherent base. Catalog: Cinematic: teal-orange (Teal & Orange: warm skin over teal shadows, extra saturation; blockbuster), bleach-bypass (Bleach Bypass: desaturated and silvery, crushed blacks; gritty), matinee (Matinee: very warm, rich saturated color; classic Hollywood), thriller (Thriller: cool and muted, darker, crushed blacks; tense), night-market (Night Market: warm and saturated, slightly dim; night streets), sci-fi (Sci-Fi: cooler and slightly muted, clean blacks; clinical), western (Western: warm golden and dusty, lifted blacks), indie (Indie: faded and muted, lifted blacks, soft contrast); Film: 16mm (16mm: slightly muted, lifted cool shadows; handheld film), super-8 (Super 8: faded home-movie color, lifted blacks, muted), faded-film (Faded Film: heavily faded, low contrast, washed-out color), chrome (Chrome: very warm and saturated, deep blacks; slide film), push-process (Push Process: high contrast, dark midtones, bright whites; pushed film), cine-print (Cine Print: dark, dense print contrast, warm), tungsten (Tungsten: warm and saturated, softly lifted blacks), instant (Instant: bright, washed-out instant photo, lifted blacks), expired (Expired: faded and color-shifted, lifted blacks, muted); Warm: golden-hour (Golden Hour: warm, saturated sunset glow), amber (Amber: strong amber cast, lifted blacks), honey (Honey: soft warm yellow, gentle), sunbaked (Sunbaked: warm, a little more saturated, deep blacks), peach (Peach: warm pink-orange, softly lifted blacks), sepia (Sepia: brown-toned, near monochrome), ember (Ember: intense red-orange, deep blacks), bronze (Bronze: warm and metallic, muted, bright whites); nightlife), arctic (Arctic: cold blue-cyan, muted), storm (Storm: dark, muted and cool; overcast), velvet (Velvet: deep warm red, dark and rich); B&W: mono (Mono: neutral black and white), silver (Silver: soft, silvery black and white, lifted blacks), ink (Ink: hard-contrast black and white, deep blacks), high-key (High Key: bright, airy black and white), low-key (Low Key: dark, moody black and white), charcoal (Charcoal: flat grey black and white), soft-mono (Soft Mono: soft, low-contrast black and white), grit (Grit: gritty, high-contrast black and white). Start amount around 0.6–0.8 and tune to taste; protect_skin when a strong palette would discolor faces.
- set_color_grade — the sliders. Light: exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, brilliance, fade — exposure/contrast for tone, highlights/shadows for the ends, whites/blacks for the end points, brilliance to open the darks and hold the brights at once, fade for lifted film blacks. Color: temperature, tint, saturation, vibrance — temperature/tint for balance, saturation/vibrance for color amount (vibrance favors the muted colors and spares what is already rich). Detail: sharpen, clarity — sharpen for fine edges, clarity for local contrast, both on luma only.
- set_color_curves — tonal shape: curve_contrast for an s-curve, explicit points when you need a precise remap; red/green/blue channels split-tone tonally (a touch of blue lifted in shadows = cool shadows).
- set_color_wheels — the four wheels lift/gamma/gain/offset: lift for shadows, gamma for midtones, gain for highlights, offset for the whole picture; split-toning is a hue on lift and another on gain (teal shadows dx<0, warm highlights dx>0 is the classic pairing); luma trims each range's brightness.
- set_color_hsl — one hue only (red, orange, yellow, green, aqua, blue, purple, magenta): "sky bluer" = aqua/blue sat up, "mute the greens" = green sat down, skin smoothing = orange band, gently.
- match_color_grade — a reference exists (attached image or another clip): the grade sliders are fitted from the pixels through the clip's real pipeline, then refine with the tools above. Matching adjacent clips for consistency is this too — match the odd clip to its neighbor.
- copy_color_grade — "apply this look to everything": one clip's whole grade (LUT included) onto ids or all_clips, as one undo step. Every set_color_* tool also takes ids or all_clips for the same patch on several clips.
- save_color_grade / apply_saved_grade — keep a finished grade under a name in the Library (editor_state.savedGrades) and put it on clips in this or any project.

**Craft.** Skin lives around orange at modest saturation — check faces after strong saturation, hue, or wheel moves, and reach for protect_skin or a gentle orange-band correction rather than abandoning the look. Keep adjacent clips consistent: one preset + per-clip correction reads as one film; different presets per clip read as a slideshow. Strong looks sit better with contrast established first. Grades are per-clip — "grade the whole video" means every clip, same recipe: grade one, then copy_color_grade all_clips.
