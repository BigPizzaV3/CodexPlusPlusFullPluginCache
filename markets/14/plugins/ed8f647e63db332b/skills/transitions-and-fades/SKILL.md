# Transitions, animations, looks & fades
Route the ask to the right feature:
- set_transition: a styled join between one video clip and the next, blended across the cut. It never moves clips or shortens anything, and it plays only while the pair touches. One style, audiocross — the cross dissolve — blends the sound alone: the picture cuts while the two clips ramp equal-power past each other, half the length either side of the cut, each playing into the other's time out of the media its trim left behind, so the mix holds its loudness through the join (it plays at cuts only, so it does nothing on a clip's open head or tail; a clip trimmed to the end of its source has nothing to cross with and its side stops at the cut). In the UI it is picked from the Effects tab's Sound family, beside the treatments on the sound, and its bar sits across the cut rather than before it; every style that blends the picture is picked from the Transitions tab.
- set_animation: one clip's own entrance ("in") or exit ("out") — the clip fades/blurs/zooms/pops/slides on its own edge. Never moves neighbors. For "fade this clip in", "make it slide in", use this. A wipe is a transition, not an animation.
- set_background: the color of the frame itself, behind everything, for the whole cut. For "make the background white", or any cut whose picture is titles and shapes rather than footage. Covered in the graphics skill.
- update_audio fadeIn/fadeOut: audio-only ramps on one soundtrack clip ("fade the music out").
- An ask beyond every preset here — a reference template, a grid or mosaic reveal, a multi-panel build — is a construction job: the graphics skill's composite-builds section shows how overlay video tracks, masks, and keyframes compose into it.

set_transition styles (clipId = the leading clip; 0 clears, max 2s): crossfade blends; crosszoom adds a zoom punch; dipblack/dipwhite dip through a solid color; blur defocuses through the cut; pushleft/right/up/down shove the old frame out; sliceleft cuts the frame into a top and a bottom half that whip apart (top left, bottom right) and sliceup into a left and a right column (left up, right down), motion-blurred; wipeleft/right/up/down reveal with a traveling edge; clockwipe sweeps the new shot in like a clock hand pinned at the top-left corner; circleopen/close and splitopen/close are shape reveals; feather softens a wipe, circle or split edge into a blurred band; audiocross — the cross dissolve — cuts the picture and dissolves only the sound across the cut, for a handover the eye takes as a cut and the ear takes as a blend. Directional names describe the motion.
set_animation styles: fade (audio follows), zoom, pop, slideleft/right/up/down — the slide names are the motion direction (slideleft enters from the right edge moving left, or exits off the left). Each edge holds one effect and the last pick wins: set_animation on a transitioned edge replaces that transition, set_transition clears the animations adjacent to its joint.

Bars and the debris they leave: a transition lives on the transitions row as its own object at an absolute time, listed in editor_state `transitions` with the boundary it `plays`. It plays whatever cut or clip edge its window lines up with; a bar lining up with nothing reads `parked: true` and plays nothing — the badge is on the row, the blend is gone. Moving, retiming, or replacing clips is what parks a bar, so every timeline mutation returns `parkedTransitions` when its edit left one. Own that debris in the same turn you make it: a bar you parked and know the intent of, reattach with set_transition/set_animation on the edge it belongs to, or clear with remove_transition. When you can't tell what a parked bar was for — it predates your edits, or the user's ask doesn't say where the blend should end up — do not guess and do not leave it silent: name what's there ("there's a 0.5s dip to black at 29.4s that isn't on a cut any more") and ask whether to remove it or move it to a specific joint. The same holds for anything else on the timeline that reads as leftover from your own edits: say what you see and confirm before deleting the user's work.
Picking for a vibe: "smooth" → crossfade; "let the sound carry over a hard cut", "audio crossfade", "cross dissolve", "dissolve just the audio" → audiocross; "punchy/energetic" → crosszoom or a push; "dramatic scene change" → dipblack 0.5–0.8s; "dreamy" → blur (or the dreamy look); "retro" → vhs or vintage look. Between clips 0.4–0.8s reads well on the picture; 1s+ is slow and cuts total duration. The cross dissolve is shorter — 0.3s covers the jump in room tone without letting two voices be heard at once, and it asks less of the trim each clip has left. The editing-taste skill covers when a joint should stay a hard cut and how to keep transition styling consistent across the video.
UI: select a clip → Inspector "Effects" opens the panel (Transition / In / Out / Looks tabs). Preview and export render the same treatment.
What the user sees on the transitions row: one badge per bar, and nothing else — the row holds no buttons, no blocks, and no add-affordances. A playing bar is blue; a parked one is the same badge in grey. So a user reporting "greyed out transitions", "grey circles", or "dead/lingering transitions" on that row is describing parked bars, which are real objects saved in their project — never dismiss them as editor chrome or claim they won't export, because the badge is the transition itself. Read `transitions` in editor_state and answer from the parked entries there. A bar can also be hidden (set_transition_hidden): it grays out but keeps its cut, so the joint plays as a hard cut and the bar is there to switch back on — that is how to audition a cut without its blend. Grey elsewhere means something different: a clip, element, or track shown grayed is hidden (the `hidden` flag), which does drop out of playback and export.
