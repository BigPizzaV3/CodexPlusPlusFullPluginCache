# First-pass diligence checklist

Work through each item. Mark it **Verified** (with the source and the record date), **Not found** (the source has no record), or **Founders** (only the company can answer). Do not skip items; an honest "Not found" is useful information, and for a US private company it is common.

## A. Corporate existence and status

1. Legal entity exists. Sources, in order of strength: an SEC filing naming the entity (Form D, 10-K, S-1), a GLEIF record, a federal award on USAspending. For non-US companies, GLEIF usually names the national register and the registration number.
2. Exact legal name and identifier match what the pitch deck or website says. Note any trading-name versus legal-name gap.
3. Status is active. GLEIF status INACTIVE, a lapsed LEI registration, or a Form D issuer that later filed a different name all need an explanation.
4. Incorporation or first-filing date. Compare with the founding year the founders claim. Form D records the issuer's year of incorporation; a gap of more than a year suggests a reincorporation, a flip into a US parent, or a shell.
5. Company type is appropriate for venture investment. Form D records the entity type: corporation (usually Delaware) is customary; LLCs taxed as partnerships, sole proprietorships and non-profits need a conversation.
6. State of incorporation. Delaware is customary; an unusual state or a foreign jurisdiction chosen for opacity is a question.
7. Group structure: GLEIF reports direct and ultimate parents when the entity declares them. Confirm which entity the investment goes into and which one owns the IP and employs the team.

## B. People and control

8. Related persons named on the latest Form D (executive officers, directors, promoters) with their roles. Compare with the founders named in the deck.
9. Changes between successive Form D filings: officers who appear or disappear.
10. For SEC registrants: officers and directors in the latest 10-K or proxy statement, and 8-K item 5.02 departures in the last 24 months.
11. Federal footprint (USAspending): UEI, parent company and parent UEI, business types as registered, location, and the award history: agencies, amounts, dates and what was bought, where present.
12. Any officer appearing across many unrelated issuers (serial promoter or formation agent). Confirm who actually runs the company.

## C. Filings and compliance

13. Form D history: every exempt raise with date, total offering amount, amount sold, and whether it is a new notice or an amendment. The sequence is the public funding history.
14. For SEC registrants: last 10-K, last 10-Q, 8-Ks in the last 12 months and what triggered them, NT filings (late notices).
15. SAM.gov registration current or expired (manual check at sam.gov; not connected as a tool).
16. LEI registration status and next renewal date (a lapsed LEI is a small compliance signal).
17. Former names in the SEC submissions record and GLEIF other names.

## D. Financials (only from filings or founder-provided figures)

18. For SEC registrants: Revenues, NetIncomeLoss, Assets, StockholdersEquity for the last two fiscal years from the company-concept endpoint, with the form each value came from.
19. Form D offering amounts as the only public financing figures for private companies; say explicitly that Form D does not disclose revenue or valuation.
20. Going-concern language or auditor matters in the latest annual report, when there is one.

## E. Technical and online footprint

21. Primary domain: registration date, registrar, registrant organisation if disclosed. A domain registered years before incorporation can mean a pivot or a prior business; one registered weeks before a raise is a question.
22. GitHub organisation: number of public repositories, stars, last push dates, whether flagship repositories are archived or forks, and the languages used. Absence is normal for closed-source companies; stale public repositories while claiming an active developer community is a flag.
23. Open-source licence of the core product, if any, and whether it matches the commercial model described.

## F. Consistency check

24. Every claim in the pitch that the public record can test: founding date, headquarters, team, funding history, customers that are themselves filers or registrants. List each as consistent, inconsistent, or untestable.

## Output discipline

Group findings under the headings above. One line per item. Facts carry a source and a date. Questions for the founders go to the end of the memo, phrased so a founder can answer them in one sentence each.
