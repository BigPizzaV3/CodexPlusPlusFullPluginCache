# Red flags and how to report them

A red flag is a public-record fact that an investor must resolve before proceeding. Report each one with the triggering fact, its source and date, and the question it raises. Do not soften them and do not speculate about motive.

## Existence and status

- **No record in any source** for a company that claims to have raised institutional money. A priced seed or Series A in the US almost always leaves a Form D within 15 days of the first sale. Ask for the exact legal name, state and the Form D accession number.
- **GLEIF status INACTIVE**, or an LEI registration that lapsed years ago while the company is described as operating.
- **Federal awards that stop abruptly** for a company that sells to the federal government, or a SAM.gov registration the founders describe as current but cannot show.
- **Small-business designation on a company with large federal totals** (USAspending business types include `small_business` while awards run to hundreds of millions). Often explained by size standards per industry code; ask which contracts relied on it.
- **Several UEIs or parent records for one company** in USAspending. Ask which entity holds the contracts, the IP and the staff.
- **Incorporated within the last 90 days** (Form D year of incorporation, or GLEIF creation date) while the pitch describes years of trading. Possible reincorporation; ask which entity holds the contracts, IP and bank accounts.

## People and control

- **An officer or director on the latest Form D who is absent from the pitch**, or a founder named in the pitch who is not a related person on the Form D of the entity being invested in.
- **Officers who disappear between Form D amendments**, especially the founding CTO or CFO without a replacement.
- **A promoter listed on Form D** who is not part of the team.
- **Officers who appear across many unrelated issuers**: often a serial promoter or formation agent. Confirm who actually runs the company.
- **For SEC registrants: 8-K item 5.02 departures** of the CEO, CFO or an independent director in the last 24 months.

## Filings and compliance

- **Form D amount sold far below the amount offered** across several amendments: a round that did not fill.
- **A round described in the pitch that left no Form D**, or a Form D that describes a round the pitch does not mention (a bridge nobody talks about).
- **Form D filed late** (more than 15 days after the stated date of first sale).
- **SEC: NT 10-K or NT 10-Q notices, restatements, 8-K items 4.01 (auditor change) or 4.02 (non-reliance on previously issued financials).**
- **Auditor resignation**, going-concern emphasis, or a qualified opinion in the latest annual report.
- **Former names** in the SEC record or GLEIF that match negative news.

## Group structure

- **Investment entity is not the operating entity.** The company that owns the IP, employs the team or signs customer contracts is a different legal person from the one issuing shares. GLEIF parent relationships and Form D issuer names show this.
- **Holding company in a jurisdiction chosen for opacity** rather than a customary venture structure (Delaware, Cayman for certain funds, England and Wales, Netherlands, Singapore).

## Technical footprint

- **Domain registered after the claimed launch date**, or registered to an unrelated organisation.
- **"Open-source company" whose public repositories are archived, forks, or have had no commits in a year.**
- **Flagship repository licence changed recently** from permissive to source-available without mention in the pitch.

## How to phrase a flag

Pattern: *Fact (source, date). Why it matters in one clause. Question for the founders.*

Example: *Form D filed 2026-03-02 reports $4.0M offered and $1.1M sold (SEC EDGAR, accession 0001234567-26-000012); a later amendment shows no change. Suggests the round did not fill. Who committed the remaining $2.9M and on what terms?*
