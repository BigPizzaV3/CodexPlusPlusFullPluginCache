# Direct a creative palette

## Interpret the concept

1. Extract the concept's emotional temperature, energy, time, material qualities, light, and narrative tension.
2. Identify any literal associations, then move beyond them. Use one or two recognizable anchors and let the remaining colors express atmosphere, contrast, and surprise.
3. Treat living cultures and historical styles with specificity and care. Avoid reducing them to stereotypes or claiming authenticity from vague references.

## Art-direct the colors

- Build a deliberate visual arc, normally five to seven colors, including a field, supporting tones, a grounding dark or light, and one controlled accent.
- Avoid generic rainbow coverage and predictable keyword-to-color mappings when a more distinctive interpretation fits.
- Balance originality with the intended medium. Ask about medium only if it materially changes the palette; otherwise explain the assumed use.
- Give colors short evocative role names, but keep hex values and practical relationships clear.
- If the prompt names a protected brand or living artist, draw from high-level attributes and the user's stated concept rather than claiming an exact official palette or direct stylistic replica.

## Deliver

- Give the direction a concise title and a two- or three-sentence creative rationale.
- Invoke `palette-picker` with ordered uppercase `#RRGGBB` colors and a `reason` capturing the narrative arc and assumed medium.
- Keep the result editable and offer iteration through concrete dimensions such as warmer, stranger, quieter, more cinematic, or more restrained.
