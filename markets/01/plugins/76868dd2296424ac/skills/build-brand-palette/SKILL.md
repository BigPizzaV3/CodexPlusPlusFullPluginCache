# Build a brand palette

## Gather the brief

- Identify the brand, audience, offering, desired traits, unwanted associations, market context, media, and any colors that must be kept or avoided.
- Use supplied facts without asking the user to repeat them. Ask only for information whose absence would materially change the result; otherwise state a reasonable assumption.

## Develop the direction

1. Translate the brief into three to five visual attributes such as assured, warm, precise, energetic, or restrained.
2. Choose a color strategy that expresses those attributes and differentiates the brand from obvious category conventions.
3. Treat required colors as anchors. Adjust supporting colors around them rather than silently replacing them.
4. Build a focused palette, normally five to eight colors, with purposeful roles: primary, secondary, accent, light neutral, dark neutral, and optional supporting or status colors.
5. Avoid redundant near-duplicates, muddy mixtures, accidental national or political symbolism, and trendy choices unsupported by the brief.
6. Consider legibility and reproduction across the user's stated media. Do not claim formal accessibility compliance unless contrast has been calculated for specified pairings.

## Present the result

- Briefly name the direction and connect the major choices to the brief.
- List each color's intended brand role. Keep the explanation compact so the editable palette remains the main artifact.
- Invoke `palette-picker` with the ordered hex colors and a `reason` summarizing the brand direction. Use uppercase `#RRGGBB` values unless transparency is explicitly needed.
- Invite refinement through the rendered palette rather than presenting the colors only as prose.
