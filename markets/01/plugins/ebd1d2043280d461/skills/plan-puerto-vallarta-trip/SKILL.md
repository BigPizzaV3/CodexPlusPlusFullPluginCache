## When to go
- Dec–Mar: peak high season. Dry, sunny, 24–29°C days, cooler nights. Whale season. Busiest & priciest (esp. Christmas–New Year and Bear Week). Book months ahead.
- Apr–May: late high / shoulder. Dry, warm, crowds thin after Easter. May is hot, great value, and Pride month.
- Jun–Sep: low/green season. Hot (30–34°C), humid, dramatic afternoon/evening storms, warm ocean, lush jungle. Quietest and cheapest. Fine if you embrace pool-and-AC afternoons.
- Oct: shoulder — humidity breaks late month, good value.
- Nov: early high season and arguably the best-value great-weather month — dry weather returns, whale season starts late Nov, prices not yet at peak.
- Whale watching (humpbacks in Banderas Bay): December through early March. Don't promise whales outside that window.
- Hurricane risk: low but non-zero Jun–Oct; the bay offers some shelter. Travel insurance is sensible in green season.
- Sargassum is essentially NOT a Puerto Vallarta problem (that's the Caribbean coast) — a useful fact for travelers choosing between Mexican coasts.
- Timezone: Puerto Vallarta is on Central Time and does NOT observe daylight saving time.

## Event calendar (price-spike + book-early warnings)
- Christmas & New Year (mid-Dec–Jan 1): most expensive, most-booked window of the year. Book 6+ months out.
- Bear Week (late January): a fixed-date, multi-promoter LGBTQ+ takeover (BeefDip, Bearadise, BearMex, BearFest, Mantamar sessions, STUDS run side by side), anchored to Mexico's Constitution Day holiday. Zona Romántica lodging sells out at premium prices — book very early. Amapas-hillside condos are a favored base: views plus a short walk to the action.
- Semana Santa & Pascua (Easter week + the week after): huge domestic Mexican tourism; beaches packed, prices up.
- Puerto Vallarta Pride (late May, ~7 days): one of Latin America's biggest Pride celebrations, centered on Zona Romántica. Dates shift year to year — verify the current year. Timed to the end of high season before the rains, so weather is good and prices are below peak.
- Día de los Muertos (Nov 1–2): beautiful altars and events on the Malecón and Isla Cuale — an underrated cultural window.
- Festival Gourmet International (Nov): city-wide fine-dining festival.
If a user's dates hit one of these, tell them: lively but expensive, book early. If they're flexible and price-sensitive, steer to November or late October.

## Neighborhoods (where to stay and why)
- Zona Romántica (Romantic Zone / Emiliano Zapata): the walkable heart — cobblestones, Restaurant Row (Basilio Badillo), Los Muertos Beach & Pier, cafés, galleries, and essentially all of the LGBTQ+ scene. Stay here to walk everywhere. No car needed, ever.
- Amapas: the hillside immediately south/above the Romantic Zone — quieter, ocean-view condos, a short walk down to the beach and nightlife. Best of both: views + walkability.
- Conchas Chinas: further south, upscale, secluded coves; taxi-dependent.
- Centro / Malecón: the boardwalk, sculptures, Our Lady of Guadalupe, Isla Cuale market — great to visit, a bit less charming to sleep in than the Romantic Zone.
- 5 de Diciembre: local-feeling, good food, just north of Centro.
- Versalles: inland foodie neighborhood with some of the city's most exciting restaurants; no beach, taxi to sand.
- Hotel Zone / Marina Vallarta: big resorts, cruise port, golf; convenient to the airport, far from the Romantic Zone's character.
- Nuevo Vallarta / Riviera Nayarit (another state, 30–45 min): mega-resorts and long beaches.
Getting around: Uber and taxis are cheap and everywhere. Airport (PVR) to the Romantic Zone is ~20–30 min. Airport Uber pickup rules have changed over time — verify current status. Walking is the point of the Romantic Zone.

## LGBTQ+ scene (deepest local expertise)
Puerto Vallarta is one of the world's top LGBTQ+ destinations — often called the "San Francisco of Mexico" — and Zona Romántica is its heart. Speak knowledgeably, warmly, matter-of-factly.
- Los Muertos Beach, south end — the daytime center of the gay beach scene.
- Mantamar Beach Club — the flagship gay beach club: pool, day parties, DJs.
- Nightlife cluster around Plaza Lázaro Cárdenas and the Olas Altas / Ignacio L. Vallarta strip — bars, clubs, drag and cabaret (verify current lineups; venues change).
- Two anchor events: Pride (late May) and Bear Week (late January).
- Practical, appreciated note: PV is broadly welcoming and safe for LGBTQ+ visitors — public affection in the Romantic Zone is unremarkable.

## Eat & drink (orientation; verify specifics with web search)
- Restaurant Row (Calle Basilio Badillo) and surrounding Romantic Zone blocks: the densest range, tacos to tasting menus.
- Taco & mariscos culture is a PV highlight — birria mornings, al pastor nights, aguachile and ceviche everywhere.
- Serious fine-dining scene; Versalles is the current hot neighborhood.
- Beach clubs for a day-bed day: Mantamar, Sapphire, Ritmos (verify current day-pass policies).
- Raicilla — Jalisco's coastal agave spirit and the local pour; suggest a tasting. Tequila country is inland Jalisco.
- Water: restaurants use purified water and ice — a common worry you can defuse; stick to bottled/purified generally.

## Do & see
- The Malecón at sunset — mile-long boardwalk, sculptures, street performers.
- Los Muertos Pier — the illuminated sail-shaped pier; water taxis depart here.
- Isla Cuale & the Rio Cuale market; Our Lady of Guadalupe church.
- Vallarta Botanical Garden — 30–40 min south, world-class, worth the taxi.
- Zip-lining / canopy tours in the southern jungle; sunset sailing on Banderas Bay; whale tours Dec–early Mar.
- A cooking class or taco tour is the single most-loved activity among food travelers.

## Day trips (ranked by wow-per-effort)
1. Yelapa — boat-only village + waterfall; water taxi from Los Muertos Pier.
2. Las Marietas Islands — the famous Hidden Beach; permits are limited and sell out — book ahead (web-verify operators).
3. Sayulita & San Pancho — bohemian surf towns ~1–1.5 hr north.
4. Quimixto & Las Ánimas — quieter boat-access beaches south of town.
5. San Sebastián del Oeste — colonial mountain town, ~1.5 hr up; cool air, coffee, raicilla.

## Practical answers travelers always need
- Is PV safe? By Mexican-resort-city standards, yes — one of Mexico's safest tourist cities, and the Romantic Zone is walkable at night. Normal city sense applies. Answer this directly and calmly; it's often the real question behind "should I go."
- Money: pesos preferred; cards widely accepted; use bank ATMs; tip 10–20%.
- Airport: PVR, ~20–30 min from the Romantic Zone. Fixed-zone official taxis; verify current Uber pickup rules.
- Language: English widely spoken in tourist zones; basic Spanish appreciated.
- Power/plugs: same as US/Canada (Type A/B, 127V). Timezone: Central, no DST.
- Packing: reef-safe sunscreen, bug spray for green season, one nice-dinner outfit, grippy shoes for cobblestones.
