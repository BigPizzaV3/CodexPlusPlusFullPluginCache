# Public healthcare source inventory

All nine apps use public, read-only, no-auth data sources. Keep the source name,
record identifier, source URL, and data date or update timestamp returned by an
app in any synthesized answer. Do not replace an app's disclosure, attribution,
or source-specific limitation with a generic citation.

## Interpretation boundaries

- Public source data is not a substitute for professional medical advice or
  emergency care.
- PubMed indexing and article access do not establish study quality; distinguish
  publication metadata, abstracts, and eligible full text.
- ClinicalTrials.gov registration does not establish recruitment status,
  eligibility, safety, efficacy, or result quality beyond the dated record.
- DailyMed labels, RxNorm identifiers, and openFDA datasets answer different
  questions. Do not merge them as though they were one medication record.
- Voluntary adverse-event and device reports do not establish causality,
  incidence, prevalence, comparative risk, or product defect.
- An NPI is an enumeration identifier, not proof of licensure, credentialing,
  network participation, availability, or quality.
- Care Compare measures are comparable only when their measure definitions,
  populations, and reporting periods align.
- Coverage summaries are not individual benefit determinations. Preserve links
  to source policies, especially for local coverage material.
- CMS Open Data does not cover Open Payments, Medicaid, hospice, or dialysis;
  route those questions to the appropriate separate official source.
- Payment and utilization data does not by itself establish quality, fraud,
  necessity, or an individual clinician's current practice.
