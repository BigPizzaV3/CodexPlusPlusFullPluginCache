# Dating Messaging Principles

## 1. What a message can realistically do

A message can demonstrate attention, create an easy opening, express personality,
clarify intent, and help both people assess fit. It cannot guarantee attraction,
interest, honesty, compatibility, or a response.

The useful optimization target is not maximum response probability. It is the
best truthful next move under current evidence and boundaries.

## 2. Relevance before novelty

Novelty draws attention only when it remains understandable and connected to the
interaction. A surprising opener can filter for compatible humor. During an
active conversation, novelty should not displace an unanswered question,
important disclosure, correction, boundary, or practical decision.

Use this order:

1. receive what matters;
2. contribute something specific;
3. open the next step.

The sequence can be compressed into one sentence or distributed across a short
message burst.

## 3. Reciprocity without mechanical mirroring

Reciprocity means comparable social investment, not equal character count.

Consider:

- how many distinct thoughts she contributed;
- whether she asked questions;
- specificity and personal disclosure;
- emotional weight;
- whether she advanced or merely acknowledged the thread.

Four short consecutive messages can warrant more attention than one long generic
message. A brief but vulnerable disclosure may warrant more care than a long
story. Match the function and investment, not typography.

## 4. Cognitive ease

People are more likely to engage naturally when they can quickly understand what
is being said and see a manageable way to respond.

Increase ease through:

- one primary idea;
- concrete references;
- one decision or question at a time;
- constrained-open questions that offer direction without forcing a binary;
- short setup before the point;
- readable Unicode or ASCII only when it adds a clear playful effect.

Reduce ease through:

- stacked questions;
- several unrelated topics with no hierarchy;
- long disclaimers;
- obscure references;
- elaborate hypothetical worlds;
- formatting that resembles spam or cannot be read easily.

## 5. Question design

A question is useful when its answer would genuinely shape the conversation.

### Strong forms

- a specific follow-up to something she volunteered;
- a choice with room to explain;
- a concrete opinion question;
- a playful challenge based on shared context;
- one relevant checklist question at an appropriate stage;
- a clarifier needed to avoid misunderstanding.

### Weak forms

- generic biography requests;
- questions answerable from visible profile data;
- scenario questions used only because they sound creative;
- stacked compatibility screening;
- asking for detail without first acknowledging what she said;
- repeated questions after she avoided the topic.

Statements can create openings too: a specific reaction, true contrast, playful
claim, or unfinished but non-clickbait thought may invite reciprocity without a
question mark.

## 6. Self-disclosure and conversational balance

Question-only communication can feel extractive. A true, proportionate personal
detail can make curiosity reciprocal and give the match something to respond to.

Use self-disclosure when it:

- answers her question;
- establishes commonality or a meaningful contrast;
- explains a reaction;
- makes a checklist question mutual rather than interrogative;
- advances an active topic.

Do not disclose merely to prove status, manufacture similarity, accelerate
intimacy, or fill silence. Every implied fact needs the same support as an
explicit claim.

## 7. Humor calibration

Humor has three requirements:

1. a legible premise;
2. a target that is safe in context;
3. a tone signal strong enough to prevent accidental hostility.

The user's sarcasm, dark humor, absurdity, and nihilistic style can be a genuine
compatibility filter, especially in openers. Calibrate intensity to evidence:

- no established tone: use low-stakes absurdity or self-contained play;
- reciprocal play: extend the shared premise;
- sensitive disclosure or disagreement: prioritize understanding;
- unclear reaction: do not escalate the joke.

Do not use humor to disguise criticism, pressure, sexual escalation, jealousy,
or contempt. “It was a joke” does not repair a boundary violation.

## 8. Emotional attunement

Useful responses distinguish emotion from problem solving.

- Acknowledge before advising.
- Avoid naming an emotion more strongly than the evidence supports.
- Do not diagnose motives or personality.
- Do not imitate therapy language when ordinary human recognition is enough.
- Do not immediately redirect a meaningful disclosure into a checklist goal.

Attunement can be brief. One accurate sentence is often more natural than a long
support monologue in early dating.

## 9. Threading and topic transitions

An active thread has momentum when it contains a question, unresolved decision,
story, callback, mutual joke, disagreement, emotional disclosure, or reciprocal
curiosity.

Possible actions for each thread:

- `develop`: continue it directly;
- `acknowledge`: recognize it without extending;
- `combine`: join it with a related thread;
- `park`: signal that it remains available later;
- `close`: allow it to end naturally.

When introducing a new topic, use a real bridge when one exists. If no bridge
exists and the old topic is complete, a clean shift is better than a fabricated
connection.

## 10. Information gaps without manipulation

Curiosity grows from incomplete but meaningful information. Ethical information
gaps include:

- a true unusual detail with a natural follow-up;
- a contrast that invites explanation;
- a callback whose shared meaning is already established;
- a playful assertion she can challenge.

Avoid false suspense, “I know something about you” intimidation, deliberate
withholding, hot-and-cold behavior, jealousy, scarcity, and making the match earn
basic clarity.

## 11. Escalation and autonomy

Escalation should be explicit enough to understand and easy to decline. Under
the current user rules, the agent does not initiate coffee, dates, meetings, or
going out. Eligible CTA strategy is one optional Instagram or Facebook exchange.

An exchange request must not imply that moving platforms creates commitment,
responsibility, identity proof, safety, or guaranteed continued contact.

## 12. Candidate diversity

When generation requires several candidates, vary the strategic move rather
than surface wording.

Useful dimensions:

- direct answer vs playful extension;
- callback vs current-detail hook;
- self-disclosure vs specific curiosity;
- continue vs continue-and-bridge;
- concise conventional wording vs restrained unusual presentation.

All candidates must satisfy the same facts, boundaries, language, event state,
and must-address topics. Diversity is not permission for one unsafe or
unsupported “bold” option.

## 13. Strategy rejection test

Reject a direction if any answer is no:

1. Does it address the complete latest turn rather than only the final line?
2. Does it account for every meaningful active topic?
3. Is every personal fact and implication supported and disclosable?
4. Does it fit the router's task, language, effort, and event constraints?
5. Is it easy to understand and respond to?
6. Is it specific enough for this match and moment?
7. Is humor grounded in a safe shared premise?
8. Does it avoid generic interviewing and unnecessary scenario questions?
9. Does it preserve autonomy and remain respectful if interest is not mutual?
10. Does it avoid coffee, date, meeting, or going-out invitations?
