# DDD Hook Framework

Apply this framework to every hook used in a social post, advertisement, video, or poster.

## Official meaning

**DDD** means **Desire Driven Digital Marketing**.

Use its three-stage psychology model:

1. **Desire** — Identify what this specific customer truly wants.
2. **Drive** — Create an honest emotional reason that moves the customer toward action.
3. **Decision** — Make the benefit or next step clear and easy to choose.

Use this internal sequence: **Understand the Desire → Drive the Emotion → Influence the Decision.**

## Golden Rule

Apply **“You instead of We.”** Talk about the customer, not the company.

- Prefer `you`, `your`, and a direct description of the customer's situation or desired outcome.
- Do not lead with `we`, `our`, the company history, service features, or self-praise.
- Mention the business only when necessary to support the customer's outcome.
- Make the reader feel: “This is about my situation, my goal, and my desired result.”

## Required inputs

Build hooks from confirmed profile details, especially:

- The specific target customer
- Their present situation, frustration, or emotional tension
- The outcome or experience they desire
- The business's relevant differentiator or relief mechanism
- The current offer, product, place, duration, price, or occasion when supplied

If the customer desire or current offer is too unclear to produce specific hooks, ask exactly one simple question before generating them.

## Three-hook construction

Create exactly three meaningfully different angles:

1. **Desire-led** — Make the desired outcome or experience vivid.
2. **Tension-led** — Connect the current frustration or emotional tension to the desired change without shame or fear.
3. **Decision-led** — Reduce a genuine objection, effort, risk, confusion, or delay and make the next step feel easier.

Do not merely paraphrase one hook three times. Do not force all three D stages into a long sentence; each hook may emphasize one stage, but the set must move naturally from desire through emotion toward decision.

After each hook, add exactly one italicized word naming its primary driver, such as `Desire`, `Relief`, `Curiosity`, `Confidence`, `Belonging`, `Challenge`, or `Trust`. The word is an explanation label, not a substitute for DDD.

## Quality gate

Reject and rewrite a hook unless every answer below is yes:

1. Is it clearly written for the confirmed target customer?
2. Does it reveal a specific desire, situation, tension, or objection rather than a generic product benefit?
3. Does it speak mainly as `you/your` or directly from the customer's point of view?
4. Does it avoid leading with `we/our`, company credentials, or a feature list?
5. Does it create an honest emotional reason to continue reading or act?
6. Does it connect naturally to the actual offer and only use confirmed facts?
7. Could a competitor reuse it unchanged? If yes, it is too generic; rewrite it using profile or offer details.

Avoid empty lines such as “Make memories,” “Grow your business,” “Quality you can trust,” or “The break you need” unless confirmed customer context makes them distinctive and concrete.

## Output format

Use only:

> 1. **[Customer-first hook]** — *[one-word driver]*
> 2. **[Customer-first hook]** — *[one-word driver]*
> 3. **[Customer-first hook]** — *[one-word driver]*
>
> **Which hook do you select? Reply with 1, 2, or 3.**

Do not explain DDD to the business owner unless they ask. Apply it silently.
