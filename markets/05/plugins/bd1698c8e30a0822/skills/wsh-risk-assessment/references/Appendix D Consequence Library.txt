# Appendix D: Consequence Library

## Purpose

This appendix provides examples of credible consequences associated with common workplace hazards. The examples are intended to support hazard analysis, consequence identification, and severity assessment.

Consequences should be:

* Reasonably foreseeable.
* Proportionate to the hazard.
* Consistent with the exposure pathway.
* Based on credible outcomes rather than worst-case scenarios alone.

A single hazard may have multiple possible consequences of differing severity.

**Note:** Consequences should describe the harm, injury, illness, damage, or loss resulting from a hazard. Hazardous events (e.g., fire, explosion, toxic gas release, vessel rupture) may be included where they directly represent the harmful outcome of the initiating hazard.

# Severity Calibration Reference

Examples of typical severity levels:

### Severity 1 – Negligible

* Temporary discomfort
* No treatment required

### Severity 2 – Minor

* First-aid injury
* Minor cuts
* Mild irritation

### Severity 3 – Moderate

* Injury requiring medical treatment
* Minor fracture
* Heat exhaustion
* Chemical burn requiring treatment

### Severity 4 – Major

* Permanent eye injury
* Amputation
* Serious occupational disease
* Severe radiation injury

### Severity 5 – Catastrophic

* Fatality
* Multiple fatalities
* Permanent total disability
* Major explosion causing multiple casualties
* Uncontrolled release causing multiple casualties

The examples above are guidance only. Severity ratings should always be based on the specific activity, exposure scenario, and reasonably foreseeable outcome.
