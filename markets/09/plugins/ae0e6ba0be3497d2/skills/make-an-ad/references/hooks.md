# Hook mechanics: the first two seconds of a video ad

A hook is a visual device, not a clever line. These mechanics show up again and again in long-running ads on
Meta, TikTok and YouTube. Use them by name when you plan or brief a video ad. The user's own idea for the opening
always wins over this list.

## The mechanics
1. **Direct callout**: the creator leans into the lens and names the exact viewer in the first second ("if you can't fall asleep, watch this").
2. **Open mid-problem**: start inside the painful, relatable moment before any setup. The pain is the hook.
3. **Before and after snap**: an instant cut from the bad state to the good state inside about two seconds.
4. **Object into frame**: the product slides, rolls or is set down into frame with a confident thunk.
5. **Big reaction**: a genuine gasp or double take opens the clip. The emotion stops the thumb.
6. **Satisfying macro**: an extreme close-up of a pour, a peel, a snap or a texture.
7. **Pattern interrupt**: one unexpected, slightly absurd visual in the very first frame.
8. **Provocative question**: a bold, specific on-screen question the viewer cannot scroll past.
9. **Surprising number**: a true, specific number fills the screen as the cold open.
10. **POV**: first person; the viewer is the character ("POV: you finally...").
11. **Whip or snap zoom**: a fast whip pan or snap zoom that rushes into the subject.
12. **Unexpected place**: the product somewhere surprising, played completely straight.
13. **Countdown to reveal**: a quick 3, 2, 1 build that pays off on an immediate reveal.
14. **List tease**: promise a numbered payoff ("3 reasons I switched") to open a curiosity loop.
15. **Mishap then deadpan**: a small mishap in the first beat, then the creator carries on unbothered.
16. **Street interview**: a handheld-mic vox pop; a stranger's take, then the product, then the reaction.
17. **Calm in a surreal setting**: the creator calmly reviews the product somewhere impossible.

## Picking one
- Talking head or UGC style: 1, 2, 10, 15 and 16 fit naturally; 4 and 6 work when the product has a satisfying physical action.
- Product only, no person on screen: 3, 4, 6, 12 and 13. Never 1 or 10, which need a face.
- Text-led hooks (8, 9, 14) work when the text is added cleanly in editing, which Hermoso's video ads do.
- Apps and software: 3 (before and after on the screen), 8, 9 and 14; show the real interface within three seconds.
- Health, finance and other serious categories: avoid 15 and 17, and prefer 2, 8 or 9 with a specific, true claim.

## Writing the beat
Write the hook as one sentence of action a camera can film, then the line spoken or shown, then the cut. For
example: "Object into frame: a hand sets the bottle down on marble with a thunk, label to camera. Line: 'Ninety
minutes of hot yoga and it did not move.' Cut to the product in use." Then check that the product is on screen by
second three.

## Testing
Make versions of the same ad that differ only in the hook mechanic and keep the one that wins. The body of an ad
is rarely why one version loses. `hook_variants` does this for a finished video.
