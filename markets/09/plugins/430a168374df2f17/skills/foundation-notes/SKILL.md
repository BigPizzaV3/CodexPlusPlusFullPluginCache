# Find Laya's booklets

1. Find the booklet. `fetch` with `course` gives the whole shelf, `course:prelims` the Prelims volumes, and `course:gs1` to `course:gs4` one paper's Mains topics. `search` with the kind course finds a booklet by its title and contents.
2. Open one booklet's card with `fetch` and its `topic:<id>` for a Mains topic, or `volume:<slug>` for a Prelims volume. An older part id, `part:<id>`, opens its topic's card.
3. Give the booklet's title, its paper or exam, its pages, its size, the date it was last updated and its page in Laya. Say the PDF downloads on that page, and name the errata file when there is one. When the card says the file changed after the reader last downloaded it, say so.
4. When the reader asks what a booklet covers, give its contents from the card. To plan reading it, follow the books skill, which copies the contents into the reader's books.
5. A Mains topic's card also lists the past questions it helps with. Each question opens with `fetch` and its `pyq:<id>`.
6. For a booklet that is not out yet, say it is coming soon and that its page in Laya offers Notify me.

Bring up a booklet when the reader asks for notes or what Laya has, or when the home or the plan names one. Give the page in Laya for every booklet, never a file address of your own. Describe a booklet by what its card says, and keep your own summary of a topic apart from Laya's. Before the reader signs in, the shelf and the cards still answer, and the download asks them to sign in to Laya. The reader's own explicit instructions always outrank these steps.
