# PART ONE — GOVERNANCE

## 1.1 Two principles

**Subsidiarity.** Decisions are made at the smallest scale competent to make them well. A neighbourhood tool library is governed locally. A watershed is governed bioregionally. Atmospheric carbon is governed planetarily.

**Affectedness.** Voice follows affectedness. Those materially affected by a decision have standing in it.

Everyone is a member of Earth and therefore has some voice in planetary commons matters. Local ledger decisions belong primarily to the members of that locality or entity. Other communities or councils may have input where materially affected.

Where the two principles conflict, name the conflict rather than resolving it silently.

## 1.2 Three communities

Every individual belongs to three: **Local**, **Regional**, and **Global**.

Each Local community is autonomous and self-governing. Autonomy lets citizens chart their own course and fosters diversity through experimentation. Existing city and country borders can initially demarcate Local and Regional, though the flexibility to change those classifications stays with the populace.

Population-based allocations are part of the architecture. Proposed Bonus PC/CC allocations are intended to be mathematically bounded and fair across Local, Regional, and Global scales; their formulas are not supplied or validated, and capture resistance is not proven.

Within each scale, people participate through **councils**, **clubs**, and **co-ops**.

### Institutional freedom — canonical block

> **Creditism fixes the architecture and leaves the institutions to the people inside it.** Who holds authority, what that authority may never do, where productive capital lives, and how Credit flows are all settled. *How* a community exercises its authority is not, and is not meant to be. Communities build decision institutions, test them, copy what works elsewhere, and replace what does not.
>
> **The members always outrank the institution.** Any council, committee, panel, or representative a community creates can be revised, replaced, or recalled by the people who created it. An institution that cannot be undone by its members is not a Creditist institution, whatever else it is.
>
> **Recourse runs upward.** Where a Local abuses its authority or a dispute cannot be settled where it arose, appeal to the Regional or Global scale is available. That this recourse exists is settled. Its procedure, who has standing, what thresholds apply, and what remedies follow are undesigned. See `05` C2.
>
> **This is not an architectural gap.** Capitalism is not judged to lack an architecture because Delaware corporate law differs from German codetermination. Variation is legible as variation because the constants are stateable. Creditism's constants are stateable, and the list of what no institution may do is at `02` 2.5.

## 1.3 The Talking Stick

Integrated voting at local, regional, and global scale, with crowdsourced crafting of legislative proposals and transparent records of council members' and political figures' votes and activities.

A decision protocol that scales from small groups to large communities: voting thresholds, time structures, transparent records of what was decided and why.

**Open source. Forkable.** Communities can modify it. It cannot be quietly changed by a single actor.

It also includes structures for ecological consideration: representatives for affected ecosystems, proxies for future generations, scientific advisors accountable to the community rather than to capital.

## 1.4 What governance does not guarantee

Creditism does not guarantee good decisions. No governance system does.

It guarantees that decisions are made by the people affected, in the open, with the right to revise.

Real democracy means the players can change the rules. A system that cannot be revised by the people living inside it is not a democracy.

**Not no rules. No rulers.**

## 1.5 Open governance questions

Voting thresholds and quorum. Protocol revision procedure. Appeal paths for contested contribution scores, and the procedure for higher-tier recourse generally, which exists in principle and is unspecified in mechanism. Technical reconciliation of transparent flows with protected privacy. Protection of minorities against local majorities. Concentration of Community Credit allocation authority through gifts, delegation, and pooling. See `05`, C2.

# PART TWO — COMMONS AND PROPERTY

## 2.1 What "commons" means

**Commons** means resources held and governed in common by a defined community.

Not an unmanaged free-for-all. A commons has members, rules, stewards, and accountability. Ostrom demonstrated that communities can govern shared resources successfully under identifiable institutional conditions, without either private ownership or state enforcement.

## 2.2 What "stewardship" means

A legal and social relationship with obligations built in: maintain regenerative capacity, coordinate with neighbouring stewards, leave the land able to do what it was doing before you arrived.

Stewardship differs from unrestricted ownership because it carries enforceable obligations and does not create an unlimited, freely alienable claim over the underlying commons. A stewardship position may arise through transition, succession, selection, transfer agreement, or a payment mechanism, and still not become conventional ownership. What distinguishes it is the obligation and the limit on alienation, not the absence of any exchange.

Existing instruments give this shape now, at small scale: community land trusts, conservation easements, commons trusts, watershed councils.

## 2.3 Five property categories

**Personal property.** Belongings, creative works, tools, savings. **Protected.** Private property still exists in Creditism.

**Stewardship homes.** Your primary residence. Yours to live in and maintain. Succession is **continuity of occupancy, not hereditary title** — see the canonical block at 2.5. *Working proposal.*

**Cooperative production assets.** Workshops, fabrication facilities, co-op infrastructure. Listed on Community Ledgers and checked out under community-set usage rights and time limits. Not owned by the workforce, not owned by anyone. **Settled — see 2.6.**

**Commons resources.** Land, watersheds, atmosphere, digital infrastructure, knowledge. **Stewarded democratically** by the communities that depend on them.

**Speculative and rentier claims.** Absentee landlordism, financial derivatives, ownership positions whose value comes primarily from commanding other people's future hours. **These dissolve.** The claim dissolves; the stake converts. What the holder contributed at purchase becomes Credit. Nothing is taken without conversion. See 5.1a. *Principle settled; mechanism open.*

**The normative claim:** rentier and speculative rights should not continue indefinitely merely because existing law recognises them. What the transition dissolves is the recognition, in law and in ledger, of a particular kind of ownership that was always a claim on other people's lives. The assets underneath remain in the world. The fields still grow food. What changes is who holds enforceable demands on the labour required to use them.

**The legal reality, stated plainly:** under current legal systems, compulsory removal or conversion of recognised property rights may constitute expropriation, and would require democratic authority, due process, and in many jurisdictions compensation. That is a fact about existing law, not a refutation of the normative claim. The two are separate questions, and the transition has to answer the second through legitimate process rather than assertion.

Expect the word *confiscation* to be used. The honest response is not to deny the legal category but to argue the normative one, and to insist that legitimate process is what distinguishes a transition from a seizure.

## 2.4 The ledgers

### The Ledger of Life — canonical block

> The Ledger of Life maps the planet's living systems: land, waterways and bodies, species, habitats, ecosystems, biomes, natural resources, ecological condition, and the existing ownership or stewardship claims attached to them. Mapping records what exists. It does not change legal ownership, does not make an asset available, and does not place it under stewardship. Mapping, ownership, availability, and stewardship are four separate fields. Local, Regional, Global, and Group Ledgers record only what those entities have actually reclaimed, been given, made available, operate, or steward.

**Group Ledgers** record group production or service. Enterprises operate through group accounts within the relevant community ledger and stewardship arrangements. Individual proprietors remain distinct from group Enterprises. One-person Enterprises are permitted under governance and CC safeguards. Directing CC to a group in which the director is a member is subject to bounds preventing community spending power from becoming private spending power; exact caps and technical posting remain open.

Real CC expenditure, virtual performance accounts, and resource accounting remain separate. Labor is an input even in a labor-only Enterprise. Actual CC procurement does not avoid virtual economic accountability. Virtual allowance exhaustion does not automatically remove actual CC authority; withdrawal of operating authorization or use rights is a separate community decision. Detailed enforcement and review remain open; see `02`, *production accounting*.

**Personal Private Ledgers** are sovereign accounts.

### Privacy — canonical block

> Public and group flows are auditable. Personal ledgers — identities, balances, possessions, and individual transaction details — remain private, accessible only to the individual or family, except where disclosure is voluntarily authorised or legally required. How aggregate transparency and personal privacy are technically reconciled remains open.

## 2.5 Housing stewardship — canonical block

> **One primary residence per person, plus up to two vacation stewardships.** Three residences is the ceiling. Nobody holds two homes to live in. *The vacation figure is a working proposal, not settled.*
>
> **The ceiling is a response to scarcity, not a moral limit.** Personal Credit is personal. It is never gifted and never inherited, so nobody ever acquires a home out of someone else's accumulation. Community Credit can move between people, and it buys no housing for anyone: only group accounts spend it, and never on a person's residence. A person choosing among available homes is the design working as intended. The number moves with availability: if homes are short, the vacation allowance narrows, and if they are plentiful it can widen. What the ceiling can never do is let one person hold housing that no one lives in while someone else has none.
>
> **The entitlement is per person and begins at eighteen.** Below that age a person lives in a household rather than holding a residence of their own.
>
> **Housing Level qualifies a person for a class of home.** It begins from lived record: caretaking, contribution, and Personal Credit.
>
> **Ledgers never combine. Housing Levels stack for choice.** Every ledger is personal and private for life. Marriage and household are conventions capitalism required and Creditism does not; a person may marry, and it has nothing to do with their ledger. A Housing Level is likewise permanently individual. Nothing transfers, and nobody's level rises because they moved in with someone.
>
> **What stacking changes is the range of homes the group may choose from.** Two adults at Level 5 living apart hold a 5-rated one-bedroom and a 5-rated two-bedroom: two dwellings between them. Agreeing to live together puts them in one. Because that uses less housing rather than more, together they qualify for a better dwelling than either could take alone. A 7-rated two-bedroom, or a 6-rated three-bedroom, or a 5-rated four-bedroom. Rating trades against bedrooms. Single occupancy of a large home still requires a correspondingly high level. *The exact schedule is a working figure.*
>
> **Both people are still Level 5.** Neither level moved, and neither will, until its holder spends Credit to raise it, directly or by improving the home they hold. The stack lives in the qualification, never on the ledger.
>
> **The incentive is the point.** Sharing a home releases a dwelling for someone else, and the reward for releasing it is a better one. Every unit freed this way is a unit that does not have to be built, and a candidate for short-stay use.
>
> **This is also why the entitlement is per person.** Couples end. When two people who shared a home separate, each still holds their own level, unchanged, and each falls back to what that level qualifies them for alone. Nothing has to be untangled, because nothing was ever entangled.
>
> **Housing Level can also be raised with Credit.** Once a starting level is set, a person may spend Credit to raise it, either directly or by improving the home they hold. Improvement raises the level because the improvement stays with the house when the person leaves it, and the level is what travels with them instead. This is how an additional residence is obtained: not by paying a price for a dwelling, but by holding the level that dwelling requires. **Credit spent this way deletes like any other purchase.** *Working proposal. Rate, ceiling, and whether a level decays are open.*
>
> **Succession is continuity of occupancy.** When the stewards of a home die, anyone who lived there with them may claim it, by swapping their current residence for it. That claim holds **even if the claimant's own Housing Level sits below the home's rating** — lived occupancy is the qualification, not standing.
>
> **The claim is use-based and it expires.** A household that moved out and took another home has no claim on the one they left. If someone entitled to a family home declines it and chooses elsewhere instead, ordinary Housing Level rules apply to that choice.
>
> **Vacation stewardship does not pass at all.** Like Credit, it ends. On the steward's death the home returns to availability for a living person. It is not a relative's inheritance. There is no need for one.
>
> **Why this cannot concentrate.** The ceiling is a count of dwellings, not a budget, so no amount of Credit moves a person past the number in force. Credit buys the level, and the level buys nothing but housing: it does not transfer, it carries no claim on anyone's labour, and it does not pass at death. Succession moves a person *into* a home. A dwelling passing to a surviving occupant is one household continuing in place, not an estate accumulating.

**Under consideration for allocating genuinely scarce homes:** ranked preferences — a nominated top three — queueing for specific dwellings, and prior stewards holding first claim. *Open. None of these is settled.*

## 2.6 Community assets and checkout — canonical block

> **Cooperative and productive assets are listed on Community Ledgers.** They are not owned by their workforce and not owned by an individual. The community holds them, and they are **checked out**.
>
> **Any club, cooperative, council, or group of local citizens may check an asset out,** and the checkout is visible on the ledger. Usage rights and time limits are set by the community that holds the asset.
>
> **Community Credit purchases enter the same system.** When a club, cooperative, or council spends Community Credit on something, the purchase is recorded on the Community Ledger and checked out to the group that bought it. The group holds use, not title. **When the group disbands, the asset returns to availability** for another person or group.
>
> **A disbanded group's assets are not stranded.** Returning to availability means available: another group may check the asset out, or the community may list it in the Exchange and sell it. A specialised machine from a venture that failed does not sit collecting dust by rule. What it recovers depends on whether anyone else can use it, which is a fact about the machine rather than a rule of the system.
>
> **Community assets are lent to people, never given.** A community may make anything in its inventory available for a member to take out and use. It does not gift the thing away. **From a community to a person, permanent transfer happens only through sale**, where it is priced, recorded, and auditable. Gifting runs the other way: a person may give a thing to a community, and there is no reason to restrict that.
>
> **Communities can sell to communities.** What can be sold may be sold by one community to another for Community Credit, and the same applies at regional and planetary scale.

> **The mature Exchange reallocates already-held goods and assets.** Personal and community account holders may list eligible held assets, including zero-basis assets, at negotiated prices. Buyer Credit deletes, with separate seller issuance bounded by remaining recognized basis and buyer expenditure. Community-held assets are eligible for disposition under their stewardship rules. A later ordinary resale does not charge completed historical production costs again. Produced output cannot avoid recognized production-cost priority by being labeled an Exchange transaction.

**Capital basis and stewardship are separate.** An eligible actual CC purchase can establish recoverable basis under the open recording protocol. Virtual capital acquisition or use entries create neither basis nor ownership. Basis recovered through production reduces what remains for later Exchange disposition: a fully recovered community oven has zero remaining acquisition basis even while it remains useful or changes Enterprise users. Historical acquisition expenditure is not counted repeatedly; genuine maintenance, energy, labor, and resource use remain distinct. Allocation, depreciation, timing, and disposal posting remain open.

**Funding terms govern closure and recovered CC.** Returnable project-directed funds and other enforceable obligations settle first; remaining unrestricted balances return under the governing community arrangements. A returnable Regional grant to a Local Enterprise returns to Regional. Recovery keeps the restrictions of its originating expenditure and follows that agreement's retention, redirection, or return terms. Community assets remain community-held, and recognized labor is not canceled. Posthumous project refunds remain open and create no CC inheritance right.

# PART THREE — THE AYU PROPERTY BOUNDARY

**The most commonly misunderstood material in the pack.**

## 3.1 Four separate statuses

| Status | Means |
|---|---|
| **Mapped** | Recorded in a ledger. Known to exist. |
| **Owned** | Under existing legal title, unchanged. |
| **Available** | Offered by its holder for use, on stated terms. |
| **Stewarded** | Transferred into a stewardship relationship with obligations. |

An asset can be mapped and still be privately owned, unavailable, and unstewarded. **Mapping is knowledge, not transfer.**

## 3.2 What AYU listing does not do

**A house listed during AYU does not become freely claimable by any AYU member.**

**AYU is not a fully populated planetary marketplace.** It is a participation network with a growing inventory record.

Assets operating during AYU still face taxes, maintenance costs, mortgages, rent, insurance, and every other obligation the existing legal and economic system imposes.

AYU Phase 2 Marketplace eligibility depends on no remaining beneficial sale claim, not newness, previous use, or legal title alone. Eligible donated or fully recognized output may qualify when voluntarily available; price is only in AYU Credit and buyer Credit deletes. AYU Exchange permits agreed combinations of value and AYU Credit transfers. Separate contributor recognition must not duplicate the same already-recognized labor; legitimate private input and ownership claims can remain. Evidence, restrictions, and claim disputes require an open protocol. This transitional claim test is not the universal venue test for mature Creditism.

## 3.3 How things actually move

Communities, businesses, and property owners may **gradually** transfer assets or stewardship into a local structure.

Transfers can happen **partially and conditionally.**

Existing owners might cash out gradually for Credits.

Transition agreements may include trust conditions or reversal provisions while the system is being proven. Nobody is asked to make an irreversible transfer into an unproven structure.

*Legal instruments for asset transition are not designed beyond naming the existing tools. See `05`, C3.*

# PART FOUR — TRANSITION

## 4.1 The frame

**Three Horizons. Five Stages. Three Phases.**

**Horizons** — the civilisational crossing. H1: Game A in decline. H2: the transition, where AYU lives. H3: Creditism established. Horizons overlap rather than succeed each other.

**Stages** — the five societal scaling steps below.

**Phases** — the three AYU implementation phases. See `03`.

Never use one word for another.

## 4.2 The principle: parallel infrastructure

**You don't ask permission.**

You don't wait for the existing system to reform itself. The people in charge of a system rarely dismantle it voluntarily, because their power and identity are bound up in it.

Email did not lobby the postal service. Wikipedia did not lobby Britannica. Linux did not lobby Microsoft. None required permission from the system they displaced. None had to win a direct confrontation. They made the old model's core assumptions increasingly optional.

**Not war. Phase change.**

The printing press did not overthrow the Church. It made the Church's monopoly on scripture impossible to maintain at scale. The transition was not painless — it produced religious wars, censorship, and centuries of institutional struggle. Phase changes are not painless. But the direction was set.

**Transitions are not inevitable.** Many fail. Many better systems get crushed or co-opted before reaching scale. A transition becomes *likely* when a better trust layer exists, people can see it working, and the old system keeps generating the conditions that push people toward alternatives. Two of those three are visible now. The third is what AYU is being built to prove.

### 4.2a Why the ledger has to be visible

Parallel infrastructure does something beyond demonstration. It manufactures **common knowledge**.

It is not enough for people to believe there is an alternative. They have to know that other people know. Nobody moves first alone, and a belief held privately by millions has the coordinating power of a belief held by nobody. A visible membership, a public record of participation, a count anyone can check: those are not promotion. They are the mechanism by which private agreement turns into coordinated capacity.

That is also the answer to why AYU builds a network before it builds an economy.

**Scattered agreement has no force. Organised agreement does.** Creditism does not abolish power, and any claim that it does should be treated as a red flag. It democratises the two kinds of power that decide outcomes: the power to organise, and the power to compensate.

## 4.3 What can begin right now

Legally available today, in most jurisdictions, with existing tools, without legislative approval:

- A **founding pod** can run a contribution ledger, recognising unpaid labour and building a shared record of real human capacity
- A **cooperative** can allocate internal participatory budgets on Community-Credit-inspired principles
- A **housing trust** can convert ownership toward stewardship through land trust structures that already work
- A **city** can pilot participatory budgeting credits
- A **regional network** can map unused tools, buildings, land, and skills
- A **school, farm, maker space, or care network** can log real contribution, proving that enormous amounts of real value are invisible to the existing ledger

> **On payment:** during Phase 1, **AYU Points cannot be used as payment.** From Phase 2, eligible AYU Marketplace prices are exclusively in AYU Credit and buyer Credit deletes; AYU Exchange accepts agreed combinations of value and any AYU Credit transfers. Earlier participatory allocations are CC-inspired, not actual mature CC. A possible Phase 2 AYU CC experiment is a working proposal, not operational or guaranteed.

None of this replaces the whole system at first. That is the point.

## 4.4 The five Stages

### Stage 1 — Founding Networks (now)

CPF, AYU, early adopters joining a parallel ledger, testing governance from the inside, building infrastructure before the world is paying attention.

The work is unglamorous: tools, protocols, documented failures, onboarding, legal and operational structures.

Most movements die here. The results are slow. The temptation to declare victory prematurely is intense. The discipline is to keep building when the world isn't watching.

### Stage 2 — Pilot Communities

Neighbourhoods. Cooperatives. Intentional communities. Small towns running parallel contribution ledgers.

A pilot does not replace an economy. It tests five claims: that people can verify participation without a boss; that communities can allocate shared resources without capital ownership; that contribution can be logged without becoming surveillance; that disputes can be resolved without reverting to hierarchy; and that the system produces more trust than it consumes.

**Tests, not demonstrations.** A pilot designed to prove something can only ever confirm it. That is not evidence, and a serious reader knows it.

Every pilot states in advance what it is testing, what failure would look like, which harms stop the trial, what data gets published whichever way the result falls, and how the rules get revised afterward. The purpose is not to show that Creditism is right. It is to find out where it is wrong while the stakes are still small enough to absorb.

**Mondragon** is the useful reference, and it has to be cited precisely, because the sloppy version of the citation is easy to demolish.

Founded 1956 in the Basque Country. In 2024 the federation reported €11.213 billion in sales and 70,085 people employed across roughly a hundred cooperatives.

**Employed, not members.** Those are different numbers and the gap is the honest part. Mondragon cooperatives own subsidiaries that are not themselves cooperatives, mostly retail in Spain and industrial plants abroad. Depending on sector and year, worker-members run between about a third and a half of total employment. Anyone who cites *seventy thousand members* will be corrected by the first reader who knows the case, and will lose the argument they were actually making.

**It has also failed inside itself.** Fagor Electrodomésticos, the founding industrial cooperative, entered insolvency in 2013 and was not rescued by the federation. Displaced worker-members were substantially, though not entirely, absorbed into other cooperatives.

So the claim is narrower than the one usually made, and more useful. Democratic ownership survives complexity, scale, and seventy years of market pressure. It does not make an enterprise immortal, and it thins at the edges under competitive pressure. Both halves are evidence, and the second half is what AYU has to answer.

Analogues: Emilia-Romagna's cooperative networks, Quebec's solidarity economy, worker-owned enterprises worldwide. None are Creditism. All prove a component.

### Stage 3 — City and Regional Networks

Cities need not begin by replacing legal tender. They can begin with participatory budgeting credits, civic contribution ledgers, local procurement networks, cooperative voucher systems, community benefit accounts.

Cities also have underused fiscal tools: differential taxation on non-resident wealth extraction to fund local buyouts of land and productive capital; preferential procurement favouring collective local ownership; municipal land trusts moving housing out of speculative cycles. None requires national reform.

**Existing evidence:** the **WIR Bank** in Switzerland has run a complementary business currency since 1934, through multiple recessions. **BerkShares** in western Massachusetts demonstrates sustained appetite for regional economic self-determination. The **Bristol Pound** wound down its digital scheme in 2020 — a real failure worth naming. Durability depends on institutional depth, genuine community benefit, and offering something the national currency cannot.

When fifty or a hundred communities coordinate, network effects become significant. This is where the existing system notices and responds.

### Stage 4 — National Adoption

Eventually a nation commits. Likely a smaller country already in monetary or debt crisis, where political conditions allow structural reform stable systems resist. A prediction, not a guarantee.

**A debt jubilee.** Historical precedent is substantial. Ancient Mesopotamia had them. Biblical Israel encoded them. Athens had one under Solon in the sixth century BCE. Graeber documented them as the mechanism by which civilisations released accumulated debt tension. No modern economy has attempted a society-wide clean slate on anything like that scope. Modern states do cancel, restructure, and write down debt, in categories and in crises — sovereign restructurings, wartime write-offs, student and medical relief. What has gone missing is the periodic, expected, whole-society reset that everyone can plan around. That absence is the anomaly.

**Currency restructuring.** The intended architecture uses one nominal Credit denomination, with agreed currency-specific conversion rates for national currencies, conceptually comparable to different currencies entering the euro. AYU and mature Credit have nominal 1:1 denomination while their issuance, transfer, deletion, account, and jurisdictional rules may differ. AYU's proposed basket supplies an in-network unit reference; exact currency rates, historical valuation windows, composition, and governance require agreement and modeling. This supersedes literal one-unit-of-every-national-currency-to-one-Credit conversion. No illustrative rate is approved.

**Existing assets convert at purchase price, not market price.** *Working proposal.* Stocks, bonds, real estate, and other held assets convert into Credit at what was paid for them rather than at their market value on the day of activation.

Two purposes. It leaves existing asset markets as undisturbed as possible, so the approach of transition doesn't set off a scramble. And it removes any advantage from repositioning, betting, or trading in anticipation of the changeover. **Nobody should have to game the transition in order not to lose by it.**

Activation ratio, cost-basis evidence, treatment of assets held without a clear purchase price, and treatment of gifts and inheritances remain open.

**A share carries a claim on control as well as a claim on money.** This rule converts the monetary claim only. The control claim converts separately. See §5.3.

> **The recorded member entitlement remains a distinct formal surrender route.** It is bounded by verified direct issuance plus the applicable Early Member bonus and remains the established working proposal at `03`, *member transition entitlement*. Conversion and legitimate cross-boundary spending are different events. Broader treatment of AYU holdings above that route is under review; no additional conversion entitlement is approved. No new valuation benefit follows from this note.

**Housing reclamation.** Phased. Homeowners become caretakers. Investment property and corporate landlordism phase out with fair terms.

**Community Credit replacing taxation.** Progressive. Some tax mechanisms persist transitionally.

**Pensions and the initial allocation.** Pension obligations do not carry forward. Every person receives an initial allocation of Credit by age, and Universal Income continues for life. Canonical block at 5.1b.

**A possible fiat baseline first.** *Working transition option:* a jurisdiction may introduce a fiat-denominated universal baseline **before** full Credit activation. This could immediately reduce inequality and survival insecurity while Credit infrastructure, conversion rules, productive-capacity models, and legal structures are prepared. It may also expand the fiat supply, create price pressure, or weaken the currency's exchange value. Those effects must be modeled rather than denied, and weighed against effects on productive capacity, household security, imports, exchange rates, and eventual Credit conversion. The eventual conversion may still preserve the intended relative accounting, because existing currency and asset values would convert through the established exchange framework. **Possible, not mandatory.**

**Other available levers:** corporate share reform converting ownership into non-transferable per-employee voting shares (Varoufakis); pension fund and endowment redirection toward cooperative buyouts (Korten, McDonald, Fullerton); legal prohibition on business ownership of residential housing with phased exit; municipal commons tools.

**National adoption doesn't have to invent its tools.** They already exist, dispersed across decades of strategic work. The transition is the act of fitting them together on a coherent game board.

Expect resistance: capital flight, market disruption, international institutional pressure, possibly sanctions. The first mover needs preparation for economic warfare.

### Stage 5 — Planetary Coordination

The longest and most uncertain. International coordination, Community Credit issued at planetary level for global commons: atmospheric stability, ocean health, biodiversity, climate restoration. The Ledger of Life operational at planetary scale.

Not a destination. An ongoing practice.

# PART FIVE — WHAT HAPPENS TO EXISTING WEALTH

Three separate conversations. A retiree, a small business owner with a building, and a hedge fund manager with derivatives are not in the same situation.

## 5.1 Ordinary household security — protected

**Your home stays yours.** You live there, you are safe there, you cannot be removed. Mortgage gone. Property tax gone. Insurance premium gone. The savings embodied in ordinary homeownership translate into participation power under rules designed to protect household security without preserving speculative markets.

### 5.1b Pensions and the initial allocation — canonical block

> **Pension obligations do not carry forward, and no past redistribution claim continues.** A pension is a scheme for surviving old age inside a system where survival had to be bought. Creditism does not have that problem to solve. Nobody receives welfare, so nobody needs an exemption from it either.
>
> **Every person receives an initial allocation of Credit at activation.** The figure is **3,000 Credit multiplied by the person's age, to a peak at fifty**, and reduces by 3,000 for each year above fifty. A person of thirty receives 90,000. A person of fifty receives 150,000. A person of seventy receives 90,000. It is the same rule for everyone and it is not means-tested, contribution-tested, or claim-based.
>
> **The initial allocation is additional to conversion.** Primary-residence equity, savings under agreed currency-specific conversion rates, and released property convert on their own terms. The allocation is a starting position, not a settlement of anything owed.
>
> **Universal Income continues for life, from that day forward.** The baseline flow of Personal Credit does not stop at any age, is not reduced by age, and is not conditional on having worked. Housing carries no rent, mortgage, property tax, or insurance premium. That, and not a pension, is what an older person's security rests on.
>
> **Why it climbs, then tapers.** The allocation is a starting stock, and a stock is sized to the span of life it has to carry. Two measures meet at fifty. Below it, the figure rises because it recognises years already lived and contributed under the old system. Above it, the figure falls because the stock is supporting a shorter remaining span. The amount also tracks the annual logic of Universal Income rather than standing apart from it. This is not a settlement of anything owed and not a reward for having lived long; it is a quantity matched to a life.
>
> *Working proposal. The schedule and the crossover age are working figures.*

**Your savings convert** under rules **designed to preserve ordinary household security**, using agreed currency-specific rates into the common Credit denomination. Where savings are an eligible asset with a purchase price, the recorded asset-conversion proposal recognizes that amount under its applicable valuation and currency rules rather than a speculative transition price. Exact rates, valuation windows, eligibility, and legal treatment remain open.

**A conversion relationship is not a purchasing-power guarantee.** What a Credit buys depends on pricing and issuance, which have not been modeled. The current intended common denomination does not promise stable prices, automatic account conversion, or access to particular goods.

**Equity contributed, not sticker price.** Where an asset converts at what was paid for it, that means the equity the holder put in, not the headline price of the thing. A property bought for 500,000 with 100,000 down converts on 100,000.

### 5.1a Housing at transition — canonical block

> **You choose which residences you keep, up to three.** One primary residence and up to two vacation stewardships. Everything above that is released. A person holding one primary, two vacation homes, and five rentals keeps three of the eight and chooses which.
>
> **The primary residence converts at market value less the mortgage owed.** That equity becomes Personal Credit. The mortgage, the property tax, and the insurance premium all end.
>
> **Retained vacation stewardships convert nothing while they are held.** Their debts, taxes, and premiums end anyway. If the steward later releases the title, conversion happens at that point.
>
> **A released vacation residence converts at what the holder put in.** Equity at purchase plus every principal payment the holder made. Not the market value.
>
> **A released rental converts at equity at purchase alone.** Principal payments do not count, because tenants made them. The owner gets back what the owner put in.
>
> **Three bases, one principle: you get back what you put in, and the primary residence gets more than that.** The home you live in is your own security and is protected in full, appreciation included. Beyond it, appreciation was never yours to keep, and a mortgage other people paid was never your contribution. **Nothing is taken without conversion.** *Working proposal. Release timing is open. See `05` C3.*

## 5.2 Productive assets and enterprise — preserved, restructured

Productive businesses, individual proprietors, and authorized group Enterprises can continue under the applicable transition and stewardship rules. Marketplace buyer Credit deletes rather than transferring to the producer. Verified labor and eligible input-basis recovery are separately authorized issuance; compensation enters production cost and informs asking price. A sale may be below cost or fail altogether, so improved margins are not guaranteed. Commissioned production is permitted, and a community may later dispose of the acquired asset through the Exchange.

Productive cooperatives, farms, studios, workshops, maker spaces all continue. What changes is the extraction layer above them: rent, interest, dividend flowing upward to people who owned without contributing. Remove it and the productive activity underneath often flourishes.

**"Collectively owned" is the wrong frame and is retired.** Cooperative and productive assets sit on Community Ledgers and are checked out under community-set usage rights and time limits. Not owned by the workforce, not owned by anyone. Held, and used. See 2.6.

## 5.3 Rentier and speculative claims — do not survive intact

Derivatives, speculative real estate beyond primary residence, ownership positions generating income without contribution. They are structurally what the transition is ending.

A share bundles two claims: money and control. At transition they separate.

The **monetary claim** converts to Credit at purchase price, under the Stage 4 rule above. The **control claim** does not convert into a tradable form at all. Governance in the successor cooperative becomes non-transferable participation rights, held by the people who work in and are affected by the enterprise. A former shareholder receives the monetary recognition and nothing more. Control follows contribution, not prior ownership. *Working proposal.*

Speculative real estate returns to availability and passes into community stewardship. The equity its holder contributed at purchase converts to Credit under 5.1a. Financial derivatives largely cease to exist because the logic that made them valuable no longer holds.

*Exact conversion mechanics, the qualification rule for participation rights, and legal instruments remain open. Second-residence treatment is now stated at 5.1a as a working proposal. See `05`, C3.*

**The productive economy stays.** The fields still grow food. The machines still make parts. The teachers still teach. What dissolves is the claim structure on top.

# PART SIX — RESISTANCE

The transition will be resisted. Not by evil people defending a system they know is wrong, but by people protecting what they understand to be legitimate interests inside a system whose premises they accept. That makes the resistance more durable, not less.

Predictable forms: legal challenges to pilots; financial pressure on experimenting municipalities; media campaigns framing every experiment as Venezuela or Weimar; institutional pressure from international financial bodies; and the deep cultural resistance of people whose identities are built around accumulation.

The printing press was banned in multiple jurisdictions. Early internet encryption was classified as a munition. Resistance is part of the process, not evidence against it.

**The parallel infrastructure has to be deep enough by the time resistance peaks that unplugging it becomes impractical.** That is what Phase 1 is for.

Parallel infrastructure is the primary strategy because it survives capture and needs no permission. It is not the only mode. Organised civic resistance — consumer boycotts, labour and student strikes, constitutional assemblies — runs alongside it.

# PART SEVEN — REFUSAL AND OPT-OUT

Phase-specific.

**Now, at the AYU stage.** Entirely voluntary. Opt-in. You can observe, experiment, leave, and return.

**In pilot communities.** Contractual and community-based. You agree to a community's norms when you join, as with any cooperative charter.

**At municipal adoption.** If a city democratically adopts Creditist tools, those apply the way any public policy applies. No different from a zoning law you disagree with.

**At national transition.** A national system cannot say "you can opt out" of roads, housing law, currency, and property regimes, any more than you can opt out of the current ones. National transition requires democratic legitimacy, legal authority, rights protections, and constitutional safeguards. It requires majorities, process, courts, and time.

**At the planetary level.** Opt-out exists culturally and locally. But no one can privately appropriate the atmosphere, the oceans, or the commons that sustain all life.

## The hard cases

Addiction. Severe antisocial behaviour. Deep despair. People who, even in a fair system, will not contribute and will draw on common resources.

They exist. They always have. The threat of starvation never solved them; it made them worse.

Creditism does not claim to fix the hard cases. It claims to stop using them as the justification for a system that punishes the other ninety-nine percent. Baseline access remains. Community responsibility remains. What goes is the moral excuse.
