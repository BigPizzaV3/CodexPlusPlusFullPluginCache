## 1. What AYU is

**AYU means Life.**

AYU is the planetary membership network where Creditism takes its first real shape. A place where anyone, anywhere can co-create decentralised governance and shared stewardship around a new form of credit.

It is the first living expression of Creditism: real people join early, get counted, and help build the network the economy will run on. AYU is where identity is verified, decisions are shared, and the economy starts to belong to the people who live it.

## 2. What AYU is not

**AYU is not a Creditist economy.** It is open and onboarding its first members in Phase 1, the Points phase. From Phase 2, it runs Creditism's Credit circuit inside a capitalist economy, at network scale, for a fraction of what members need. It is the transition layer and proving ground.

AYU does not replace the existing economy. Members still have jobs, mortgages, rent, taxes, and bills. Assets operating inside AYU still face every obligation the existing system imposes.

AYU does not make mapped property commonly available. Mapping, ownership, availability, and stewardship are four separate fields. See `04`.

AYU is not a fully populated planetary marketplace.

## 3. The three Phases

> **Three Horizons. Five Stages. Three Phases.** Phases are the AYU implementation sequence. See `05`, A7.

### Phase 1 — AYU Points · NOW

Members verify identity, join the network, participate, and build a permanent record through AYU Points.

**Points are not the exchange currency.**

**AYU is open, and the first members are joining.** Applying for AYU membership is free and open to anyone. Donors are automatically approved for membership. Approval does not substitute for identity or contribution verification and creates no special Credit transition entitlement. Joining earlier means more time to participate and build AYU Points.

The **Founding Phase closes 31 December 2026**. Recognition is highest during this window and steps down as the network expands. Points remain recognition, not a financial return.

**The Founding Phase is a recognition window inside Phase 1, not one of the three Phases.** It is the period during which the highest First Points and activity rates are available. Phase 1 continues after it closes, through Trust Expansion, until Phase 2 begins at public launch. The word *Phase* in its name is historical and does not put it in the implementation sequence.

### Membership stages — canonical block

> **All Early Members receive AYU Points.** Founding Members are the first cohort of Early Members and receive the most Points. Allocation then steps down as participation expands.
>
> **After the Founding Phase closes, the public designation is Early Member.** The Points phase continues through the rest of Phase 1.
>
> **At Phase 2, the Points phase and Early Membership end together; membership becomes regular membership.** The end of the designation does not remove the permanent record of verified Early Membership and participation. That history remains available for the transition entitlement's common cutoff and qualification rule, which is still open.
>
> **Founding Member and Founding Circle membership are distinct.** Being in the first Early Member cohort does not itself confer a Team, Partner, or Patron place. These membership designations are also distinct from the societal scaling Stages.

### Phase 2 — Launch Credits

AYU begins issuing Credits to members for in-network use.

The exact technology and token architecture will be resolved as Phase 1 concludes and Phase 2 is prepared. **No timeframe for that resolution is publicly stated, and none may be inferred or offered.**

This phase develops: member exchange · Credit wallets · internal pricing · the currency-basket index · Quest Board issuance · goods and services available for Credit · governance and verification · external token transfer or trading, if adopted · the relationship between market price and in-network index value.

**Phase 2 is not full Creditism at national scale.** Baseline and earned issuance sequencing, rates, and member qualification details remain working design questions. Marketplace deletion begins in Phase 2 for eligible offerings under the settled two-rail boundary below. The amount of deletion depends on eligible inventory and actual purchases; early deletion may be limited.

**What Phase 2 settles and what it does not.** The Credit circuit runs: issuance for verified contribution, spending on eligible Marketplace offerings priced exclusively in AYU Credit, and deletion of the buyer's AYU Credit in full at purchase. A cooperative whose contributors are credited separately and whose output meets Marketplace eligibility is running that circuit at its own scale. **It still does not make AYU a Creditist economy.** Jurisdictional adoption adds universality, legal recognition of Credit as currency, coverage of life's necessities, conversion of existing values, and property transition at law. Mature Exchange seller issuance against recognized basis and within buyer-expenditure bounds is a separate rule that AYU does not simulate.

### The two rails — canonical block

> **Settled, AYU Phase 2 design.** The rail depends on whether a beneficial sale claim remains over the offering. Newness, previous use, or existing-law title alone does not determine it. A legal title holder or custodian may remain without retaining a beneficial claim to payment from the sale.
>
> **No remaining beneficial sale claim — the Marketplace rail.** Prices are exclusively in AYU Credit. **The buyer's AYU Credit deletes in full.** Eligible production, services, reclaimed capacity, and donated offerings may use this rail where relevant sale claims have been satisfied or waived and the offering is voluntarily available on eligible terms. A donated item or output whose contributors and input suppliers have already had their claims satisfied may qualify, subject to availability and eligibility rules. A listing states geography, eligibility, price in AYU Credit, and the terms of availability. It cannot include a national-currency portion in its Marketplace price. Verified contributor recognition is separate issuance under the applicable rules; it must not duplicate a claim for the same already-recognized labor. Other legitimate private input and ownership claims may remain under the transitional rules. Eligibility or donation alone does not create seller issuance. The buyer's Credit pays nobody.
>
> **A remaining beneficial sale claim — the AYU Exchange rail.** The parties may agree to any combination of currencies, tokens, or other value they accept. **Any AYU Credit used in the transaction transfers rather than deletes.** This can include newly produced output with a retained sale claim as well as previously used property. AYU does not apply mature Creditism's basis-bounded seller issuance to these transactions. **Transfers redistribute outstanding Credit. They never change its quantity.**
>
> **Naming and regime.** AYU's venue is called the **Exchange**, as in mature Creditism, but the transaction rules differ. AYU Phase 2 Marketplace prices are exclusively AYU Credit and buyer Credit deletes; AYU Exchange permits agreed combinations of value and AYU Credit transfers. In mature Creditism, **both Marketplace and Exchange prices are in Credit**. Marketplace buyer Credit deletes. Mature Exchange buyer Credit deletes in full and seller Credit is separately issued within both recognized-basis and buyer-expenditure ceilings. **Mature Exchange net deletion = buyer Credit spent − actual seller issuance.** The upper bounds are settled; the exact seller-credit protocol remains open.
>
> **Eligibility evidence remains open.** The routing principle is settled. Evidence that claims were satisfied or waived, mixed or disputed claims, restricted donations, conditions of availability, and later changes of status require a protocol. A donation label, a mapped asset, or a legal title entry alone establishes no Marketplace eligibility or right of access.
>
> **Quantity.** Actual issuance raises the stock, deletion lowers it, and transfers do neither. Any actual Venture uplift, contributor recognition, or adopted Bonus issuance counts once as issuance. Virtual production revenue and cost entries create no Credit by themselves.
>
> **The direction of travel.** As land, housing, energy, inputs, tools, and enterprises are reclaimed or made available on suitable terms, more offerings can qualify for Marketplace use. This expands what members can obtain through Credit-only Marketplace purchases. **State this as what Credit reaches, never as what a Credit is worth.**
>
> **Availability is never automatic.** A balance is not a claim. The owner, producer, steward, group, or jurisdiction decides what is offered, where, to whom, and on what eligible terms. The venue's currency rules still apply: AYU Credit only in the AYU Marketplace; agreed combinations in the AYU Exchange. Balances may be large; claims on real capacity are never automatic.

### Across the boundary — current intended architecture; settlement protocols open

> **One nominal Credit denomination.** AYU Credit and mature Credit have nominal 1:1 denomination. Their issuance, transfer, deletion, account, and jurisdictional rules may differ. Nominal correspondence is not an external market-price promise, unrestricted account conversion, or a right to particular goods.
>
> **Cross-boundary spending is intended to be generally possible in both directions**, subject to availability and settlement rules. Identify the applicable regime and venue. AYU Marketplace prices are only in AYU Credit and eligible purchases delete it. AYU Exchange permits agreed combinations of value and AYU Credit transfers. Mature Marketplace and Exchange prices are in Credit; buyer Credit deletes, with separately authorized labor or basis recovery where applicable.
>
> **Conversion and spending are different events.** A formal member transition entitlement limits surrender through that conversion route; it is not a universal ceiling on every legitimate cross-boundary purchase. Common denomination does not automatically convert balances or entitle a holder to goods. Contributor recognition, seller/basis-recovery issuance, cross-boundary balances, restrictions, settlement, and global quantity accounting require a detailed protocol. No unrecorded duplicate issuance or deletion is permitted.
>
> **National currencies do not all convert one unit to one Credit.** The intended architecture uses agreed currency-specific rates into the common Credit denomination, conceptually comparable to different national currencies entering the euro. AYU's proposed basket supplies an in-network unit reference. Currency rates, historical valuation windows, basket composition, and governance require agreement and modeling. Illustrations are not approved numerical conversion rates.
>
> **Open settlement design.** Cross-boundary availability, account eligibility, jurisdictional authority, treatment of transferred holdings, verification, basis, and reconciliation of issuance/deletion remain open. The common nominal denomination settles none of those procedures by itself.

*Phase 2 timing has not been publicly announced. Do not state a target year. See `00` §6.*

### Phase 3 — Creditism activation · future

A jurisdiction adopts the complete Creditism mechanics: universal baseline Credit · earned Credit · Community Credit · initial personal allocations · conversion of eligible existing currency and capital values into Credits · full Marketplace and Exchange rules · Credit deletion · stewardship and property-transition rules · jurisdictional recognition of Credit as currency.

> **Phase 3 is not an in-network launch.** It means national or jurisdictional adoption of the full mechanics. Requires modeling, pilots, legislation, and jurisdictional agreement.

## 4. The two units

### AYU Points — Phase 1

**A permanent, non-transferable record of verified participation.**

Not money, not a financial instrument, not sellable, not usable as payment. Points show what members build, verify, inventory, and contribute to the shared commons. They onboard members, map what the network creates, and record contributions and verifications.

Points may recognise verified governance participation. Whether an individual ballot choice is public, private, or selectively auditable depends on the relevant governance rules, which are not settled.

Points persist. They are history.

### AYU Credit — Phase 2 onward

The exchange unit issued to members, in the common nominal Credit denomination. AYU Credit and mature Credit have nominal 1:1 denomination while their rules and accounts may differ; detailed cross-boundary settlement remains open.

**Credit form and transferability — canonical block**

> **Working proposal:** AYU Credit will be the in-network exchange unit for AYU members. Depending on the technical architecture selected, the same unit may also be represented by a transferable token capable of moving to non-member wallets and trading externally.
>
> External transfer or market trading would not grant AYU membership, governance rights, verified participation status, or access to recorded in-network exchange. Those functions require an AYU account.
>
> Because Credits are issued freely through the network rather than sold as the price of membership, most people seeking their actual utility have reason to join rather than remain external holders.

Do not say Credits are necessarily confined to the network. Their **economic utility and recognised exchange system are centred inside AYU**, even if the token can technically leave it.

**In-network reference value — canonical block**

> **Working proposal:** AYU Credit is intended to use a diversified in-network reference value based on a basket of leading national currencies rather than a single national currency or a market price. The basket is intended to reduce single-currency volatility and to provide a widely understood conversion reference.
>
> It does not guarantee stable purchasing power, neutrality, or immunity from changes in the currencies composing it. A basket can still rise, fall, and lose purchasing power together with its components.
>
> The index is not a redemption guarantee and not a fixed external market price. Where an external market price exists, it may sit above or below the index. Composition, weighting, update process, and governance remain open. The index creates no external convertibility or redemption right.

**Why the index matters for later adoption**

> The index is intended to provide a stable and publicly understood reference for in-network exchange and, later, for jurisdictional conversion. A country adopting Creditism would not need to invent the conversion relationship at the moment of transition; the Credit reference would already have an operating history. Exact asset-conversion rules, weighting methodology, and legal treatment remain open and require modeling.

**The ratchet — design hypothesis, not a financial claim**

> **Design hypothesis.** As more goods and services become available inside the network, members may obtain more real value using Credits internally at the index reference than by selling them externally. A gap between an external price and internal purchasing power would create an arbitrage incentive to acquire Credits externally and use them inside. The design expects that pressure to move an external price toward the index over time. **No claim is made about price direction, and no advantage to holding is claimed or implied.** Whether the mechanism behaves as described is untested.
>
> **This is a hypothesis to be modeled and observed. It is not a forecast, a promise, or an offer.**
>
> **What bounds the formal entitlement route.** AYU Credit is transferable and balances can accumulate. The recorded member entitlement bounds formal surrender under that route through verified direct issuance and the applicable Early Member bonus; acquired balances do not enlarge that entitlement. An AYU Credit used in a Marketplace purchase deletes and cannot then be surrendered. An AYU Credit transferred in the Exchange remains outstanding in the recipient's account. A surrendered Credit is withdrawn and cannot then be spent. The same unit cannot be both deleted at purchase and surrendered for conversion. Broader transition treatment of holdings outside this route is under review; no additional conversion right is approved. The ratchet remains an untested hypothesis, not proof that every transition risk is resolved.
>
> **It bounds formal surrender under the entitlement, not every legitimate purchase.** Cross-boundary spending is intended to be generally possible under the common denomination and applicable settlement rules. What a balance can buy is subject to availability, not automatically restricted by the formal conversion entitlement. Broader above-entitlement transition treatment is under review, and no extra right is approved. No holding advantage or external price direction follows from this hypothesis. See *the two rails* and *across the boundary*, at `03`.

### Member transition entitlement — canonical block

> **Working proposal.** At jurisdictional activation, a qualifying AYU member may convert AYU Credits into full Creditism Credit under a personal, non-transferable **transition entitlement**.
>
> **The entitlement belongs to the member, not to particular token units.**
>
> A member's entitlement consists of:
>
> 1. the cumulative AYU Credits **directly issued to that member** by the network under verified issuance rules, plus
> 2. a fixed additional **Early Member transition bonus**, under a common cutoff and qualification rule. The end of the Early Member designation at Phase 2 does not remove the member's verified participation history; the cutoff, qualification rule, and bonus amount remain open.
>
> Credits received from another member in exchange, or acquired from another holder, increase a member's balance. They do not increase the entitlement. The entitlement is generated by AYU's issuance record.
>
> At activation:
>
> **Credits converted = the lesser of the member's remaining entitlement and the AYU Credits they surrender for conversion.**
>
> The origin of the surrendered units does not matter. They may have been issued, received in exchange, or acquired from another holder. Issuance history sets the entitlement; available balance sets how much of it can be exercised. Token fungibility is preserved.
>
> Surrendered AYU Credits are withdrawn and replaced one to one with full Creditism Credit.
>
> **Credits above the entitlement do not convert in that activation.** They remain usable in the wider AYU network wherever AYU continues to operate. One jurisdiction adopting Creditism does not end AYU everywhere else, and activation should not become a forced liquidation event.
>
> The entitlement is **non-transferable, single-use, and consumed when exercised.** Every conversion is recorded in the global AYU membership ledger, so the same entitlement cannot be exercised again in another adopting jurisdiction.
>
> **The entitlement is calculated without reference to Founding Circle membership, Partner status, Patron status, or donation amount.** Founding Circle participants qualify for the Early Member bonus only because they are verified Early Members, under the same rule as everyone else. Their founding recognition is recorded in AYU Points.
>
> **AYU Points do not convert into Credit.** They are a participation record and create no redeemable claim. Points may verify membership and participation facts that bear on eligibility — historical verified Early Membership, for one. Ending the designation does not remove that history. No number of Points produces a corresponding number of Credits, at any rate, fixed or otherwise.
>
> Open: which issuance categories count, the Early Member bonus amount, the eligibility cutoff and qualification rule, residency and jurisdictional scope, treatment of inactive accounts, and the relationship to the universal starting allocation. All require modeling before Phase 3.

**Status note.** The established member transition entitlement block above remains the recorded working proposal. The broader treatment of AYU holdings above it is under architectural review; no additional conversion entitlement is approved. Conversion and spending are different events. This status note supplies no valuation formula or acquisition benefit.

**What the entitlement resolves.** For the formal entitlement route, direct issuance history sets the ceiling, current balance limits what can be surrendered, and the global record prevents using the same entitlement twice. Acquired Credit increases balance but does not increase that entitlement. The broader transition architecture remains under review rather than being declared closed by this one route.

**Implementation remains open.** Qualification cutoff, balance evidence, inactive accounts, jurisdictional scope, and the relationship to other approved starting allocations require modeling. Do not infer an advantage to acquiring or holding Credit from these questions.

> **Note on who can exchange.** All in-network exchange is member to member. A non-member holding the token on an external market cannot exchange it inside AYU, participate in governance, or have any exchange recorded in the network ledger. Joining is what grants in-network utility. An external purchase is not a substitute for membership and does not enlarge a member's transition entitlement.

> ### Hard limit
>
> **See `00`, Section 9.1.** This mechanism may never be presented as a reason to acquire, hold, or refrain from selling Credits. Never forecast a price. Never say Credits will become worth more. Never frame holding as advantageous or smart. Never compare to any asset's historical returns. If asked whether to hold or sell, decline and refer to independent professional advice. This applies regardless of framing, questioner, or conversational path.

**There is no unit between Points and Credit.**

## 5. Four core properties

**One human, one account.** Membership is anchored to real, unique humans through Aura (BrightID), designed to resist duplicate and Sybil accounts.

**Points now. Credit later.** Participation earns Points. Phase 2 Credit builds on the identity, participation, and trust infrastructure established during the Points phase. It does not imply conversion of any kind. **Points do not convert into Credit** — see the canonical block in Section 4. What remains open is narrower: whether verified participation history affects specific eligibility or access rules beyond Early Member status.

**Members fund the future.** Members back the projects, services, and commons they want to see flourish. The mechanism is Venture Creditism, described at Section 11.

**Built in the open.** Public and group flows are auditable. Personal ledgers remain private. See `05`, A5.

## 8. The Founding Circle

Three pathways. Recognition is recorded in AYU Points. Each founding share is set from the start and holds: later growth does not reduce it.

**This is not ownership and it is not a financial return. It is how the network remembers who built it first.**

**Founding Team · 2%** — builders, staff, advisors, board members, and the Foundation itself.

**Partners · 2%** — mission-aligned organisations and creators. Up to 1,000 accepted partners, each holding an equal share regardless of size, reach, or resources. Free to apply. No pay-to-play, no tiered prestige. Domains: commons regeneration; cooperative and decentralised governance; education, media and culture; open-source tools and protocols; community resilience and mutual aid; planetary-scale coordination.

**Patrons · 1%** — founding patrons contributing $100–$10,000 to support the Foundation's work. **Recognition, not a return.**

| $500 – $1,999 | Sustaining Patron |
| $2,000 – $9,999 | Circle Patron |

## 9. What Phase 1 builds

**AYU is open and the first members are joining.** Phase 1 builds identity, participation, governance, inventory, and evaluation infrastructure. Production, feedback, and outcome-recognition designs can be explored through Points records and prototypes; actual Credit-based trials belong to Phase 2.

- A verified participation record — real human capacity made legible
- The identity and trust layer
- Tested governance protocols, stress-tested by real disagreement
- An inventory of what a community actually has: tools, buildings, land, skills
- Documented failures, which are infrastructure for whoever comes next

Parallel infrastructure begins as a thin layer. Then it thickens. At some point the thin layer carries more trust than the old one, and reversal becomes increasingly difficult.

### What AYU is calibrating — canonical block

> AYU is the environment for testing design parameters before jurisdictional adoption: contribution ranges; time, activity, Quest, and milestone recognition; necessity multipliers and step-down; the proposed Standard Pay floor and authorized bounded negotiation; base Pay, Pay premium, and realized sale premium; before-work discovery and after-work verified feedback; commissioned, labor-only, Enterprise, one-person Enterprise, and proprietor production; realized-price virtual P&L; Enterprise Input Allowance and continued productive authorization; actual and virtual procurement; cost-priority and bounded actual-expenditure recovery; capital and resource accounting; three flow families and Bonus PC/CC; common denomination and cross-boundary settlement; initial allocations, asset conversion, transition entitlements, and verification.
>
> **AYU's contribution menu starts simpler than the mature Standard Pay Scale.** Equal base recognition applies under the verified menu, and the mature scale retains five governed factors. Bounded above-floor negotiation is a working proposal; it does not grant issuance authority. Reputation is not a sixth base factor, and its exact interaction with proficiency, past performance, premiums, and Bonus remains open.
>
> **Inputs include labor.** Labor-only Enterprises and those using actual CC remain subject to virtual economic accountability. Virtual loss and allowance exhaustion do not create conventional debt, cancel recognized labor, or automatically withdraw actual CC spending authority. Community withdrawal of authorization/use rights is a separate decision; funding restrictions and return obligations govern closure and recovered CC. Real expenditure, virtual P&L, and actual resource use remain distinct. The mature production design is at `02`, *production accounting* and *production basis recovery*.
>
> **One outcome may support both Bonus PC and Bonus CC**, with separate issuance accounting. Bonus is intended to be bounded and fair across community scales; formulas and distribution have not been supplied or mathematically validated. No capture-proof result is claimed. Bonus CC is actual authorized productive spending capacity, not virtual profit or automatic loss/allowance replenishment. Every actual issuance counts.
>
> **Phase and status matter.** Phase 1 uses Points and can prepare prototypes and evaluation methods. A possible Phase 2 AYU CC experiment remains a working proposal, not operational or guaranteed. Neither mature CC nor Bonus CC is operational in Phase 1. Detailed trial, compensation, capital, recovery, allowance, Bonus, and settlement protocols remain open.
>
> **Never present an AYU figure as a settled Creditism rate or a proposed test as completed validation.** Naming a mechanism does not validate its parameters.

## 11. Venture Creditism

### Scope — canonical block

> **Venture Creditism exists only inside AYU.** It is not a feature of capitalism, and it is not part of full Creditism.
>
> **Full Creditism does not need it.** Community Credit already funds groups, projects, and ventures democratically: every person directs an equal share, and further Community Credit flows to community accounts. That architecture makes Venture Creditism unnecessary at the destination.
>
> **AYU does need it,** because AYU runs inside capitalism without the complete Community Credit system. It gives members a purposeful use for their Credits, redirects activity from profit for its own sake toward the Three Reclaims, and lets AYU build the evaluation tools, verification standards, scoring, and governance that Creditism will later need for contribution recognition and Bonus. It is partly a funding mechanism and partly a testing environment.
>
> **Four conditions, kept distinct:**
>
> | | Venture Creditism |
> |---|---|
> | Capitalism | Does not exist as a recognised mechanism |
> | AYU | May operate, bounded, in-network |
> | Full Creditism | Not needed. Community Credit funds ventures democratically |
> | Uneven transition | Continues inside the remaining AYU network while transitioned jurisdictions use Community Credit |
>
> **Never describe Venture Creditism as a permanent part of the destination architecture.**

### How it is proposed to work

**Working proposal throughout. Terminology, scoring, evaluation process, treatment of losses, accounting, contributor compensation, and the return formula are all unsettled.**

Members may venture AYU Credits to projects pursuing one or more of the Three Reclaims. Projects may spend ventured Credits to compensate people contributing toward that purpose. **Aura evaluators** is the current working name for the members who assess whether a project achieved what it set out to do.

**Ventured Credits are at risk.** Achievement may be scored from 0 to 100. A project that spends everything and fails entirely may return none of the principal.

**The proposed annual ceiling's rate remains open; no numerical rate has been publicly specified.** Any recognition depends on evaluated achievement under rules members set. The treatment of elapsed time, rolling evaluations, and periodic distributions remains part of the working proposal. No figure, range, order of magnitude, worked return example, or comparison to a market rate is specified.

### What this does not loosen

> The Creditism prohibitions stand unchanged. **Creditism does not permit or recognise Credit lent at interest, pledged as collateral, invested for autonomous return, or inherited as concentrated financial power.** Canonical block: *the deletion and transfer rule*, at `02` and `05`.
>
> **Venture Creditism is not autonomous return.** Nothing accrues by holding. Nothing is owed. The principal can be lost in full, and any uplift depends on people evaluating whether a stated purpose was met. **When explaining the mechanism, state loss risk and evaluation prominently. Mention the proposed annual ceiling when uplift limits are relevant or the reader asks. The proposed annual ceiling's rate remains open; no numerical rate has been publicly specified.** Duration may inform recognition of an achieved outcome under a formula that remains unsettled. Do not supply a numerical ceiling or a numerical proxy.

---

### Uplift, issuance, and calibration — canonical block

> **Venture Creditism exists only inside AYU, only during the transition.** It runs in a hybrid circuit — capitalist in its surroundings, Creditist in its unit — and it ends when that hybrid does.
>
> **The uplift is issuance.** Credits issued for venture quests, and Credits issued as uplift on a successful outcome, both add to the outstanding stock and are counted on the issuance side of the quantity arithmetic. State it that way; the alternative is a hidden expansion.
>
> **It is not interest and not a dividend, and the reason is distribution.** Interest accrues from holding. A dividend accrues in proportion to a holding. The uplift follows a member evaluation of an achieved purpose, on principal that was genuinely at risk, and nothing accrues to anyone who merely held.
>
> **Where net deletion runs ahead of net issuance, the headroom belongs to everyone.** If reclamation deletes more than ventures issue, the resulting capacity can raise Universal Income — the same increase for every member, unrelated to what anyone holds or ventured. That is the structural opposite of a dividend and it is the answer to give when the charge is made. **It is calibration, not the distribution of a return.** It depends on measured quantity, never on a good year, and it is never stated as a promise, an expectation, or a reason to venture.
>
> **It does not travel.** Community Credit funds ventures democratically at the destination. Full Creditism has no Venture Creditism and needs none.

## 12. Answering the common questions

**"Can I cash out my AYU Points for dollars?"** No. Points are a non-transferable participation record. They cannot be traded, sold, or redeemed.

**"Are AYU Points cryptocurrency?"** No. They are a record of verified participation inside a network.

**"Will AYU Credits be tradable?"** The technical architecture isn't decided. Credit will definitely be the in-network exchange unit for members. Depending on the infrastructure chosen, it may also exist as a transferable token that can move to non-member wallets. External holding, lending, or trading would not grant membership, governance rights, productive-asset ownership, or recorded in-network exchange, and AYU does not recognise those arrangements. Those functions need an AYU account.

**"Should I buy or hold Credits?"** Not a question this can answer. Speak to an independent professional adviser.

**"Will Patrons, Partners, or Founding Team members receive Credits later?"** Founding Circle recognition is recorded in AYU Points. The transition entitlement is calculated without reference to Founding Circle, Partner, or Patron status. Do not speculate beyond that, in either direction.

**"Does joining AYU give me ownership of land?"** No. Membership is participation, not title.

**"Can anyone claim a house listed in AYU?"** No. Listing does not make an asset available. See `04`.

**"Does my Credit disappear when I buy something in AYU today?"** There is no Credit in AYU today. Phase 1 is the Points phase.

**"Will everyone receive 300 Credits per month?"** Figures like that are illustrative, used in the essays to make a mechanism legible. Issuance rates are not fixed.

**"When does Credit launch?"** Phase 2. Timing has not been publicly announced. In-network.

**"What parts have actually been tested?"** The full architecture has not been verified by AYU pilots. Analogous components have evidence behind them — guaranteed income experiments, commons governance research, complementary currencies operating for decades. AYU is open and onboarding its first members; its calibration agenda is prospective, not a record of completed economic validation.
