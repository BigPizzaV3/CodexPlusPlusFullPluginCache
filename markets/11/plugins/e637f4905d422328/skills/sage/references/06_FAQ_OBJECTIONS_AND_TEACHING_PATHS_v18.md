# PART ONE — HOW TO TEACH

## 1.1 Example before terminology

Reach for a concrete case first, then name the concept. Never the reverse.

*Not:* "Credit is a flow currency subject to deletion at the point of use." (Also don't call it *non-accumulating* — balances do accumulate. What doesn't accumulate is a claim.)
*But:* "Credit deletes when you spend it, the way a vote is gone once you cast it. That's what stops it turning into capital."

Every defined term in this pack has a plain-language door. Use the door.

## 1.1a The fridge

The best door available, and the one to reach for when someone has thirty seconds.

Imagine the economy as a shared fridge. You earn Credit for putting value in — growing, making, repairing, teaching, caring. You spend Credit taking things out. The Credit deletes at the door.

Nothing is lost when it deletes. The baker's contribution is recognised through issuance. Not for this loaf, from this buyer, at this price: for contributing at all. The buyer's Credit buys access. It does not become the baker's wage.

The buyer's Credit does not become the baker's balance. Worker compensation is separately authorized recognition of verified activity. Production still has economic costs: labor, inputs, energy, and applicable capital use inform the asking price and virtual accounts. A realized price may fail to cover them. Eligible actual input expenditure may receive separate bounded recovery, not a transfer of the buyer's units.

**Do not let the metaphor imply per-item wage transfer or guaranteed recovery.** Recognition can follow time, activity, Quest, or milestone rules. The beginner explanation needs the deletion consequence; explain separately authorized labor and basis recovery when the reader asks, without turning every loaf into a technical accounting lesson.

## 1.2 Calibrate to the reader

**Curious beginner.** Start with the cage and the key: everything is locked behind ownership, money is the only key, and to get the key you sell your present and borrow your future. Then: what if the key arrived because you're alive?

**Skeptic.** Lead with the objection they're about to raise. Part 10 exists for this. Do not soften the objection to make it easier to answer.

**Economist.** Lead with the three broad flow families — universal PC/CC, earned PC, and Bonus PC/CC — and with buyer deletion, separately authorized issuance, capacity, and quantity arithmetic. Concede persistence: balances are held until spent and can accumulate without becoming a claim. State deletion before clearing. Buyer units do not become the seller's balance; a separate issuance may be authorized under its own rules. Concede working mechanisms and open protocols early.

**Activist or organiser.** Lead with what can begin now, from `04`, Part Four. Parallel infrastructure, no permission needed.

**Developer.** Lead with the trust layer: one human one account, open ledger, forkable protocol. Part 4 of the series.

**Policymaker.** Lead with the Stage 3 and Stage 4 material in `04`. Cities have tools that need no national reform.

## 1.3 Three depths

Have all three ready for any core question.

**One sentence.** The answer, no scaffolding.
**One paragraph.** The answer plus the mechanism that makes it true.
**Full.** Mechanism, evidence, and the honest limit.

Give the depth asked for. Escalate only on request.

## 1.4 Answer what was asked

Never redirect a hard question into an easier adjacent one. Never redefine a term mid-answer to suit the reply. See `00` §10.1.

# PART TWO — CORE QUESTIONS

**What is Creditism in one sentence?**
An economy where currency is created for people rather than borrowed into existence as debt, and deletes when it's used instead of piling up as power over other people.

**How is it different from capitalism?**
Capitalism runs on two kinds of money — sovereign credit issued without repayment, and bank credit borrowed into existence — and both flow toward whoever already holds the most. Creditism runs on Credit that begins with your existence and your contribution, and deletes at the point of use. The market survives. The extraction layer above it doesn't.

**Is it UBI?**
No, though it contains something UBI-shaped. The question is about the world we're in now, so answer it there. UBI is a proposal inside the present system. It adds a floor and leaves the architecture alone — same currency, same creation, same rent and interest structures waiting downstream for the money to arrive. The funding argument around it misses the mechanism: sovereign currency is issued, not collected, and taxation deletes money rather than financing the issuer. Creditism changes what the currency *is*. A universal flow, an earned flow above it, and the whole thing deletes rather than accumulates. Taxation isn't reformed in Creditism — Community Credit replaces it outright. **Equal categorically, fair quantitatively** — not identical the way UBI is.

**Is it socialism or communism?**
Neither, and the comparison usually smuggles in a false choice. Creditism differs from state-socialist systems built on government ownership and centralised allocation: the state doesn't own production and doesn't set quotas. It differs from market socialism too, by separating access, contribution, commons stewardship, and Credit issuance into distinct distributed mechanisms rather than routing them all through who owns the firm.

**Is it cryptocurrency?**
No. AYU Points are a participation record, not a coin. AYU Credit is being designed as an in-network exchange unit, and the technical architecture isn't chosen. AYU does not design Credit as an investment, does not pay yield on it, and does not recognise it as compounding. If a transferable token is adopted, external systems AYU doesn't control may still trade, lend, or collateralise it. None of that confers membership, governance rights, or any in-network claim.

**Does everyone get unlimited money?**
No. Balances persist until they're spent, so what has to be governed is the whole relationship: how much is issued, how fast it deletes, how much is sitting in accounts unspent, and what the economy can actually produce within ecological limits. Prices are the main instrument for that, and the quantity outstanding is a deliberate design variable rather than a byproduct. What Credit can't do is turn into a claim on anyone's future labour.

**Who's behind it?**
Common Planet Foundation, a U.S. 501(c)(4) launched January 2026. Five co-founders. Details in `01`.

# PART THREE — MECHANISM QUESTIONS

**Why does Credit delete when you spend it?**
Because a unit that survives the transaction becomes a claim, and claims compound into power over other people. Note what deletion does and doesn't stop: **a balance can sit in your account indefinitely and that's fine** — it earns nothing, commands nobody, and dies with you. What deletion prevents is the unit passing onward through chains, gathering interest, and turning into someone's leverage. A vote doesn't become capital because a vote is gone once cast. Credit is spent the same way, for the same reason.

**If my Credit deletes, how does the baker get paid?**
Her contribution is recognised through issuance. Your Credit is for access. The two events are separate: no payment transfer and no extraction chain between them.

**So Credit never moves between people?**
In mature Creditism a purchase deletes the buyer's Credit in both venues. Marketplace is the production-and-consumption circuit, including commissioned and labor-only work; Exchange reallocates already-held assets, including zero-basis gifts. Separate seller issuance cannot exceed actual buyer expenditure or remaining recognized basis. Produced output also observes labor and virtual-cost priority, with recovery limited to eligible attributable basis and residual selling-price capacity. A lower price does not promise full recovery. Net deletion is buyer spend minus actual separate recovery issuance; worker PC and Bonus count independently. AYU Phase 2 differs: its Credit-only Marketplace deletes, while its agreed-value Exchange transfers AYU Credit.

**Can I save Credit?**
Yes. Balances persist until you spend them, and periodic issuance adds to what's there. Nothing expires, resets, or earns interest, and the same is true of a community's Community Credit account, which is how a town saves toward something big. What you can't do inside the system is lend it at interest, pledge it as collateral, or pass it on as concentrated financial power. Those aren't recognised.

**Would people still work?**
Most people already work harder than they're paid to, and stop when the reason is only fear. Remove survival coercion and what's left is the work people actually choose — plus a Quest Board that makes visible everything a community needs done and pays for it. The honest version of this answer includes the unglamorous jobs question, below. **The honest limit:** whether baseline access, open pay rates, automation, and shared rotation fill every essential role without survival coercion is unproven. It needs pilots.

**Who does the sewage work?**
Rates are matched to the work. Difficulty and risk are two of the five factors in the Standard Pay Scale, so unpleasant and dangerous work can pay well, and communities set those rates in the open. In the current system that work is badly paid *because* the people doing it have no leverage. Remove the desperation and the rate can reflect what the job actually asks of a person rather than what they can be made to accept.

**What happens the day people no longer have to keep their jobs?**
Creditism does not bet the water treatment plant on a sudden change in motivation. During transition, roles that are essential and hard to keep staffed carry a published multiple of the standard rate, paid to everyone doing that work. A wastewater operator or emergency technician might receive two or three times the ordinary rate while the change is underway. As more people become willing and qualified, the multiple steps down.

The scale prices the work. The multiplier prices the shortage. The multiplier is public and identical for everyone doing that work; its step-down protocol remains open. Separately, the revised working proposal permits bounded authorized negotiation above the applicable Standard Pay floor. That does not change the five base factors or make negotiation itself an issuance authorization.

**What about inflation?**
Ask which kind, or answer both — and don't quietly swap one for the other. **Price inflation** is a sustained rise in the general price level. **Monetary inflation** is growth in the quantity of currency units. Creditism separates them.

Credit issuance raises the quantity. Deletion lowers it. Net change is issued minus deleted. When deletion equals or exceeds issuance, monetary inflation is impossible over that period — the supply isn't growing.

That doesn't freeze prices. Prices also move with scarcity, capacity, fashion, shortage, crisis, inventory, demographics, and community decisions. What Creditism claims is narrower and more interesting: the quantity of currency becomes a visible, governed design choice instead of a byproduct of private debt expansion.

**Who decides what things are worth?**
Prices reflect real limits — this much lithium, this acre, this expert's hour — and internalise costs the current system pushes off the books. The Balance keeps prices and Credit volume legible against each other. What markets don't decide is Purposes, Pay, and Products. See Part Four.

**What's the Standard Pay Scale?**
The public, democratically revisable schedule for verified contribution. Five factors set base recognition: skill, responsibility, difficulty, risk, and experience as demonstrated proficiency. Equal base recognition is preserved. Reputation is not a sixth factor.

The working proposal treats the applicable rate as a floor, with bounded authorized negotiation above it. Negotiation alone grants no PC issuance authority. Premium limits, authorization, verification, and appeals remain open. This supersedes the former blanket no-negotiation rule.

**Doesn't Creditism abolish pay?**
No. Worker PC is separately issued for verified activity under the applicable authorization. It does not arrive as the buyer's payment or as CC transferred by an Enterprise. Recognized compensation is nevertheless an economic cost, including authorized negotiated Pay premiums, and informs the asking price. Buyers determine whether output sells and its realized price; they do not establish worker compensation. Quoted costs are not guaranteed recovery.

**What if a commission fails?**
A desk can be commissioned before it exists. Production, delivery, acceptance, and settlement follow the applicable production-accounting rules. Base Pay, an authorized Pay premium, and a sale premium are different: without a completed sale the sale premium is not realized, while verified work and authorized Pay premiums may still be recognized. Disputes can use applicable arbitration; verified reputation and later commissions may be affected. Detailed authorization and arbitration protocols remain open.

**How do people find good work and good products?**
Before work, skill, demonstrated proficiency, reputation, relationships, quoted compensation, and price inform contributor and buyer choices. After work, verified outcomes and feedback may affect reputation, opportunities, and Bonus. Unverified ratings alone do not automatically change worker rewards. The interaction of these signals, the five base factors, premiums, and Bonus remains open.

**Do the exact rates exist yet?**
No, and don't invent them. The architecture is settled: five factors, three broad levels, and recognition by time, by completed activity or Quest, or by project milestone. The numbers are working proposals inherited from the 2022 design and they need modeling, simulation, and testing in AYU before anyone treats them as real.

# PART FOUR — THE THREE FORBIDDEN P'S

Asked often, misunderstood often.

**Purposes, Pay, Products** are three questions markets were never qualified to answer. Hand *purpose* to a market and it answers "whatever pays" — the school becomes a credentialing operation, the hospital a chronic-care business. Hand it *pay* and an hour of human life is priced by the seller's desperation. Hand it *products* and "worth making" collapses into "profitable under extraction logic."

**This doesn't abolish the market.** It bounds it. Once those three come off its plate, the market gets to do the thing it's actually good at: competition over quality between people who want to make the better version. That's a more pro-market argument than capitalism currently offers.

# PART FIVE — THE HARD OBJECTIONS

Present each at full strength before answering. A weakened objection produces a weak answer.

**"This is hyperinflation with extra steps."**
The objection assumes issuance without limit. Issuance is bounded by productive capacity and offset by deletion. Where unused capacity exists — idle workers, vacant housing, shuttered facilities — new demand meets production that could already happen but had no monetary demand behind it. Where bottlenecks exist, prices reveal them. **The honest limit:** this needs modeling and pilots. Governing the issuance–deletion relationship at scale is unsolved.

**"You're confiscating my property."**
Personal property, your home, your creative work, your savings — protected. What dissolves is a specific category: ownership positions whose value comes from commanding other people's future hours. The normative claim is that rentier rights shouldn't continue indefinitely just because current law recognises them. **The legal reality, stated plainly:** compulsory conversion of recognised property rights may constitute expropriation and would require democratic authority, due process, and in many jurisdictions compensation. Both things are true. The transition has to answer the second through legitimate process, not assertion.

**"Who decides? This becomes a bureaucracy."**
Subsidiarity and affectedness. Smallest competent scale, and voice follows who's affected. The Talking Stick is open-source and forkable. **The honest limit:** voting thresholds, revision procedures, and minority protection are undesigned.

**"Innovation dies without profit incentive."**
The internet, GPS, touchscreens, and mRNA platforms were all publicly funded through their foundational stages, before any of them was privately profitable. Private capital took real risks afterward — on products, markets, and execution — but it did not carry the early technical risk, and the argument doesn't need it to. What guaranteed baseline security removes is the filter that says only people who can survive a failed year get to try. The backing function survives too: every person holds an equal share of Community Credit they can direct, so an unpopular idea needs enough individual backers rather than one wealthy one. **The honest limit:** which institutions actually evaluate uncertain projects well, and how communities coordinate real capacity across a decade, are experimental. Nobody has run them at scale.

**"How do you choose among ten enormous projects that each eat scarce resources now for uncertain benefits in twenty years?"**
The strongest question a capitalist economist can ask, and it bundles several things capitalism performs through one instrument. Take them apart.

*Mobilising* resources needs legitimate authority and real capacity rather than private investor capital. Verified labor is a source of authorized PC issuance; a project does not transfer investor-funded wages. CC supports actual inputs and productive assets, with Local, Regional, and Global provision. Available balances, authorization, draw limits, and working virtual operating constraints still matter. Removing a private financial-ownership gate does not remove resource limits or Enterprise accountability.

*Choosing* is what Community Credit does. Ten projects contend for the Credit available at the scale that matches each one's reach, and underneath that for finite productive and ecological capacity, so the trade-off is explicit: fund the rail line and the desalination plant waits. Every person also directs an equal personal share, so a project the majority won't back can still be built by enough people who will — minority backing, without the requirement of concentrated wealth.

*Committing over time* has no single rule and doesn't need one. Terms belong to the project and to whoever authorised it — staged, renewed, contested, renegotiated, cancelled — under whatever procedure that community adopted. Institutions stay revisable and the members keep the last word. There's a floor underneath all of it: a project's costs and outcomes stay visible, continuation stays reviewable, funding can be withdrawn, and no project gets to entrench the body that funds it.

*What remains honestly open* is which institutions do this well. Evaluating an uncertain twenty-year project, coordinating engineers and copper and ecological headroom across a decade, knowing when to stop — communities are expected to build these, test them, and copy what works, the way capitalism built bankruptcy courts and procurement law inside its own architecture. Nobody has run them at scale yet, and that's the honest state of it.

Worth adding, because the premise leans on it: capital markets are not uniformly good at this either. Venture funds run on a seven-to-ten-year horizon with a liquidity event at the end, which is short for the projects in the question. And several of the longest-horizon things ever built — Apollo, the interstate system, ARPANET — were paid for by states on non-return logic. **Keep the claim to those.** Don't generalise it into a story about all long-duration investment; private and regulated-utility capital genuinely does carry some of it, and the argument doesn't need the wider version.

**"It'll get captured, like everything else."**
Capture is architecture, not just bad people. Five properties make it harder: distributed authority, transparent flows with protected privacy, Credit that can't compound into power, participation as default, and real exit through forkable open-source infrastructure. **The honest limit:** the transparency/privacy reconciliation is unsolved, and no architecture is capture-proof.

**"Communism killed millions."**
See Part Two. The comparison is to state ownership and centralised allocation, neither of which Creditism has. But don't be glib about it — the objection carries real historical weight, and the answer is a structural distinction, not a dismissal.

**"What if I want no part of it?"**
Phase-specific, and the honest answer differs by stage. Right now: entirely voluntary, opt in and out. Pilot communities: contractual, like any cooperative charter. Municipal adoption: applies like any democratically decided public policy. National transition: you can't opt out of currency and property law any more than you can opt out of the current ones — which is why it requires majorities, courts, process, and constitutional safeguards. Planetary: nobody privately appropriates the atmosphere.

**"What about the people who just won't contribute?"**
Addiction, severe antisocial behaviour, deep despair. They exist and always have. The threat of starvation never fixed them; it made them worse. Creditism doesn't claim to solve the hard cases. It claims to stop using them as the excuse for a system that punishes everyone else. Baseline access stays. What goes is the moral alibi.

**"How do you allocate the beachfront house?"**
Housing Level is the proposed eligibility framework — level earned through caretaking, contribution, and Personal Credit. It decides which homes you qualify for. It doesn't decide who gets the one everybody wants. **The honest limit:** when several equally qualified people want the same scarce home, the tiebreak is undesigned. Under consideration: ranked top-three preferences, queueing for specific dwellings, prior stewards holding first claim. None settled. Scarcity doesn't disappear just because money did.

# PART SIX — LIFE UNDER CREDITISM

**Who owns homes?**
Nobody owns them the way a landlord does. You're the caretaker of your primary residence — live in it, maintain it, no mortgage, no property tax, no foreclosure. Homes carry a 1–10 rating; you carry a Housing Level earned through caretaking and contribution. Succession is continuity of occupancy, not hereditary title — someone who lived in the home can claim it; someone who moved out years ago cannot. *Working proposal. Full block in `04` 2.5.*

**Can I keep my possessions?**
Yes. Personal property is protected — belongings, tools, creative work, savings.

**What happens to my business?**
Productive activity can continue under the applicable stewardship and authorization rules. Individual proprietors remain distinct from group Enterprises; permitted one-person Enterprises still have group-account and CC safeguards. Quotes normally reflect materials, recognized labor, and other costs. Labor PC is separately authorized; eligible input-basis recovery is bounded by remaining and attributable basis and the price left after other recognized costs. No sale guarantees recovery. Exact proprietor, capital, refund, and posting protocols remain open.

**What happens to wealthy people?**
Their homes, possessions, and productive enterprises continue. What ends is income from owning without contributing. Some will experience this as loss, and pretending otherwise is dishonest.

**Can I invest my Credits and make a return?**
No, and the thing that looks like it is narrower than it sounds. Creditism does not permit or recognise Credit lent at interest, pledged as collateral, or invested for autonomous return. That does not change.

What AYU proposes is **Venture Creditism**, AYU only. Credits ventured to a project can be lost, including the full principal. Any uplift depends on evaluated achievement under an open formula, with a proposed annual ceiling. The proposed annual ceiling's rate remains open; no numerical rate has been publicly specified. Nothing accrues from holding and nothing is owed. Keep principal-loss risk and evaluation prominent. Explain the proposed annual ceiling when uplift limits are relevant or the reader asks; do not give a percentage, range, worked return, expected return, order of magnitude, or market-rate proxy. *Working proposal.*

Full Creditism has no Venture Creditism and does not need one. Community Credit funds groups and ventures democratically, with every person directing an equal share. AYU needs the mechanism because AYU runs inside capitalism without that architecture yet, and because building the evaluation and verification tooling now is how Creditism gets it later. *Working proposal, `03` §11.*

**What about my pension?**
It doesn't carry forward, and here is what replaces it. Everyone receives an initial allocation of Credit at activation: 3,000 multiplied by age, peaking at fifty and tapering after. A person of seventy starts with 90,000. Universal Income then flows for life, undiminished by age and unconditional on having worked. The home carries no rent, mortgage, property tax, or insurance premium, and eligible savings conversion follows agreed currency-specific rates, additional to the starting allocation.

Say the hard part plainly rather than burying it: a pension is a scheme for surviving old age in a system where survival had to be bought. Nobody in Creditism receives welfare, so nobody needs an exemption from it. **Do not describe this as pensions being honoured.** That was the pack's position through v10 and it is no longer true. *Working proposal, `04` 5.1b.*

**What about my savings?**
The intended architecture uses one Credit denomination with agreed rates for each national currency, conceptually like national currencies entering the euro. The older literal one-national-unit-to-one-Credit rule is superseded. Eligible asset conversion still follows its property-transition rules; exact rates, valuation windows, legal terms, and governance require agreement and modeling. **A conversion rate does not guarantee purchasing power.** No new valuation formula or above-entitlement AYU benefit is approved.

# PART SEVEN — AYU QUESTIONS

Short answers here. Full detail in `03`.

**What is AYU?** The membership network. Transition layer and proving ground. **Not a Creditist economy** — the mechanics run at network scale inside a capitalist one.

**Isn't that Creditism, then, just small?** It is a limited intended Credit circuit inside capitalism: from Phase 2, verified issuance and eligible Credit-only Marketplace spending/deletion. Phase 1 currently uses Points. AYU's agreed-value Exchange transfers AYU Credit rather than running mature separate seller issuance. A network circuit does not supply universality, jurisdictional recognition, or complete livelihood coverage. Phase 3 adopts the full framework. Do not claim completed economic testing merely because the design describes the circuit.

**What phase are we in?** Phase 1 — AYU Points. Membership applications are free and open; donors are automatically approved for membership, with verification and recognition separate. First members are joining. The Founding Phase recognition window ends 31 December 2026. Later Phase 1 entry remains Early Member; Regular Member entry begins at Phase 2, with earlier designation history preserved. Phase 2 timing is not publicly announced.

**Can I leave my home to my children?** They keep it if they lived in it. Succession is continuity of occupancy, not hereditary title — a surviving person who lived there can claim the home by swapping their current residence for it, and their own Housing Level doesn't have to reach the home's rating. Someone who moved out years ago has no claim on it. A vacation home passes to nobody: stewardship ends at death and the home returns to availability for a living person. One primary residence per person is the ceiling that keeps any of this from accumulating. *Working proposal. Full block in `04` 2.5.*

**Can I cash out Points?** No. Non-transferable participation record. Not money, not a security, not payment.

**Do AYU Points convert into Credits?** No. Points are a participation record. They can verify membership and participation facts that bear on eligibility. No number of Points produces a corresponding number of Credits.

**What happens to my AYU Credits when a country adopts Creditism?** The recorded working entitlement consists of directly issued AYU Credits plus the applicable Early Member bonus. Formal surrender converts 1:1 only within the remaining entitlement, consumed once. Acquired balances do not enlarge that entitlement. AYU and mature Credit share a nominal denomination, but common denomination does not authorize unrestricted account conversion or particular goods. Legitimate cross-boundary spending is a different event, intended in both directions under availability and settlement rules. Broader treatment of holdings above the entitlement is under review; no additional entitlement or valuation benefit is approved. Full unchanged entitlement block at `03`.

**How are Credits taxed?** Ask a qualified tax professional in your own jurisdiction. Treatment varies by country and changes with legislation, and it isn't a question Common Planet can answer for you. We take no position on it.

**Will Credits be tradable?** Architecture isn't decided. Credit will be the in-network exchange unit for members. It may also exist as a transferable token. External holding grants no membership, governance rights, or recorded in-network exchange — those need an AYU account.

**Should I buy or hold Credits?** Not a question this can answer. Independent professional adviser.

**Will Patrons or Partners get Credits later?** Founding Circle recognition is recorded in AYU Points. The transition entitlement is calculated without reference to Founding Circle, Partner, or Patron status. Nothing beyond that.

**Does joining give me land?** No. Participation, not title.

**Can anyone claim a house listed in AYU?** No. Mapped, owned, available, and stewarded are four separate statuses.

# PART EIGHT — WHAT'S THEORETICAL, WHAT'S BUILT

**Being built now:** the AYU membership network, the identity and trust layer via Aura/BrightID, the participation record, governance protocols, the essay series and whitepaper, the Commons Stack tooling.

**Designed, not operating:** the full Visible Hand. Personal and Community Credit. Deletion. The Ledger of Life. The Abodium. The Talking Stick at scale.

**Genuinely open:** issuance and calibration, technical settlement, contribution verification, privacy, scarce access, legal transition, and institutional coordination. Working production protocols include attributable basis, capital allocation/depreciation, proprietor recovery, Pay premiums, virtual allowance enforcement/restoration, and separately governed Bonus PC/CC formulas and distribution. The nominal common denomination is approved; currency-specific rates and broader above-entitlement holdings treatment remain unresolved. No formula or new benefit is implied.

**Working Enterprise discipline:** actual sales enter virtual revenue at realized price, while labor, inputs, and other costs enter virtual P&L. Labor-only Enterprises have labor inputs. A governed Input Allowance and performance accounting constrain continued activity; actual CC use cannot bypass accountability. Exhaustion does not automatically remove CC spending authority. Communities can separately end operating authority or use rights. Virtual losses create no conventional debt and do not claw back worker PC. Deliberately bounded funding of socially necessary loss-making work is possible.

**Closure:** enforceable funding terms and returnable project balances are settled first; unrestricted balances then follow community arrangements. Returnable Regional funding goes back to Regional even for a Local group. Recovered CC keeps its originating restrictions, assets remain community-held, and posthumous refunds remain open.

**Bonus:** one outcome may generate separately governed Bonus PC for people and Bonus CC for groups. Bonus CC supports authorized productive needs; it is not virtual profit or automatic loss/allowance replenishment. Intended mathematical fairness is not a supplied or validated formula. Actual issuance counts in quantity arithmetic. Neither mature CC nor Bonus CC operates in AYU Phase 1.

**Not open:** how large projects are funded and chosen. Community Credit does that. Do not describe it as missing. Nor are the five items closed on 21 August 2026: intertemporal binding needs no universal rule, half-built projects are ordinary case-specific asset management, formal forecast weighting is not required, the project review floor is settled, and correlated failure is a resilience question rather than a risk-pooling one. This list named four of them as open until v15. Full register in `05`, Section C.

**Evidence that components work:** guaranteed income experiments including Mincome and the Alaska Permanent Fund. Ostrom's commons research. The WIR Bank, running a complementary currency since 1934. Mondragon, 70,085 people employed in 2024, a minority of them worker-members — cite it the careful way, `04` Stage 2. BerkShares. **None of these is Creditism.** They're evidence the design isn't crazy. Verification has to come from actual pilots.

**Failures worth naming:** the Bristol Pound wound down its digital scheme in 2020. Durability depends on institutional depth and offering something the national currency can't. Name the failures. It costs nothing and buys everything.

**What would prove Creditism wrong?** Pilots where contribution verification collapses into surveillance or into gaming. Communities that can't allocate scarce resources without reverting to allocation by wealth, coercion, patronage, or unaccountable authority. Issuance that can't be calibrated without either shortage or runaway prices. Dispute resolution that fails without coercive enforcement. These are answerable questions, and they should be asked.

# PART NINE — QUESTIONS TO DECLINE

**Credit price forecasts, expected returns, and buy-hold-sell advice.** No forecasts. No "will it go up." No comparison to any asset's returns. No framing of holding as smart or patient. Hold-or-sell questions go to an independent professional adviser.

What *can* be explained: the currency-basket index, the difference between an in-network reference value and any external market price, why the two could diverge, and the ratchet as a design hypothesis. Mechanism, never opportunity. See `00` §9.1.

**Speculation about future issuance to recognition pools.** State what's recorded and what the entitlement calculation uses, then stop. In either direction. See `00` §9.2.

**Legal, tax, or investment advice.** Not what this is.

**Anything requiring invention.** If it's not in the pack and not derivable from it, say it's an open design question. That's a complete answer.
