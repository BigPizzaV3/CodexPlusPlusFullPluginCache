## 1. Three layers

Common Planet is not one thing. It is three, and the distinction matters in almost every answer.

**Common Planet Foundation (CPF)** is the nonprofit steward. It researches, educates, holds the legal structure, raises funds, builds partnerships, and protects the mission for the long term.

**Creditism** is the economic architecture. The destination system. A full successor design for how currency is created, how resources are stewarded, how work is recognised, and how communities decide. It is not operating at scale anywhere.

**AYU** is the living network. The membership layer where people actually enter, build, and practice. The transition layer and the proving ground.

**CPF, AYU, and the Creditism framework together constitute Common Planet.** CPF holds the mission. AYU gathers the people. Creditism is what they are building toward.

**AYU is not a Creditist economy.** It is open and onboarding members in Phase 1 through Points. From Phase 2, its Credit circuit runs inside a capitalist economy, at network scale, for a fraction of what members need.

## 2. Mission

To empower human potential on a sustainable, regenerative planet. To dance hand-in-hand around the world, free from debt, coerced work, and systems that place money above life.

**Life** — Guaranteed housing and baseline Credit. A floor beneath everyone. Life before debt, always.

**Liberty** — No job you must obey to survive. Work becomes yours to choose. Move freely. Build what matters.

**Love** — When survival stops being a competition, people build and care for one another. Belonging, meaning, home.

AYU means Life. The triad is the same triad.

**North star:**

> "Make the world work, for 100% of humanity, in the shortest possible time, through spontaneous cooperation, without ecological offense or the disadvantage of anyone."
> — Buckminster Fuller

## 3. How it started

**Spring 2015.** A family in Oregon kept arguing about everything going wrong. Remzi Bajrami and his wife Ardijana had spent years reading and watching and debating unstable economics, ecological breakdown, and the systems underneath. One evening, mid-documentary, their teenage daughter Adelina walked in and told them to shut up. Stop talking about problems. Find solutions.

That interruption changed the work. Remzi moved from studying what was broken to asking what could be built. Before any model could matter, one question had to come first:

> **"What kind of world do you want to live in?"**

**Summer 2015.** The housing breakthrough. A way to rate and guarantee housing. With that piece, the rest began to fit, and an economy built on Credit had a destination clear enough to design a path toward.

**Fall 2015.** EarthVote.org launched as a public research initiative while the deeper architecture developed privately.

**2016–2018.** EarthVote became Common Planet. The first manifestos appeared. In 2018 the project took the name.

**2021–2022.** A book-length draft consolidated years of work. *Common Planet: A New Game of Life* published 22 February 2022.

**June 2022.** Jim Rutt interviewed Remzi and challenged the book's central category. The book made Property the engine of the existing system. Rutt argued the engine was profit, and money-on-money return. Six months of rereading capital and credit followed, and the challenge landed: Property was one word doing too many jobs. The project was not only reclaiming Property. It was reclaiming Credit. By fall 2022 the framework had a name: **Creditism**.

**Late 2023.** Remzi opened the work to others. He had met people online over the years who cared about the same questions, and began sharing a co-writing document, slowly at first, then more widely. Most were curious. A handful built. **Eli Capracotta**, **Avi Volah**, and **Adam Stallard** did the most to push Creditism forward, and Adam's decentralised identity and development protocols became the backbone of AYU.

**January 2026.** Common Planet Foundation launched.

**Now.** AYU is open and the first members are joining in Phase 1. Applying is free and open to anyone; donors are automatically approved for membership. Approval does not replace identity or contribution verification and creates no special Credit entitlement. The Founding Phase closes 31 December 2026. Earlier joining provides more time to participate and build AYU Points.

More than a decade on, one question has become a framework, a foundation, and a team. The question hasn't changed.

## 7. What is not claimed

CPF does not claim Creditism has been proven. It is a developing architecture with a coherent design and real unresolved questions, some of which can only be settled by pilots.

CPF does not claim AYU is a Creditist economy, or that anyone can live inside one. AYU is a participation record and a trust layer.

CPF does not claim AYU Points are money, securities, equity, ownership, tradable assets, or any promise of financial return.

CPF does not speak with legal, investment, or financial authority.

Part 10, *The Hardest Questions*, closes with a list titled "What We Still Have to Prove." That list is canon.

## 8. Intellectual lineage

**Thomas Paine** — *Common Sense* supplies the form: a pamphlet addressed to ordinary readers, in plain language, demanding they judge the system themselves. The book opens on a Paine pastiche with (Corporations) substituted for the King and (Nations) for Parliament. That substitution is where the capitalised terms of art come from. Also on land: *Man did not make the earth.*

**Buckminster Fuller** — the north star quotation, *Grunch of Giants*, the observation that we keep inventing jobs to justify existence, and emergence through emergency.

**David Graeber** — *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* on debt jubilees and money as credit; *Bullshit Jobs*.

**Elinor Ostrom** — *Governing the Commons*. Demonstrated that communities can govern shared resources successfully under identifiable institutional conditions, without private ownership or state enforcement.

**James Carse** — finite and infinite games. "Whoever must play, cannot play."

**Michael Hudson** — finance as rent extraction; the lost tradition of debt cancellation.

**Mariana Mazzucato** — *The Entrepreneurial State*. Foundational innovation came substantially from public investment.

**Yanis Varoufakis** — one-employee-one-share-one-vote; technofeudalism.

**Ilya Prigogine** — systems far from equilibrium reorganise rather than improve. Phase change, not reform.

**Resource-based economics and the Venus Project** — the early influence showing an economy could be organised around life and resources rather than debt and profit. What remained unclear was the path.

**Yuval Noah Harari** — Corporations and Nations as shared fictions: stories that let strangers cooperate at scale, and that hold only while enough people believe them. The reason those words are capitalised in this work.

**Daniel Schmachtenberger** — instability originating at the level of foundational axioms, which is why retrofits fail; the omni-win-win constraint; and *discrete phase shift*, the ancestor of *Not war. Phase change.*

**Arash Kolahi** — affectedness: democratic say proportional to effect. The principle underneath subsidiarity in the governance design.

**Perry Gruber / Copiosis and Larry Mason's "No-POM"** — the non-transferable, no-physical-object-money property Remzi adopted in 2015 and considers foundational.

**Robert A. Heinlein** — money as a symbol for balancing, healing, and growing closer.

**Marc Gauvin** — money's unresolved conflict between being a *measure* of value and being an ownable commodity in its own right. Behind the scorekeeper joining the game as a player at `02` 1.1.

**Michael Suk-Young Chwe** — *Rational Ritual*. Common knowledge as a coordination requirement: knowing is not enough, everyone has to know that everyone knows. The reason AYU's visible membership and participation layer counts as transition infrastructure rather than promotion. `04` 4.2a.

**John Kenneth Galbraith** — *The Anatomy of Power*. The separation of sources of power from instruments of power, and organisation as the decisive one. Behind the claim that Creditism democratises power rather than abolishing it. `04` 4.2a.

**John Boik** — systemic change as research rather than advocacy: designed trials, stated hypotheses, published results whichever way they fall. Behind the pilot discipline at `04` Stage 2.

**Las Indias Cooperative Group** — parallel institutions maturing inside the old system rather than confronting it. The Stage 1 and Stage 2 strategy in practice.

**Robin Wall Kimmerer** — *Braiding Sweetgrass*. Reciprocity and the gift as economic relations, and accountability to generations that cannot vote. Carried in the essay series.

Also drawn on: Ellen Meiksins Wood, David Harvey, Thomas Piketty, Herman Daly, Kate Raworth, Robert Michels, Janos Kornai, David Korten, Álvaro J. De Regil Castilla, Nick Marconi.

## 9. Published writing — index

Published under the Common Planet Foundation byline at commonplanet.substack.com.

**Front matter**

*Another World Is Possible / CREDITISM: Economic Liberation* — introduction and invitation.

*The Civilizational Upgrade* — we are technologically advanced and civically primitive. Capital sits at the centre of our economic universe; it should be Credit. Introduces the Copernican frame.

**The Substack series**

*This index covers published writing only. The full text lives in `CP_PUBLISHED_WRITING`, which advances whenever a piece ships. Nothing here states how many pieces exist, and drafts are not listed: a count in canon goes stale on publication day and takes a release to correct.*

**1 · Nations, Corporations, and the First Economic Game**
Ownership came first. Finders keepers hardened into custom, then law, then morality. Capitalism doesn't begin with markets, it begins with ownership positions that generate ongoing claims. Corporations made capture immortal; nations made it legal.
*Terms used: Game A. Capture first, charge later.*

**2 · How Money Really Works**
Banks captured the ledger. Two kinds of money: permanent credit created by governments, temporary credit created by banks as debt. Taxes delete money, they don't fund spending. Every price contains layers of tribute.
*Coinages: The debt IS the money. Scarcity is a political choice, not an economic fact.*

**3 · The Creditism Evolution: From Debt to Freedom**
The architecture. Cage and key. Personal Credit, deletion, Community Credit, reclaimed commons. What Creditism is not. What the system no longer needs.
*Coinages: The subscription fee for being alive. Debt is "I owe you." Credit is "We need you."*

**4 · The Trust Revolution**
Why Creditism couldn't exist until now. Every civilisation is shaped by its available technologies of trust. The binding constraint was never scarcity — it was the need for a trusted intermediary to hold the ledger. That constraint broke.
*Coinages: The ledger as commons. You are a person because other people recognise you as one.*

**5 · A Play For Life: 2050**
A day lived from the inside. Lucía in Oaxaca, Jin-ho in Lagos, Marcus in Brooklyn. The Visible Hand, the Quest Board, the Talking Stick, the Sphere, Community Credit in a plaza council.
*Coinages: It arrives like air. You don't worry about air.*

**6 · Credit is Democracy, Money Isn't**
Money is a vote. The economy is an election. One person one vote built our politics; one dollar one vote built our economy. Personal Credit is the ballot you cast for your life; Community Credit is the one we cast together.
*Coinages: Democracy with a body. Not no rules — no rulers.*

**7 · After the Wage**
Money isn't time. Money is enforceable claims on other people's time. The time-capture stack: wages take the present, debt takes the future, inherited wealth takes the past.
*Coinages: The time-capture stack. Unowned hours. Your hours come home.*
