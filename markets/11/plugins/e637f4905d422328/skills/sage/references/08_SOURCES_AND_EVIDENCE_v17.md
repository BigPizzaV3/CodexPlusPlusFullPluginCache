# PART ONE — HOW TO USE THIS FILE

The conceptual canon is strong. The empirical layer under it is thinner, and this file exists to hold it up.

**Three rules.**

**Date every figure.** A membership count, a circulation volume, an employee number — all of these drift. Give the year the figure belongs to, or don't give the figure.

**Qualify contested claims.** Where economists disagree, say they disagree. The argument does not need a false consensus, and claiming one is how a reader finds the seam.

**Never overstate monetary sovereignty.** The claims below about issuance apply to a government issuing its own free-floating currency. They do not apply to a municipality, a euro-area member state, a dollarised economy, or any household or firm. Currency *users* face a budget constraint. Currency *issuers* face a real-resources constraint. Collapsing the two is the most common way this argument gets discredited by someone who knows the subject.

**This is a scope rule, not an instruction to hedge.** A sovereign issuer of its own currency does not need to obtain that currency from elsewhere before executing authorised spending. What must be established every time is that the entity in question actually issues its own currency.

**This file carries cleared claims only.** Everything here has been checked, or carries its own qualification in the text. Nothing in it is pending.

Items still awaiting verification live in the maintenance register, not here. That is deliberate: an editorial marker inside a public file either leaks internal process to a reader or, worse, gets retrieved without its warning attached and stated as fact.

# PART TWO — MONETARY CLAIMS

## 2.1 Bank money creation

**Claim.** Commercial banks create deposit money when they lend. Lending is not the intermediation of pre-existing savings.

**Primary source.** Bank of England, *Quarterly Bulletin* 2014 Q1: "Money creation in the modern economy," McLeay, Radia and Thomas. This is a central bank describing its own system, which is why it carries weight that a heterodox paper would not. Pair it with the Bundesbank's April 2017 monthly report, which reaches the same conclusion.

**The empirical source.** Richard A. Werner, "Can banks individually create money out of nothing? — The theories and the empirical evidence," *International Review of Financial Analysis* 36 (December 2014), 1–19. The central banks describe the system. Werner tested it: a real loan, tracked through a cooperating bank's internal records, settling credit creation against the intermediary and fractional-reserve accounts. Where a reader wants evidence rather than testimony, this is the citation. It is the technical spine of the book's Banks chapter.

**How to use it.** Cite the institution, not the school of thought. The strength of this claim is that it is no longer contested by the institutions that would have most reason to contest it.

## 2.2 Sovereign issuance and taxation

**Claim.** A government issuing its own currency spends by issuance and removes currency through taxation. Taxes do not mechanically fund the issuer.

**Status: contested.** This is the functional-finance and MMT position. Mainstream macroeconomics does not generally describe it this way, and there is genuine disagreement about the constraints — inflation, exchange rates, and political economy — that bind an issuer in practice.

**How to state it honestly.** Describe the operational mechanics, which are less contested than the policy conclusions drawn from them. Then name the constraint: an issuer is limited by real productive capacity and by inflation, not by revenue. Do not present the conclusion as settled economics. `02` §2.7 already models the right posture.

**The scope trap.** This claim fails completely for a euro-area member, a US state, a city, or any currency user. Say which kind of entity you mean, every time.

## 2.3 Debt jubilee

**Claim.** Periodic debt cancellation is an old and recurrent institution, not a modern fantasy.

**Sources.** Michael Hudson, *…and forgive them their debts* (2018), on Bronze Age Mesopotamian *andurarum* and *misharum* proclamations. David Graeber, *Debt: The First 5000 Years* (2011), for the wider anthropological argument.

**Qualification.** Ancient clean-slate proclamations cancelled specific categories — agrarian and consumer debt, debt bondage — in economies structured very differently from ours. They are precedent for the *institution*, not a template for the mechanism. Hudson's readings of specific tablets are debated by Assyriologists, and that dispute should be acknowledged rather than stepped around.

# PART THREE — COMPLEMENTARY AND COOPERATIVE PRECEDENTS

The honest use of this section is to show that components have worked, and to be candid about where they failed. `04` already states the rule: none of these is Creditism, all of them prove a component. Failures are more useful to us than successes, because they name what AYU has to survive.

## 3.1 WIR — Switzerland, 1934 to present

Founded in 1934 in Basel after the 1929 crash, as a cooperative response to currency shortage. Still operating as Bank WIR. It issues the WIR franc, an electronic-only unit with no paper money, used alongside the Swiss franc in dual-currency transactions, and serves small and medium enterprises across hospitality, construction, manufacturing, retail, and professional services.

**Why it matters.** Nine decades of continuous operation is the strongest existing evidence that a parallel unit can persist at scale inside a functioning national economy. There is also a research literature — Stodder's work is the usual citation — arguing WIR volumes move countercyclically, expanding when the Swiss franc economy contracts.

**Qualify.** Membership and volume figures vary by source and by year; the commonly repeated "60,000 members, 45,000 SMEs" figure is undated in most sources and should not be quoted without one. The countercyclical finding is a research claim, not a settled result.

## 3.2 Bristol Pound — 2012 to 2021, ceased

Launched September 2012. At its peak it was the largest alternative to sterling in the UK, usable for bus and train fares, groceries, council tax, and partial payment of civil servants' wages. The digital scheme closed in August 2020, paper notes were withdrawn in September 2020, and reimbursement ran to December 2021. The successor platform, Bristol Pay, was discontinued in mid-2023 for lack of funding.

**Never cite this as a live example.** It is defunct, and describing it as operating is the kind of error that ends a reader's trust in everything around it.

**Why it belongs here anyway.** Its failure modes are precisely the ones AYU has to answer, and naming them first is stronger than having them raised against us:

- **The convertibility ceiling.** Major suppliers, wholesalers, and utilities didn't accept it, so the currency always had to convert back to sterling. A unit that must exit to be useful cannot escape the system it exits into.
- **Administration cost per unit circulating** was high, and critics did the arithmetic publicly.
- **Adoption stayed tiny and demographically narrow** — a very small fraction of the city, skewed heavily toward degree-holders and managers.
- **Ordinary payments got easier.** Contactless and mobile payment removed the friction the project had been solving against.

The Creditism answer is that Bristol built an *alternative medium* inside unchanged property and income structures, while Creditism changes what the currency is and where it deletes. That answer has to be argued, not asserted.

## 3.3 Mondragón — Basque Country, 1956 to present

Founded 14 April 1956. In its 2024 annual report the federation gave sales of €11.213 billion, 70,085 people employed, and net income of €632 million, across roughly a hundred cooperatives in industry, finance, retail, and knowledge.

**The number that must be stated correctly.** 70,085 is people *employed*, not worker-members. Mondragón cooperatives own subsidiaries that are not cooperatives, mostly retail in Spain and industrial plants abroad. In the scholarly literature the worker-member share of total employment runs roughly a third to a half depending on sector and year. Citing "70,000 members" as evidence of democratic ownership at scale overstates the case by a factor of two or more, and it is the first thing a reader who knows Mondragón will correct.

**The failure belongs in the citation.** Fagor Electrodomésticos, the founding industrial cooperative, entered insolvency in 2013 and was not rescued by the federation. Displaced worker-members were substantially, though not entirely, absorbed elsewhere in the group. Omitting this from a resilience claim is the kind of selective citation that costs a reader's trust in everything around it.

**The defensible claim.** Democratic ownership survives complexity, scale, and seventy years of market pressure. It does not confer immortality on an enterprise, and it thins at the edges under competitive pressure. `04` Stage 2 states it this way and should not drift back.

## 3.4 BerkShares and Emilia-Romagna

BerkShares in the Berkshires, Massachusetts; the cooperative networks of Emilia-Romagna. Each is cited in `04` as proving a component rather than the whole. Give no scale figure for either without a year attached to it.

# PART FOUR — INNOVATION AND PUBLIC INVESTMENT

**Claim.** Foundational technologies in widely cited cases — internet protocols, GPS, touchscreen interfaces, mRNA platforms — were publicly funded through their foundational stages.

**Source.** Mariana Mazzucato, *The Entrepreneurial State* (2013), is the standard reference and assembles the case studies.

**State it at the right strength.** The defensible claim is about *which* risk public funding carried: the early technical risk, before a market existed. Private capital took real risks afterward — product, market, timing, execution — and pretending otherwise invites an easy correction. `06` states it this way and should not drift back.

**Contested.** Mazzucato's thesis has serious critics who argue it selects on successes and understates private-sector basic research. Acknowledge the dispute; the narrow claim survives it.

# PART FIVE — COMMONS GOVERNANCE

**Claim.** Commons can be governed sustainably by their users, without either privatisation or central control.

**Source.** Elinor Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990), and the eight design principles. Nobel Prize in Economics, 2009.

**The qualification that must travel with it.** Ostrom studied common-pool resources at community scale — irrigation systems, fisheries, forests, pastures — with identifiable users, workable monitoring, and graduated sanctions. She did not demonstrate that the principles scale to planetary coordination, and she was explicit that no single institutional form fits every case. `01` already carries this qualification. Keep it.

# PART SIX — CPF AND AYU FACTS

**The book.** *Common Planet: A New Game of Life*, Remzi Bajrami, published 22 February 2022. ISBN-10 `0578381087`, ISBN-13 `978-0578381084`. Trade paperback. Creative Commons BY-NC-ND 4.0, distributed free as a PDF alongside the paid print edition. *Third-party listings date it 25–26 February. Those are listing dates. Use 22 February.*

**The book did not coin Creditism, and does not mention AYU.** It calls the system *Common Planet* and the unit *Credit*. Creditism was named in the autumn of 2022, after publication. Attributing either name to the book is a factual error, and a reader holding the book can check it in seconds. Book-era vocabulary is mapped at `05` B2.

**Organisational facts drift, so date them or route them.** 501(c)(4) status, fiscal sponsorship, the founding team, the Founding Phase close date, the deployment address, and the count of published essays all change or expire. `01` carries the current statements. Where a figure here would duplicate one there, it is omitted on purpose: a fact stated in two files drifts in one of them.

**The publication count is not kept here, or anywhere in the pack.** `CP_PUBLISHED_WRITING` holds what has published and carries its own version. A number written into a canon file is wrong the day a piece ships and needs a release to correct, which is the one organisational fact that goes stale with nobody touching the file.

# PART SEVEN — WHAT THIS FILE DOES NOT COVER

No source here establishes that Creditism works. The precedents establish that components have worked, at smaller scale, under different conditions, and in several cases that they later failed. That is the honest claim, it is the one `04` already makes, and inflating it is the fastest available route to losing a reader who knows the material.
