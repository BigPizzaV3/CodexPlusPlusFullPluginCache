# Neuro photoshoot: a series from a brief

The method's task is a series of shots of one product with exactly the degree of visual
connection and variation the user asked for. Consistency rests on the anchors chosen for the series and on the
reference capabilities of the chosen model.

## Stable anchors and variables

Before the series, fix only the shared anchors that must stay stable:

- the background and its character (white / neutral / a specific scene);
- the character of the light (soft studio / directional / natural) and its direction;
- the palette and the overall mood (clean e-commerce / warm lifestyle / premium);
- the product itself as a fact: its cut, color, material, logo and hardware stay as in the
  source.

Background, light, palette and mood may be shared or may change between frames; the goal of the
series decides this, not a fixed template.

## Set of angles

A series for a listing usually takes angles that complement each other rather than duplicates:

- the main frame — the product large and whole, from the front;
- a detail — a close-up of the texture, a seam, the hardware or the key feature;
- in context — the product in hands or in use;
- lifestyle or scene — the product in an interior or a setting of use.

Adapt the set to the product type and the request. Do not chase the number of frames; make sure
each frame shows something new and fits the purpose of the series.

## Keeping the series unified

1. Define the stable anchors and the variables from the user's goal.
2. Give each frame a self-contained brief with the shared anchors it needs and its own changes.
3. For the identity you need to hold, use the reference controls and limits from the chosen
   model's live contract.

## Light, background, format

Keep light, background and format shared only when this is part of the chosen consistency or the
purpose of the series; otherwise follow the requested variation. Take the marketplace's exact
size and background requirements from current verified materials when they affect the result.
