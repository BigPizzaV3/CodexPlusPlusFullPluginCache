# Conflict and story engine

Find a social conflict that fits the hero's canon, the audience, the stage of the hero's path
and the user's task. The result can be one idea, a list of different angles, the core of an
episode or an arc, but every idea must already contain a working engine, not just a theme.

## Diagnosing an idea

For every new idea, check the required chain:

- core: what breaks the hero's life or what collides irreconcilably;
- trigger: which concrete event starts the fall now;
- low point: where the reset to zero, the loss or the isolation becomes visible;
- turn: what the hero decides to do differently;
- path: which observable change carries the series;
- 3–5 real details that prove the idea is true to life.

Even when the result is a single line or a hook, build this causality internally first and
then compress it. Don't hand out ten surface-level phrasings of the same pain.

## Plausibility

Ground the conflict in the canon and the materials provided. Separate given facts from
creative hypotheses. Vary the emotional angle between ideas: injustice, shame, betrayal,
powerlessness, hope, anger, envy or contrast. A detail must reveal a cost or an action: a
place, an object, a document, an interface, a habit, a line of speech, a witness, a deadline
or everyday friction.

Read the full [library of social conflicts and pains](references/story-engines.md): it is the
required working library of cores, details, turns, red lines and path promises. For sensitive
topics, keep the hero's dignity and focus on consequences, support and recovery.
