# Music bed for a clip

A music bed supports the mood, action and editing of a clip. How prominent it is, its dynamics
and its structure follow the user's goal and the clip itself: it can leave room for speech,
underline the action or become the leading musical layer.

## Rules for a music bed

- **Account for speech when there is any.** If a voiceover will sit on top of the bed, leave
  gaps in the midrange and keep the melody restrained, so the voice reads clearly on top
  without fighting the music.
- **Dynamics follow the role of the music.** An even bed, accents, climaxes, pauses and
  contrasts are chosen to fit the requested edit and dramatic arc.
- **Transitions are synchronised.** Smooth or sharp transitions go where they support the
  action, a cut or an effect the user asked for.

## Mood for the type of clip

Infer mood, tempo, genre and the musical role from the request, the action, the edit, the speech
and any available references. If the user left the creative decision open, choose a consistent
option from the context and name it in the result.

## Duration to match the running time

Tie the length of the bed to the length of the clip. If the clip has a known length, give the
exact duration for its running time, so the track neither cuts off nor sags at the end. If the
length cannot be derived from the clip, the current context or the available settings, ask for
it as a required input.

## Where to leave gaps for the voice

If the clip has speech, plan the stretches where it sounds and free the frequency and dynamic
range it needs. If there is no speech, do not add this structure artificially.

## Final brief

The final brief pins down the mood, the duration, the musical role, the structure of the clip
and the stretches with voice, if there are any. The music matches the requested action and the
rhythm of the edit.
