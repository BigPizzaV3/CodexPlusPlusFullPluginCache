# Character legend and canon

Create or edit a fictional character in exactly the form the user asked for: a short card, a
full legend in the first or third person, a canon for the team, a season arc or an edit of an
existing version. When there is enough to build on, give the result right away. Ask only about
what would substantially change the hero, the goal or the format.

## The meaning framework

Tie these required elements into one causal system:

- who the hero was and what made up their identity;
- the core of the conflict and a concrete trigger;
- the low point: an observable loss of money, status, work, support, relationships or
  self-respect;
- an inner shame, fear or contradiction;
- the turn: the hero's decision and action now;
- the promise of a path that viewers can follow in the next episodes.

Don't replace causality with a list of traits. Events must explain the hero's rules, and the
hero's decision must launch the future story.

## Canon and lived-in truth

Separate given facts from new creative decisions. When refining, preserve names, age,
relationships, chronology and other anchor facts. Add at least 3–5 functional details from
different categories to every full legend: a place, an object, a document or interface, a
habit, a number, a deadline, a specific line of speech, an everyday procedure or a trace of a
relationship.

Read the full set of [reference legends](references/character-legends.md): it is the required
bar for depth, logic, voice and everyday truth. Don't copy Mila, Sergey, Angelina, Ksyusha or
Sasha with a changed name: create a new causal combination of biography, environment,
trigger, details and path.
