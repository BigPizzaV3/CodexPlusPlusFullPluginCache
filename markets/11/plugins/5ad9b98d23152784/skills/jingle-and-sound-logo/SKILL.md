# Jingles and sound logos

A jingle or a sound logo is a short, recognisable form. Its structure, density and duration
follow its role in the clip, the music brief and the contract of the chosen model.

## Choosing the form

Pick the form to fit the brand's task. Several approaches are valid; choose by context:

- **Cold hook** — a musical hook at the very start, when the result must grab attention
  immediately.
- **Mnemonic (sound logo)** — a short recognisable motif that works as a sonic signature.
- **Tagline sting** — a musical sting under a spoken slogan. If a voice is needed, this links
  to the voiceover (see the voiceover-for-video skill).

If the brief does not make clear what is needed, propose a form yourself and name your choice.

## Duration

Take the duration from the request, from the jingle's place in the clip and from the values the
model supports. If the jingle falls into a known edit interval, fit the form to that interval
without silently shortening or stretching the user's material.

## Tie to the brand mood

Put together a short mood brief before you assemble the prompt:

1. **Brand character** — energetic, premium, warm, technological or playful.
2. **Tempo and energy** — fast and upbeat, or calm and confident.
3. **Instrument palette** — what sets the character (for example, synths for a tech brand,
   acoustic instruments for a warm one). Propose it yourself if the user has not set it.

## Restraint

The density of the arrangement follows the brief. Bring out a recognisable hook and describe
only the instruments, changes and sound qualities that the requested result needs. Do not add
limits on the number of elements without a reason in the request or in the model's contract.

## Final brief

The final brief pins down the form, the memorable hook, the mood, the instrument palette and the
duration.
