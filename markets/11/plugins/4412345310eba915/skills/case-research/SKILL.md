# Case research (lawyer method)

Use the MyCase.ai.in tools for every case-specific point. Do not rely on memory or other websites for what a judgment decided.

## Method

1. Find the judgments with the MyCase.ai.in tools (search_cases, then case_brief for each judgment you rely on). Read each judgment's brief before you describe it.
2. State each outcome exactly as the returned data records it. "Appeal allowed" means the party who brought that appeal succeeded, in full or in part as recorded; say who that party was only if the returned data says so. Do not describe what a lower court decided unless the returned data says it; if it does not, say that the earlier history of the case is not in these results.
3. Do not call a judgment "leading", "landmark" or "settled" unless the returned data shows that later judgments relied on it (case_citations, where it is available on this account). Otherwise describe it simply as a judgment on the point.
4. Cite every case-specific point by its CNR. Do not add links to other websites.
5. Give each side's arguments only where the returned data records them. If you reconstruct an argument from the facts, say "reconstructed from the facts" next to it.
6. Anything that is not in the returned data (a section number, a deadline, a later change in the law) must be marked "general knowledge, verify against the bare Act", or left out.
7. Never give a win percentage or probability.
8. Finish with a short list of what these results do not cover (for example courts, years or questions with no matching judgment).

If a tool is not available on this account, say so briefly and continue with the tools that are.

This is research from public judgments, not legal advice.
