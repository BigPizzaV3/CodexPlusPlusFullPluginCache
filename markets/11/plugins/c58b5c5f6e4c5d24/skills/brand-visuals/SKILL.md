# Brand visuals

## When this applies

Any image for one app that must look like that app: screenshots, the App Store header, an event card, Google Play graphics, an icon, a campaign variant. Also reading, filling or changing the app's brand guide. What a listing needs and the stores' rules belong to store-visuals; an event's theme and dates to live-moments.

## The job and what good looks like

The person gets an image that someone who knows the app recognizes at once: same characters or product, line, shading and colours, at the size the store shows it. The brand guide holds what makes the app itself: colours and their roles, typefaces, what to keep, what may change and what to avoid, notes, reference images and a recipe per kind of visual. Each reference has a role. Identity shows what must stay recognizable; finish shows the rendering to match; composition shows placement only, never style.

## How a pro decides

- **Look, not list.** Read the guide and open its identity and preferred finish images. A file name, a link or a description is not a look.
- **A brief of constraints.** Write it from the guide's keep, may change and avoid, and from its recipe for this kind of visual, turned into concrete terms: outline weight, face or product shapes, material and shading, colour treatment, density of detail. "Match the brand" or "premium" alone is no brief.
- **Name the app.** The generators apply its guide only when they know which app it is.
- **Judge against the references.** Look at the result beside them at the size it is shown; compare identity, line, shading, density and silhouette. An attractive image that changes the drawing language is a miss.
- **Correct, don't reroll.** Name the specific drift and correct that, at most twice; past that, report what still differs.
- **Different, not decorated.** A new campaign changes through scene, action, framing and subject, not a tiny seasonal prop or a colour shift.

## Do

- Say which references and which preferred finish guided the result.
- When the guide has no references, or none fit this visual, say what is missing and ask for it.
- Keep prices, claims and words that must stay editable out of the image when the guide says so.
- Label a direction for an app without a guide as a proposal until the person chooses it.
- Save an approved result, a new rule or a new preferred finish into the guide when the person says so.

## Don't

- Don't present a generic category image as the app's when references are missing.
- Don't take the icon as sole reference for a full illustration when richer images exist.
- Don't invent offers, discounts, features or seasonal content as product facts.
- Don't take a passing technical check for the person's yes to a new preferred style.

## Pitfalls

- A seasonal finish marked preferred leaks its theme into an evergreen visual: inherit its drawing, not its season.
- Newest is not preferred, and kept is not approved.
- Drift hides at full size; at the store's size a thinner line or a changed face shows.
- A generator's own check against the references does not replace yours.
