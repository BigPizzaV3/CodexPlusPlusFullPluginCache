# Acquisition pages

## When this applies

Giving one audience, search intent or campaign its own page: a custom product page on the App Store, a custom store listing on Google Play, or the app's landing page for traffic that starts outside the stores. The main listing's words belong to store-copy and its images to store-visuals.

## The job and what good looks like

Each page answers what its visitor came for in its first screen and first headline: someone who searched for a bass tuner lands on a bass. A page has its own concept, a subject, a scene or a palette, staged around the app's real interface, with headlines in the words of the query. A landing page tells in one screen what the app is, why it is good and where to get it, with facts from the store listing. A few pages that someone measures beat many that nobody looks at, since each one costs review and upkeep.

## How a pro decides

- **Store page or landing page.** When the traffic arrives in the store, from search or a store ad, make a store page. When it comes from the web, social networks, a QR code or an email, make a landing page.
- **Group by intent, not by word.** Words that express one need make one page. Rank the groups by the traffic they bring and the demand behind them, and prefer words the app already ranks for.
- **What changes and what stays.** Say what each page changes, such as the subject, the scene or the season, and what it keeps: the app's real interface, its brand type and its layout.
- **Which screen leads.** Reorder so the screen answering the query comes first; design a new one when no existing screen shows the feature.
- **Keep or retire.** Compare each page with the default page over time; rework or retire one that converts worse.

## Do

- Start each page in the app's primary language and add languages once it works.
- Give the person the campaign link of each page they will use in an ad.
- Write a landing page natively in each language, from the store description.
- Show every page of a batch, not only the last one.

## Don't

- Don't stage a feature the app does not have: it costs a rejection and a bad review.
- Don't reuse iPhone or iPad renders on Google Play.
- Don't put one shared sentence on every screen of every page.
- Don't make pages nobody will measure.
- Don't invent a rating, a review, a download count, an award or a link.
- Don't set the landing page as the listing's marketing link unless the person asks.

## Pitfalls

- A keyword the app does not rank for never brings anyone to its page.
- A word another page already holds needs a decision to move it, not a duplicate.
- A page created halfway exists: fix it rather than making a second one.
- Taking a landing page offline breaks every ad, QR code and listing that points at it.
