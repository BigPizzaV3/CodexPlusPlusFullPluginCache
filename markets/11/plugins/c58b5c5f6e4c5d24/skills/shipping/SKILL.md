# Shipping

## When this applies

Moving a version from preparation to the public on either store: its number and build, testers, the submission to App Review, the release after review, Google Play's tracks and rollouts, and reading a rejection. The listing's words belong to store-copy; privacy answers and the policy to privacy-and-legal.

## The job and what good looks like

The person knows in one sentence where the app stands: which version, which build, what Apple or Google holds and what is left to them. Every public step happens after they saw its full content: what the submission carries, which build goes out, which release action follows. Statuses come from a fresh read of the app, and an older one is given with the time it was read. Nothing about a release is guessed.

## How a pro decides

- **Risky or routine.** Offer a phased release for an update that touches purchases, sign-in or a new SDK; the person chooses.
- **Which track.** An Android build starts on internal testing unless the person named another track.
- **What App Review needs to know.** Rewrite the review notes for each version: what changed and how to test it, with a demo account when sign-in is needed.
- **Why it was rejected.** Read this app's rejection and the guideline it cites before answering; a general reason is not this app's reason.
- **Wait or act.** A store that is processing or reviewing needs time, not another attempt.

## Do

- Say how a store wants builds signed before the person goes looking for a file.
- Give the share of users a rollout has reached, since that is what people want to know.
- Ask which build when several could go and none is attached.
- Show the open advice on the version, such as stale notes or a link on another site, before a submission.
- Name the SDKs you see in readable code when export compliance or privacy needs an answer.

## Don't

- Don't add a language to a version unless the person asks.
- Don't guess a version code, a build or an export compliance answer.
- Don't record a check the person has not confirmed in their own words.
- Don't retry a publish or a submission in a loop: each attempt can be real and irreversible.
- Don't answer "why was it rejected?" before reading this app's rejection.

## Pitfalls

- A submission takes everything waiting with it, not just the version.
- Withdrawing from review takes the whole submission back.
- A first release on Google Play can have its own requirements, for new developer accounts and for the target level.
- A build still processing is not missing; it needs minutes, not a new upload.
